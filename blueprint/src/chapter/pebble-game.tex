\section{The \texorpdfstring{$(k, \ell)$}{(k, l)}-pebble game}
\label{sec:pebble-game}

Lee and Streinu~\cite{leeStreinu2008} decide whether a graph is
$(k, \ell)$-sparse by an algorithm, the \emph{$(k, \ell)$-pebble game}.
It generalizes the pebble game of Jacobs and
Hendrickson~\cite{jacobsHendrickson1997} for rigidity in the plane,
which in Lee and Streinu's terms is the $(2, 3)$-pebble
game~\cite[p.~1426]{leeStreinu2008}. The game considers the edges of
the input graph one at a time. It keeps the accepted edges oriented,
with pebbles on the vertices, and it accepts an edge when at least
$\ell + 1$ free pebbles can be gathered on its two endpoints. We
formalize Lee and Streinu's basic pebble
game~\cite[\S 3]{leeStreinu2008} on simple graphs, for $\ell < 2k$, the
range they consider. We prove that it accepts every edge of a graph $G$
if and only if $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse. When it rejects an edge, it
returns a set of vertices that shows that $G$ is not $(k, \ell)$-sparse.

In \cref{sec:pebble-game-state} we describe the state of the game, a
partial orientation from which the free pebbles are counted, and its
two moves, which reverse a directed path and insert an arc. In
\cref{sec:pebble-game-invariants} we prove Lee and Streinu's four
invariants for the orientations that these moves reach. In
\cref{sec:pebble-game-algorithm} we define the algorithm; it finds
pebbles with the depth-first search of \cref{sec:dfs}. We prove the two
directions of correctness in \cref{sec:pebble-game-soundness} and
\cref{sec:pebble-game-completeness}, and combine them in
\cref{sec:pebble-game-correctness}. The first direction follows from the
fourth invariant. For the second, we show that when the game rejects an
edge, the set $V'$ of vertices reachable from its endpoints spans more
than $k|V'| - \ell$ edges of $G$. In the formalization, the game
returns its answer together with these proofs, as a verdict
(\cref{sec:pebble-game-verdict}). In
\cref{sec:pebble-game-matroid} we return to the $(k, \ell)$-count
matroid of \cref{sec:count-matroid}: the game decides whether the edge
set of a graph is independent in it, and so, at $(k, \ell) = (2, 3)$,
in the planar rigidity matroid.

Lee and Streinu play the game on multigraphs, which may have loops and
parallel edges. On simple graphs their invariants simplify
(\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-specialisation}). We do not formalize their
component pebble game (\S 5). It maintains the components of the
accepted graph (\cref{sec:count-matroid}), rejects an edge at once when
both its endpoints lie in one component, and runs in time $O(n^2)$ on
$n$ vertices~\cite[p.~1436]{leeStreinu2008}. For the details of its
implementation, Lee and Streinu refer to Lee, Streinu and
Theran~\cite{leeStreinuTheran2005}, who introduce for them a data
structure problem, union pair-find. Nor do we
formalize the Henneberg sequences of Lee and Streinu's \S 6, which
extend the Henneberg construction (\cref{sec:henneberg}) from Laman
graphs to tight graphs.

Lee and Streinu bound the running time of their basic game by
$O(m_a n)$, where $m_a$ is the number of accepted edges, counting at
most $\ell + 1$ depth-first searches for each
edge~\cite[p.~1430]{leeStreinu2008}. Our pebble search marks only the
vertices of the walk it follows, so it may visit a vertex once for each
simple walk to it (\cref{sec:dfs}). Their count therefore does not
apply to it, and we prove no bound on the running time of our game.

\subsection{State and moves}
\label{sec:pebble-game-state}

Lee and Streinu play the pebble game on a directed graph $D$ on $V$,
which starts with no edges and with $k$ pebbles on each
vertex~\cite[\S 3]{leeStreinu2008}. An edge is inserted into $D$
oriented away from an endpoint that holds a pebble, and that pebble is
removed. On a simple graph, $D$ has no loops and at most one arc
between two vertices, so it is a partial orientation
(\cref{def:partial-orientation}). By Lee and Streinu's
Invariant~(1)~\cite[Lemma~10]{leeStreinu2008}, the number of free
pebbles at a vertex is then $k$ minus its out-degree. So we do not keep
the pebbles separately, and define them from $D$
(\cref{def:pebble-counts}). The game changes $D$ by two moves. It
reverses a directed path, which moves a pebble from the end of the
path to its start (\cref{def:path-reversal}). It also inserts arcs
(\cref{def:arc-insertion}).

\begin{definition}[Partial orientation]
  \label{def:partial-orientation}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.underline}
  \leanok
  Let $V$ be a set. A \emph{partial orientation} on $V$ is a finite set
  $D \subseteq V \times V$ such that:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item (no loops) $(v, v) \notin D$ for every $v \in V$;
    \item (no antiparallel pairs) if $(u, v) \in D$, then
      $(v, u) \notin D$.
  \end{enumerate}
  The elements of $D$ are its \emph{arcs}, and an arc $(u, v)$ has
  \emph{tail} $u$ and \emph{head} $v$. An edge $uv$ of the complete
  graph $K_V$ is \emph{oriented} in $D$ if $(u, v) \in D$ or
  $(v, u) \in D$. The \emph{underlying edge set}
  $\underline{D} \subseteq E(K_V)$ is the set of edges oriented in $D$.
  The \emph{empty orientation} has no arcs.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Out-degree, free pebbles and span]
  \label{def:pebble-counts}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.out,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.peb,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.span,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.outOn,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.pebOn}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation}
  Let $D$ be a partial orientation on $V$, and let $k$ be a
  non-negative integer. For a vertex $v$, the \emph{out-degree}
  $\mathrm{out}_D(v)$ is the number of arcs of $D$ with tail $v$, and
  the number of \emph{free pebbles} at $v$ is
  \[
    \mathrm{peb}_{D,k}(v) = \max\bigl(k - \mathrm{out}_D(v), 0\bigr).
  \]
  For a finite set $V' \subseteq V$, let
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathrm{peb}_{D,k}(V') = \sum_{v \in V'}
      \mathrm{peb}_{D,k}(v)$;
    \item $\mathrm{span}_D(V')$ be the number of arcs of $D$ with both
      ends in $V'$;
    \item $\mathrm{out}_D(V')$ be the number of arcs of $D$ with tail in
      $V'$ and head outside $V'$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

These are Lee and Streinu's functions $\mathrm{peb}$, $\mathrm{span}$
and $\mathrm{out}$~\cite[\S 4]{leeStreinu2008}, for a graph without
loops. The maximum with $0$ matters only when $\mathrm{out}_D(v) > k$.
This does not happen at the orientations the game reaches, the
$(k, \ell)$-reachable ones of \cref{def:reachable-orientation}, as the
proof of Invariant~(1) in \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants} shows.

\begin{fmlnote}
  In order, the five pins define $\mathrm{out}_D(v)$,
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D,k}(v)$, $\mathrm{span}_D(V')$, $\mathrm{out}_D(V')$
  and $\mathrm{peb}_{D,k}(V')$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{definition}[Path reversal]
  \label{def:path-reversal}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.reverse,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.underline_reverse_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation}
  Let $D$ be a partial orientation on $V$. A \emph{directed path} in
  $D$ from $u_0$ to $u_m$ is a simple directed walk
  $u_0, u_1, \ldots, u_m$ along the arcs of $D$
  (\cref{def:directed-walk}): the vertices $u_i$ are distinct, and
  $(u_{i-1}, u_i) \in D$ for $1 \le i \le m$. \emph{Reversing} the
  path replaces $D$ by the partial orientation
  \[
    D' = \bigl(D \setminus \{(u_{i-1}, u_i) : 1 \le i \le m\}\bigr)
      \cup \{(u_i, u_{i-1}) : 1 \le i \le m\}.
  \]
  The underlying edge sets of $D'$ and $D$ are equal.
\end{definition}

The set $D'$ is a partial orientation. It has no loops, since the
vertices of the path are distinct. Each arc of $D'$ is either an arc
of $D$ off the path or the reverse $(u_i, u_{i-1})$ of an arc of the
path. Two arcs of $D$ off the path are not antiparallel, since $D$ has
no antiparallel pairs. The arc $(u_i, u_{i-1})$
is not antiparallel to an arc of $D$ off the path, since
$(u_{i-1}, u_i)$ is on the path. Nor is it antiparallel to the reverse
of another arc of the path, since a path with distinct vertices does
not use the edge $u_{i-1} u_i$ twice.

Let $m \ge 1$. Reversing the path lowers $\mathrm{out}_D(u_0)$ by $1$,
raises $\mathrm{out}_D(u_m)$ by $1$, and leaves every other
out-degree unchanged: an inner vertex $u_i$ loses the arc
$(u_i, u_{i+1})$ and gains the arc $(u_i, u_{i-1})$. Suppose that
$\mathrm{out}_D(u_0) \le k$ and $\mathrm{out}_D(u_m) < k$. Then $u_0$
gains a free pebble and $u_m$ loses one, so the move takes a pebble
from $u_m$ to $u_0$. This is the move by which Lee and Streinu collect
a pebble~\cite[\S 3, Fig.~4]{leeStreinu2008}.

\begin{definition}[Arc insertion]
  \label{def:arc-insertion}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.addArc,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.underline_addArc}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation}
  Let $D$ be a partial orientation on $V$, and let $u \ne v$ be
  vertices with $(v, u) \notin D$. \emph{Inserting} the arc $(u, v)$
  replaces $D$ by the partial orientation $D \cup \{(u, v)\}$. Its
  underlying edge set is $\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$.
\end{definition}

The set $D \cup \{(u, v)\}$ is a partial orientation, since $u \ne v$
rules out a loop and $(v, u) \notin D$ rules out an antiparallel pair.
If the arc is new, that is, if $(u, v) \notin D$, the insertion raises
$\mathrm{out}_D(u)$ by $1$ and leaves every other out-degree
unchanged. If moreover $\mathrm{out}_D(u) < k$, then $u$ loses a free
pebble. This is Lee and Streinu's edge
insertion~\cite[\S 3, Fig.~4]{leeStreinu2008}.

\subsection{Invariants}
\label{sec:pebble-game-invariants}

Lee and Streinu~\cite[Lemma~10]{leeStreinu2008} prove four invariants
of their game. We prove them for the
orientations obtained from the empty one by the two moves, each made
under the conditions the game imposes on it
(\cref{def:reachable-orientation}). Invariant~(4) bounds the number of
underlying edges inside a set of vertices by the sparsity count, and
the soundness of the game (\cref{sec:pebble-game-soundness}) follows
from it.

\begin{definition}[$(k, \ell)$-reachable orientation]
  \label{def:reachable-orientation}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:path-reversal, def:arc-insertion}
  Let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers. The
  \emph{$(k, \ell)$-reachable} partial orientations on $V$ form the
  smallest set of partial orientations that contains the empty
  orientation and is closed under the following two moves, applied to
  a member $D$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item reversing a directed path in $D$ from $u_0$ to $u_m$ with
      $\mathrm{out}_D(u_m) < k$ (\cref{def:path-reversal});
    \item inserting an arc $(u, v)$ with $u \ne v$, $(u, v) \notin D$,
      $(v, u) \notin D$, $\mathrm{out}_D(u) < k$ and
      $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) + \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \ge \ell + 1$
      (\cref{def:arc-insertion}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Invariants of the pebble game]
  \label{lem:pebble-game-invariants}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.out_le,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.peb_add_out_eq,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.pebOn_add_span_add_outOn,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.pebOn_add_outOn_ge,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.span_add_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:reachable-orientation, def:arc-insertion, def:path-reversal}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers
  with $\ell < 2k$, and let $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$. Let $v \in V$, and let $V' \subseteq V$ with
  $\ell \le k|V'|$. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) + \mathrm{out}_D(v) = k$;
    \item $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \mathrm{span}_D(V') +
      \mathrm{out}_D(V') = k|V'|$;
    \item $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \mathrm{out}_D(V') \ge \ell$;
    \item $\mathrm{span}_D(V') \le k|V'| - \ell$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:reachable-orientation, def:arc-insertion, def:path-reversal}
  We argue by induction on the moves that produce $D$ from the empty
  orientation (\cref{def:reachable-orientation}).

  For (1) it suffices to show that $\mathrm{out}_D(v) \le k$, by the
  definition of $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v)$. All out-degrees are $0$ at the
  empty orientation. Reversing a path raises no out-degree other than
  that of its last vertex $u_m$, which it raises by at most $1$, and
  the move requires $\mathrm{out}_D(u_m) < k$. Inserting an arc raises
  no out-degree other than that of its tail $u$, which it raises by
  $1$, and the move requires $\mathrm{out}_D(u) < k$.

  For (2) we need no induction. Summing (1) over $V'$ gives
  \[
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \sum_{v \in V'} \mathrm{out}_D(v) = k|V'|.
  \]
  An arc with tail in $V'$ has its head either in $V'$ or outside it.
  So the sum of the out-degrees over $V'$ is
  $\mathrm{span}_D(V') + \mathrm{out}_D(V')$.

  For (3), at the empty orientation the left-hand side is
  $k|V'| \ge \ell$. Reversing a path does not change
  $\mathrm{span}_D(V')$, since it replaces each arc of the path by an
  arc with the same ends. By (2), before and after the move, it does not
  change $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \mathrm{out}_D(V')$ either.
  Inserting a new arc $(u, v)$ with $\mathrm{out}_D(u) < k$ lowers
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V')$ by $1$ if $u \in V'$, and leaves it
  unchanged otherwise. It raises
  $\mathrm{out}_D(V')$ by $1$ if $u \in V'$ and $v \notin V'$, and
  leaves it unchanged otherwise. So the insertion lowers the left-hand
  side of (3) by $1$ if $u, v \in V'$, and leaves it unchanged
  otherwise. If $u, v \in V'$, then before the move
  \[
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') \;\ge\; \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) +
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \;\ge\; \ell + 1,
  \]
  so the left-hand side is at least $\ell$ after it.

  Part~(4) follows from (2) and (3).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pebble-game-specialisation}
  Lee and Streinu state their Lemma~10 for multigraphs, and the
  setting here simplifies it in two ways. First, a simple graph has no
  loops. Their invariant $\mathrm{peb}(v) + \mathrm{span}(v) +
  \mathrm{out}(v) = k$, in which $\mathrm{span}(v)$ counts the loops at
  $v$, therefore becomes (1). Second, they assume $|V'| \ge 1$ when
  $\ell \le k$ and $|V'| \ge 2$ when $k < \ell < 2k$. Both give
  $\ell \le k|V'|$, which is the hypothesis used here. The formal
  statement of (4) is $\mathrm{span}_D(V') + \ell \le k|V'|$, which
  avoids the truncated subtraction of natural numbers. Under
  $\ell \le k|V'|$ the two forms agree. The first two pins are (1), in
  the equivalent form $\mathrm{out}_D(v) \le k$ and as stated, and the
  other three are (2) to (4).
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{The basic algorithm}
\label{sec:pebble-game-algorithm}

Lee and Streinu's basic pebble game~\cite[\S 3, Fig.~4]{leeStreinu2008}
considers the edges of the input graph one at a time. An edge $uv$ is
accepted, and inserted as an arc, when $u$ and $v$ together hold at
least $\ell + 1$ free pebbles. While they hold fewer, the game searches
$D$ for a free pebble on another vertex, reachable from $u$ or from
$v$, and brings it to $u$ or $v$ by reversing a directed path. If there
is no such pebble, the edge is rejected. Lee and Streinu go on after a
rejected edge, in order to classify the input graph. Our game stops at
the first rejected edge, and returns a set of vertices which shows that
the graph is not $(k, \ell)$-sparse.

We define the algorithm in three levels. The pebble search
(\cref{def:tryReachPebble}) looks for a vertex with a given property,
here a free pebble, and reverses the path to it. Processing an edge (\cref{def:tryAddEdge})
repeats pebble searches until it can insert the edge or must reject it.
The pebble game (\cref{def:runPebbleGame}) processes a list of edges in
turn.

The search of \cref{sec:dfs} visits the out-neighbours of a vertex in
the order of a list. In this subsection, each partial orientation $D$
on $V$ comes with a list $N^+_D(a)$ for each vertex $a$, whose entries
are the out-neighbours of $a$ in $D$, that is, the vertices $b$ with
$(a, b) \in D$. Every result below holds for every choice of these
lists.

\begin{definition}[Pebble search]
  \label{def:tryReachPebble}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryReachPebbleWith,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryReachPebble,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.TryReachPebbleResult.newOrient}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts, def:path-reversal,
    thm:reachable-finding-correct}
  Let $D$ be a partial orientation on a finite set $V$, and let each
  vertex $a$ have a list $N^+_D(a)$ whose entries are the
  out-neighbours of $a$ in $D$. Let $P$ be a predicate on $V$, and let
  $v \in V$. The \emph{pebble search for $P$ from $v$ in $D$} runs the
  search for $P$ from $v$ (\cref{def:reachable-finding}) with these
  lists, so that the search follows the arcs of $D$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If the search returns a vertex $w$ and a walk $p$, then $p$
      is a directed path in $D$ from $v$ to $w$, and $P(w)$ holds
      (\cref{thm:reachable-finding-correct}). The pebble search returns
      $w$, $p$, and the partial orientation obtained from $D$ by
      reversing $p$ (\cref{def:path-reversal}).
    \item If the search fails, the pebble search fails.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first two pins are the pebble search, with the lists $N^+_D(a)$
  as an argument and with mathlib's lists
  (\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}). The third computes the
  partial orientation obtained from $D$ by reversing $p$, from the data
  that the search returns.
\end{fmlnote}

When the processing of an edge $uv$ cannot gather $\ell + 1$ free
pebbles on $u$ and $v$, it rejects $uv$ and returns the following data.

\begin{definition}[Failure witness]
  \label{def:workhorseWitness}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.WorkhorseWitness}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:reachable-orientation}
  Let $V$ be a set, and let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers. A
  \emph{failure witness} consists of a partial orientation $D$ on $V$,
  a finite set $V' \subseteq V$, and a pair $(u, v)$ of distinct
  vertices in $V'$, such that:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $D$ is $(k, \ell)$-reachable
      (\cref{def:reachable-orientation});
    \item the edge $uv$ is not oriented in $D$;
    \item no arc of $D$ has its tail in $V'$ and its head outside $V'$,
      that is, $\mathrm{out}_D(V') = 0$;
    \item $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') \le \ell$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A failure witness does not mention a graph. Let $\ell < 2k$, and let
$G$ be a finite simple graph on $V$ whose edges include $uv$ and the
edges in $\underline{D}$. Then $\ell \le k|V'|$, and $V'$ spans more
than $k|V'| - \ell$ edges of $G$
(\cref{lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}).

\begin{definition}[Processing an edge]
  \label{def:tryAddEdge}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryAddEdgeWith,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryAddEdge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:tryReachPebble, def:partial-orientation, def:arc-insertion}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, and let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative
  integers. Let each partial orientation on $V$ come with lists
  $N^+_D(a)$ as in \cref{def:tryReachPebble}. Let $D$ be a
  $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial orientation on $V$, and let $u \ne v$
  be vertices such that the edge $uv$ is not oriented in $D$. Let $P_D$
  be the predicate that holds at a vertex $w$ when $w \ne u$, $w \ne v$
  and $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(w) > 0$. \emph{Processing $uv$ at $D$} runs
  as follows.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) + \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \ge
      \ell + 1$, it accepts $uv$. It inserts the arc $(u, v)$ if
      $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) > 0$, and the arc $(v, u)$ otherwise
      (\cref{def:arc-insertion}), and returns the result.
    \item Otherwise, it runs the pebble search for $P_D$ from $u$ in
      $D$. If this search does not fail, it processes $uv$ at the
      partial orientation $D'$ that the search returns.
    \item Otherwise, it runs the pebble search for $P_D$ from $v$ in
      $D$. If this search does not fail, it processes $uv$ at the
      partial orientation $D'$ that the search returns.
    \item Otherwise it rejects $uv$. It returns the failure witness
      made of $D$, the set $\mathrm{Reach}_D(u) \cup \mathrm{Reach}_D(v)$
      and the pair $(u, v)$. Here $\mathrm{Reach}_D(x)$ is the
      reachability region of $x$ in $D$, the set of vertices reachable
      from $x$ along the arcs of $D$ (\cref{def:reachClosureComputable}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

We check that each step is defined and that the processing stops. In
step~1 the inserted arc is new, and its tail has a free pebble, since
$\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \ge \ell + 1 \ge 1$ when
$\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) = 0$. So the insertion is a move of
\cref{def:reachable-orientation}, and the result is
$(k, \ell)$-reachable.

In step~2, the pebble search returns a directed path from $u$ to a
vertex $w$ with $w \ne u, v$ and $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(w) > 0$. So the
path has at least one arc, and $\mathrm{out}_D(w) < k$. Hence $D'$ is
$(k, \ell)$-reachable, and $uv$ is not oriented in $D'$, since the
reversal keeps the underlying edge set. Since $D$ is
$(k, \ell)$-reachable, $\mathrm{out}_D(u) \le k$, as in the proof of
Invariant~(1) in \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants}. So the reversal
raises $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u)$ by $1$. It leaves
$\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v)$ unchanged, since $v$ is not an end of the
path. Step~3 is the same with $u$ and $v$ exchanged. Hence
$\ell + 1 - \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) - \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v)$ drops by
$1$ each time the processing starts again, so step~1 or step~4 is
reached.

Lee and Streinu mark $u$ and $v$ before the search, so that it neither
passes through them nor moves their
pebbles~\cite[\S 3, Fig.~4]{leeStreinu2008}. Here the predicate $P_D$
only excludes $u$ and $v$ as ends of the path. A search may pass
through $u$ or $v$, but reversing a path does not change the
out-degrees of its inner vertices.

The data returned in step~4 form a failure witness. By hypothesis, $D$
is $(k, \ell)$-reachable and $uv$ is not oriented in $D$. The set
$V' = \mathrm{Reach}_D(u) \cup \mathrm{Reach}_D(v)$ contains $u$ and
$v$. No arc of $D$ leaves $V'$, since the head of an arc whose tail is
reachable from $u$ is reachable from $u$, and likewise for $v$. Both
pebble searches failed, so no vertex reachable from $u$ or from $v$
satisfies $P_D$ (\cref{thm:reachable-finding-correct}, part~(2)).
Hence every vertex of $V'$ other than $u$ and $v$ has no free pebble,
and
\[
  \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') \;=\; \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) +
  \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \;\le\; \ell.
\]
The set $V'$, and the argument that no arc leaves it, are from Lee and
Streinu's proof of their Lemma~13~\cite{leeStreinu2008}.

When the game rejects a graph, it returns a set of vertices of the
following kind.

\begin{definition}[Blocking witness]
  \label{def:blockingWitness}
  \uses{def:isSparse}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $k$ and $\ell$ be
  non-negative integers. A \emph{blocking witness} for $G$ is a finite
  set $V' \subseteq V$ with $\ell \le k|V'|$ that spans more than
  $k|V'| - \ell$ edges of $G$, that is,
  $|\edgesIn[G]{V'}| + \ell > k|V'|$.
\end{definition}

A blocking witness shows that $G$ is not $(k, \ell)$-sparse
(\cref{def:isSparse}).

\begin{definition}[The pebble game]
  \label{def:runPebbleGame}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:tryAddEdge, def:workhorseWitness, def:partial-orientation}
  Let $V$, $k$, $\ell$ and the lists $N^+_D(a)$ be as in
  \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. Let $D_0$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$, and let $(u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ be a
  list of pairs of vertices. The \emph{pebble game on this list from
  $D_0$} starts with $D = D_0$, and for $i = 1, \ldots, m$ in turn
  runs as follows.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $u_i = v_i$, or $(u_i, v_i) \in D$, or
      $(v_i, u_i) \in D$, it skips the pair.
    \item Otherwise it processes $u_i v_i$ at $D$. If this rejects
      $u_i v_i$, the game stops and returns the failure witness. If it
      accepts $u_i v_i$ and returns $D'$, the game sets $D = D'$.
  \end{enumerate}
  If no pair is rejected, the game returns the final orientation $D$.

  Now let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $\ell < 2k$. The
  \emph{pebble game on $G$} is the pebble game from the empty
  orientation on a list of the edges of $G$ in which each edge $uv$
  appears once, as $(u, v)$ or as $(v, u)$. It \emph{accepts} $G$ if
  this game returns a partial orientation $D$. It \emph{rejects} $G$,
  with the set $V'$, if the game returns a failure witness whose set is
  $V'$.
\end{definition}

The game is defined at every step, since processing an edge returns a
$(k, \ell)$-reachable orientation when it accepts. It stops, since the
list is finite. In \cref{sec:pebble-game-soundness} we show that if the
game accepts $G$ with $D$, then $\underline{D} = E(G)$, and $G$ is
$(k, \ell)$-sparse. In \cref{sec:pebble-game-correctness} we show that
if it rejects $G$ with $V'$, then $V'$ is a blocking witness for $G$.
The formal pebble game on $G$ (\cref{def:runPebbleGame-verdict})
returns its answer together with proofs. With $D$ it returns proofs that
$\underline{D} = E(G)$ and that $D$ is $(k, \ell)$-reachable. With
$V'$ it returns a proof that $V'$ is a blocking witness for $G$, for
which it uses $\ell < 2k$.

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}
  The pebble search, the processing of an edge and the pebble game on
  a list are formalized with the lists $N^+_D(a)$ as an argument, one
  for each partial orientation $D$ and vertex $a$, together with a
  proof that the entries of $N^+_D(a)$ are the out-neighbours of $a$
  in $D$. These forms (the declarations whose names end in ``With'')
  can be computed whenever the lists can, and the results below are
  proved for them. A second form of each procedure takes for
  $N^+_D(a)$ mathlib's list of the elements of the finite set of
  out-neighbours, so it is the first form with particular lists. For
  the pebble game, the second form is the formal pebble game on $G$. It
  also lists the edges of $G$ in this way, and writes each edge as an
  ordered pair by a choice. As in \cref{sec:dfs},
  mathlib lists the elements of a finite set by a choice that cannot be
  computed, so the second forms cannot be computed. In
  \cref{sec:executable} we use lists sorted by a linear order on $V$
  instead, which can be computed.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Soundness}
\label{sec:pebble-game-soundness}

We show that if the pebble game on $G$ accepts $G$, then $G$ is
$(k, \ell)$-sparse. Lee and Streinu prove this direction of their
Theorem~8 first, as part of their
Corollary~12~\cite{leeStreinu2008}. Like theirs, our proof rests on
Invariant~(4) of \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants}. Suppose that the
game accepts $G$ with $D$. It runs on a list in which each edge of $G$
appears once, so it skips no edge, and since it returns $D$, it accepts
every edge. Hence $\underline{D} = E(G)$
(\cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq}). For a set $V'$ of vertices,
$\mathrm{span}_D(V')$ is then the number of edges of $G$ inside $V'$
(\cref{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}), and Invariant~(4) becomes the count
that defines sparsity.

\begin{lemma}[The edges oriented by an accepted run]
  \label{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryAddEdgeWith_underline,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith_underline_subset}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:tryAddEdge, def:runPebbleGame, def:partial-orientation,
    def:path-reversal, def:arc-insertion}
  Let $V$, $k$, $\ell$ and the lists $N^+_D(a)$ be as in
  \cref{def:tryAddEdge}, and let $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $u \ne v$ be vertices such that the edge $uv$ is not
      oriented in $D$. If processing $uv$ at $D$ accepts $uv$ and
      returns $D'$, then $\underline{D'} = \underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$.
    \item Let $(u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ be a list of pairs of
      vertices. If the pebble game on this list from $D$ returns a
      partial orientation $D'$, then
      $\underline{D} \subseteq \underline{D'}$, and every edge in
      $\underline{D'} \setminus \underline{D}$ is $u_i v_i$ for some
      $i$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For (1), processing $uv$ at $D$ stops in step~1 or step~4 of
  \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. Otherwise, in step~2 or step~3, it processes
  $uv$ again at a partial orientation $D''$ obtained from $D$ by
  reversing a directed path. We argue by induction along this
  recursion, which stops, as shown after \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. Step~4
  rejects $uv$, so it is not reached. Step~1 inserts the arc $(u, v)$
  or the arc $(v, u)$, and either way the underlying edge set becomes
  $\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$ (\cref{def:arc-insertion}). In steps~2
  and~3, $\underline{D''} = \underline{D}$ (\cref{def:path-reversal}),
  and processing $uv$ at $D''$ accepts $uv$ and returns $D'$. By
  induction, $\underline{D'} = \underline{D''} \cup \{uv\} =
  \underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$.

  For (2) we argue by induction on $m$. If $m = 0$, then $D' = D$. Let
  $m \ge 1$. If the game skips $(u_1, v_1)$, it plays the rest of the
  list from $D$, and the claim follows by induction. Otherwise it
  processes $u_1 v_1$ at $D$. Since the game returns a partial
  orientation, the processing accepts $u_1 v_1$ and returns a
  $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial orientation $D_1$, with
  $\underline{D_1} = \underline{D} \cup \{u_1 v_1\}$ by (1). The game
  then plays $(u_2, v_2), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ from $D_1$ and returns
  $D'$. The induction hypothesis at $D_1$ gives both claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An accepted run orients every listed edge]
  \label{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith_mem_underline,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith_underline_eq,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGame_edges_discharges}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:tryAddEdge, def:runPebbleGame,
    def:reachable-orientation, lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset,
    lem:pebble-game-invariants}
  Let $V$, $k$, $\ell$ and the lists $N^+_D(a)$ be as in
  \cref{def:tryAddEdge}, and let $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$. Let $(u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ be a list of
  pairs of vertices with $u_i \ne v_i$ and
  $u_i v_i \notin \underline{D}$ for every $i$, such that the edges
  $u_1 v_1, \ldots, u_m v_m$ are distinct. If the pebble game on this
  list from $D$ returns a partial orientation $D'$, then
  $u_i v_i \in \underline{D'}$ for every $i$.

  Moreover, let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let the pairs
  $(u_i, v_i)$ list the edges of $G$, each once, as $(u, v)$ or as
  $(v, u)$. If the pebble game on this list from the empty orientation
  returns $D'$, then $\underline{D'} = E(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We argue by induction on $m$; for $m = 0$ there is nothing to prove.
  Let $m \ge 1$. Since $u_1 \ne v_1$ and $u_1 v_1 \notin \underline{D}$,
  neither $(u_1, v_1)$ nor $(v_1, u_1)$ is an arc of $D$, so the game
  does not skip $(u_1, v_1)$. As in the proof of
  \cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(2), processing
  $u_1 v_1$ at $D$ accepts it and returns a $(k, \ell)$-reachable
  partial orientation $D_1$ with
  $\underline{D_1} = \underline{D} \cup \{u_1 v_1\}$. For $i \ge 2$, the
  edge $u_i v_i$ is neither in $\underline{D}$ nor equal to $u_1 v_1$,
  so the rest of the list satisfies the hypotheses at $D_1$. By
  induction, $u_i v_i \in \underline{D'}$ for $i \ge 2$. Also
  $u_1 v_1 \in \underline{D_1}$, and
  $\underline{D_1} \subseteq \underline{D'}$ by
  \cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(2).

  For the second part, the edges of $G$ are not loops, none of them is
  oriented in the empty orientation, and each is listed once. So the
  first part gives $E(G) \subseteq \underline{D'}$. Conversely, by
  \cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(2), every edge in
  $\underline{D'}$ is one of the $u_i v_i$, and so an edge of $G$.

  The pebble game on $G$ (\cref{def:runPebbleGame}) runs on a list of
  this kind, since a list of the elements of a finite set has no
  repetitions (\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}). So if it
  accepts $G$ with $D$, then $\underline{D} = E(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Arcs and edges inside a set]
  \label{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.span_eq_ncard_edgesIn}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq, def:edgesIn}
  Let $D$ be a partial orientation on $V$, and let $G$ be a simple graph
  on $V$ with $\underline{D} = E(G)$. Then every finite set
  $V' \subseteq V$ satisfies
  \[
    \mathrm{span}_D(V') = |\edgesIn[G]{V'}|.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Map each arc $(a, b)$ of $D$ with both ends in $V'$ to the edge $ab$.
  This edge lies in $\underline{D} = E(G)$ and inside $V'$, so it is in
  $\edgesIn[G]{V'}$. The map is injective: two arcs with the same
  underlying edge are equal or antiparallel, and $D$ has no
  antiparallel pairs. It is surjective: an edge $ab$ of $G$ inside $V'$
  lies in $\underline{D}$, so $(a, b)$ or $(b, a)$ is an arc of $D$, and
  both its ends are in $V'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the pebble game]
  \label{thm:pebble-game-soundness}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith_sound}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:runPebbleGame, lem:pebble-game-invariants, def:isSparse,
    lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq, lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $k$
  and $\ell$ be non-negative integers with $\ell < 2k$. Let the lists
  $N^+_D(a)$ be as in \cref{def:tryAddEdge}, and let the pairs
  $(u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ list the edges of $G$, each once, as
  $(u, v)$ or as $(v, u)$. If the pebble game on this list from the
  empty orientation returns a partial orientation, then $G$ is
  $(k, \ell)$-sparse. In particular, $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse if the
  pebble game on $G$ accepts $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pebble-game-invariants, lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq,
    lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $D$ be the partial orientation returned. It is
  $(k, \ell)$-reachable, since processing an edge returns a
  $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial orientation when it accepts
  (\cref{def:tryAddEdge}). By \cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq},
  $\underline{D} = E(G)$. Let $V' \subseteq V$ with $\ell \le k|V'|$.
  Invariant~(4) of \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants} gives
  $\mathrm{span}_D(V') \le k|V'| - \ell$. By
  \cref{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}, the left-hand side is
  $|\edgesIn[G]{V'}|$. Hence $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse. The pebble game
  on $G$ runs on a list of the kind above
  (\cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq}), which gives the last claim.

  The first claim holds without the hypothesis $\ell < 2k$: neither
  this proof nor that of \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants} uses it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completeness}
\label{sec:pebble-game-completeness}

We now prepare the converse, which \cref{sec:pebble-game-correctness}
completes: if the pebble game on $G$ rejects $G$ with $V'$, then $V'$
is a blocking witness for $G$, so $G$ is not $(k, \ell)$-sparse.

Following Lee and Streinu~\cite[\S 2]{leeStreinu2008}, we call an edge
$uv$ that is not oriented in a partial orientation $D$
\emph{independent} in $D$ if the graph on $V$ with edge set
$\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse. Their Lemma~14
states that the game inserts $uv$ if and only if $uv$ is independent in
$D$. One direction follows from Invariant~(4): if processing $uv$ at
$D$ accepts $uv$, then $uv$ is independent in $D$
(\cref{lem:pebble-game-tryAddEdgeWith-isSparse}).
For the other, Lee and Streinu apply their Lemma~13 repeatedly. While
$u$ and $v$ hold at most $\ell$ free pebbles, their Lemma~13 gives a
free pebble on another vertex reachable from $u$ or from $v$, and
reversing a directed path brings it to $u$ or to $v$. They remark that
the vertices reachable from $u$ and from $v$ may change after such a
move, and that each application of their Lemma~13 needs $uv$ to be
independent~\cite[p.~1433]{leeStreinu2008}. It is, since reversing a
path does not change $\underline{D}$.

We prove Lee and Streinu's Lemma~13 as
\cref{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble-graph}, from a form
that does not mention a graph
(\cref{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble}). For the correctness
of the game, however, we argue at the rejection. Processing $uv$ at $D$
rejects $uv$ only in step~4 of \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. The failure
witness that it returns there shows that $uv$ is not independent in $D$
(\cref{lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}). The inequalities are those of
Lee and Streinu's Lemma~13, read in the contrapositive.

\begin{lemma}[A free pebble for an independent edge, counting form]
  \label{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.independent_brings_pebble}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:reachable-orientation, lem:pebble-game-invariants}
  Let $V$ be a set, let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers, and let
  $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial orientation on $V$. Let
  $u \ne v$ be vertices, and let $V' \subseteq V$ be a finite set that
  contains $u$ and $v$ and that no arc of $D$ leaves, that is,
  $\mathrm{out}_D(V') = 0$. Suppose that
  \[
    \mathrm{span}_D(V') + 1 + \ell \;\le\; k|V'|
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) + \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \;\le\; \ell.
  \]
  Then $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(w) \ge 1$ for some vertex $w \in V'$ other
  than $u$ and $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pebble-game-invariants}
  As in the proof of Invariant~(2) of
  \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants},
  \[
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \mathrm{span}_D(V') + \mathrm{out}_D(V')
    = k|V'|.
  \]
  Since $\mathrm{out}_D(V') = 0$, the first inequality gives
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') \ge \ell + 1$. By the second, $u$ and $v$
  hold at most $\ell$ of these pebbles, so another vertex of $V'$ holds
  one.

  In Lee and Streinu's proof of their
  Lemma~13~\cite{leeStreinu2008}, $V'$ is the set of vertices reachable
  from $u$ or from $v$, and the first inequality follows from the
  independence of $uv$, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble-graph}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A free pebble for an independent edge]
  \label{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble-graph}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.Reachable.independent_brings_pebble_simpleGraph_form}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:pebble-counts,
    def:reachable-orientation, def:isSparse,
    lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble,
    lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers
  with $\ell < 2k$, and let $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$. Let $u \ne v$ be vertices such that the edge $uv$
  is not oriented in $D$ and the graph on $V$ with edge set
  $\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse. If
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(u) + \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(v) \le \ell$, then
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(w) \ge 1$ for some vertex $w \ne u, v$ that is
  reachable from $u$ or from $v$ along the arcs of $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble,
    lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $G$ be the graph on $V$ with edge set $\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$,
  and let $V' = \mathrm{Reach}_D(u) \cup \mathrm{Reach}_D(v)$ be the set
  of vertices reachable from $u$ or from $v$ along the arcs of $D$. Then
  $V'$ contains $u$ and $v$, and no arc of $D$ leaves $V'$, as shown
  after \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. Since $|V'| \ge 2$ and $\ell < 2k$, we
  have $\ell \le k|V'|$. As $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse, $V'$ spans at
  most $k|V'| - \ell$ edges of $G$.

  As in the proof of \cref{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}, the arcs of $D$
  inside $V'$ have distinct underlying edges, and these are edges of $G$
  inside $V'$. The edge $uv$ is one more, since it is not oriented in
  $D$. Hence
  \[
    \mathrm{span}_D(V') + 1 \;\le\; |\edgesIn[G]{V'}| \;\le\;
    k|V'| - \ell,
  \]
  and \cref{lem:pebble-game-independent-brings-pebble} gives the
  vertex $w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A failure witness gives a blocking witness]
  \label{lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.WorkhorseWitness.certifies_against}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:workhorseWitness, lem:pebble-game-invariants,
    lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers with $\ell < 2k$, and let
  $(D, V', (u, v))$ be a failure witness (\cref{def:workhorseWitness}).
  Let $G$ be a finite simple graph on $V$ whose edges include $uv$ and
  the edges in $\underline{D}$. Then $\ell \le k|V'|$ and
  \[
    |\edgesIn[G]{V'}| + \ell \;>\; k|V'|.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pebble-game-invariants, lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  The distinct vertices $u$ and $v$ lie in $V'$, so $|V'| \ge 2$ and
  $\ell < 2k \le k|V'|$. Since $D$ is $(k, \ell)$-reachable,
  Invariant~(2) of \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants} gives
  \[
    \mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') + \mathrm{span}_D(V') + \mathrm{out}_D(V')
    = k|V'|.
  \]
  By the definition of a failure witness, $\mathrm{out}_D(V') = 0$ and
  $\mathrm{peb}_{D, k}(V') \le \ell$. So
  $k|V'| \le \mathrm{span}_D(V') + \ell$.

  As in the proof of \cref{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}, the arcs of $D$
  inside $V'$ have distinct underlying edges, and these are edges of $G$
  inside $V'$. The edge $uv$ is one more, since it lies inside $V'$ and
  is not oriented in $D$. Hence
  $\mathrm{span}_D(V') + 1 \le |\edgesIn[G]{V'}|$, and so
  $k|V'| < |\edgesIn[G]{V'}| + \ell$. Thus $V'$ is a blocking witness
  for $G$ (\cref{def:blockingWitness}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An accepted edge is independent]
  \label{lem:pebble-game-tryAddEdgeWith-isSparse}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.tryAddEdgeWith_isSparse}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:tryAddEdge, def:reachable-orientation, def:isSparse,
    lem:pebble-game-invariants, lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $V$, $k$, $\ell$ and the lists $N^+_D(a)$ be as in
  \cref{def:tryAddEdge}. Let $D$ be a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
  orientation on $V$, and let $u \ne v$ be vertices such that the edge
  $uv$ is not oriented in $D$. If processing $uv$ at $D$ accepts $uv$,
  then the graph on $V$ with edge set $\underline{D} \cup \{uv\}$ is
  $(k, \ell)$-sparse.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pebble-game-invariants, lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}
  Let $G$ be this graph, and let $D'$ be the partial orientation that
  the processing returns. It is $(k, \ell)$-reachable
  (\cref{def:tryAddEdge}). By
  \cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(1),
  $\underline{D'} = \underline{D} \cup \{uv\} = E(G)$. Let
  $V' \subseteq V$ with $\ell \le k|V'|$. As in the proof of
  Invariant~(4) of \cref{lem:pebble-game-invariants},
  $\mathrm{span}_{D'}(V') \le k|V'| - \ell$. By
  \cref{lem:span-eq-ncard-edgesIn}, the left-hand side is
  $|\edgesIn[G]{V'}|$. Hence $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Correctness}
\label{sec:pebble-game-correctness}

We now prove the part of Lee and Streinu's
Theorem~8~\cite[p.~1431]{leeStreinu2008} that concerns sparse graphs:
the game accepts every edge of $G$, an outcome they call
well-constrained or under-constrained (\S 3), if and only if $G$ is
$(k, \ell)$-sparse. One direction is \cref{thm:pebble-game-soundness}.
Lee and Streinu prove the other from their Lemma~15 (p.~1433); here it
follows from part~(2) of the theorem below. We state the theorem for
every list of the edges of $G$ and every choice of the lists
$N^+_D(a)$, so that it applies to each formal form of the game
(\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}).

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of the pebble game]
  \label{thm:pebble-game-correct}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGameWith_correct}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:runPebbleGame, def:workhorseWitness, def:isSparse,
    thm:pebble-game-soundness, lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq,
    lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $k$
  and $\ell$ be non-negative integers with $\ell < 2k$. Let the lists
  $N^+_D(a)$ be as in \cref{def:tryAddEdge}, and let the pairs
  $(u_1, v_1), \ldots, (u_m, v_m)$ list the edges of $G$, each once, as
  $(u, v)$ or as $(v, u)$. Then $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse if and only if
  the pebble game on this list from the empty orientation returns a
  partial orientation. More precisely:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item if the game returns a partial orientation $D$, then
      $\underline{D} = E(G)$, and $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse;
    \item if the game returns a failure witness $(D, V', (u, v))$, then
      $V'$ is a blocking witness for $G$ (\cref{def:blockingWitness}).
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular, $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse if and only if the pebble
  game on $G$ accepts $G$, and if it rejects $G$ with $V'$, then $V'$ is
  a blocking witness for $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pebble-game-soundness, lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq,
    lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}
  Part~(1) is \cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq} and
  \cref{thm:pebble-game-soundness}.

  For part~(2), we first show that every partial orientation that the
  game reaches orients only edges of $G$. We argue by induction on the
  list, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(2). The empty
  orientation orients no edge. When the game accepts $u_i v_i$, the
  underlying edge set gains only the edge $u_i v_i$
  (\cref{lem:runPebbleGameWith-underline-subset}~(1)), and $u_i v_i$ is
  an edge of $G$.

  Now suppose that the game returns a failure witness
  $(D, V', (u, v))$. Then for some $i$, processing $u_i v_i$ at a
  partial orientation $D_{i-1}$ that the game has reached rejects
  $u_i v_i$. By step~4 of \cref{def:tryAddEdge}, $(u, v) = (u_i, v_i)$,
  so $uv$ is an edge of $G$. The processing reaches $D$ from $D_{i-1}$ by
  reversing directed paths in steps~2 and~3. So
  $\underline{D} = \underline{D_{i-1}}$ (\cref{def:path-reversal}), and
  every edge in $\underline{D}$ is an edge of $G$
  (\texttt{runPebbleGameWith\_\allowbreak{}witness\_\allowbreak{}bridges}).
  Since $G$ is finite,
  \cref{lem:workhorseWitness-certifies} shows that $V'$ is a blocking
  witness for $G$. This is the only step of the proof that uses
  $\ell < 2k$.

  The game returns either a partial orientation or a failure witness.
  If $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse, then no set of vertices is a blocking
  witness for $G$, by \cref{def:isSparse}. So by (2), the game returns a
  partial orientation. The converse is (1). The pebble game on $G$
  (\cref{def:runPebbleGame}) runs on a list of the kind above, which
  gives the last claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The verdict}
\label{sec:pebble-game-verdict}

We now define the verdicts and the formal pebble game on $G$, which
returns one, and restate \cref{thm:pebble-game-correct} for them. The
formal pebble game on $G$ uses mathlib's lists
(\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}). The computable pebble
game on $G$ of \cref{sec:executable} differs from it only in its lists,
which are sorted by a linear order on $V$. It also returns a verdict,
for which the restated theorem holds as well
(\cref{thm:runPebbleGameExec-isAccept-iff}).

\begin{definition}[Verdict]
  \label{def:pebbleGameResult}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PebbleGameResult,
    CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PebbleGameResult.isAccept}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:partial-orientation, def:reachable-orientation, def:isSparse,
    def:edgesIn, def:workhorseWitness, def:blockingWitness}
  Let $G$ be a finite simple graph on $V$, and let $k$ and $\ell$ be
  non-negative integers. A \emph{verdict} on $G$ is one of the
  following:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item an \emph{acceptance}, given by a $(k, \ell)$-reachable partial
      orientation $D$ on $V$ with $\underline{D} = E(G)$
      (\cref{def:reachable-orientation});
    \item a \emph{rejection}, given by a blocking witness $V'$ for $G$
      (\cref{def:blockingWitness}).
  \end{enumerate}
  A verdict \emph{accepts} $G$ if it is an acceptance.
\end{definition}

A verdict on $G$ shows whether $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse. An acceptance
given by $D$ shows that $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse, as in the proof of
\cref{thm:pebble-game-soundness}. A rejection shows that $G$ is not
$(k, \ell)$-sparse (the paragraph after \cref{def:blockingWitness}).

\begin{definition}[The formal pebble game]
  \label{def:runPebbleGame-verdict}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGame}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:runPebbleGame, def:pebbleGameResult,
    lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq, lem:workhorseWitness-certifies,
    def:reachClosureComputable, lem:mem-reachClosureComputable}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $k$
  and $\ell$ be non-negative integers with $\ell < 2k$. The
  \emph{formal pebble game on $G$} runs the pebble game from the empty
  orientation on mathlib's list of the edges of $G$, with mathlib's
  lists of out-neighbours as the lists $N^+_D(a)$
  (\cref{fmlnote:pebble-game-computable-core}). It returns the verdict
  on $G$ (\cref{def:pebbleGameResult}) that is an acceptance, given by
  $D$, if the game returns a partial orientation $D$, and a rejection,
  given by $V'$, if the game returns a failure witness whose set is
  $V'$.
\end{definition}

The verdict is well defined. Mathlib's list of the edges of $G$ lists
each edge once, as $(u, v)$ or as $(v, u)$
(\cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq}). So the game is the pebble game
on $G$ (\cref{def:runPebbleGame}). If it returns $D$, then
$\underline{D} = E(G)$ by the same lemma, and $D$ is
$(k, \ell)$-reachable, since processing an edge returns a
$(k, \ell)$-reachable orientation when it accepts. If it returns a
failure witness, then the witness's pair is an edge of $G$, and every
edge oriented by its partial orientation is an edge of $G$, as in the
proof of \cref{thm:pebble-game-correct}~(2). Since $\ell < 2k$, the
witness's set $V'$ is then a blocking witness for $G$
(\cref{lem:workhorseWitness-certifies}).

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of the pebble game, for its verdict]
  \label{thm:pebbleGameResult-isAccept-iff}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.PebbleGame.PartialOrientation.runPebbleGame_isAccept_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pebbleGameResult, thm:pebble-game-correct, def:isSparse,
    def:runPebbleGame-verdict}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$, and let $k$
  and $\ell$ be non-negative integers with $\ell < 2k$. Then $G$ is
  $(k, \ell)$-sparse if and only if the verdict that the formal pebble
  game on $G$ returns accepts $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pebble-game-correct}
  The formal pebble game on $G$ runs the pebble game from the empty
  orientation on a list of the edges of $G$ that contains each edge
  once (\cref{lem:runPebbleGame-underline-eq}). It returns an
  acceptance when this game returns a partial orientation, and a
  rejection when it returns a failure witness
  (\cref{def:runPebbleGame-verdict}). So its verdict accepts $G$ if and
  only if the game returns a partial orientation. By
  \cref{thm:pebble-game-correct}, this holds if and only if $G$ is
  $(k, \ell)$-sparse.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independence in the count matroid}
\label{sec:pebble-game-matroid}

We restate \cref{thm:pebbleGameResult-isAccept-iff} for the
$(k, \ell)$-count matroid of \cref{sec:count-matroid}, whose independent
sets are the $(k, \ell)$-sparse subsets of $E(K_V)$: the pebble game
decides whether the edge set of a graph is independent in it.

\begin{corollary}[The pebble game decides independence in the count matroid]
  \label{cor:pebble-game-countMatroid-indep}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.countMatroid_indep_iff_runPebbleGame}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pebble-game-correct, thm:pebbleGameResult-isAccept-iff,
    def:countMatroid, thm:countMatroid-indep-iff, def:runPebbleGame-verdict}
  Let $V$ be a finite set, let $k$ and $\ell$ be non-negative integers
  with $\ell < 2k$, and let $G$ be a simple graph on $V$. Then $E(G)$ is
  independent in the $(k, \ell)$-count matroid on $V$
  (\cref{def:countMatroid}) if and only if the verdict that the formal
  pebble game on $G$ returns accepts $G$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pebble-game-correct, thm:countMatroid-indep-iff,
    thm:pebbleGameResult-isAccept-iff}
  Since $G$ is simple, $E(G) \subseteq E(K_V)$, and the graph on $V$
  with edge set $E(G)$ is $G$. So $E(G)$ is independent in the count
  matroid if and only if $G$ is $(k, \ell)$-sparse
  (\cref{thm:countMatroid-indep-iff}). By
  \cref{thm:pebbleGameResult-isAccept-iff}, this holds if and only if
  the verdict accepts $G$.
\end{proof}

At $(k, \ell) = (2, 3)$, the count matroid is the planar rigidity
matroid $\mathcal{R}(V)$ (\cref{def:rigidityMatroid}). So the pebble
game decides whether $E(G)$ is independent in $\mathcal{R}(V)$, that
is, whether $E(G)$ is row-independent at some framework on $V$ in
$\R^{2}$ (\cref{thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}). A Laman
graph is a $(2, 3)$-sparse graph with $2|V| - 3$ edges
(\cref{def:isLaman}). So the game, together with a count of the edges,
decides whether $G$ is Laman, and \cref{sec:executable} makes this
decision computable. This is the setting of Jacobs and Hendrickson's
pebble game for rigidity in the plane~\cite{jacobsHendrickson1997}.
