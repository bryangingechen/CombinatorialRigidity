\section{The algebraic induction: KT Theorem 5.5 and its three cases}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction}

This chapter proves Katoh and Tanigawa's Theorem~5.5 and the results
that they derive from it~\cite[\S\S5--6]{katohTanigawa2011}. Let
$D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, where $d$ is the dimension. KT Theorem~5.5 states
that every minimal $k$-dof-graph $G = (V, E)$ (\cref{def:k-dof}) with
$|V| \ge 2$ has a panel-hinge realization $(G,p)$ in $\R^d$ with
\[
  \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V| - 1) - k,
\]
where $R(G,p)$ is the rigidity matrix (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}). If
$G$ is simple, the realization is nonparallel: the panels of any two
bodies meet in a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace (KT \S5.1). We
prove the theorem for every $d \ge 2$, in two forms
(\cref{thm:theorem-55}). Every such $G$ has a realization of this rank
whose hinges lie in the panels of the bodies they join and are
nondegenerate, that is, have nonzero extensors. If $G$ is simple, it
also has a panel-hinge realization of this rank in which any two panels
have linearly independent normals.

Katoh and Tanigawa derive their Theorem~5.6 from Theorem~5.5: every
multigraph $G = (V, E)$ has a panel-hinge realization $(G,p)$ with
$\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
where $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the deficiency of the multigraph
$\tilde G = (D-1)\cdot G$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency}). With their
Proposition~2.3, Theorem~5.6 proves the molecular conjecture of Tay and
Whiteley~\cite{tayWhiteley1984} (KT Conjecture~1.2; KT p.~670). We prove
KT Theorem~5.6 and the molecular conjecture for simple graphs
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6}, \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}). For
multigraphs we prove them with nondegenerate hinges that need only lie
in the panels of the bodies they join
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph},
\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}). KT Proposition~2.3
contains the rank formula of Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{jacksonJordan2009} at a generic body-hinge
framework on a multigraph. We prove it in a conditional form: at a
body-hinge framework, a bound on the space of motions that a generic
framework meets gives the formula (\cref{prop:rank-formula-of-motion-bound}).
A later chapter proves the formula at every generic hinge-point
assignment of a multigraph (\cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank}), and KT
Proposition~2.3 itself there (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Field generality.} We work over an arbitrary infinite
field $K$, of any characteristic. Katoh and Tanigawa work over $\R$,
which is the case $K = \R$. The extensor algebra
(\cref{sec:molecular}) and the rigidity matrix
(\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}) need no hypothesis on the field.
The arguments by genericity use only that a nonzero polynomial over an
infinite field does not vanish at every point. For KT Theorems~5.5
and~5.6 and the molecular conjecture, the statement over an arbitrary
infinite field appears to be new. For body-bar frameworks there is a
precedent. In his proof of Tay's theorem, Whiteley chooses a point of an
irreducible variety off the zeros of finitely many polynomials, and he
states this step over any infinite
field~\cite[Proposition~6 and Lemma~7]{whiteley1988}.

\medskip
We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of Theorem~5.5, an induction on
$|V|$ over minimal $k$-dof-graphs of every deficiency $k$ at once
(\cref{thm:minimal-kdof-reduction-all-k}). A graph with at least three
vertices falls into one of four cases (KT p.~671). If $G$ is not
$2$-edge-connected, the realization comes from KT Lemma~6.1. We call the
other three cases Cases~I, II and~III, after KT \S\S6.2--6.4. In Case~I,
$G$ has a proper rigid subgraph (\cref{def:rigid-subgraph}). In Cases~II
and~III, $G$ is $2$-edge-connected and has no proper rigid subgraph, with
$k > 0$ in Case~II and $k = 0$ in Case~III.

The first five subsections set up the frameworks and derive the main
results from the three cases.
\Cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-panel} builds panel-hinge
frameworks from the normals of their panels, in homogeneous coordinates.
The hinge of an edge is the meet of the panels of its ends.
\Cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-withgraph} defines two
operations on frameworks, and
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-prop11} proves the rank formula.
\Cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-base} proves KT Lemma~6.1 and
the base of the induction, states KT Theorem~5.5 and gives its
induction. It then derives KT Theorem~5.6 and the molecular conjecture
for simple graphs, and \cref{sec:molecular-coplanar-multigraph}
proves the results for multigraphs. The remaining subsections prove the
three cases in order: Case~I in
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseI}, Case~II in
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseII}, and Case~III in
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseIII}. Between Cases~I
and~II, \cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-genericity} treats the
rank at generic normals, and
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative} counts rows and
motions. The realization lemmas of all three cases use results of the
second.
