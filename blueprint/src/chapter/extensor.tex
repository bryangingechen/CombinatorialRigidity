\section{Grassmann--Cayley extensor algebra}
\label{sec:molecular}

This chapter sets up the part of the Grassmann--Cayley algebra that
Katoh and Tanigawa use~\cite[\S2.1]{katohTanigawa2011}, and proves
their Lemma~2.1. A point of $K^d$ is written in homogeneous coordinates
in $K^{d+1}$, and the affine subspace spanned by points
$p_1, \dots, p_k$ is represented by its extensor, the product of their
homogeneous coordinate vectors in the exterior algebra of $K^{d+1}$.
KT Lemma~2.1 (\cref{lem:extensor-independence}) says that if
$p_1, \dots, p_{d+1}$ are affinely independent points of $K^d$, then
the extensors of the $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$ sets of $d-1$ of these
points are linearly independent.
\Cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} builds the rigidity matrix of a
body-hinge framework from the extensors of its hinges, and the proof of
KT Theorem~5.5 uses Lemma~2.1 in its last case
(\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction}).

Nothing in the chapter uses an inner product, an order or a hypothesis
on the characteristic, so we work over an arbitrary field $K$; Katoh
and Tanigawa work over $\R$. \Cref{sec:molecular-homog} shows that
points are affinely independent if and only if their homogeneous
coordinate vectors are linearly independent
(\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}). \Cref{sec:molecular-extensor} defines
extensors and their join in the exterior algebra, following Barnabei,
Brini and Rota~\cite{barnabeiBriniRota1985}. It relates extensors to
the Plücker coordinates through which Katoh and Tanigawa define them,
and defines the extensor of an affine subspace.
\Cref{sec:molecular-lemma21} proves KT Lemma~2.1.

\subsection{Homogeneous coordinates and affine independence}
\label{sec:molecular-homog}

As in \cref{sec:body-bar} and \cref{sec:body-hinge}, $d$ is the
dimension and $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$; over $\R$, $D$ is the dimension of
the space of infinitesimal motions of a rigid body in $\R^d$. Following
Katoh and Tanigawa, we give each point of $K^d$ a homogeneous
coordinate vector in $K^{d+1}$.

\begin{definition}[Homogeneous coordinates]
  \label{def:homogeneous-coords}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.homogenize}
  \leanok
  Let $p \in K^d$. The \emph{homogeneous coordinate vector} of $p$ is
  the vector $\bar p = (p, 1) \in K^{d+1}$, obtained by appending the
  coordinate $1$.
\end{definition}

Recall that points $p_1, \dots, p_k$ of $K^d$ are affinely
independent if the only scalars $w_1, \dots, w_k$ with
$\sum_i w_i = 0$ and $\sum_i w_i p_i = 0$ are
$w_1 = \cdots = w_k = 0$.

\begin{lemma}[Affine independence in homogeneous coordinates]
  \label{lem:affine-indep-iff}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.affineIndependent_iff_linearIndependent_homogenize,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.affineIndependent_fin_iff_det_homogenize}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:homogeneous-coords}
  Let $p_1, \dots, p_k \in K^d$. Then $p_1, \dots, p_k$ are affinely
  independent if and only if $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_k$ are linearly
  independent. In particular, $d+1$ points $p_1, \dots, p_{d+1}$ of
  $K^d$ are affinely independent if and only if the
  $(d+1) \times (d+1)$ matrix with rows $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d+1}$
  has nonzero determinant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $w_1, \dots, w_k \in K$. The first $d$ coordinates of
  $\sum_i w_i \bar p_i$ are those of $\sum_i w_i p_i$, and its last
  coordinate is $\sum_i w_i$. So $\sum_i w_i \bar p_i = 0$ if and only
  if $\sum_i w_i p_i = 0$ and $\sum_i w_i = 0$. The linear relations
  among $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_k$ are therefore exactly the families
  $w$ in the definition of affine independence, and one kind of
  independence holds if and only if the other does. The second
  statement follows, since the rows of a square matrix are linearly
  independent if and only if its determinant is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Extensors, the join, and Plücker coordinates}
\label{sec:molecular-extensor}

Let $\bigwedge K^{d+1}$ be the exterior algebra of $K^{d+1}$, and let
$\bigwedge^j K^{d+1}$ be its graded piece of degree $j$, which has
dimension $\binom{d+1}{j}$. An extensor is a product of vectors in this
algebra. We follow Barnabei, Brini and
Rota~\cite[\S3]{barnabeiBriniRota1985}.

\begin{definition}[Extensor]
  \label{def:extensor}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.extensor}
  \leanok
  Let $v_1, \dots, v_j \in K^{d+1}$. The \emph{extensor} of the family
  $(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is the product $v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_j$ in
  $\bigwedge K^{d+1}$. A \emph{$j$-extensor} is an element of
  $\bigwedge K^{d+1}$ of this form.
\end{definition}

A $j$-extensor lies in $\bigwedge^j K^{d+1}$. The extensor of
$(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is linear in each $v_i$ and alternating: it is
zero when two of the $v_i$ are equal, and it changes sign when two of
them are exchanged. So it does not change when a multiple of one $v_i$
is added to another, and it is zero when $v_1, \dots, v_j$ are linearly
dependent. Conversely, let $v_1, \dots, v_j$ be linearly independent,
and extend them to a basis of $K^{d+1}$. The products of basis vectors
over the increasing sequences of indices form a basis of
$\bigwedge K^{d+1}$, and $v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_j$ is one of them.
So the extensor of $(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is nonzero if and only if
$v_1, \dots, v_j$ are linearly independent
(\texttt{extensor\_\allowbreak{}ne\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}linearIndependent}),
as Katoh and Tanigawa recall~\cite[p.~651]{katohTanigawa2011}.
Barnabei, Brini and Rota call only a nonzero product an extensor; like
Katoh and Tanigawa, we allow the zero product.

Barnabei, Brini and Rota call the product of the exterior algebra the
join.

\begin{definition}[Join]
  \label{def:join}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.join}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor}
  Let $A, B \in \bigwedge K^{d+1}$. The \emph{join} of $A$ and $B$ is
  their product $A \vee B = A \wedge B$ in $\bigwedge K^{d+1}$.
\end{definition}

The join is associative, and the join of two extensors is the extensor
of the concatenated family:
\[
  (v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_m) \vee (w_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge w_n)
  \;=\; v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_m \wedge w_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge w_n
\]
(\texttt{join\_extensor}). The extensor of $(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is
therefore the join $v_1 \vee \cdots \vee v_j$ of its vectors, and from
now on we write it so, as Katoh and Tanigawa do. Two bases of one
$j$-dimensional subspace of $K^{d+1}$ have extensors that differ by a
nonzero scalar, and a nonzero $j$-extensor determines the subspace
spanned by its vectors~\cite[Proposition~3.4]{barnabeiBriniRota1985}.
Let $F$ and $G$ be nonzero extensors of subspaces $X$ and $Y$. Then
$F \vee G = 0$ if $X \cap Y \neq 0$, and otherwise $F \vee G$ is an
extensor of $X + Y$~\cite[Proposition~3.5]{barnabeiBriniRota1985}.

Katoh and Tanigawa define extensors through coordinates, which we
define next.

\begin{definition}[Plücker coordinates]
  \label{def:plucker-coords}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pluckerCoord,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pluckerVector}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, def:join}
  Let $v_1, \dots, v_j \in K^{d+1}$, and let $A(v)$ be the
  $j \times (d+1)$ matrix whose $i$-th row is $v_i$. For
  $1 \le i_1 < \cdots < i_j \le d+1$, the \emph{Plücker coordinate}
  $P_{i_1, \dots, i_j}$ of $(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is
  $(-1)^{1 + i_1 + \cdots + i_j}$ times the determinant of the
  $j \times j$ submatrix of $A(v)$ on the columns $i_1, \dots, i_j$.
  The \emph{Plücker coordinate vector} of $(v_1, \dots, v_j)$ is the
  vector of its $\binom{d+1}{j}$ Plücker coordinates.
\end{definition}

The sign is Katoh and Tanigawa's~\cite[p.~651]{katohTanigawa2011}.
Write each $v_i$ in the standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_{d+1}$ and expand
$v_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge v_j$ by multilinearity and alternation. The
products $e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_j}$ with
$i_1 < \cdots < i_j$ form a basis of $\bigwedge^j K^{d+1}$, and the
coefficient of each is the minor of $A(v)$ on the columns
$i_1, \dots, i_j$. So up to the signs, the Plücker coordinate vector
is the coordinate vector of the extensor $v_1 \vee \cdots \vee v_j$.
Katoh and Tanigawa work with the Plücker coordinate vector and call the
extensor its symbolic version. For $j = d+1$ there is one Plücker
coordinate, $\pm \det A(v)$ (\texttt{pluckerCoord\_univ}). For
homogenized points, $\det A(v)$ is the determinant of
\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization does not prove the expansion above, which relates
  the Plücker coordinates to the extensor. The later chapters work with
  the extensor itself and with its coordinates in the basis
  $e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_j}$.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa represent an affine subspace by the extensor of the
homogeneous coordinate vectors of points that span it.

\begin{definition}[Extensor of an affine subspace]
  \label{def:affine-subspace-extensor}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.affineSubspaceExtensor,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.affineSubspaceExtensor_ne_zero_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:homogeneous-coords, def:extensor, def:join}
  Let $p_1, \dots, p_k \in K^d$. The \emph{extensor} of
  $p_1, \dots, p_k$ is
  \[
    C(p_1, \dots, p_k) \;=\; \bar p_1 \vee \cdots \vee \bar p_k
    \;\in\; \textstyle\bigwedge^k K^{d+1}.
  \]
  If $p_1, \dots, p_k$ are affinely independent and span the affine
  subspace $U$, we also write $C(U)$ for $C(p_1, \dots, p_k)$ and call
  it a $k$-extensor \emph{supporting} $U$.
\end{definition}

By \cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}, the points $p_1, \dots, p_k$ are
affinely independent if and only if $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_k$ are
linearly independent. By the criterion after \cref{def:extensor}, this
holds if and only if $C(p_1, \dots, p_k) \neq 0$
(\texttt{affineSubspaceExtensor\_\allowbreak{}ne\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}iff}).
Now let $p_1, \dots, p_k$ be affinely independent with affine span $U$,
and let $W$ be the subspace of $K^{d+1}$ spanned by
$\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_k$. A point $p$ lies in $U$ if and only if
$\bar p \in W$, so $U$ and $W$ determine each other. By Proposition~3.4
of Barnabei, Brini and Rota~\cite{barnabeiBriniRota1985}, $W$
determines its nonzero extensors up to a nonzero scalar, and each of
them determines $W$. So $C(U)$ is determined by $U$ up to a nonzero
scalar, and it determines $U$.

\subsection{Independence of the $(d-1)$-extensors (KT Lemma 2.1)}
\label{sec:molecular-lemma21}

This subsection proves KT Lemma~2.1. Katoh and Tanigawa call it a
fundamental fact about extensors and prove it for
completeness~\cite[pp.~651--652]{katohTanigawa2011}; we follow their
proof.

\begin{lemma}[Extensor independence; KT Lemma 2.1]
  \label{lem:extensor-independence}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.omitTwoExtensor,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.omitTwoExtensor_linearIndependent,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.omitTwoExtensor_linearIndependent_of_li}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:affine-subspace-extensor, def:plucker-coords, lem:affine-indep-iff}
  Let $p_1, \dots, p_{d+1} \in K^{d}$ be affinely independent. Then the
  $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$ extensors
  \[
    \bar p_1 \vee \cdots \vee \widehat{\bar p_a} \vee \cdots
      \vee \widehat{\bar p_b} \vee \cdots \vee \bar p_{d+1}
    \qquad (1 \le a < b \le d+1)
  \]
  are linearly independent in $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$, where a hat
  marks an omitted vector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:join, lem:affine-indep-iff}
  For $a < b$, write $E_{ab}$ for the extensor in the statement: the
  join of the $\bar p_i$ with $i \neq a, b$, in increasing order of
  $i$. Suppose that $\sum_{a < b} \lambda_{ab} E_{ab} = 0$ for scalars
  $\lambda_{ab}$, and fix a pair $a < b$. We join the relation on the
  left with $\bar p_a \vee \bar p_b$, which gives
  \[
    \sum_{c < c'} \lambda_{cc'}\; \bar p_a \vee \bar p_b \vee E_{cc'}
    \;=\; 0 .
  \]

  Every term with $\{c, c'\} \neq \{a, b\}$ vanishes. Indeed, one of
  $a$ and $b$, say $a$, is not in $\{c, c'\}$, so $\bar p_a$ is one of
  the vectors joined in $E_{cc'}$. Then
  $\bar p_a \vee \bar p_b \vee E_{cc'}$ is the join of a family in
  which $\bar p_a$ occurs twice, and it is zero because the join is
  alternating (\cref{def:extensor}).

  The remaining term is
  $\lambda_{ab}\, \bar p_a \vee \bar p_b \vee E_{ab}$. Its join is that
  of the family obtained from $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d+1}$ by a
  bijection of $\{1, \dots, d+1\}$: the first two places go to $a$ and
  $b$, and the remaining $d-1$ places go, in order, to the elements of
  $\{1, \dots, d+1\} \setminus \{a, b\}$ in increasing order. The
  vectors $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d+1}$ are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}), so the reordered family is linearly
  independent too, and its join is nonzero (\cref{def:extensor}).
  Hence $\lambda_{ab} = 0$. As the pair $a < b$ was arbitrary, every
  $\lambda_{ab}$ is zero.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization writes $d = e + 1$, so that the points lie in
  $K^{e+1}$ and each $E_{ab}$ is an $e$-extensor, and it indexes the
  extensors by the pairs $a < b$. The three declarations are $E_{ab}$
  (\texttt{omitTwoExtensor}), the lemma as stated
  (\texttt{omitTwoExtensor\_\allowbreak{}linearIndependent}), and the
  lemma for any $d+1$ linearly independent vectors of $K^{d+1}$
  (\texttt{omitTwoExtensor\_\allowbreak{}linearIndependent\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}li}),
  from which the lemma as stated follows by \cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}.
\end{fmlnote}

The proof uses the points only through the linear independence of
$\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d+1}$, so the lemma holds for any linearly
independent vectors $v_1, \dots, v_{d+1}$ of $K^{d+1}$. Since
$\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ has dimension $\binom{d+1}{d-1} = D$, the
$D$ extensors then form a basis of it. So a linear functional on
$\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ that vanishes on all of them is zero. Katoh
and Tanigawa use the lemma in this way in the last case of the proof
of KT Theorem~5.5: in KT Claim~6.12 for $d = 3$, and in KT Lemma~6.13
for general $d$~\cite[\S6.4]{katohTanigawa2011}. There they choose $d+1$
affinely independent points such that the affine subspace spanned by
any $d-1$ of them lies in one of $d$ given panels. Case~III of
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction} uses the lemma for linearly
independent vectors (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}).
