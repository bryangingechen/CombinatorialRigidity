\section{The matroid \texorpdfstring{$M(\tilde G)$}{M(G~)}, deficiency, and \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-dof graphs}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency}

This chapter develops the combinatorics of Katoh and Tanigawa's
proof~\cite[\S2.5 and \S3]{katohTanigawa2011}. Throughout, $G$ is a
multigraph, $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and $\tilde G = (D-1)\cdot G$ is the
multigraph of \cref{sec:body-hinge}, in which each edge of $G$ is
repeated $D - 1$ times. We study two numbers attached to $\tilde G$: the
rank of a matroid $M(\tilde G)$ on its edges, the union of $D$ copies of
its cycle matroid, and the deficiency $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, a
maximum over the partitions of $V(G)$. Katoh and Tanigawa's
Proposition~2.3, which they credit to Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}, relates both to the rank of the
rigidity matrix $R(G,p)$ of a generic body-hinge framework $(G,p)$
(\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}):
\[
  \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\;
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\;
  \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G).
\]
This chapter proves the second equality, KT eq.~(2.4), and the
inequality $\le$ in the first at every realization. The first is the
rank formula of Jackson and Jord\'an. We prove it after the algebraic
induction (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}).

\Cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-matroid} defines $M(\tilde G)$, the
deficiency, and the $k$-dof-graphs, the multigraphs with
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$. \Cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge}
proves KT eq.~(2.4) from the rank of a matroid union
(\cref{sec:matroid-union}), and characterizes deficiency zero by $D$
edge-disjoint spanning trees (KT Proposition~2.2).
\Cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-rank-bound} bounds the rank of $R(G,p)$
by the deficiency. \Cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-rigid} proves Katoh
and Tanigawa's lemmas on $k$-dof-graphs from their \S3, and computes
the deficiency at cuts, cut vertices and ears. The combinatorial
induction of the next chapter (\cref{sec:molecular-induction}) uses
these results.

\subsection{The matroid \texorpdfstring{$M(\tilde G)$}{M(G~)}, the deficiency and \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-dof-graphs}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-matroid}

This subsection follows Katoh and
Tanigawa~\cite[\S2.5 and the start of \S3]{katohTanigawa2011}. We define
a matroid $M(\tilde G)$ on the edges of $\tilde G$, the deficiency
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, a maximum over the partitions of
$V(G)$, and the partitions that attain it. Every base $B$ of
$M(\tilde G)$ has
$|B| + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (KT eq.~(2.4)),
which we prove in \cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge}. Then we
define the $k$-dof-graphs, the multigraphs $G$ with
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$, and the minimal ones.

We use the notation of \cref{sec:body-bar-matroid-union}: $V(F)$ is
the set of vertices incident with an edge of a set $F$ of edges, and
$\tilde G \restriction F$ is the multigraph with the vertices of
$\tilde G$ and the edges of $F$. Katoh and Tanigawa define $M(\tilde G)$
as the matroid on $E(\tilde G)$ induced by the submodular function
$F \mapsto D(|V(F)| - 1)$ (\cref{def:ofSubmodular}). A set $F$ is
independent in it if and only if $\tilde G \restriction F$ is
$(D, D)$-sparse (\cref{def:graph-sparse}). They remark that this
matroid is the union of $D$ copies of the cycle
matroid~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011}, and we take the union as the
definition.

\begin{definition}[The matroid $M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{def:matroid-MG}
  \lean{Graph.matroidMG, Graph.matroidMG_indep_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:edge-multiply}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. The matroid $M(\tilde G)$ is the union of
  $D$ copies of the cycle matroid of $\tilde G$
  (\cref{def:matroid-union}). A set $F$ of edges of $\tilde G$ is
  independent in $M(\tilde G)$ if and only if
  $\tilde G \restriction F$ is $(D, D)$-sparse.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  Apply \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse} to the multigraph
  $\tilde G$, with $k = D$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  As for the $k$-frame matroid
  (\cref{fmlnote:k-frame-union-restriction}), the formal union is a
  matroid on all pairs $(e, i)$ of an edge label $e$ and an index
  $1 \le i \le D - 1$, in which a pair outside $E(\tilde G)$ is a loop.
  The matroid $M(\tilde G)$ is its restriction to $E(\tilde G)$.
\end{fmlnote}

So $M(\tilde G)$ is Katoh and Tanigawa's matroid. By
\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}, its independent sets are the unions of $D$
forests of $\tilde G$. They are also the sets $F$ such that the
multigraph $\tilde G \restriction F$ has an independent body-bar
framework in $\R^d$, by Tay's theorem (\cref{thm:tay-witness}).

\begin{definition}[The $D$-deficiency]
  \label{def:D-deficiency}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef, Graph.deficiency}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. For a partition $P$ of $V(G)$ into nonempty
  parts, let $d_G(P)$ be the number of edges of $G$ that join different
  parts of $P$. The \emph{$D$-deficiency} of $P$ is
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\; D\,(|P| - 1) \;-\;
      (D-1)\,d_G(P),
  \]
  and the \emph{deficiency} $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the
  largest value of $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)$ over the
  partitions $P$ of $V(G)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:partition-labeling}
  In the formalization a partition of $V(G)$ is given by a labeling of
  the vertices whose labels are again vertices. Its parts are the sets
  of vertices of $G$ with a common label. So $|P|$ is the number of
  labels used on $V(G)$, and an edge joins different parts when its
  ends have different labels. The values of the labelings are integers,
  and the deficiency is their supremum, which is attained because there
  are finitely many labelings.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011} write
$\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = D(|P| - 1) - d_{\tilde G}(P)$, where
$d_{\tilde G}(P) = (D-1)\,d_G(P)$ is the number of edges of $\tilde G$
that join different parts of $P$. If $V(G)$ is nonempty, then the partition $\{V(G)\}$ has value $0$, so
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$. By the theorem of Tutte and
Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961} (KT
Proposition~2.2), $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ if and only if
$\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees. We prove this for
$D \ge 2$ in \cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}.

Several proofs below start from a partition of $V(G)$ of largest value.

\begin{definition}[Tight partitions]
  \label{def:tight-partition}
  \lean{Graph.IsTightPartition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. Following Jackson and
  Jord\'an~\cite[\S2]{jacksonJordan2008}, a partition $P$ of $V(G)$
  is \emph{tight} if it attains the deficiency, that is, if
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Tight partitions exist]
  \label{lem:exists-tight-partition}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isTightPartition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:tight-partition}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph whose vertex set is finite and nonempty. Then
  some partition $P$ of $V(G)$ is tight, that is,
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A finite set has finitely many partitions, so the maximum that
  defines $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is attained.
\end{proof}

The deficiency depends on the dimension $d$ through
$D = \binom{d+1}{2}$. When $d$ varies, we write
$\operatorname{def}_d(G)$ for $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ at this
$D$. So $\operatorname{def}_2(G)$ and $\operatorname{def}_3(G)$ are the
deficiencies at $D = 3$ and $D = 6$, which \cref{sec:main-component}
compares.

\begin{lemma}[On a connected graph the deficiency does not increase with the dimension]
  \label{lem:deficiency-antitone}
  \lean{Graph.Connected.deficiency_le_deficiency_of_le,
        Graph.Connected.deficiency_three_le_deficiency_two,
        Graph.Connected.numParts_le_ncard_crossingEdges_add_one}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a connected multigraph, and let $d \le d'$ be natural
  numbers. Then $\operatorname{def}_{d'}(G) \le \operatorname{def}_d(G)$.
  In particular $\operatorname{def}_3(G) \le \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $D \le D'$ be the values of $\binom{d+1}{2}$ and
  $\binom{d'+1}{2}$, and let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$. At least
  $|P| - 1$ edges of $G$ join different parts of $P$. Indeed, once these
  edges are deleted, each component lies in one part, so there are at
  least $|P|$ components. Deleting an edge raises the number of
  components by at most one, and $G$ is connected. Hence the value of
  $P$ at $D'$ minus its value at $D$ is
  \[
    (D' - D)\bigl(|P| - 1 - d_G(P)\bigr) \;\le\; 0.
  \]
  So the value of $P$ at $D'$ is at most $\operatorname{def}_d(G)$, and
  the lemma follows.
\end{proof}

By Jackson and Jord\'an's rank formula (KT Proposition~2.3;
\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}), a generic realization of $G$ has
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ degrees of freedom
(\cref{def:dof-generic}). This is why Katoh and Tanigawa speak of
$k$-dof-graphs, for $k$ degrees of freedom~\cite[\S3]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{definition}[$k$-dof-graphs and minimal $k$-dof-graphs]
  \label{def:k-dof}
  \lean{Graph.IsKDof, Graph.IsMinimalKDof, Graph.edgeFiber}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph and let $k$ be an integer. Then $G$ is a
  \emph{$k$-dof-graph} if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$. It is a
  \emph{minimal} $k$-dof-graph if moreover, for every edge $e$ of $G$,
  every base of $M(\tilde G)$ meets the set $\tilde e$ of the $D - 1$
  copies of $e$ in $\tilde G$.
\end{definition}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a $k$-dof-graph minimal if removing any edge
gives a graph that is not a $k$-dof-graph, and observe that this is the
condition above~\cite[pp.~656--657]{katohTanigawa2011}. Indeed,
removing an edge $e$ of $G$ leaves the deficiency unchanged if and only
if some base of $M(\tilde G)$ avoids $\tilde e$. This follows from KT
eq.~(2.4) (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank} below), since
$M(\widetilde{G - e})$ is the restriction of $M(\tilde G)$ to
$E(\tilde G) \setminus \tilde e$ (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}).

\subsection{The deficiency and the rank of \texorpdfstring{$M(\tilde G)$}{M(G~)}}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge}

This subsection proves KT eq.~(2.4). Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ have at
least one vertex. Every base of $M(\tilde G)$ has
$\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G)$ elements, so eq.~(2.4) says that
\[
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\;
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G).
\]
We call the right-hand side the \emph{corank} of $M(\tilde G)$. Katoh
and Tanigawa observe eq.~(2.4) from the equivalence of (ii) and (iii)
in their Proposition~2.3. They note that the deficiency is the corank
$|E(\tilde G)| - \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G)$ in the usual sense
when $\tilde G$ has $D(|V(G)| - 1)$
edges~\cite[pp.~655--656]{katohTanigawa2011}.

Since the independent sets of $M(\tilde G)$ are the unions of $D$
forests of $\tilde G$, eq.~(2.4) is the classical formula for the
largest size of such a union, applied to $\tilde G$ (see Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite[Theorem~4.1(a)]{jacksonJordan2009}). We derive it from
the rank of a matroid union (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}). For a
partition $P$ of $V(G)$, the edges of $\tilde G$ inside the parts of
$P$ give
$\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
\le D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (\cref{lem:weak-duality}). Conversely, let $Y$ be a
set of edges at which the rank formula attains its minimum. Then
equality holds at the partition of $V(G)$ into the vertex sets of the
components of $\tilde G \restriction Y$ (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}). We
begin with an upper bound on the rank that follows from sparsity alone.
Finally we characterize deficiency zero by spanning trees.

\begin{lemma}[An upper bound on the rank of $M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{lem:rank-matroidMG-le}
  \lean{Graph.rank_matroidMG_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph with at least one vertex. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \;\le\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $B$ be a base of $M(\tilde G)$. Then $\tilde G \restriction B$ is
  $(D, D)$-sparse (\cref{def:matroid-MG}). If $B$ is empty, the bound
  holds as $|V(G)| \ge 1$. Otherwise sparsity, applied to $B$ itself,
  gives $|B| \le D\,|V(B)| - D \le D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{proof}

For a set $Y$ of edges of $\tilde G$, the multigraph
$\tilde G \restriction Y$ has all the vertices of $G$, and each vertex
outside $V(Y)$ is a component of its own. So the rank of $Y$ in the
cycle matroid of $\tilde G$ is $|V(G)| - c$, where $c$ is the number of
components of $\tilde G \restriction Y$
(\cref{sec:body-bar-matroid-union}).

\begin{lemma}[The rank of the edges inside the parts of a partition]
  \label{lem:rk-within-parts}
  \lean{Graph.rk_cycleMatroid_within_parts_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:edge-multiply}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $r$ be the rank function of the cycle
  matroid of $\tilde G$. Let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$, and let $Y$
  be a set of edges of $\tilde G$ such that the ends of each edge of $Y$
  lie in one part of $P$. Then $r(Y) + |P| \le |V(G)|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The ends of each edge of $Y$ lie in one part of $P$, so each
  component of $\tilde G \restriction Y$ lies in one part. Each part is
  nonempty, so it contains a component, and $\tilde G \restriction Y$
  has at least $|P|$ components. Hence $r(Y) \le |V(G)| - |P|$.
\end{proof}

The proof of \cref{lem:weak-duality} applies the lemma to the set of all
the edges of $\tilde G$ inside the parts of $P$, and the formal proof of
\cref{thm:def-eq-corank} to the set $Y_0$ of its proof.

\begin{lemma}[Weak duality: $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{lem:weak-duality}
  \lean{Graph.rank_add_partitionDef_le, Graph.rank_add_deficiency_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG, lem:rk-within-parts}
  Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ be a multigraph with at least one vertex.
  Then every partition $P$ of $V(G)$ satisfies
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
      \;\le\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1).
  \]
  In particular,
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $r$ be as in \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}, and let $Y$ be the set
  of edges of $\tilde G$ whose ends lie in one part of $P$. The edges of
  $\tilde G$ outside $Y$ are the $D - 1$ copies of each of the $d_G(P)$
  edges of $G$ that join different parts of $P$. The matroid
  $M(\tilde G)$ is the union of $D$ copies of the cycle matroid of
  $\tilde G$ (\cref{def:matroid-MG}). So the rank of a matroid union
  (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}) gives the first inequality of
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \;\le\;
    |E(\tilde G) \setminus Y| + D\,r(Y) \;\le\;
    (D-1)\,d_G(P) + D\,(|V(G)| - |P|).
  \]
  The second holds by \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}. As
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = D(|P| - 1) - (D-1)\,d_G(P)$, this
  is the inequality at $P$. Taking $P$ of largest value gives the
  inequality for $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The deficiency is the corank of $M(\tilde G)$; KT eq.~(2.4)]
  \label{thm:def-eq-corank}
  \lean{Graph.rank_add_deficiency_eq, Graph.isBase_ncard_add_deficiency_eq, Graph.le_rank_add_deficiency}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG, lem:rk-within-parts, lem:weak-duality}
  Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ be a multigraph with at least one vertex.
  Then every base $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$ satisfies
  \[
    |B| + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1).
  \]
  That is,
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) =
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By \cref{lem:weak-duality},
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$. For the reverse inequality we find a partition $P$
  of $V(G)$ with
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
  \ge D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$.

  Let $r$ be the rank function of the cycle matroid of $\tilde G$. By the
  rank of a matroid union (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}), applied
  to the $D$ copies of this matroid, some set $Y_0$ of edges of
  $\tilde G$ satisfies
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \;=\;
    |E(\tilde G) \setminus Y_0| + D\,r(Y_0).
  \]
  Let $P$ be the partition of $V(G)$ into the vertex sets of the
  components of $\tilde G \restriction Y_0$. Then
  $r(Y_0) = |V(G)| - |P|$, as before \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}. The
  ends of an edge of $Y_0$ lie in one component of
  $\tilde G \restriction Y_0$, so in one part of $P$. Hence the $D - 1$
  copies of each of the $d_G(P)$ edges of $G$ that join different parts
  of $P$ lie in $E(\tilde G) \setminus Y_0$, and
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \;\ge\;
    (D-1)\,d_G(P) + D\,(|V(G)| - |P|) \;=\;
    D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P).
  \]
  As $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
  the reverse inequality follows. Every base $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$ has
  $|B| = \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pin is the identity for the rank, the second the identity
  for a base, and the third the reverse inequality. In the third, the
  partition into components is the labeling that sends each vertex to a
  chosen vertex of its component (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}).
  By \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}, applied to $Y_0$, this labeling has
  at most as many parts as there are components. It
  has at least as many, as different components get different labels.
\end{fmlnote}

The characterization of deficiency zero announced in
\cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-matroid} follows from KT eq.~(2.4).

\begin{lemma}[Deficiency zero and $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees; KT Proposition~2.2]
  \label{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_eq_zero_iff_exists_spanningTrees}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a multigraph with at least one vertex.
  Then $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ if and only if $\tilde G$
  contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:def-eq-corank, thm:tutte-nash-williams,
    cor:k-spanning-trees, def:k-dof}
  Suppose that $\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees.
  Each has $|V(G)| - 1$ edges, so their union $F$ has $D(|V(G)| - 1)$
  edges. As a union of $D$ forests, $F$ is independent in $M(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}). As $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$,
  KT eq.~(2.4) (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) gives
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \le D(|V(G)| - 1)$. So $F$ is a base,
  and KT eq.~(2.4) gives $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.

  Conversely, let $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, and let $B$ be a
  base of $M(\tilde G)$. Then $|B| = D(|V(G)| - 1)$ by KT eq.~(2.4). The
  multigraph $H = \tilde G \restriction B$ is $(D, D)$-sparse
  (\cref{def:matroid-MG}), so it is $(D, D)$-tight. It is also
  connected. Otherwise $V(G)$ is the union of two disjoint nonempty sets
  $V_1$ and $V_2$ with no edge of $H$ between them. For $i = 1, 2$, the
  edges of $H$ inside $V_i$ number at most $D|V_i| - D$: by sparsity if
  there are any, and as $|V_i| \ge 1$ if there are none. So
  $|B| \le D|V(G)| - 2D$, against $|B| = D|V(G)| - D$. By
  \cref{cor:k-spanning-trees}, $B$ is the union of $D$ disjoint spanning
  trees of $H$, and these are spanning trees of $\tilde G$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The deficiency and the rank of \texorpdfstring{$R(G,p)$}{R(G,p)}}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-rank-bound}

This subsection bounds the rank of the rigidity matrix $R(G,p)$ of a
body-hinge framework $(G,p)$ (\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}) by
the deficiency of $\tilde G$: first for a framework on $G$, then for one
that also has bodies on no edge of $G$.

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency bound]
  \label{lem:deficiency-rank-bound}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.screwDim_add_deficiency_le_finrank_infinitesimalMotions}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework with $V$ nonempty. Then
  \[
    \dim Z(G,p) \;\ge\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  that is,
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \le D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V$, and let $W_P$ be the space of screw
  assignments that are constant on each part of $P$. It has dimension
  $D|P|$. On $W_P$, the hinge constraint at an edge inside a part always
  holds. At an edge $e$ that joins two parts it consists of $D - 1$
  linear equations, since $C(p(e)) \neq 0$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}).
  So the infinitesimal motions in $W_P$ form a subspace of dimension at
  least
  \[
    D|P| - (D-1)\,d_G(P) \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P).
  \]
  A partition $P$ with
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  gives the bound.
\end{proof}

This bound holds at every realization, generic or not, and it is
one of the two inequalities of the rank formula in
\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}. Jackson and Jord\'an derive its
rank form from a count of independent
rows~\cite[\S4]{jacksonJordan2009}; we count motions instead.

Later chapters consider a framework on a subgraph $G$ of a fixed graph
together with all the bodies of the fixed graph, as
\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix-blocks} does. Its screw assignments
are maps $B \to \bigwedge^{d-1}K^{d+1}$ on a finite set $B$ of bodies
that contains $V(G)$, and $R(G,p)$ and $Z(G,p)$ are defined as before.
A body of $B \setminus V(G)$ lies on no edge of $G$, so no hinge
constraint involves it.

\begin{lemma}[The relative deficiency bound]
  \label{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.screwDim_mul_compl_add_deficiency_le_finrank_infinitesimalMotions,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_add_deficiency_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework whose screw assignments are
  defined on a finite set $B$ of bodies with
  $\emptyset \neq V(G) \subseteq B$, and suppose that $C(p(e)) \neq 0$
  at every edge $e$ of $G$. Then
  \[
    D\,(|B \setminus V(G)| + 1) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;\le\;
      \dim Z(G,p),
  \]
  that is,
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \le D(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$ with
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and
  let $P'$ be the partition of $B$ whose parts are those of $P$ and the
  singletons $\{b\}$ for $b \in B \setminus V(G)$. No edge meets these
  singletons, so exactly $d_G(P)$ edges join different parts of $P'$.
  The count in the proof of \cref{lem:deficiency-rank-bound},
  applied to $P'$, gives
  \[
    \dim Z(G,p) \;\ge\; D|P'| - (D-1)\,d_G(P) \;=\;
    D\,|B \setminus V(G)| + D + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P).
  \]
  The rank form follows, as
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D|B| - \dim Z(G,p)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rigid subgraphs and structural lemmas}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-rigid}

This subsection proves the properties of $k$-dof-graphs that Katoh and
Tanigawa establish in their \S3, and some deficiency computations that
later chapters use. We define rigid subgraphs, and show that a
$0$-dof-graph is $2$-edge-connected (KT Lemma~3.1) and that minimality
passes to subgraphs (KT Lemma~3.3). Then we compare the deficiency of a
multigraph with those of its pieces: at a cut of at most one edge (KT
Lemma~3.6), at a cut vertex, and along an ear. We also define the
largest value of a partition that keeps two given vertices in one part,
and show that every multigraph has a minimal spanning subgraph of the
same deficiency. Finally we treat the circuits of $M(\tilde G)$ (the
first step of KT Lemma~3.4), show that the two-vertex double edge and a
short cycle are $0$-dof-graphs, and list the minimal $k$-dof-graphs
with at most two vertices.

Katoh and Tanigawa's combinatorial induction contracts proper rigid
subgraphs (\cref{sec:molecular-induction}).

\begin{definition}[Rigid and proper rigid subgraphs]
  \label{def:rigid-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.IsRigidSubgraph, Graph.IsProperRigidSubgraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $H$ be a subgraph of a multigraph $G$. Then $H$ is a \emph{rigid
  subgraph} of $G$ if $H$ is a $0$-dof-graph. It is a \emph{proper}
  rigid subgraph if moreover $1 < |V(H)| < |V(G)|$.
\end{definition}

These are Katoh and Tanigawa's
definitions~\cite[pp.~658--659]{katohTanigawa2011}. When $V(H)$ is
nonempty, $H$ is rigid exactly when $\tilde H$ contains $D$
edge-disjoint spanning trees (KT Proposition~2.2). A subgraph with one
vertex is always rigid, as its only partition has one part; the
condition $|V(H)| > 1$ excludes these.

\begin{lemma}[A $0$-dof-graph is $2$-edge-connected; KT Lemma~3.1]
  \label{lem:two-edge-conn}
  \lean{Graph.two_le_crossingEdges_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.cutLabeling, Graph.numParts_cutLabeling}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $D \ge 1$, let $G$ be a $0$-dof-graph, and let $V'$ be a set of
  vertices of $G$ with $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$. Then at least two
  edges of $G$ join $V'$ to $V(G) \setminus V'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P = \{V', V(G) \setminus V'\}$. If at most one edge joined $V'$
  to $V(G) \setminus V'$, then $d_G(P) \le 1$, and
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;\ge\; \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
    \;=\; D - (D-1)\,d_G(P) \;\ge\; 1,
  \]
  contradicting $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal statement fixes $a \in V'$ and $b \in V(G) \setminus V'$,
  and counts the edges whose ends get different labels under the
  labeling that sends $V'$ to $a$ and every other vertex to $b$
  (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}). A second pin defines this
  labeling, and a third shows that it has two parts.
\end{fmlnote}

We name the edges of such a cut, with Katoh and Tanigawa's
notation~\cite[p.~654]{katohTanigawa2011}, and define
$2$-edge-connectivity by the condition of \cref{lem:two-edge-conn}.

\begin{definition}[Cuts and $2$-edge-connectivity]
  \label{def:cut-edges-2ec}
  \lean{Graph.cutEdges, Graph.TwoEdgeConnected}
  \leanok
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. For a set $V'$ of vertices of $G$, let
  $\delta_G(V')$ be the set of edges of $G$ that join $V'$ to
  $V(G) \setminus V'$, and let $d_G(V') = |\delta_G(V')|$. Then $G$ is
  \emph{$2$-edge-connected} if $d_G(V') \ge 2$ for every set $V'$ with
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[$2$-edge-connectivity of $0$-dof-graphs, and the degrees]
  \label{lem:two-edge-connected-degree}
  \lean{Graph.twoEdgeConnected_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.two_le_degree_of_twoEdgeConnected}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:cut-edges-2ec, def:k-dof}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. If $D \ge 1$ and $G$ is a $0$-dof-graph,
  then $G$ is $2$-edge-connected. If $G$ is $2$-edge-connected and has
  at least two vertices, then every vertex of $G$ has degree at
  least~$2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:two-edge-conn}
  The first claim is \cref{lem:two-edge-conn}. For the second, let $v$
  be a vertex of $G$. As $G$ has another vertex,
  $\emptyset \neq \{v\} \neq V(G)$, so $d_G(\{v\}) \ge 2$. The edges of
  $\delta_G(\{v\})$ are the edges at $v$ that are not loops, so
  $d_G(\{v\})$ is at most the degree of $v$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization states \cref{lem:two-edge-conn} with a labeling, as
  its formalization note explains. The formal proof identifies
  $\delta_G(V')$ with the set of edges whose ends get different labels
  under that labeling
  (\texttt{cutEdges\_\allowbreak{}eq\_\allowbreak{}crossingEdges\_\allowbreak{}cutLabeling}).
  For $V' = \{v\}$, these are the edges at $v$ that are not loops
  (\texttt{crossingEdges\_\allowbreak{}cutLabeling\_\allowbreak{}singleton\_\allowbreak{}subset}),
  and their number is at most the degree of $v$
  (\texttt{crossingEdges\_\allowbreak{}cutLabeling\_\allowbreak{}singleton\_\allowbreak{}ncard\_\allowbreak{}le}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A $0$-dof-graph is connected, of minimum degree at least two]
  \label{lem:deficiency-zero-connected}
  \lean{Graph.connected_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.two_le_degree_of_isKDof_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ be a $0$-dof-graph with at least one
  vertex. Then $G$ is connected. If $G$ has at least two vertices, then
  every vertex of $G$ has degree at least $2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:two-edge-conn, lem:two-edge-connected-degree}
  By \cref{lem:two-edge-connected-degree}, $G$ is $2$-edge-connected,
  and this gives the bound on the degrees. If $G$ were disconnected,
  the vertex set $V'$ of one of its components would have
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ and $d_G(V') = 0$.
\end{proof}

Next we show that minimality passes to subgraphs. If $H$ is a subgraph
of $G$, then $\tilde H$ is a subgraph of $\tilde G$, and their matroids
are related as follows.

\begin{lemma}[The matroid of a subgraph is a restriction]
  \label{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.matroidMG_restrict_mulTilde}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  Let $H$ be a subgraph of a multigraph $G$. Then $M(\tilde H)$ is the
  restriction of $M(\tilde G)$ to $E(\tilde H)$:
  \[
    M(\tilde G)\,|\,E(\tilde H) \;=\; M(\tilde H).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Both matroids have the ground set $E(\tilde H)$. Let
  $F \subseteq E(\tilde H)$. By \cref{def:matroid-MG}, $F$ is
  independent in $M(\tilde G)$ if and only if $\tilde G \restriction F$
  is $(D, D)$-sparse, and in $M(\tilde H)$ if and only if
  $\tilde H \restriction F$ is. Each edge of $\tilde H$ has the same ends
  in $\tilde H$ as in $\tilde G$, and sparsity depends only on the edges
  and their ends (\cref{def:graph-sparse}). So the two conditions agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Minimality passes to subgraphs; KT Lemma~3.3]
  \label{lem:subgraph-minimality}
  \lean{Graph.subgraph_minimality}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}
  Let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph, and let $H$ be a subgraph of $G$
  with $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = k'$. Then $H$ is a minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph. In particular, a rigid subgraph of a minimal
  $k$-dof-graph is a minimal $0$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's
  proof~\cite[p.~657]{katohTanigawa2011}. As $H$ is a $k'$-dof-graph,
  we must show that a base $B'$ of $M(\tilde H)$ meets $\tilde e$ for
  every edge $e$ of $H$. By
  \cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}, $B'$ is a maximal subset of
  $E(\tilde H)$ that is independent in $M(\tilde G)$. Extend $B'$ to a
  base $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$. Then $B \cap E(\tilde H)$ is independent
  and contains $B'$, so it equals $B'$. As $G$ is minimal, $B$ meets
  $\tilde e$, and $\tilde e \subseteq E(\tilde H)$ because $e$ is an
  edge of $H$. Hence $B'$ meets $\tilde e$.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa prove their Lemma~3.6 by writing $M(\tilde G)$ as a
direct sum, using their Lemmas~3.1 and~3.4; we compare partitions
directly. For
a set $X$ of vertices of $G$, $G[X]$ is the subgraph induced by $X$, and
$\tilde G[X] = (D-1)\cdot G[X]$. We first compute the value of a
partition none of whose parts meets both sides of a cut.

\begin{lemma}[The value of a separated partition]
  \label{lem:separated-partition-value}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef_split_of_sides}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $V'$ be a set of vertices of $G$ with
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$, and let $V'' = V(G) \setminus V'$. Let
  $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$ that is \emph{separated}, that is, each
  of its parts lies in $V'$ or in $V''$. Let $P'$ and $P''$ be the
  partitions of $V'$ and of $V''$ formed by these parts. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\;
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V']}(P')
    + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V'']}(P'')
    + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V').
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  An edge of $G$ joins different parts of $P$ if and only if it joins
  different parts of $P'$, or of $P''$, or lies in $\delta_G(V')$. As
  $|P| - 1 = (|P'| - 1) + (|P''| - 1) + 1$, the identity follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency at a cut of at most one edge; KT Lemma~3.6]
  \label{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_eq_of_cutEdges_ncard_le_one,
    Graph.exists_cut_decomposition_of_not_twoEdgeConnected,
    Graph.deficiency_induce_union_singleton}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $V'$ be a set of vertices of $G$ with
      $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ and $d_G(V') \le 1$, and let
      $V'' = V(G) \setminus V'$. Then
      \[
        \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\;
        \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V']) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V''])
        + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V').
      \]
      So the cut adds $1$ to the sum if $d_G(V') = 1$, and $D$ if
      $d_G(V') = 0$.
    \item If $G$ is a minimal $k$-dof-graph that is not
      $2$-edge-connected, then for some set $V'$ as in (1), $G[V']$ and
      $G[V'']$ are minimal $k'$- and $k''$-dof-graphs, where
      $k' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V'])$,
      $k'' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V''])$, and
      $k = k' + k'' + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V')$.
    \item If $G$ has no loops, $V'$ is as in (1), and an edge of $G$
      joins $V'$ to a vertex $w \notin V'$, then
      $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V' \cup \{w\}]) =
      \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V']) + 1$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:cut-edges-2ec, lem:separated-partition-value,
    lem:subgraph-minimality}
  (1) The union of a partition $P'$ of $V'$ and a partition $P''$ of
  $V''$ is a separated partition of $V(G)$, whose value is given by
  \cref{lem:separated-partition-value}. Taking $P'$ and $P''$ of
  largest value gives the inequality $\ge$ in (1).

  For $\le$, let $P$ be any partition of $V(G)$, and split each part $Q$
  that meets both $V'$ and $V''$ into $Q \cap V'$ and $Q \cap V''$. The
  result is a separated partition. If $s$ parts are split, the number of
  parts rises by $s$. Let $c$ be the number of edges that join different
  parts of the new partition but not of $P$. Each of them lies in
  $\delta_G(V')$, so $c \le 1$, and $c = 0$ if $s = 0$. So the value
  changes by $Ds - (D-1)c \ge 0$. Hence the value of $P$ is at most that
  of a separated partition
  (\texttt{exists\_\allowbreak{}sides\_\allowbreak{}separated\_\allowbreak{}partitionDef\_\allowbreak{}le}).
  By \cref{lem:separated-partition-value}, the value of a separated
  partition is at most the right-hand side of (1).

  (2) As $G$ is not $2$-edge-connected, some set $V'$ with
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ has $d_G(V') \le 1$. The formula for $k$
  is (1), and $G[V']$ and $G[V'']$ are minimal by KT Lemma~3.3
  (\cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}). This is the first step of
  Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of their
  Lemma~6.1~\cite[p.~672]{katohTanigawa2011}.

  (3) Apply (1) to the multigraph $G[V' \cup \{w\}]$ and the set
  $\{w\}$. Since $d_G(V') \le 1$, the given edge is the only edge of
  $G[V' \cup \{w\}]$ at $w$. The multigraph $G[\{w\}]$ has no edge, as
  $G$ has no loops, and its deficiency is $0$, as its only partition has
  one part. The other side is $G[V']$, and $D - (D-1) = 1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The three pins are statements (1), (2) and (3). Partitions are
  labelings in the formalization (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}).
  The value of a labeling depends only on its labels on $V(G)$
  (\texttt{partitionDef\_\allowbreak{}congr}), and it does not change
  when the labels are renamed injectively
  (\texttt{partitionDef\_\allowbreak{}comp\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}injOn}).
  The proof of (1) uses these two facts to give the two sides disjoint
  sets of labels before it combines their partitions.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency at a cut vertex]
  \label{lem:deficiency-cut-vertex}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_add_le_of_cutVertex, Graph.deficiency_eq_add_of_cutVertex,
    Graph.partitionDef_split_of_vertexTwoCut}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph without loops, and let $V_1$ and $V_2$ be sets
  of vertices of $G$ with $V_1 \cup V_2 = V(G)$ and
  $V_1 \cap V_2 = \{v\}$, such that every edge of $G$ lies inside $V_1$
  or inside $V_2$. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)
    = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_2]).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$. For $i = 1, 2$, the nonempty sets
  $Q \cap V_i$ with $Q \in P$ form a partition $P_i$ of $V_i$. Let $I$ be
  the set of parts of $P$ that meet both $V_1$ and $V_2$, so that
  $|P_1| + |P_2| = |P| + |I|$. An edge of $G$ inside $V_i$ joins
  different parts of $P$ if and only if it joins different parts of
  $P_i$. No edge lies inside both $V_1$ and $V_2$, since it would be a
  loop at $v$. Hence
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\;
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V_1]}(P_1)
    + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V_2]}(P_2) + D\,(1 - |I|).
  \]
  The part of $P$ that contains $v$ lies in $I$, so $|I| \ge 1$, and the
  value of $P$ is at most the right-hand side of the lemma.

  Conversely, let $P_1$ and $P_2$ be partitions of $V_1$ and $V_2$ of
  largest value. Let $P$ consist of the union of their two parts that
  contain $v$, together with their other parts. Then $P$ is a partition
  of $V(G)$, the partitions of $V_1$ and $V_2$ that it gives as above are
  $P_1$ and $P_2$, and $|I| = 1$. By the identity above, the value of $P$
  is the right-hand side.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  With partitions as labelings (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}), $I$
  is the set of labels used both on $V_1$ and on $V_2$. The third pin is
  the identity of the proof for two sets $V_1$ and $V_2$ that meet in two
  vertices $u$ and $v$ not joined by an edge; a cut vertex is the case
  $u = v$. The second pin is the lemma, and the first is its inequality
  $\ge$. The proof of the first renames the labels of two attaining
  labelings injectively
  (\texttt{partitionDef\_\allowbreak{}comp\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}injOn}),
  so that their sets of labels meet only in the label of $v$.
\end{fmlnote}

Let $V_1$ be a set of vertices of $G$. A path of $G$ whose ends lie in
$V_1$, possibly equal, and whose other vertices lie outside $V_1$ is an
ear on $V_1$; \cref{sec:main-component-chain} calls it open if its ends
differ and closed otherwise. The next two lemmas compare the deficiency
of $G$ with that of $G[V_1]$ when $G$ consists of $G[V_1]$ and one ear.

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency along an ear]
  \label{lem:deficiency-ear}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_induce_add_le_of_ear}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $D \ge 1$, let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $V_1$ be a set of
  vertices of $G$. Let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ ($k \ge 1$) be a path
  of $G$ with $a, b \in V_1$, possibly $a = b$, and distinct vertices
  $x_1, \dots, x_k$ outside $V_1$, such that
  $V(G) = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and every edge of $G$ off the
  path lies inside $V_1$. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;\ge\;
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) + k + 1 - D.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:separated-partition-value, lem:exists-tight-partition}
  Let $V'' = \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$, and let $P''$ be the partition of
  $V''$ into single vertices. Take a partition $P_1$ of $V_1$ of largest
  value, and let $P = P_1 \cup P''$. Each part of $P$ lies in $V_1$ or in
  $V''$, so $P$ is separated in the sense of
  \cref{lem:separated-partition-value}, with $V' = V_1$. That lemma
  gives
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\;
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])
    + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V'']}(P'')
    + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V_1).
  \]
  The edges of $G[V'']$ are among the $k - 1$ edges $x_i x_{i+1}$ of
  the path. As $D \ge 1$, the value of $P''$ is therefore at least
  $D(k - 1) - (D - 1)(k - 1) = k - 1$. The edges of $\delta_G(V_1)$ are
  among the two end edges $a x_1$ and $x_k b$ of the path, so
  $D - (D-1)\,d_G(V_1) \ge 2 - D$. Since
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)$,
  the bound follows.
\end{proof}

In the proof of \cref{lem:deficiency-ear}, the vertices of the ear form
parts of their own. If they can instead join a part that contains both ends of
the ear, the deficiency does not drop.

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency along an ear whose ends share a part]
  \label{lem:deficiency-ear-merge}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_induce_le_of_ear_of_merge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $V_1$ be a set of vertices of $G$.
  Let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ ($k \ge 0$) be a walk of $G$ with
  $a, b \in V_1$, possibly $a = b$, and vertices $x_1, \dots, x_k$
  outside $V_1$, not necessarily distinct, such that
  $V(G) = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and every edge of $G$ off the
  walk lies inside $V_1$. Suppose that some partition of $V_1$ of value
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$ has $a$ and $b$ in one part. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) \;\le\; \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P_1$ be such a partition, and let $P$ be obtained from it by
  adding $x_1, \dots, x_k$ to the part that contains $a$ and $b$. Then
  $|P| = |P_1|$. Every edge of the walk lies inside that part. Every
  other edge of $G$ lies inside $V_1$, where $P$ and $P_1$ agree, so it
  joins different parts of $P$ if and only if it joins different parts
  of $P_1$. Hence the value of $P$ is that of $P_1$, which is
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis of \cref{lem:deficiency-ear-merge} says that the largest
value of a partition of $V_1$ with $a$ and $b$ in one part is
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$. We give this largest value a name.

\begin{definition}[The deficiency with two vertices in one part]
  \label{def:deficiency-merged}
  \lean{Graph.deficiencyMerged, Graph.partitionDef_le_deficiencyMerged}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $u$ and $v$ be vertices of $G$. The
  \emph{merged deficiency} of $\tilde G$ at $u$ and $v$ is
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G; u, v) \;=\; \max_{P}\,
      \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P),
  \]
  the maximum over the partitions $P$ of $V(G)$ that have $u$ and $v$ in
  one part.
\end{definition}

So $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G; u, v)$
for every partition $P$ with $u$ and $v$ in one part, and
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G; u, v) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
We use the merged deficiency when an edge joining $u$ and $v$ is added:
the new edge lowers the value of each partition that separates $u$ and
$v$ by $D - 1$, and leaves the value of every other partition unchanged
(\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-merged}).

Katoh and Tanigawa reduce their Theorem~5.6, on the realizations of an
arbitrary multigraph, to minimal $k$-dof-graphs, by deleting edges
while the deficiency stays the same~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{lemma}[A minimal spanning subgraph of the same deficiency; KT Theorem~5.6, first step]
  \label{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isMinimalKDof_spanning_subgraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ be a multigraph with at least one vertex.
  Then $G$ has a spanning subgraph that is a minimal
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph, thm:def-eq-corank}
  Among the sets $F$ of edges of $G$ with
  $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G \restriction F}) =
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, such as $E(G)$, choose one, $F_0$, of
  least size. Then $G' = G \restriction F_0$ is a spanning subgraph of
  $G$ with $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G'}) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.

  Suppose that a base $B$ of $M(\widetilde{G'})$ misses $\tilde e$ for
  some edge $e$ of $G'$, and let
  $G'' = G \restriction (F_0 \setminus \{e\})$. Then
  $B \subseteq E(\widetilde{G''})$, so $B$ is independent in
  $M(\widetilde{G''})$ (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}). The
  multigraphs $G'$ and $G''$ have the vertices of $G$, so KT
  eq.~(2.4) (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) gives
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G''})
    \;=\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{rank} M(\widetilde{G''})
    \;\le\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - |B|
    \;=\; \operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G'}).
  \]
  As $D \ge 1$, deleting an edge does not lower the value of a
  partition, so $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G''}) \ge
  \operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G'})$. Hence $F_0 \setminus \{e\}$ keeps
  the deficiency of $G$, against the choice of $F_0$. So every base of
  $M(\widetilde{G'})$ meets $\tilde e$ for every edge $e$ of $G'$, and
  $G'$ is minimal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A circuit minus an edge is a maximal sparse subset; KT Lemma~3.4, first step]
  \label{lem:circuit-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.isSparse_diff_singleton_of_isCircuit}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $X$ be a circuit of $M(\tilde G)$, and
  let $e \in X$. Then $\tilde G \restriction (X - e)$ is
  $(D, D)$-sparse, and $X - e$ is a basis of $X$, that is, a maximal
  subset of $X$ that is independent in $M(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As $X$ is a minimal dependent set, $X - e$ is independent, so
  $\tilde G \restriction (X - e)$ is $(D, D)$-sparse
  (\cref{def:matroid-MG}). As $X$ itself is dependent, no larger subset
  of $X$ is independent.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa's Lemma~3.4 says more: $G[V(X)]$ is a rigid
subgraph of $G$, and $X - e$ is the union of $D$ edge-disjoint spanning
trees on $V(X)$~\cite[p.~658]{katohTanigawa2011}. We prove this in
the next chapter (\cref{lem:circuit-induces-rigid}), using KT
eq.~(2.4).

Katoh and Tanigawa prove their Theorem~5.5 by induction on the number of
vertices, from the case of two
vertices~\cite[p.~671]{katohTanigawa2011}. The next lemma computes the
deficiency of the two-vertex double edge, and the one after it that of
a short cycle, which the next chapter uses
(\cref{sec:molecular-induction}). The third lists the minimal
$k$-dof-graphs with two vertices, and with one.

\begin{lemma}[The two-vertex double edge is a $0$-dof-graph]
  \label{lem:two-vertex-zero-dof}
  \lean{Graph.isKDof_zero_of_parallel_pair}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $H$ be a multigraph with two vertices
  $x \neq y$ and two edges $e_1 \neq e_2$, each joining $x$ and $y$.
  Then $H$ is a $0$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The partitions of $V(H)$ are $\{V(H)\}$, of value $0$, and
  $\{\{x\}, \{y\}\}$. Both edges join the two parts of the second, so
  its value is $D - 2(D - 1) = 2 - D \le 0$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency}).
  Hence $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A short cycle is a $0$-dof-graph]
  \label{lem:cycle-zero-dof}
  \lean{Graph.isKDof_zero_of_cycle}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $3 \le m \le D$, and let $C$ be a multigraph whose vertices are
  $m$ distinct vertices $v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}$ and whose edges are $m$
  edges $f_0, \dots, f_{m-1}$, where $f_i$ joins $v_i$ and $v_{i+1}$,
  with indices modulo $m$. Then $C$ is a $0$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V(C)$ into at least two parts. Each part is
  a proper subset of $V(C)$, so it has a vertex $v_i$ whose successor
  $v_{i+1}$ lies in another part. The edge $f_i$ joins two parts, and
  different parts give different edges. So at least $|P|$ edges join
  different parts, and the value of $P$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency}) is at
  most $D(|P| - 1) - (D-1)|P| = |P| - D \le 0$, as $|P| \le m \le D$.
  The partition into one part has value $0$, so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde C) = 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The pin takes the vertices and the edges of $C$ as two maps on
  $\{0, \dots, m-1\}$, and it does not ask that the vertices be distinct.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The minimal $k$-dof-graphs with at most two vertices; KT p.~671]
  \label{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}
  \lean{Graph.isMinimalKDof_ncard_le_two_trichotomy}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph with one or two
  vertices. Then exactly one of the following holds: (i) $G$ has no
  edge, and $k = D(|V(G)| - 1)$; (ii) $G$ has two vertices and one edge,
  which joins them, and $k = 1$; (iii) $G$ has two vertices and two
  edges, each of which joins them, and $k = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph, thm:def-eq-corank,
    lem:two-vertex-zero-dof}
  We first compute three deficiencies. If a multigraph $H$ has no edge,
  then every partition $P$ of $V(H)$ has value
  $D(|P| - 1) \le D(|V(H)| - 1)$, with equality for the partition into
  single vertices, so $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = D(|V(H)| - 1)$
  (\texttt{deficiency\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}edgeSet\_\allowbreak{}empty}).
  Let $H$ have two vertices $x$ and $y$. Its partitions are $\{V(H)\}$,
  of value $0$, and $\{\{x\}, \{y\}\}$. If $H$ has one edge, joining $x$
  and $y$, the second has value $D - (D - 1) = 1$, so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = 1$
  (\texttt{deficiency\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}single\_\allowbreak{}edge}).
  If $H$ has two edges, each joining
  $x$ and $y$, the second has value $D - 2(D - 1) = 2 - D \le 0$, so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = 0$. This last computation is
  \cref{lem:two-vertex-zero-dof}.

  A minimal $k$-dof-graph has no loops. Indeed, let $p$ be a copy of a
  loop of $G$. Then $\tilde G \restriction \{p\}$ is not
  $(D, D)$-sparse, as $1 > D \cdot 1 - D$, so no base of $M(\tilde G)$
  contains $p$ (\cref{def:matroid-MG}). As every base of $M(\tilde G)$
  meets the copies of each edge of $G$, $G$ has no loop. Hence if $G$ has
  one vertex, it has no edge, and (i) holds.

  Let $G$ have two vertices $x$ and $y$, so that every edge of $G$ joins
  them. Suppose that $G$ had three edges $e_1, e_2, e_3$, and let
  $H = G \restriction \{e_1, e_2\}$. Then
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = 0$, so a base $B_H$ of $M(\tilde H)$
  has $D$ elements by KT eq.~(2.4) (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}). It is
  independent in $M(\tilde G)$ (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}),
  so $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \ge D$. By KT eq.~(2.4) for $G$,
  $k = D - \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \le 0$. As $k \ge 0$, it
  follows that $k = 0$ and that $B_H$ is a base of $M(\tilde G)$. But
  $B_H \subseteq E(\tilde H)$ misses $\tilde e_3$, against the
  minimality of $G$. So $G$ has at most two edges
  (\texttt{edgeSet\_\allowbreak{}ncard\_\allowbreak{}le\_\allowbreak{}two\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}isMinimalKDof\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}ncard\_\allowbreak{}two}),
  and the three computations give (i), (ii) and (iii). Katoh and
  Tanigawa deduce that $G$ has at most two edges from their Lemma~3.2: a
  minimal $k$-dof-graph is not $3$-edge-connected.
\end{proof}
