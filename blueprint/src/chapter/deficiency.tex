\section{The matroid \texorpdfstring{$M(\tilde G)$}{M(G~)}, deficiency, and \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-dof graphs}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency}

This chapter builds the combinatorial substrate of the molecular
conjecture, Katoh--Tanigawa~\cite[\S2.5, \S3]{katohTanigawa2011}. Where
\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} built the \emph{analytic} side (the
rigidity matrix $R(G,p)$, its rank, and the three rank lemmas), this
chapter builds the \emph{matroidal} side.
\Cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-matroid} constructs the matroid
$M(\tilde G)$, the $D$-deficiency $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and
the $k$-degree-of-freedom hierarchy; \cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-rigid}
develops rigid subgraphs and KT's structural Lemmas 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4;
and \cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge} proves the bridge
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$
(\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) that ties the two sides together. This bridge
is the matroidal half of Tay--Whiteley's Proposition~1.1, reconciling
the rank form of \cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} with the
body-hinge existence form of \cref{sec:body-hinge}; the remaining
analytic half (the generic-rank theorem $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) =
D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$) is presented with the
algebraic induction (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction}).
Throughout $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$ is the body-bar dimension and
$\tilde G$ abbreviates the multiplied graph $(D-1)\cdot G$ of
\cref{sec:body-hinge}, whose edge-fibers $\tilde e$ are the $D-1$
parallel copies of each edge $e \in E(G)$.

\subsection{The matroid \texorpdfstring{$M(\tilde G)$}{M(G~)}, the deficiency and \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-dof-graphs}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-matroid}

This subsection follows Katoh and
Tanigawa~\cite[\S2.5 and the start of \S3]{katohTanigawa2011}. We define
a matroid $M(\tilde G)$ on the edges of $\tilde G$ and the deficiency
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, a maximum over the partitions of
$V(G)$. Every base $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$ has
$|B| + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (KT eq.~(2.4)),
which we prove in \cref{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge}. Then we
define the $k$-dof-graphs, the multigraphs $G$ with
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$, and the minimal ones.

We use the notation of \cref{sec:body-bar-matroid-union}: $V(F)$ is
the set of vertices incident with an edge of a set $F$ of edges, and
$\tilde G \restriction F$ is the multigraph with the vertices of
$\tilde G$ and the edges of $F$. Katoh and Tanigawa define $M(\tilde G)$
as the matroid on $E(\tilde G)$ induced by the submodular function
$F \mapsto D(|V(F)| - 1)$ (\cref{def:ofSubmodular}). A set $F$ is
independent in it if and only if $\tilde G \restriction F$ is
$(D, D)$-sparse (\cref{def:graph-sparse}). They remark that this
matroid is the union of $D$ copies of the cycle
matroid~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011}, and we take the union as the
definition.

\begin{definition}[The matroid $M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{def:matroid-MG}
  \lean{Graph.matroidMG, Graph.matroidMG_indep_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:tay-witness}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. The matroid $M(\tilde G)$ is the union of
  $D$ copies of the cycle matroid of $\tilde G$
  (\cref{def:matroid-union}). A set $F$ of edges of $\tilde G$ is
  independent in $M(\tilde G)$ if and only if
  $\tilde G \restriction F$ is $(D, D)$-sparse.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Apply \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse} to the multigraph
  $\tilde G$, with $k = D$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  As for the $k$-frame matroid
  (\cref{fmlnote:k-frame-union-restriction}), the formal union is a
  matroid on all pairs $(e, i)$ of an edge label $e$ and an index
  $1 \le i \le D - 1$, in which a pair outside $E(\tilde G)$ is a loop.
  The matroid $M(\tilde G)$ is its restriction to $E(\tilde G)$.
\end{fmlnote}

So $M(\tilde G)$ is Katoh and Tanigawa's matroid. By
\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}, its independent sets are the unions of $D$
forests of $\tilde G$. When $d \ge 2$, they are also the sets $F$ such
that the multigraph $\tilde G \restriction F$ has an independent
body-bar framework in $\R^d$, by Tay's theorem (\cref{thm:tay-witness}).

\begin{definition}[The $D$-deficiency]
  \label{def:D-deficiency}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef, Graph.deficiency}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. For a partition $P$ of $V(G)$ into nonempty
  parts, let $d_G(P)$ be the number of edges of $G$ that join different
  parts of $P$. The \emph{$D$-deficiency} of $P$ is
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\; D\,(|P| - 1) \;-\;
      (D-1)\,d_G(P),
  \]
  and the \emph{deficiency} $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the
  largest value of $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)$ over the
  partitions $P$ of $V(G)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:partition-labeling}
  In the formalization a partition of $V(G)$ is given by a labeling of
  the vertices whose labels are again vertices. Its parts are the sets
  of vertices of $G$ with a common label. So $|P|$ is the number of
  labels used on $V(G)$, and an edge joins different parts when its
  ends have different labels. The values of the labelings are integers,
  and the deficiency is their supremum, which is attained because there
  are finitely many labelings.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011} write
$\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = D(|P| - 1) - d_{\tilde G}(P)$, where
$d_{\tilde G}(P) = (D-1)\,d_G(P)$ is the number of edges of $\tilde G$
that join different parts of $P$. If $V(G)$ is nonempty, then the partition $\{V(G)\}$ has value $0$, so
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$. By the theorem of Tutte and
Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961} (KT
Proposition~2.2), $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ if and only if
$\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees. We prove this for
$D \ge 2$ in \cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}.

The deficiency depends on the dimension $d$ through
$D = \binom{d+1}{2}$. When $d$ varies, we write
$\operatorname{def}_d(G)$ for $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ at this
$D$. So $\operatorname{def}_2(G)$ and $\operatorname{def}_3(G)$ are the
deficiencies at $D = 3$ and $D = 6$, which \cref{sec:main-component}
compares.

\begin{lemma}[On a connected graph the deficiency does not increase with the dimension]
  \label{lem:deficiency-antitone}
  \lean{Graph.Connected.deficiency_le_deficiency_of_le,
        Graph.Connected.deficiency_three_le_deficiency_two,
        Graph.Connected.numParts_le_ncard_crossingEdges_add_one}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a connected multigraph, and let $d \le d'$ be natural
  numbers. Then $\operatorname{def}_{d'}(G) \le \operatorname{def}_d(G)$.
  In particular $\operatorname{def}_3(G) \le \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rk-within-parts}
  Let $D \le D'$ be the values of $\binom{d+1}{2}$ and
  $\binom{d'+1}{2}$, and let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$. At least
  $|P| - 1$ edges of $G$ join different parts of $P$. Indeed, once these
  edges are deleted, each component lies in one part, so there are at
  least $|P|$ components. Deleting an edge raises the number of
  components by at most one, and $G$ is connected. Hence the value of
  $P$ at $D'$ minus its value at $D$ is
  \[
    (D' - D)\bigl(|P| - 1 - d_G(P)\bigr) \;\le\; 0.
  \]
  So the value of $P$ at $D'$ is at most $\operatorname{def}_d(G)$, and
  the lemma follows.
\end{proof}

By Jackson and Jord\'an's rank formula (KT Proposition~2.3;
\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}), a generic realization of $G$ has
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ degrees of freedom
(\cref{def:dof-generic}). This is why Katoh and Tanigawa speak of
$k$-dof-graphs, for $k$ degrees of freedom~\cite[\S3]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{definition}[$k$-dof-graphs and minimal $k$-dof-graphs]
  \label{def:k-dof}
  \lean{Graph.IsKDof, Graph.IsMinimalKDof, Graph.edgeFiber}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph and let $k$ be an integer. Then $G$ is a
  \emph{$k$-dof-graph} if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$. It is a
  \emph{minimal} $k$-dof-graph if moreover, for every edge $e$ of $G$,
  every base of $M(\tilde G)$ meets the set $\tilde e$ of the $D - 1$
  copies of $e$ in $\tilde G$.
\end{definition}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a $k$-dof-graph minimal if removing any edge
gives a graph that is not a $k$-dof-graph, and observe that this is the
condition above~\cite[pp.~656--657]{katohTanigawa2011}. Indeed,
removing an edge $e$ of $G$ leaves the deficiency unchanged if and only
if some base of $M(\tilde G)$ avoids $\tilde e$. This follows from KT
eq.~(2.4) (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank} below), since
$M(\widetilde{G - e})$ is the restriction of $M(\tilde G)$ to
$E(\tilde G) \setminus \tilde e$ (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}).

\subsection{Rigid subgraphs and structural lemmas}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-rigid}

This subsection proves the properties of $k$-dof-graphs that Katoh and
Tanigawa establish in their \S3, and some deficiency computations that
later chapters use. We define rigid subgraphs and show that a
$0$-dof-graph is $2$-edge-connected (KT Lemma~3.1). Then we compute
the deficiency of a multigraph from those of its pieces: at a cut of at
most one edge (KT Lemma~3.6), at a cut vertex, and along an ear. Next we
show that minimality passes to subgraphs (KT Lemma~3.3), and that every
multigraph has a minimal spanning subgraph of the same deficiency.
Finally we treat the circuits of $M(\tilde G)$ (KT Lemma~3.4) and the
minimal $k$-dof-graphs with at most two vertices.

Katoh and Tanigawa's combinatorial induction contracts proper rigid
subgraphs (\cref{sec:molecular-induction}).

\begin{definition}[Rigid and proper rigid subgraphs]
  \label{def:rigid-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.IsRigidSubgraph, Graph.IsProperRigidSubgraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $H$ be a subgraph of a multigraph $G$. Then $H$ is a \emph{rigid
  subgraph} of $G$ if $H$ is a $0$-dof-graph. It is a \emph{proper}
  rigid subgraph if moreover $1 < |V(H)| < |V(G)|$.
\end{definition}

These are Katoh and Tanigawa's
definitions~\cite[pp.~658--659]{katohTanigawa2011}. When $V(H)$ is
nonempty, $H$ is rigid exactly when $\tilde H$ contains $D$
edge-disjoint spanning trees (KT Proposition~2.2). A subgraph with one
vertex is always rigid, as its only partition has one part; the
condition $|V(H)| > 1$ excludes these.

\begin{lemma}[A $0$-dof-graph is $2$-edge-connected; KT Lemma~3.1]
  \label{lem:two-edge-conn}
  \lean{Graph.two_le_crossingEdges_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.cutLabeling, Graph.numParts_cutLabeling}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $D \ge 1$, let $G$ be a $0$-dof-graph, and let $V'$ be a set of
  vertices of $G$ with $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$. Then at least two
  edges of $G$ join $V'$ to $V(G) \setminus V'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P = \{V', V(G) \setminus V'\}$. If at most one edge joined $V'$
  to $V(G) \setminus V'$, then $d_G(P) \le 1$, and
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;\ge\; \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
    \;=\; D - (D-1)\,d_G(P) \;\ge\; 1,
  \]
  contradicting $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal statement fixes $a \in V'$ and $b \in V(G) \setminus V'$,
  and counts the edges whose ends get different labels under the
  labeling that sends $V'$ to $a$ and every other vertex to $b$
  (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}). A second pin defines this
  labeling, and a third shows that it has two parts.
\end{fmlnote}

We name the edges of such a cut, with Katoh and Tanigawa's
notation~\cite[p.~654]{katohTanigawa2011}, and define
$2$-edge-connectivity by the condition of \cref{lem:two-edge-conn}.

\begin{definition}[Cuts and $2$-edge-connectivity]
  \label{def:cut-edges-2ec}
  \lean{Graph.cutEdges, Graph.TwoEdgeConnected, Graph.cutEdges_eq_crossingEdges_cutLabeling,
    Graph.twoEdgeConnected_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.two_le_degree_of_twoEdgeConnected,
    Graph.crossingEdges_cutLabeling_singleton_subset,
    Graph.crossingEdges_cutLabeling_singleton_ncard_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. For a set $V'$ of vertices of $G$, let
  $\delta_G(V')$ be the set of edges of $G$ that join $V'$ to
  $V(G) \setminus V'$, and let $d_G(V') = |\delta_G(V')|$. Then $G$ is
  \emph{$2$-edge-connected} if $d_G(V') \ge 2$ for every set $V'$ with
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$. If $D \ge 1$, every $0$-dof-graph is
  $2$-edge-connected. If $G$ is $2$-edge-connected and has at least two
  vertices, then every vertex of $G$ has degree at least $2$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:two-edge-conn}
  The first claim is \cref{lem:two-edge-conn}. For the second, let $v$
  be a vertex of $G$. As $G$ has another vertex,
  $\emptyset \neq \{v\} \neq V(G)$, so $d_G(\{v\}) \ge 2$. The edges of
  $\delta_G(\{v\})$ are the edges at $v$ that are not loops, so
  $d_G(\{v\})$ is at most the degree of $v$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization states \cref{lem:two-edge-conn} with a labeling, as
  its formalization note explains. One pin identifies $\delta_G(V')$
  with the set of edges whose ends get different labels under that
  labeling. Two more show that for $V' = \{v\}$ these
  are the edges at $v$ that are not loops, and bound their number by
  the degree of $v$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A $0$-dof-graph is connected, of minimum degree at least two]
  \label{lem:deficiency-zero-connected}
  \lean{Graph.connected_of_isKDof_zero, Graph.two_le_degree_of_isKDof_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $D \ge 1$, and let $G$ be a $0$-dof-graph with at least one
  vertex. Then $G$ is connected. If $G$ has at least two vertices, then
  every vertex of $G$ has degree at least $2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:two-edge-conn}
  By \cref{def:cut-edges-2ec}, $G$ is $2$-edge-connected, and this
  gives the bound on the degrees. If $G$ were disconnected, the vertex
  set $V'$ of one of its components would have
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ and $d_G(V') = 0$.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa prove their Lemma~3.6 by writing $M(\tilde G)$ as a
direct sum, using their Lemmas~3.1 and~3.4; we compare partitions
directly. For
a set $X$ of vertices of $G$, $G[X]$ is the subgraph induced by $X$, and
$\tilde G[X] = (D-1)\cdot G[X]$.

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency at a cut of at most one edge; KT Lemma~3.6]
  \label{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef_congr, Graph.partitionDef_comp_of_injOn,
    Graph.partitionDef_split_of_sides,
    Graph.exists_sides_separated_partitionDef_le,
    Graph.deficiency_eq_of_cutEdges_ncard_le_one,
    Graph.exists_cut_decomposition_of_not_twoEdgeConnected,
    Graph.deficiency_induce_union_singleton}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $V'$ be a set of vertices of $G$ with
      $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ and $d_G(V') \le 1$, and let
      $V'' = V(G) \setminus V'$. Then
      \[
        \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\;
        \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V']) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V''])
        + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V').
      \]
      So the cut adds $1$ to the sum if $d_G(V') = 1$, and $D$ if
      $d_G(V') = 0$.
    \item If $G$ is a minimal $k$-dof-graph that is not
      $2$-edge-connected, then for some set $V'$ as in (1), $G[V']$ and
      $G[V'']$ are minimal $k'$- and $k''$-dof-graphs, where
      $k' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V'])$,
      $k'' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V''])$, and
      $k = k' + k'' + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V')$.
    \item If $G$ has no loops, $V'$ is as in (1), and an edge of $G$
      joins $V'$ to a vertex $w \notin V'$, then
      $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V' \cup \{w\}]) =
      \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V']) + 1$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:cut-edges-2ec, thm:def-eq-corank}
  (1) Call a partition of $V(G)$ \emph{separated} if each of its parts
  lies in $V'$ or in $V''$. A separated partition $P$ is the union of a
  partition $P'$ of $V'$ and a partition $P''$ of $V''$. An edge of $G$
  joins different parts of $P$ if and only if it joins different parts
  of $P'$, or of $P''$, or lies in $\delta_G(V')$. As
  $|P| - 1 = (|P'| - 1) + (|P''| - 1) + 1$, it follows that
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \;=\;
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V']}(P')
    + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[V'']}(P'')
    + D - (D-1)\,d_G(V').
  \]
  Taking $P'$ and $P''$ of largest value gives the inequality $\ge$ in
  (1).

  For $\le$, let $P$ be any partition of $V(G)$, and split each part $Q$
  that meets both $V'$ and $V''$ into $Q \cap V'$ and $Q \cap V''$. The
  result is a separated partition. If $s$ parts are split, the number of
  parts rises by $s$. Let $c$ be the number of edges that join different
  parts of the new partition but not of $P$. Each of them lies in
  $\delta_G(V')$, so $c \le 1$, and $c = 0$ if $s = 0$. So the value
  changes by $Ds - (D-1)c \ge 0$. Hence the value of $P$ is at most that
  of a separated partition, which by the identity above is at most the
  right-hand side of (1).

  (2) As $G$ is not $2$-edge-connected, some set $V'$ with
  $\emptyset \neq V' \neq V(G)$ has $d_G(V') \le 1$. The formula for $k$
  is (1), and $G[V']$ and $G[V'']$ are minimal by KT Lemma~3.3
  (\cref{lem:subgraph-minimality} below). This is the first step of
  Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of their
  Lemma~6.1~\cite[p.~672]{katohTanigawa2011}.

  (3) Apply (1) to the multigraph $G[V' \cup \{w\}]$ and the set
  $\{w\}$. Since $d_G(V') \le 1$, the given edge is the only edge of
  $G[V' \cup \{w\}]$ at $w$. The multigraph $G[\{w\}]$ has no edge, as
  $G$ has no loops, and its deficiency is $0$, as its only partition has
  one part. The other side is $G[V']$, and $D - (D-1) = 1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  Partitions are labelings in the formalization
  (\cref{fmlnote:partition-labeling}). Two of the pins say that the
  value of a labeling depends only on its labels on $V(G)$, and that it
  does not change when the labels are renamed injectively. The proof of
  (1) uses them to give the two sides disjoint sets of labels before it
  combines their partitions. The other pins are the identity for
  separated partitions, the splitting step, and statements (1), (2) and
  (3).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Deficiency adds at a cut vertex]
  \label{lem:deficiency-cut-vertex}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_add_le_of_cutVertex, Graph.deficiency_eq_add_of_cutVertex,
    Graph.partitionDef_split_of_vertexTwoCut}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be loopless, with $V(G) = V_1 \cup V_2$, $V_1 \cap V_2 = \{v\}$,
  and every edge of $G$ inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$. Then for every $n$
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)
    = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_2]).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  For any labeling $f$ of the bodies, the value of $f$ on $G$ is the sum
  of its values on the two sides plus $D(1 - |I|)$, where $I$ is the set
  of labels used on both sides: the parts count by inclusion and
  exclusion, and every edge crosses on exactly one side, since an edge
  inside both would be a loop at $v$. As $f(v) \in I$, this gives $\le$.
  For $\ge$, normalize attaining labelings of the two sides into their
  own sides, as in \cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}, with $v$ as the
  label of its part on both; glued, they form one labeling with
  $I = \{v\}$, whose value on $G$ is the sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency along an ear]
  \label{lem:deficiency-ear}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_induce_add_le_of_ear}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $V_1 \subseteq V(G)$, and let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  ($k \ge 1$) be a path of $G$ with $a, b \in V_1$, possibly $a = b$, and
  distinct bodies $x_1, \dots, x_k$ outside $V_1$, such that
  $V(G) = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and every edge of $G$ not on the
  path lies inside $V_1$. Then, at any $D \ge 1$,
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;\ge\;
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) + k + 1 - D.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  Take a partition $P_1$ of $V_1$ attaining
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$ and add each $x_i$ as a part of its
  own. The value of this partition splits over $V_1$ and the path bodies,
  as in the proof of \cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}. The $k$ new parts
  are joined to one another by at most $k - 1$ edges of the path, which
  contributes $D(k - 1) - (D - 1)(k - 1) = k - 1$ at least, and to $V_1$
  by at most the two end edges of the path, which contributes
  $D - 2(D - 1) = 2 - D$ at least. The sum is the bound.
\end{proof}

When the ends of the path lie in one part of a partition attaining the
deficiency of $G[V_1]$, the path bodies can join that part, and the
deficiency does not drop.

\begin{lemma}[The deficiency along an ear whose ends share a part]
  \label{lem:deficiency-ear-merge}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_induce_le_of_ear_of_merge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $V_1 \subseteq V(G)$, and let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  ($k \ge 0$) be a path of $G$ with $a, b \in V_1$, possibly $a = b$, and
  distinct bodies $x_1, \dots, x_k$ outside $V_1$, such that
  $V(G) = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and every edge of $G$ not on the
  path lies inside $V_1$. If some partition of $V_1$ attaining
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$ has $a$ and $b$ in one part, then,
  at any $D$,
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) \;\le\; \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Put every $x_i$ into the part containing $a$ and $b$. The number of
  parts is unchanged, every edge of the path lies inside that part, and
  every other edge of $G$ lies inside $V_1$ with its status unchanged, so
  the value of the partition on $G$ is its value on $G[V_1]$.
\end{proof}

The partitions keeping two given bodies in one part have a maximum of
their own, which measures how much the deficiency depends on separating
the two bodies.

\begin{definition}[The deficiency with two bodies in one part]
  \label{def:deficiency-merged}
  \lean{Graph.deficiencyMerged, Graph.partitionDef_le_deficiencyMerged}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $u, v$ be bodies of $G$. The \emph{merged deficiency} of $\tilde G$
  at $u, v$ is
  \[
    \operatorname{def}(\tilde G; u, v) \;=\; \max_{P}\,
      \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P),
  \]
  the maximum over the partitions $P$ of $V(G)$ that have $u$ and $v$ in
  one part. In particular $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G; u, v)$ for every such partition.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Matroid restriction descends to subgraphs]
  \label{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.matroidMG_restrict_mulTilde}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  For a subgraph $H \le G$, restricting $M(\tilde G)$ to the edge set
  $E(\tilde H)$ of the smaller multiplied graph recovers $M(\tilde H)$:
  \[
    M(\tilde G)\,|\,E(\tilde H) \;=\; M(\tilde H).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Matroid restriction commutes with the $D$-fold cycle-matroid union
  construction. Both sides are checked equal on independent sets: an
  edge set $E' \subseteq E(\tilde H)$ is independent in either matroid
  iff it is $(D,D)$-sparse, and the two sparsity conditions agree
  because the incidences of an edge of $\tilde H$ coincide with its
  incidences in $\tilde G$ (the subgraph relation cannot be seen by a
  restriction to a fiber subset of the smaller graph). This is the
  identity Katoh--Tanigawa~\cite[Lemma 3.3]{katohTanigawa2011} runs on.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Subgraph minimality via restriction; KT Lemma 3.3]
  \label{lem:subgraph-minimality}
  \lean{Graph.subgraph_minimality}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}
  Let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph and let $H \le G$ be a subgraph
  with $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = k'$. Then $H$ is a minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph. In particular a rigid subgraph ($k' = 0$) of a
  minimal $k$-dof-graph is a minimal $0$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = k'$ half is the choice of $k'$;
  the content is the base / fiber-meeting minimality transport. By
  \cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}, $M(\tilde H) = M(\tilde
  G)\,|\,E(\tilde H)$, so any base $B'$ of $M(\tilde H)$ is an
  $M(\tilde G)$-basis of $E(\tilde H)$ and extends to a base $B$ of
  $M(\tilde G)$ with $B' = B \cap E(\tilde H)$ by maximality. Each
  edge-fiber $\tilde e$ of an edge $e \in E(H) \subseteq E(G)$ lies
  inside $E(\tilde H)$, so $G$'s minimality ($B \cap \tilde e \ne
  \emptyset$) descends: $B' \cap \tilde e = (B \cap E(\tilde H)) \cap
  \tilde e = B \cap \tilde e \ne \emptyset$. This is
  Katoh--Tanigawa~\cite[Lemma 3.3]{katohTanigawa2011}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A deficiency-preserving minimal spanning subgraph exists; KT Theorem 5.6, first step]
  \label{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isMinimalKDof_spanning_subgraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $D = \operatorname{bodyBarDim}\,n \ge 1$ and let $G$ be a graph with
  $V(G) \ne \emptyset$. Then $G$ has a \emph{spanning} subgraph $G' \le G$
  ($V(G') = V(G)$) that is a minimal $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$-dof-graph:
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde{G'}) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and no edge
  of $G'$ can be deleted without raising the deficiency. This is the converse
  direction to \cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}, which starts from an already
  minimal graph; here $G$ is arbitrary and the strip removes the
  deficiency-irrelevant edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph, thm:def-eq-corank}
  Among the edge subsets $F \subseteq E(G)$ whose restriction $G
  \restriction F$ keeps $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ --- $F = E(G)$ is one
  such --- pick $F_0$ of minimal cardinality and set $G' = G \restriction
  F_0$; then $V(G') = V(G)$ and $\operatorname{def}(\tilde{G'}) =
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ by the choice of $F_0$. For the minimality:
  if a base $B$ of $M(\tilde{G'})$ missed the fiber $\tilde e$ of some $e \in
  E(G')$, the restriction identity (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph})
  would make $B$ independent in $M(\widetilde{G \restriction (F_0 \setminus
  \{e\})})$ of the full size $D(|V(G)|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde{G'})$
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}), forcing $F_0 \setminus \{e\}$ to also keep the
  deficiency --- contradicting the minimality of $|F_0|$. This is
  Katoh--Tanigawa's first step towards Theorem~5.6
  \cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}: delete edges from $G$ while the
  deficiency is unchanged, until no further edge can be removed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A circuit minus an edge is a maximal sparse subset; KT Lemma 3.4]
  \label{lem:circuit-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.isSparse_diff_singleton_of_isCircuit}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigid-subgraph, def:matroid-MG}
  Let $X$ be a circuit of $M(\tilde G)$ and let $e \in X$. Then
  $X - e$ is $(D,D)$-sparse --- equivalently, $X - e$ is an
  $M(\tilde G)$-basis of $X$: a circuit is exactly one edge short of
  being independent on its vertex span.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  $X - e$ is independent since $X$ is a minimal dependent set, so by
  boundary-regime cleanliness (\cref{def:matroid-MG}) it is
  $(D,D)$-sparse; and $X$ is dependent, so $X - e$ is a maximal
  independent --- i.e.\ basis --- subset of $X$. This is the
  upper-bound / maximal-sparse-subset half of
  Katoh--Tanigawa~\cite[Lemma 3.4]{katohTanigawa2011}. KT's full
  conclusion --- that the vertex-induced subgraph $G[V(X)]$ is rigid,
  more precisely that $X - e$ partitions into $D$ edge-disjoint
  spanning trees on $V(X)$, i.e.\ $|X - e| = D(|V(X)| - 1)$ exactly ---
  is the separate node \cref{lem:circuit-induces-rigid}, which builds
  the vertex-induced-subgraph construction this node's statement does
  not need and combines it with the $\operatorname{def} =
  \operatorname{corank}$ min--max (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$|V| \le 2$ trichotomy for minimal $k$-dof-graphs; KT p.~671]
  \label{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_of_edgeSet_empty, Graph.deficiency_of_single_edge,
    Graph.edgeSet_ncard_le_two_of_isMinimalKDof_of_ncard_two,
    Graph.isMinimalKDof_ncard_le_two_trichotomy}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph, thm:def-eq-corank}
  Let $D = \operatorname{bodyBarDim}\,n \ge 2$ and let $G$ be a minimal
  $k$-dof-graph with $V(G) \ne \emptyset$ and $|V(G)| \le 2$.  Then
  exactly one of the following holds: (i) $E(G) = \emptyset$ and
  $k = D(|V(G)| - 1)$, covering $|V| = 1$ (looplessness forces $E =
  \emptyset$) and $|V| = 2$; (ii) $|V(G)| = 2$, $|E(G)| = 1$ (a single
  edge $e$ between the two vertices), and $k = 1$; or (iii) $|V(G)| = 2$,
  $|E(G)| = 2$ (a parallel pair $e, f$ between the two vertices), and
  $k = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph, thm:def-eq-corank}
  The deficiency of an edge-free graph
  ($\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = D(|V(G)|-1)$, the discrete partition
  maximizes the sup) and of a single-edge graph
  ($\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 1$, the two-part cut crosses once,
  $D - (D-1) = 1$) are direct computations.

  At $|V| = 2$ with vertices $\{x, y\}$, every edge links $x$ to $y$
  (looplessness).  The parallel-class bound ($|E(G)| \le 2$) follows by
  contradiction: given three distinct edges $e_1, e_2, e_3$, restrict
  to $H := G \restriction \{e_1, e_2\}$.  Then $H$ is $0$-dof
  (\cref{def:k-dof}, the parallel-pair criterion), so $M(\tilde H)$ has
  rank $D$ and a base $B_H$ of size $D$.  By the restriction identity
  (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}) $B_H$ is $M(\tilde
  G)$-independent, giving $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \ge D$.
  Combined with the corank bridge (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) and
  nonnegativity of $k$, this forces $k = 0$ and $B_H$ a $M(\tilde
  G)$-base.  But $B_H \subseteq E(\tilde H)$ avoids $\tilde e_3$,
  contradicting minimality ($B_H \cap \tilde e_3 \ne \emptyset$).
  With $|E(G)| \le 2$ established, the $k$-values follow from the
  deficiency computations and the parallel-pair $0$-dof criterion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The deficiency \texorpdfstring{$=$}{=} corank bridge}
\label{sec:molecular-deficiency-bridge}

\begin{lemma}[Rank upper bound for $M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{lem:rank-matroidMG-le}
  \lean{Graph.rank_matroidMG_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG}
  For a multigraph $G$ with $V(G) \ne \emptyset$,
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \;\le\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Every base $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$ is independent, hence (boundary-regime
  cleanliness, \cref{def:matroid-MG}) the bar set $B$ is $(D,D)$-sparse
  in $\tilde G$. Applying sparsity to $B$ itself gives $|B| + D \le D
  \cdot |V'|$, where $V' \subseteq V(\tilde G) = V(G)$ are the vertices
  $B$ spans; since $|V'| \le |V(G)|$ and $|V(G)| \ge 1$, this rearranges
  to $|B| \le D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$. This is the matroidal upper-bound half of
  \cref{thm:def-eq-corank} (the codimension fact $\operatorname{rank} R
  \le D(|V|-1)$ on the analytic side,
  \cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Partition-respecting cycle-matroid rank bound]
  \label{lem:rk-within-parts}
  \lean{Graph.rk_cycleMatroid_within_parts_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-MG, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$ and let $Y \subseteq E(\tilde G)$ be the set
  of fibers of $\tilde G$ whose two endpoints lie in the same part of $P$
  (the non-crossing fibers). Then the cycle-matroid rank of $Y$ satisfies
  \[
    \operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y) + |P| \;\le\; |V(G)|,
  \]
  i.e.\ $\operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y) \le |V(G)| - |P|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By the cycle-matroid rank--component identity
  $\operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y) + c(\tilde G \restriction Y) = |V(G)|$
  (the edge-restriction $\tilde G \restriction Y$ keeps every vertex, so
  $V(\tilde G \restriction Y) = V(\tilde G) = V(G)$). It remains to see
  $c(\tilde G \restriction Y) \ge |P|$. A fiber of $Y$ joins two
  equally-labelled vertices, so along any walk of $\tilde G \restriction Y$ the
  part label is constant; hence the part containing a vertex is a connected-
  component invariant, and the map sending each part to the component of one of
  its vertices is injective. Thus the number of components is at least the
  number $|P|$ of parts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weak duality: $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$]
  \label{lem:weak-duality}
  \lean{Graph.rank_add_partitionDef_le, Graph.rank_add_deficiency_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG, lem:rk-within-parts}
  For a multigraph $G$ with $V(G) \ne \emptyset$ and $D \ge 1$, and every
  partition $P$ of $V(G)$,
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)
      \;\le\; D\,(|V(G)| - 1);
  \]
  maximising over $P$ gives
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1)$, i.e.\ $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le
  \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Fix $P$, encoded by $f$, and let $Y \subseteq E(\tilde G)$ be the
  non-crossing fibers (those whose underlying edge does not cross $P$). The
  Edmonds matroid-partition rank formula (\cref{sec:matroid-union},
  \texttt{Union\_pow\_rk\_eq}) applied to the $D$-fold cycle-matroid union on
  $X = E(\tilde G)$ with the witness $Y$ gives
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \le D \cdot
  \operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y) + |E(\tilde G) \setminus Y|$. By
  \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}, $D \cdot \operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y)
  \le D(|V| - |P|)$; and the crossing fibers $E(\tilde G) \setminus Y$ number
  exactly $(D-1)\,d_G(P)$ (each of the $d_G(P)$ crossing edges of $G$
  contributes its $D-1$ parallel copies). Substituting,
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) =
  \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) + D(|P|-1) - (D-1)\,d_G(P) \le D(|V| - 1)$.
  Maximising over $P$ (a conditionally-complete supremum, bounded by the
  per-partition bound) gives the deficiency form.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$; Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{thm:def-eq-corank}
  \lean{Graph.rank_add_deficiency_eq, Graph.isBase_ncard_add_deficiency_eq, Graph.le_rank_add_deficiency}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG, lem:rank-matroidMG-le, lem:rk-within-parts, lem:weak-duality}
  For a multigraph $G$ with $V(G) \ne \emptyset$ and any base $B$ of
  $M(\tilde G)$,
  \[
    |B| + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \;=\; D\,(|V| - 1),
  \]
  i.e.\ $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde
  G)$ inside the rank-$D(|V|-1)$ ambient. This is the project framing of
  the generic-rank identity of Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite[Theorem
  6.1]{jacksonJordan2009}, whose Corollary~6.2 reads off rigidity
  ($\operatorname{def} = 0$) as $\tilde G$ packing $D$ edge-disjoint
  spanning trees.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The rank of the $D$-fold graphic-matroid union is the partition
  formula of Edmonds (\cref{sec:matroid-union}); the corank against the
  ambient $D(|V|-1)$ is exactly the deficiency maximised over
  partitions. One inequality is weak duality (\cref{lem:weak-duality}):
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$,
  from the Edmonds rank formula and the partition-respecting bound
  \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}, alongside the rank upper bound
  \cref{lem:rank-matroidMG-le} (matching, on the matroidal side, the
  body-hinge reduction of \cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}). The reverse
  inequality $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge \operatorname{corank}
  M(\tilde G)$ --- the substantive Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}
  min--max content --- exhibits a vertex-partition attaining the rank: the
  existence half of the Edmonds formula provides an optimal edge set
  $Y_0 \subseteq E(\tilde G)$ with $D\cdot
  \operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y_0) + |E(\tilde G) \setminus Y_0| \le
  \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G)$, and the partition $P$ into the connected
  components of $\tilde G \restriction Y_0$ is then attaining. For that $P$
  every fiber of $Y_0$ joins same-component (so equally-labelled) vertices,
  whence $\operatorname{r}_{\mathrm{cycle}}(Y_0) + |P| = |V|$ exactly (the
  $\le$ is \cref{lem:rk-within-parts}, the $\ge$ the rank--component
  identity together with the components-refine-the-partition map run in
  reverse); and every crossing edge has all $D-1$ of its fibers outside
  $Y_0$, so $(D-1)\,d_G(P) \le |E(\tilde G) \setminus Y_0|$. Substituting,
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \ge D(|V| - |P|) + (D-1)\,d_G(P) =
  D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P)$, so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge \operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$. The
  two inequalities give the equality.
\end{proof}

This bridge is the matroidal half of Tay--Whiteley's
Proposition~1.1, which equates the rank form
$\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D(|V|-1)$ of
\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} with the standard-basis existence
form (\cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}) of \cref{sec:body-hinge}. The
reconciliation routes through $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) =
\operatorname{corank} M(\tilde G)$ (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}); the
remaining \emph{analytic} link --- the generic-rank theorem
$\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
(Jackson--Jord\'an Thm 6.1, geometric side) --- wires the
rigidity-matrix rank to the matroid corank and is presented with the
algebraic induction (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction}).

The deficiency-zero condition has a classical combinatorial
reformulation, the packing form in which the body-and-hinge
realization theorems are usually stated.

\begin{lemma}[Deficiency zero is a spanning-tree packing]
  \label{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_eq_zero_iff_exists_spanningTrees}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:matroid-MG}
  For a multigraph $G$ with nonempty vertex set and $D \ge 2$,
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ if and only if
  $\tilde G = (D-1) \cdot G$ contains $D$ pairwise edge-disjoint
  spanning trees.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:def-eq-corank, thm:tutte-nash-williams,
    cor:k-spanning-trees}
  If $\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees, their
  union is an independent set of size $D(|V|-1)$ in $M(\tilde G)$:
  each tree is acyclic and the trees are edge-disjoint, and
  independence in the $D$-fold cycle-matroid union is exactly a
  decomposition into $D$ forests
  (\cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams}). Since the deficiency is
  nonnegative, $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \le D(|V|-1)$
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}), so this independent set is a base and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ --- the argument of
  \cref{cor:molecule-generic-square-packing}, at general $D$.
  Conversely, if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ then any base
  $B$ of $M(\tilde G)$ has $|B| = D(|V|-1)$
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}); being independent in the union, the
  restriction $\tilde G \restriction B$ is $(D,D)$-sparse and
  decomposes into $D$ edge-disjoint forests
  (\cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams}). The edge count makes the
  restriction $(D,D)$-tight, and a tight graph is connected --- were
  it disconnected, summing the sparsity bound over two unions of
  components would give $|B| \le D|V| - 2D$, short of the tight
  count --- so each forest of the packing is a spanning tree
  (\cref{cor:k-spanning-trees}).
\end{proof}
