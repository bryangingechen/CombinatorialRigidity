\section{The main component of the pencil configuration space}
\label{sec:main-component}

\Cref{sec:pencil-main-component-route} reduced the hinge-pencil question
to the two main-component statements
(\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}): every simple
two-edge-connected multigraph has a pencil realization at the deficiency
rank with adjacent concurrency points distinct, and a generic one
whenever a nondegenerate realization exists at all. This section proves
both over every infinite field (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-generic-attains}),
and with them the pencil conjecture (\cref{thm:pencil-conjecture}). The
rest of this introduction outlines the argument; the subsections follow
it in order.

\emph{The main component.} Draw the bodies in the plane,
$v \mapsto q_v = (x_v, y_v) \in K^2$, with adjacent bodies drawn apart
and no closed neighbourhood drawn on a line, and lift each body to the
point $p_v = (x_v, y_v, z_v, 1) \in K^4$ at a height $z_v$. The lifted
points of every closed neighbourhood lie in a common plane exactly when
the heights agree with an affine function of the drawing on each closed
neighbourhood. That condition is linear, so the heights form a linear
space $L(q)$, the lifting space of the drawing
(\cref{lem:pencil-condition-linear}). Each such configuration is a pencil
realization, with concurrency point $p_v$, with panel the plane through
the closed neighbourhood of $v$, and with the joins $p_u \wedge p_v$ of
adjacent points as hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization},
\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). The drawings of least
$\dim L(q)$ contain the non-roots of a nonzero polynomial
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}), and over them the
configurations form a vector bundle whose closure is the main component
(\cref{rem:pencil-x0-main-component}). Its general
configuration \emph{attains} when, off the zero set of a nonzero
polynomial in the drawing and, fibre by fibre, off the zero set of a
nonzero polynomial in the heights, the rank is the target
$6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
(\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). The rank is lower semicontinuous, so one
configuration at the target over a drawing of least $\dim L(q)$ forces
the general one, and that configuration is itself an adjacent-distinct
realization (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}). The main component is
never formed: its irreducibility is used only in the form that two
nonempty Zariski-open conditions on one fibre $L(q)$ meet.

\emph{The flat rank and Jackson and Jord\'an's equality.} Write
$\operatorname{def}_2$ and $\operatorname{def}_3$ for the deficiency of
\cref{def:D-deficiency} at $n = 2$ and $n = 3$, so that
$\operatorname{def}_3(G) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. At a flat
configuration, all heights zero, every hinge lies in one plane and the
rank is exactly $6|V(G)| - 3 - \dim L(q)$ (\cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank});
a partition count bounds $\dim L(q)$ below by
$3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}).
At a general drawing of a simple graph the bound is an equality
(\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}). This is the form in which the argument
uses Jackson and Jord\'an's pin-collinear theorem~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin};
here it is derived from the planar case of Katoh and Tanigawa's rank
formula (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows}), over every infinite field. So
where the two deficiencies agree the flat configuration attains
(\cref{cor:pencil-jj-flat}).

\emph{The induction.} The general configuration attains at every graph
satisfying the standing hypotheses, finite, simple and connected with
minimum degree at least two (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), by a strong
induction on the number of bodies (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-reduction-attains},
\cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}). Each step deduces that the general
configuration of $G$ attains from the same conclusion at smaller graphs
on the same bodies and edge labels. It takes one drawing that is general
for the smaller graphs and of least $\dim L(q)$ for $G$, and finds the
heights inside the single fibre $L_G(q)$. At a cut vertex and along a
chain of bridges, $G$ is a fibre product of two smaller graphs whose
ranks and deficiencies add (\cref{sec:main-component-cut}). Across an ear
the rank grows with the span of its hinges together with the relative
screws of its two ends. With five or more interior bodies the hinges span
the whole screw space at a general configuration, as one hexagon of joins
certifies (\cref{sec:main-component-chain}). With fewer, the span is
counted at a suitable configuration: with two interior bodies, from three
independent joins; with one, by choosing the drawing of the interior body
together with the heights of the rest; and with three or four, or two at
non-adjacent ends, against $G$ with its second interior body suppressed,
which is then put back on a line through a neighbour
(\cref{sec:main-component-short}, \cref{sec:main-component-orbit}). A body
of degree two whose neighbours are not adjacent is split off as in
Jackson and Jord\'an's proof~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin}: it starts on the
line through the points of its neighbours and moves along a curve to a
general position, where the rank is at least its starting value
(\cref{sec:main-component-splitoff}). At a rigid core the step is Katoh
and Tanigawa's contraction case~\cite[Lemma~6.3]{katohTanigawa2011}, read
along a curve on which the core shrinks to one point and the heights
solve a rescaled lifting system whose kernel does not jump at the end of
the curve (\cref{sec:main-component-contract},
\cref{sec:main-component-contract-additive}). That every graph
satisfying the standing hypotheses admits one of these steps is a case
analysis on $\operatorname{def}_2$ and $\operatorname{def}_3$ alone
(\cref{sec:main-component-sparse}, \cref{sec:main-component-coverage}): a
bridge or a cut vertex; a cycle; equal deficiencies; a planar-rigid set,
contracted at a maximal one; and otherwise, in a sparse 2-connected graph
that is not a cycle, a chain of bodies of degree two to which an ear or
split-off step applies, or a rigid core at which the planar deficiencies
add (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-theorem-s}). The distinct statement follows
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement}).

\emph{The generic statement.} It does not follow fibre by fibre: at two
pencil hubs with three common neighbours, $K_{2,3}$ the smallest, no
configuration over a general drawing is nondegenerate
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-two-hubs-obstruction}). It is proved instead inside
the induction of the pencil reduction (\cref{thm:pencil-reduction}), from
the conditioned pair at every smaller graph
(\cref{thm:pencil-generic-step}). Without a planar-rigid set of two or
more bodies, Jackson and Jord\'an's equality, at $G$ and at $G$ with one
new body joined to two of its bodies, separates the planes of those two
bodies, and the general configuration is nondegenerate
(\cref{thm:pencil-x0-base-generic}). Otherwise a minimal planar-rigid set
gives a body of degree two between two hubs across which the deficiency
does not drop, or a pendant triangle at a body of degree at least four
(\cref{lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear}). The generic realization of the
smaller graph, steered to meet the conditions the new bodies need,
extends across it (\cref{lem:pencil-generic-steer},
\cref{lem:pencil-generic-one-ear},
\cref{lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle}). The graphs this induction
meets that are not two-edge-connected are the reduction's cut-edge case
(\cref{lem:pencil-cut-case}), and the result is the conditioned pair at
every multigraph with at least one body
(\cref{thm:pencil-conditioned-pair-nonempty}).

\subsection{Planar pictures, the lifting space, and the main component}
\label{sec:main-component-carrier}

Throughout, $G$ is a finite simple connected multigraph of minimum
degree at least two and $K$ is an infinite field. This subsection
introduces planar pictures and their lifting spaces, reads every
configuration over an admissible picture as a pencil realization, and
proves that one configuration at the deficiency rank over a main
picture forces the general one (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}).

\begin{definition}[Admissible planar picture]
  \label{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate}
  A \emph{planar picture} of $G$ is a map $q \colon V(G) \to K^2$,
  written $q_v = (x_v, y_v)$; its \emph{homogeneous picture points} are
  the vectors $(x_v, y_v, 1) \in K^3$. The picture is \emph{admissible}
  if $q_u \neq q_v$ for every edge $uv$ and, for every body $v$, three
  members of the closed neighbourhood of $v$
  (\cref{def:pencil-nondegenerate}) have linearly independent homogeneous
  picture points, that is, the picture points over the closed
  neighbourhood are not collinear.
\end{definition}

Admissibility is a finite conjunction of non-vanishing conditions, one
coordinate difference for each edge and one $3 \times 3$ determinant for
each body, so the admissible pictures form a Zariski-open subset of
$K^{2|V(G)|}$. It is nonempty, since $K$ is infinite and every closed
neighbourhood has at least three members by the degree hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[The lifting space]
  \label{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  \lean{Graph.liftingSpace, Graph.affineLifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $q$ be a planar picture of $G$. A \emph{height} is a map
  $z \colon V(G) \to K$. The \emph{lifting space} $L(q)$ is the set of
  heights $z$ such that, for every body $v$, the restriction of $z$ to
  the closed neighbourhood of $v$ agrees with an affine function of the
  picture there: there are $a, b, c \in K$ with
  $z_w = a x_w + b y_w + c$ for every $w$ in that neighbourhood. The
  space $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ of \emph{globally affine} heights is the
  subspace of heights $z_w = \alpha x_w + \beta y_w + \gamma$ for a
  single triple $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \in K^3$, the same at every
  body.
\end{definition}

The conditions are linear in $z$, so $L(q)$ is a linear subspace of
$K^{V(G)}$.

\begin{lemma}[The lifting space contains the affine heights]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  \lean{Graph.affineLifts_le_liftingSpace, Graph.finrank_affineLifts,
        Graph.three_le_finrank_liftingSpace}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  $\mathrm{Aff}(q) \subseteq L(q)$ at every picture $q$. At an
  admissible picture, provided $G$ has at least one body,
  $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ is three-dimensional; in particular
  $\dim L(q) \geq 3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A single triple $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ already restricts to an
  affine function of the picture on every closed neighbourhood, giving
  the containment with no hypothesis on $q$. At an admissible picture,
  some body's closed neighbourhood carries three members with linearly
  independent homogeneous picture points, so the coefficients
  $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ are pinned down by their values there: the
  linear map from coefficients to heights is injective, hence an
  isomorphism onto its image $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$. The dimension bound
  follows from the containment by monotonicity of dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-lifting-space}
  A height is a function on the whole ambient set of bodies that
  vanishes off $V(G)$, so that for a graph not spanning the body set
  the dimension of $L(q)$ is still that of a space of heights on
  $V(G)$.
\end{fmlnote}

When $G$ is a subgraph of a larger graph, a picture of the larger graph
is read as a picture of $G$ by restriction to $V(G)$, and only the bodies
of $G$ matter.

\begin{lemma}[Admissibility and the lifting space read only the bodies of the graph]
  \label{lem:pencil-picture-local}
  \lean{Graph.isAdmissiblePicture_congr, Graph.liftingSpace_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ and $q'$ be planar pictures of a set of bodies containing
  $V(G)$ that agree at every body of $G$. Then $q$ is admissible for $G$
  if and only if $q'$ is, and $L_G(q) = L_G(q')$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Admissibility reads the picture at the two ends of each edge and on
  each closed neighbourhood, and the lifting space on each closed
  neighbourhood; all of these consist of bodies of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Configurations and their planes]
  \label{def:pencil-configuration}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigPoint,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicture,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicture_ne_zero_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicturePoly}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  A planar picture $q$ and a height $z$ give the \emph{configuration}
  $p = (q, z)$ of homogeneous points
  $p_v = (x_v, y_v, z_v, 1) \in K^4$. Given a choice of three bodies
  $a_v, b_v, c_v$ at every body $v$, the \emph{plane normal} of $p$ at
  $v$ is the vector $n_v \in K^4$ with
  $n_v \cdot w = \det[p_{a_v}, p_{b_v}, p_{c_v}, w]$ for every
  $w \in K^4$.
\end{definition}

The plane normal is polynomial in the picture coordinates and, at a
fixed picture, in the height coordinates; it is orthogonal to the
three chosen points, and nonzero exactly when they are linearly
independent. When the three chosen bodies lie in the closed
neighbourhood of $v$ with non-collinear picture points and
$z \in L(q)$, it is, up to a nonzero scalar
(\cref{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}), the normal of the
plane containing the points of the whole closed neighbourhood
(\cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}), whichever three are
chosen.

\begin{lemma}[The plane of a selector contains the closed neighbourhood]
  \label{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.dotProduct_pencilNormalOfPicture_eq_zero_of_mem_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-configuration, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ be a picture, $z \in L(q)$, and $v$ a body of $G$ whose
  three chosen bodies $a_v, b_v, c_v$ lie in the closed neighbourhood
  of $v$. Then the plane normal $n_v$ is orthogonal to $p_w$ for every
  $w$ in the closed neighbourhood of $v$, not only the three chosen
  bodies.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Membership in $L(q)$ gives, at $v$, coefficients $h \in K^3$
  expressing every point of the closed neighbourhood as the image,
  under one fixed linear map $K^3 \to K^4$ built from $h$, of its
  homogeneous picture point. Four images under one linear map of four
  vectors of the $3$-dimensional $K^3$ are always linearly dependent,
  so the determinant defining $n_v \cdot p_w$ vanishes for every $w$ in
  the neighbourhood, including the three chosen bodies themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The plane normal is independent of the selector, up to a scalar]
  \label{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_smul_pencilNormalOfPicture_eq_of_mem_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  Let $q$ be admissible, $z \in L(q)$, and $v$ a body of $G$. For two
  choices of three bodies at $v$, each with non-collinear picture
  points and lying in the closed neighbourhood of $v$, the two
  resulting plane normals differ by a nonzero scalar.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Both normals are nonzero, since a plane normal vanishes exactly when
  its three defining points are dependent, and here both triples are
  independent. Fix the second choice's three points: the first normal
  is orthogonal to them by \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  (they lie in the closed neighbourhood), and the second normal is
  orthogonal to them by its own defining orthogonality. Those three
  points are independent, so their common perp in $K^4$ is
  one-dimensional; two nonzero vectors of a one-dimensional space are
  scalar multiples of one another.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The pencil condition is linear in the heights]
  \label{lem:pencil-condition-linear}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.mem_liftingSpace_of_coplanar,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.exists_smul_eq_interpolant}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-configuration,
        def:pencil-panel-realization}
  Let $q$ be admissible and $z$ a height. The configuration $p = (q, z)$
  has every closed neighbourhood coplanar if and only if $z \in L(q)$.
  Every such configuration is a pencil realization
  (\cref{def:pencil-panel-realization}): adjacent bodies carry distinct
  points, and each body's plane is unique and non-vertical.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd,
        lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar,
        lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}
  If $z \in L(q)$, admissibility gives at $v$ three closed-neighbourhood
  members with non-collinear picture points, whose plane normal is
  nonzero and, by \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd},
  orthogonal to the whole closed neighbourhood: the neighbourhood is
  coplanar. Conversely, if some nonzero normal annihilates the whole
  closed neighbourhood of $v$, evaluating it at those three non-collinear
  members forces its height coefficient nonzero --- else it would already
  annihilate three independent picture points --- and solving for $z_w$
  exhibits $z$ as an affine function of the picture on the neighbourhood,
  giving $z \in L(q)$.

  Adjacent points are distinct because $q_u \neq q_v$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}). If $z_w = ax_w + by_w
  + c$ on the neighbourhood of $v$, its points lie on the plane
  $\zeta = a\xi + b\eta + c$; by the argument of
  \cref{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}, any nonzero normal of the
  neighbourhood is a nonzero scalar multiple of $(a, b, -1, c)$, so its
  height coefficient is nonzero (the plane is non-vertical) and it is
  determined by $a, b, c$ up to scale (the plane is unique).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Main pictures]
  \label{def:pencil-main-picture}
  \lean{Graph.IsMainPicture}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $\ell_0$ be the least value of $\dim L(q)$ over admissible
  pictures $q$ of $G$. A \emph{main picture} of $G$ is an admissible
  picture $q$ with $\dim L(q) = \ell_0$, and $U$ denotes the set of main
  pictures.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The general configuration attains]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-attains}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-configuration, def:panel-hinge-framework,
        def:rigidity-matrix, def:D-deficiency}
  The \emph{rank} of a configuration $p = (q, z)$ of $G$ is the rank of
  the rigidity matrix (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}) of the panel-hinge
  framework on $G$ whose panel normal at each body $v$ is $p_v$
  (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). The \emph{general configuration of
  $G$ attains} if there is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture
  coordinates such that every picture $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is
  admissible and the heights $z \in L(q)$ at which $(q, z)$ has rank
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency},
  at $n = 3$) contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $L(q)$.
\end{definition}

The hinge of that framework at an edge $uv$ is the meet of the planes
$p_u^{\perp}$ and $p_v^{\perp}$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}),
the image of the line $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the complement
isomorphism, and it is nonzero whenever $q_u \neq q_v$. An invertible
linear map of the screw space applied to every hinge leaves the rank
unchanged (\cref{lem:screw-map-rows}), so the rank of a configuration
is also the rank of the body-hinge framework with hinges
$p_u \wedge p_v$, the framework of a pencil realization. Taking the
plane normals $n_v$ as panel normals instead would not give this rank:
where two adjacent planes coincide the meet is zero, and a zero hinge
allows no relative motion of its two bodies.

\Cref{def:pencil-x0-attains} makes sense over any field, but only over
an infinite field does $P \neq 0$ make the good pictures a nonempty
Zariski-open set: over a finite field a nonzero polynomial can vanish at
every point, and the condition then holds vacuously.

\begin{lemma}[The rank of a configuration is its point-join rank]
  \label{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_span_rigidityRows_pencilConfigFramework,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigFramework,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.pencilConfigFramework_supportExtensor_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:body-hinge-framework, def:extensor,
        lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular, lem:screw-map-rows}
  Let $p = (q, z)$ be a configuration of $G$, and let $F_p$ be the
  body-hinge framework on $G$ (\cref{def:body-hinge-framework}) whose
  hinge at each edge $uv$ is the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  (\cref{def:extensor}). Then the rigidity matrix of $F_p$ has the rank
  of $p$ (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). If $q$ is admissible, every
  hinge of $F_p$ is nonzero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The hinge at $uv$ of the panel-hinge framework defining the rank of
  $p$ is the meet of the planes $p_u^{\perp}$ and $p_v^{\perp}$, the
  image of $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the polarity
  (\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}), an invertible linear map of
  the screw space over every field; by \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} the two
  rigidity matrices have the same rank. Over an admissible picture
  adjacent picture points differ, so the points $(x_u, y_u, z_u, 1)$
  and $(x_v, y_v, z_v, 1)$ are linearly independent and their join is
  nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  A body-hinge framework carries a hinge for every label of the ambient
  set of edge labels, including labels that are not edges of $G$. The
  formal framework $F_p$ carries there one fixed nonzero line, which no
  rigidity row reads, so that every hinge is nonzero as a pencil
  realization requires.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A configuration over an admissible picture is a pencil realization]
  \label{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.hasDistinctPencilRealization,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.hasPencilPanelRealization_pencilConfigFramework}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd,
        def:pencil-panel-realization, def:pencil-distinct-motive}
  Let $q$ be an admissible picture of $G$, $z \in L(q)$ and
  $p = (q, z)$. Choose at every body $v$ three members of its closed
  neighbourhood with non-collinear picture points, and let $n_v$ be the
  resulting plane normal (\cref{def:pencil-configuration}). Then the
  framework $F_p$ of \cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}, with
  panel normals $n_v$ and concurrency points $p_v$, is a pencil
  realization of $G$ (\cref{def:pencil-panel-realization}) whose
  adjacent points are linearly independent. If moreover $p$ has rank
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, then $G$ has an
  adjacent-distinct pencil realization (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive},
  at $n = 3$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The three chosen configuration points at $v$ are independent, since
  their picture points are, so $n_v \neq 0$. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}, $n_v$ is orthogonal
  to $p_w$ for every $w$ in the closed neighbourhood of $v$.

  For an edge $uv$ both $u$ and $v$ lie in the closed neighbourhoods of
  $u$ and of $v$, so $p_u$ and $p_v$ are orthogonal to $n_u$ and to
  $n_v$: the line $p_u \wedge p_v$ lies in both panels, and it passes
  through both points by construction. Taking $w = v$ gives the
  incidence $p_v \cdot n_v = 0$, and $p_v \neq 0$ since its last
  coordinate is $1$. Adjacent points are independent because adjacent
  picture points differ, and every hinge is nonzero
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). The rank of $F_p$ is the
  rank of $p$ by the same lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scaling and affine shifts of the heights preserve the rank]
  \label{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_smul_add_affineLifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank, def:pencil-lifting-space,
        lem:screw-map-rows}
  Let $q$ be a picture of $G$, $z$ a height, $t \in K$ nonzero and
  $a \in \mathrm{Aff}(q)$. Then the configurations $(q, z)$ and
  $(q, tz + a)$ have the same rank (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Write $a_w = \alpha x_w + \beta y_w + \gamma$ on $V(G)$. The linear
  map $(\xi, \eta, \zeta, \omega) \mapsto
  (\xi, \eta, t\zeta + \alpha\xi + \beta\eta + \gamma\omega, \omega)$ of
  $K^4$ is invertible, since $t \neq 0$, and carries the point of
  $(q, z)$ at every body to the point of $(q, tz + a)$ there. It induces
  an invertible linear map of the screw space taking each line
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ to the line through the two image points, so by
  \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} the two frameworks of
  \cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank} have rigidity matrices of the
  same rank, and that lemma converts both to the rank of a
  configuration.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The hinges are affine in the heights]
  \label{rem:pencil-hinge-affine}
  Fix a picture $q$. For an edge $uv$ the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  (\cref{def:extensor}) is an affine function of the height $z$: each of
  its Grassmann coordinates is a $2 \times 2$ minor of the two points
  $(x_u, y_u, z_u, 1)$ and $(x_v, y_v, z_v, 1)$, and the minors reading
  the height coordinate are linear in $z$, the others constant in $z$.
  Consequently the augmented motion matrix at $(q, z)$, whose unknowns
  are a screw $m_v$ for every body and a scalar $\omega_e$ for every
  edge, subject to $m_u - m_v = \omega_e (p_u \wedge p_v)$ at each edge
  $e = uv$, is a picture-only matrix plus one linear in $z$ and supported
  on the $\omega$ columns; when every hinge is nonzero its rank is
  $|E(G)|$ plus the rank of the rigidity matrix of the pencil
  realization. Only one consequence of this is used here, the invariance
  of the rank under scaling and affine shifts of the heights, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift} proves it directly.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[One attaining configuration forces the general one]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_exists,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.exists_mvPolynomial,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.supportExtensor_ne_zero,
        Graph.liftingMatrix, Graph.map_ker_liftingMatrix,
        Graph.finrank_ker_liftingMatrix,
        Matrix.exists_mvPolynomial_section_mulVec_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture, def:pencil-x0-attains,
        def:panel-support-extensor, lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Let $G$ have at least one body, let $q_0$ be a main picture of $G$
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}) and $z_0 \in L(q_0)$, and suppose the
  configuration $(q_0, z_0)$ has rank at least
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). Then the general configuration of $G$
  attains.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Admissibility is the non-vanishing of finitely many polynomials in the
  picture, one coordinate difference for each pair of adjacent bodies and
  one $3 \times 3$ determinant for each body, so some polynomial nonzero
  at $q_0$ has only admissible non-roots. Next, $L(q)$ is the projection
  to the heights of the kernel of a linear system $M(q)$ in the heights
  $z$ and one coefficient vector $h_v \in K^3$ per body, requiring
  $z_w = h_v \cdot (x_w, y_w, 1)$ on the closed neighbourhood of $v$ and
  vanishing off $V(G)$; its entries are affine in $q$. Over an admissible
  picture the projection is injective, because three non-collinear
  picture points in each closed neighbourhood determine $h_v$, so there
  the kernel has dimension $\dim L(q)$, least at the main picture $q_0$.

  Lift $z_0$ to a kernel vector $x_0$ at $q_0$. Take a projection $\pi$
  onto $\ker M(q_0)$ and a left inverse $H$ of the stacked matrix
  $[M(q_0); \pi]$ (the rows of $M(q_0)$ above those of $\pi$), which is
  injective; then $S(q) = H\,[M(q); \pi]$ is a square matrix polynomial
  in $q$ with $S(q_0) = 1$. At an admissible
  picture $q$ with $\det S(q) \neq 0$ the stack is injective, so $\pi$
  embeds $\ker M(q)$ into $\ker M(q_0)$, onto because $\dim \ker M(q)$ is
  at least $\dim \ker M(q_0)$. The preimage $x$ of $x_0$ has
  $S(q) x = x_0$, and by Cramer's rule
  $\operatorname{adj} S(q)\, x_0 = \det S(q)\, x$ lies in the kernel: a
  section of the kernel, polynomial in $q$, passing through $x_0$ at
  $q_0$. Its height part $Z(q)$ lies in $L(q)$.

  The rank of a configuration is the rank of the panel-hinge framework
  with the configuration points as panel normals. Its hinges are nonzero
  over an admissible picture (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), since
  two points $(x, y, z, 1)$ over distinct picture points are
  independent. So \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} gives a
  polynomial $Q$ in the configuration coordinates, nonzero at
  $(q_0, z_0)$, whose non-vanishing forces the target rank as a lower
  bound. Take $P$ to be the admissibility polynomial times $\det S(q)$
  times $Q$ at the configuration $(q, Z(q))$, and, for a picture $q$ with
  $P(q) \neq 0$, take $R = Q$ at $(q, \cdot)$. Then $P(q_0) \neq 0$, the
  height $Z(q) \in L(q)$ has $R(Z(q)) \neq 0$, and at every $z \in L(q)$
  with $R(z) \neq 0$ the rank is at least the target and, every hinge
  being nonzero, at most the target
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

The lifting system in this proof reads each closed neighbourhood through
the homogeneous picture points $(x_w, y_w, 1)$. The contraction step
(\cref{sec:main-component-contract}) uses the same system with other
vectors in their place.

\begin{definition}[The lifting system with weights]
  \label{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system}
  \lean{Graph.weightedLiftingMatrix}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $\omega$ assign a vector $\omega(v, w) \in K^3$ to every ordered
  pair of bodies. The \emph{lifting system with weights $\omega$} of $G$
  is the homogeneous linear system in the heights $z$ and one coefficient
  vector $h_v \in K^3$ per body $v$ of $G$ requiring
  $z_w = h_v \cdot \omega(v, w)$ for every body $v$ and every $w$ in the
  closed neighbourhood of $v$.
\end{definition}

With $\omega(v, w) = (x_w, y_w, 1)$ this is the lifting system $M(q)$ of
\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}'s proof, whose solutions have heights
exactly $L(q)$.

\begin{lemma}[The main pictures are nonempty and Zariski-open]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isAdmissiblePicture, Graph.exists_isMainPicture,
        Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_isMainPicture}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture}
  There is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that
  every picture $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is a main picture. In particular
  the set $U$ of main pictures is nonempty and Zariski-open.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  On the moment curve $v \mapsto (t_v, t_v^2)$ at pairwise distinct
  parameters $t_v$, adjacent bodies lie at distinct points, and any three
  members of a closed neighbourhood, which has at least three by the
  degree hypothesis, lie at non-collinear points, since three points of a
  parabola at distinct parameters never are collinear: this picture is
  admissible.
  Among admissible pictures the natural number $\dim L(q)$ therefore
  attains a least value at some admissible $q_0$, which is a main
  picture. The semicontinuity argument of \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness},
  run at the trivial kernel vector $0$ in place of a witness height, gives
  a second polynomial, nonzero at $q_0$, off whose zero set $\dim L(q)$
  does not exceed $\dim L(q_0)$; off the product of that polynomial with
  the one witnessing admissibility near $q_0$, $q$ is admissible with
  $\dim L(q) \le \dim L(q_0)$, hence a main picture.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}
  In place of the standing hypotheses on $G$, the formal statements
  assume the two consequences the construction uses: $G$ has no loops,
  and every closed neighbourhood has at least three members.
\end{fmlnote}

  member $z$. Apply \cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization} to
  $(q, z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two open conditions on a fibre meet]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}
  \lean{MvPolynomial.exists_mem_eval_ne_zero₂}
  \leanok
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $L$ be a linear subspace of $K^\sigma$
  for an index set $\sigma$. If two polynomials in the coordinates of
  $\sigma$ are each nonzero somewhere on $L$, some single point of $L$
  makes both nonzero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Restrict both polynomials to the line through the two witnessing
  points of $L$, an affine substitution of one variable for the
  coordinates of $\sigma$ turning each into a univariate polynomial.
  Each restriction is nonzero, since it agrees with the corresponding
  hypothesis at one of the line's two ends, so their product is a
  nonzero univariate polynomial over the infinite field $K$ and has a
  non-root there. The corresponding point of the line lies in $L$,
  since a linear subspace is closed under it, and both original
  polynomials are nonzero there.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The main component]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_isMainPicture,
        MvPolynomial.exists_mem_eval_ne_zero₂,
        Graph.x0Attains_of_exists,
        Graph.X0Attains.hasDistinctPencilRealization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture, def:pencil-x0-attains,
        def:pencil-distinct-motive, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $G$ have at least one body, no loops, and
  at least three members in every closed neighbourhood. Then the main
  pictures of $G$ (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}) contain the non-roots
  of a nonzero polynomial in the picture coordinates. Over any picture
  $q$, two polynomials in the heights that are each nonzero somewhere on
  $L(q)$ are nonzero together somewhere on $L(q)$. Moreover, if a single
  configuration $(q, z)$ with $q$ a main picture and $z \in L(q)$ has
  rank at least $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, then the
  general configuration of $G$ attains (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}),
  and $G$ has an adjacent-distinct pencil realization
  (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive}, at $n = 3$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The first claim is \cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}, and the
  second is \cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection} applied to the
  linear subspace $L(q)$ of the heights. For the third, $L(q)$ is at
  every picture the height projection of the kernel of the lifting system
  (\texttt{Graph.map\_ker\_liftingMatrix}), so a Cramer section of that
  system through the witness, composed with a rank polynomial nonzero at
  the witness, shows that the general configuration attains
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}); an attaining general configuration
  gives the realization (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The geometry of the main component]
  \label{rem:pencil-x0-main-component}
  Over the set $U$ of main pictures the dimension of $L(q)$ is constant,
  equal to $\ell_0$, and $L(q)$ is the height projection of the kernel of
  a matrix polynomial in $q$, so the pairs $(q, z)$ with $q \in U$ and
  $z \in L(q)$ form a vector bundle over $U$. Its closure $X_0$, the
  \emph{main component} of the pencil configuration space, is
  irreducible of dimension $2|V(G)| + \ell_0$, and it contains every
  flat configuration $(q, 0)$ with $q \in U$. The statements of this
  subsection never form $X_0$ itself: in them, ``general'' means off the
  zero set of a nonzero polynomial in the picture coordinates and, fibre
  by fibre, off the zero set of a nonzero polynomial in the heights
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). The irreducibility of $X_0$ enters only
  through the fibre statement of \cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}, by
  which two polynomials each nonzero somewhere on one fibre $L(q)$ are
  nonzero there together, and through products of picture polynomials;
  the dimension of $X_0$ is not used.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The flat rank}
\label{sec:main-component-flat}

This subsection computes the rank of a \emph{flat} configuration
$(q, 0)$, all heights zero, over an admissible picture $q$, and derives
from it the case of the induction in which the two deficiencies agree.
Every hinge of a flat configuration lies in the plane $z = 0$, so the
screw space splits into a part the edges glue together and a planar
part. The planar part's motions are the families of affine functions on
$K^2$ that agree at both ends of every edge, and the map evaluating each
at its own picture point identifies them with the lifting space. So the
flat rank is exactly $6|V(G)| - 3 - \dim L(q)$
(\cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank}), and a partition count on the planar part
bounds $\dim L(q)$ below by $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$
(\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}). This is an instance of the
classical correspondence between the motions of a flat panel structure
and the polyhedra over it (Crapo--Whiteley~\cite{crapoWhiteley1982},
Example~4.4, for the tetrahedron), with $L(q)$ the space of liftings of
the picture in the sense of scene analysis
(Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1996}, \S 8.3); the proofs below are
self-contained. Throughout, $\operatorname{def}_2$ and
$\operatorname{def}_3$ are the deficiencies of \cref{def:D-deficiency}
at $n = 2$ and $n = 3$ ($D = 3$ and $D = 6$).

\begin{definition}[The lifting planes]
  \label{def:pencil-lifting-planes}
  \lean{Graph.liftingPlanes}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $q$ be a planar picture of $G$, and identify a vector
  $h \in K^3$ with the affine function $(x, y) \mapsto h \cdot (x, y, 1)$
  on $K^2$. The space of \emph{lifting planes} $F(q)$ is the set of
  families $(h_v)_{v \in V(G)}$ of affine functions with
  $(h_u - h_v)(q_u) = (h_u - h_v)(q_v) = 0$ for every edge $uv$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The lifting planes and the lifting space]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_liftingPlanes}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ be admissible. Then $\dim F(q) = \dim L(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Define $\Phi(h)_w = h_w(q_w)$. For a neighbour $u$ of $v$,
  $h_u(q_u) = h_v(q_u)$, so $\Phi(h)$ restricts on the closed
  neighbourhood of $v$ to the affine function $h_v$, and
  $\Phi(h) \in L(q)$. Conversely, for $z \in L(q)$ let $h_v$ be an affine
  function agreeing with $z$ on the closed neighbourhood of $v$; both
  ends of an edge $uv$ lie in both closed neighbourhoods, so
  $(h_v)_v \in F(q)$, and $\Phi$ maps it to $z$. If $\Phi(h) = 0$, then
  $h_v$ vanishes at every point of the closed neighbourhood of $v$,
  three of which are not collinear, so $h_v = 0$. Hence $\Phi$ is an
  isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-lifting-planes}
  The families of lifting planes range over the whole ambient set of
  bodies and are unconstrained off $V(G)$, as the infinitesimal motions
  of every framework are. Each body off $V(G)$ adds three to
  $\dim F(q)$, and six to the dimension of the motions in
  \cref{lem:pencil-flat-split}; these terms cancel in every rank.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The lifting planes are the motions of a plane framework]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_pencilPicturePoint,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $q$ be admissible, and let $P_q$ be the panel-hinge framework in
  the plane on $G$ whose panel normal at each body $v$ is
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}, at $n = 2$). Then
  the infinitesimal motions of $P_q$ form a space isomorphic to $F(q)$,
  and $\operatorname{rank} R(P_q) = 3|V(G)| - \dim L(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor}
  The screw space of the plane is $K^3$. The hinge of $P_q$ at an edge
  $uv$ is the meet of the lines with normals $(x_u, y_u, 1)$ and
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), a nonzero vector
  orthogonal to both, since $q_u \ne q_v$. So a relative screw is a
  multiple of the hinge exactly when it is orthogonal to both normals,
  which is the condition defining $F(q)$. The rank follows from
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim} by rank--nullity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The flat lower bound on the lifting space]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  \lean{Graph.three_add_deficiency_le_finrank_liftingSpace}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ have at least one body and let $q$ be admissible. Then
  $\dim L(q) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, where
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \max_P\,[3(|P| - 1) - 2d(P)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  By \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions} and \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}
  it suffices to bound the motions of $P_q$ below by
  $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Its bodies are $3$-dimensional and each
  hinge imposes two conditions. For a partition $P$, the part-wise
  constant assignments form a $3|P|$-dimensional space, on which the
  edges inside a part impose nothing and each crossing edge at most two
  conditions; this is the relative deficiency bound
  \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}, at $D = 3$, and gives at least
  $3|P| - 2d(P) = 3 + [3(|P| - 1) - 2d(P)]$ motions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The flat split]
  \label{lem:pencil-flat-split}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.flatScrewEquiv,
        Graph.finrank_linkConstants,
        Graph.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_pointJoinFramework_flat}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  Let $G$ be connected, let $q$ be admissible, and let $F_{(q,0)}$ be the
  body-hinge framework whose hinge at each edge $uv$ is the line
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ of the flat configuration $p = (q, 0)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). Then its infinitesimal
  motions form a space of dimension $3 + \dim F(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, def:hinge-constraint}
  At $z = 0$ every hinge $p_u \wedge p_v$ lies in
  $W_\Pi = \operatorname{span}(e_{01}, e_{03}, e_{13})$, the Pl\"ucker
  coordinates of lines in the plane $z = 0$. Split
  $\Lambda^2 K^4 = W' \oplus W_\Pi$ with
  $W' = \operatorname{span}(e_{02}, e_{12}, e_{23})$ and write a screw as
  $m_v = (m'_v, m^\Pi_v)$. The hinge condition
  $m_u - m_v \in \operatorname{span}(p_u \wedge p_v)$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) splits into $m'_u = m'_v$ and
  $m^\Pi_u - m^\Pi_v \in \operatorname{span}(p_u \wedge p_v)$. Since $G$
  is connected, $m'$ is constant, a $3$-dimensional contribution.
  Identifying $W_\Pi = \Lambda^2 \hat\Pi$, $\hat\Pi = \operatorname{span}
  (e_0, e_1, e_3)$, with the affine functions on $K^2$ by
  $a \wedge b \mapsto \det(a, b, \cdot)$, the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  becomes the affine functions vanishing at $q_u$ and $q_v$, so the
  planar part's solutions are $F(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The flat rank]
  \label{thm:pencil-flat-rank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_le,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_eq_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-lifting-space, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be connected and let $q$ be admissible. Then the rank
  $\operatorname{rank} R(q, 0)$ of the flat configuration
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}) is exactly
  $6|V(G)| - 3 - \dim L(q)$; hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(q, 0) \le 6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_2(G)$,
  with equality if and only if $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-flat-split, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  The rank of $(q, 0)$ is that of $F_{(q,0)}$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}), which is $6|V(G)|$ minus
  the dimension $3 + \dim F(q) = 3 + \dim L(q)$ of its motions
  (\cref{lem:pencil-flat-split} and \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}). The
  bound and its equality case then follow from
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The flat configuration attains]
  \label{cor:pencil-flat-attains}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_liftingSpace_eq_three_add_deficiency_three_iff,
        Graph.x0Attains_of_finrank_liftingSpace_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-flat-rank, def:pencil-x0-attains}
  Let $G$ be connected and let $q$ be admissible. The flat configuration
  at $q$ attains $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ if and only if
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, if and only if equality holds
  in \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} at $q$ and
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$. If
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency, lem:deficiency-antitone,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness, def:pencil-main-picture}
  The first equivalence is the exact rank of \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank}. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-antitone},
  $\dim L(q) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$,
  which gives the second, and shows that under
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ every admissible picture
  has $\dim L \ge \dim L(q)$: $q$ is a main picture
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}), and the flat configuration, at rank
  at least the target, is a witness for \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A flat main component attains]
  \label{cor:pencil-flat-x0}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_finrank_liftingSpace_eq_three,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_of_finrank_eq_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:pencil-flat-attains, lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  Let $G$ be connected, and suppose that $\dim L(q) = 3$ at some
  admissible picture $q$, so that $\ell_0 = 3$ and the main component is
  flat. Then $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G) = 0$, the
  flat configuration at every admissible $q$ with $\dim L(q) = 3$ has
  rank $6(|V(G)| - 1)$, and the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-flat-rank, lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency,
        lem:deficiency-antitone}
  \Cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} gives
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) \le 0$, so both deficiencies vanish
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-antitone}; both are nonnegative). Then
  \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank} gives the rank $6|V(G)| - 6$, and
  \cref{cor:pencil-flat-attains} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Jackson and Jord\'an's equality}
\label{sec:main-component-jj}

By \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank} the flat configuration reaches the
$\operatorname{def}_2$ bound exactly when $\dim L(q) = 3 +
\operatorname{def}_2(G)$. That this equality holds at the general
picture is the form in which the argument uses Jackson and Jord\'an's
pin-collinear theorem~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin}, at $G$ itself and at
the smaller graphs of the contraction, chain and split-off steps. This
subsection derives it over every infinite field from the planar rank
theorem, Katoh and Tanigawa's rank formula~\cite{katohTanigawa2011} at
$d = 2$ (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows}), read in the chart
$(x_v, y_v, 1)$ of the plane framework of
\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}. The graph need not be
connected and need not span the set of bodies.

\begin{lemma}[Rescaling the panel normals]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-rescale}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.panelSupportExtensor_smul_right,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ofNormals_smul,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_smul,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_smul}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:panel-support-extensor,
        def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $(n_v)$ be panel normals and $(c_v)$ nonzero scalars. At every
  edge $e$ with recorded ends $u, v$, the panel-hinge framework on $G$
  with normals $c_v n_v$ has the hinge $c_u c_v\, C(e)$, where $C(e)$ is
  its hinge at the normals $n_v$. So the two frameworks have the same
  infinitesimal motions and rigidity matrices of the same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  The support extensor is linear in each of its two normals
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), and a nonzero multiple of a hinge
  spans the same line, which is all the hinge constraint reads
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). Equal motion spaces give equal row
  spans (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The planar rank theorem in the chart]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-chart}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_le_finrank_span_rigidityRows_pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-rows, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be simple with at least two bodies.
  There is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that
  at every picture $q$ with $P(q) \ne 0$ the plane framework $P_q$ of
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}, with panel normal
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ at each body, has
  $\operatorname{rank} R(P_q) \ge 3(|V(G)| - 1) -
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank, def:panel-general-position,
        lem:general-position-support-nonzero, lem:pencil-jj-rescale}
  \Cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows} at $n = 2$ ($D = 3$) gives a realization
  of $G$ at rank $3(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Its rank
  polynomial (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}), a polynomial
  whose non-roots are in general position
  (\cref{def:panel-general-position}), and the product of the third
  coordinates of the normals have a common non-root $n$, since $K$ is
  infinite. Dividing each normal $n_v$ by its third coordinate gives the
  normals $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ of a picture $q'$, at the same rank and with
  nonzero hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-rescale},
  \cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). The rank polynomial at
  $q'$, composed with the chart $q \mapsto (x_v, y_v, 1)$, is nonzero at
  $q'$; it is the required $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relabelling the edges]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}
  \lean{Graph.embedEdges, Graph.vertexSet_embedEdges, Graph.embedEdges_isLink,
        Graph.edgeSet_embedEdges, Graph.closedNbhd_embedEdges, Graph.Simple.embedEdges,
        Graph.liftingSpace_embedEdges, Graph.finrank_liftingSpace_embedEdges,
        Graph.isAdmissiblePicture_embedEdges_iff, Graph.isMainPicture_embedEdges_iff,
        Graph.deficiency_embedEdges}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture,
        def:pencil-main-picture, def:D-deficiency}
  Relabel the edges of $G$ injectively into a larger set of labels.
  This keeps $G$ simple if it is simple, and it changes neither the
  lifting spaces $L(q)$, nor the admissible and the main pictures, nor
  $\operatorname{def}_n(G)$ for any $n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The bodies, the pairs of adjacent bodies and hence the closed
  neighbourhoods are unchanged, and a partition crosses exactly the
  images of the edges it crossed before.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-jj-embed-edges}
  The formal planar rank theorem is stated over a fixed type of edge
  labels with a supply of spare labels (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}),
  and \cref{lem:pencil-jj-chart} carries that supply. Jackson and
  Jord\'an's equality below mentions no edge label, so it first relabels
  the edges into a label set with room to spare
  (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}); it carries no hypothesis on the
  labels.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[Jackson and Jord\'an's equality]
  \label{thm:pencil-jj-equality}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.IsMainPicture.finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.exists_isMainPicture_finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.closedNbhd_subset_vertexSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-main-picture,
        def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be simple, with at least one body and
  at least three members in every closed neighbourhood. There is a
  nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that every
  picture $q$ with $P(q) \ne 0$ is a main picture with
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Consequently
  $\ell_0 = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$: every main picture has
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-jj-chart, lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  Relabel the edges (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}). The main
  pictures contain the non-roots of a nonzero polynomial
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}), and \cref{lem:pencil-jj-chart}
  gives a second, since $G$ has at least three bodies (a closed
  neighbourhood of a body lies in $V(G)$); let $P$ be their product. At a
  non-root $q$ the rank of
  the plane framework is $3|V(G)| - \dim L(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}), so
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} gives the reverse. A main
  picture has $\dim L(q)$ at most its value at such a non-root, and at
  least $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ by the same lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The general configuration attains when the two deficiencies agree]
  \label{cor:pencil-jj-flat}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_deficiency_two_eq_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be connected and simple, with at least
  three members in every closed neighbourhood. If
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-jj-equality, cor:pencil-flat-attains}
  \Cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality} gives an admissible picture with
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$,
  and \cref{cor:pencil-flat-attains} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cut vertices and bridges}
\label{sec:main-component-cut}

The steps of the induction share one shape. Each deduces that the
general configuration of $G$ attains the \emph{target rank}
$\operatorname{tgt}(G) = 6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
(\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}, at $n = 3$) from the same conclusion at
smaller graphs on the same bodies and edge labels. It picks one
picture generic for the smaller graphs and main for $G$, and it finds the heights inside the single lifting space
$L_G(q)$, where two nonempty Zariski-open conditions always meet
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). One configuration at the
target rank then suffices (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}). This
subsection states the standing hypotheses and the restriction of
heights to a subgraph, and proves the first two steps. At a cut vertex
and along a chain of bridges the graph is a fibre product of two
smaller graphs, and attaining passes from the two pieces to the whole.

\begin{definition}[The standing hypotheses]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-standing}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph, Graph.three_le_ncard_closedNbhd,
    Graph.IsX0Graph.three_le_ncard_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  A graph $G$ satisfies the \emph{standing hypotheses} if it is finite,
  simple and connected, with minimum degree at least two. Then every
  closed neighbourhood of $G$ has at least three members: a body of
  degree $d$ in a simple graph has $d$ distinct neighbours.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Heights restrict to a subgraph]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  \lean{Graph.liftingRestrict, Graph.liftingRestrict_apply,
    Graph.liftingRestrict_mem_liftingSpace,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigPoint_liftingRestrict,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.restrictPoly,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eval_restrictPoly}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-configuration}
  Let $G' \le G$ and let $q$ be any picture. The height that agrees with
  $z \in L_G(q)$ on $V(G')$ and vanishes elsewhere lies in
  $L_{G'}(q)$, and its configuration points agree with those of $z$ at
  the bodies of $G'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A closed neighbourhood in $G'$ lies in the corresponding closed
  neighbourhood in $G$, where $z$ agrees with an affine function of the
  picture.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rank reads only the bodies of the graph]
  \label{lem:pencil-rank-congr}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  The rank of the rigidity matrix of the panel-hinge framework on $G$
  depends only on the panel normals at the bodies of $G$, and not on
  which end of each edge is recorded first.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:hinge-constraint,
        lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  The hinge at an edge is built from the normals at its two ends, and
  swapping them negates it (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}); the
  hinge constraint reads only the line it spans
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). So the motion spaces agree, and equal
  motion spaces give equal row spans
  (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The heights at a cut vertex]
  \label{lem:pencil-cut-fibre}
  \lean{Graph.exists_liftingRestrict_eq_of_cutVertex}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  Let $G = G_1 \cup G_2$ with $V(G_1) \cap V(G_2) = \{v\}$ and disjoint
  edge sets. At every picture $q$, both restriction maps
  $L_G(q) \to L_{G_i}(q)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}) are onto.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Given $z^1 \in L_{G_1}(q)$, extend it across $V(G_2)$ by the affine
  function of its closed neighbourhood at $v$. Every closed
  neighbourhood of $G$ other than $N_G[v]$ lies in one side, and
  $N_G[v] = N_1[v] \cup N_2[v]$ carries that affine function on both, so
  the extension lies in $L_G(q)$ and restricts to $z^1$. The other side
  is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally more holds: at every $q$ admissible for $G_1$ and $G_2$,
  $L_G(q)$ is the set of pairs $(z^1, z^2) \in L_{G_1} \times L_{G_2}$
  whose planes at $v$ coincide, so $\dim L_G(q) = \dim L_{G_1} +
  \dim L_{G_2} - 3$. Only the surjectivity is formalized, which is all
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut} uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A cut vertex]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-cut}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_cutVertex}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), with $V(G) = V_1 \cup V_2$,
  $V_1 \cap V_2 = \{v\}$ and every edge of $G$ inside $V_1$ or inside
  $V_2$. If the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and $G[V_2]$ attain,
  then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-cut-fibre, lem:block-rank-cut-vertex,
        lem:deficiency-cut-vertex, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Take a picture $q$ generic for both sides and main for $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). The attaining heights of
  each side contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $L_{G[V_i]}(q)$.
  Since the restrictions are onto (\cref{lem:pencil-cut-fibre},
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}), their preimages are nonempty
  Zariski-open subsets of $L_G(q)$, and they meet
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). At such a height $z$ the
  rank of each side is its target (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}), the
  ranks add (\cref{lem:block-rank-cut-vertex}), and so do the
  deficiencies (\cref{lem:deficiency-cut-vertex}), while the body counts
  overlap in one. So $(q, z)$ has rank at least the target of $G$, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally the converse holds as well: at a general configuration of
  $G$ the ranks add and each is at most its target, so if $G$ attains,
  both sides attain. Only the ``if'' half is formalized, the half the
  induction uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The heights along a chain of bridges]
  \label{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_zero,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_eq_or_exists,
    Graph.exists_liftingRestrict_eq_of_bridgePath}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  Let $G$ be $G_1 \sqcup G_2$ plus a path $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  ($k \ge 0$ new vertices), with $a \in V(G_1)$, $b \in V(G_2)$, and
  every other edge of $G$ inside $G_1$ or inside $G_2$. At every
  picture $q$, both restriction maps $L_G(q) \to L_{G_i}(q)$ are onto.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Given $z^1 \in L_{G_1}(q)$, take an affine function $h$ agreeing
  with $z^1$ on the closed neighbourhood $N_{G_1}[a]$
  (\cref{def:pencil-lifting-space}), and extend $z^1$ by $h$ on every
  body outside $G_1$. The body $a$ is the only body of $G_1$ with an
  edge leaving $G_1$: the path's own bodies and $b$ lie outside $G_1$,
  so only the path's first edge, at $a$, can cross. So the closed
  neighbourhood in $G$ of a body of $G_1$ other than $a$ lies in $G_1$,
  where the extension is $z^1$; the closed neighbourhood of $a$, or of
  a body outside $G_1$, meets $G_1$ only inside $N_{G_1}[a]$, so the
  extension equals $h$ on all of it. The extension is therefore affine
  on every closed neighbourhood of $G$: it lies in $L_G(q)$ and
  restricts to $z^1$. The other restriction is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally more holds: at every $q$ admissible for $G$, $G_1$ and
  $G_2$, $\dim L_G(q) = \dim L_{G_1} + \dim L_{G_2} + k - 2$. The $k$
  path heights are new, the closed neighbourhoods of $a$ and $b$ each
  add one condition, and those of the path bodies, of three bodies
  each, add none. Only the surjectivity is formalized; it needs neither
  the admissibility of $q$ nor this count, and it is all
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-bridge} uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A chain of bridges]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-bridge}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_bridgePath,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_cases,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_eq_of_val_eq_succ,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_rev,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pathVertex_mem_union_image_iff,
    Graph.cutEdges_union_image_of_bridgePath,
    Graph.deficiency_induce_union_range_of_bridgePath,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.add_le_finrank_span_rigidityRows_induce_union_range_of_bridgePath}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses. Let
  $V(G)$ be the disjoint union of $V_1$, $V_2$ and path bodies
  $x_1, \dots, x_k$ ($k \ge 0$), with a path $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  from $a \in V_1$ to $b \in V_2$, every other edge of $G$ lying inside
  $V_1$ or inside $V_2$. If the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and
  $G[V_2]$ attain, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre, lem:block-rank-cut, lem:cut-edge-decomposition,
        lem:pencil-lifting-restrict, lem:pencil-rank-congr,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  As for \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut}, with \cref{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre}
  for the heights, applied to the second side from $b$ along the
  reversed path. The ranks and deficiencies are counted as numbers, not
  by asking an intermediate graph to attain: a path body on its own has
  a closed neighbourhood of one body, so no picture of it is admissible.
  Write $S_j = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_j\}$. Every edge off the path
  lies inside $V_1$ or inside $V_2$, so the only edge of $G$ leaving
  $S_j$ is the path edge from $S_j$ to the next path body, or to $b$
  when $j = k$. The last bridge therefore splits $G$ into $G[S_k]$ and
  $G[V_2]$, adding $D - 1 = 5$ to the rank (\cref{lem:block-rank-cut},
  all hinges being nonzero at an admissible picture) and $1$ to the
  deficiency (\cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}). Inside $G[S_k]$ each
  path body $x_{j+1}$ hangs from $S_j$ by a single edge, and the same two
  lemmas, applied in $G[S_{j+1}]$ at $S_j$, add at least $5$ to the rank
  and exactly $1$ to the deficiency. Against the two sides, the body
  term $6(|V(G)| - 1)$ gains $6(k + 1)$ and the deficiency gains
  $k + 1$, so
  $\operatorname{tgt}(G) = \operatorname{tgt}(G_1) +
  \operatorname{tgt}(G_2) + 5(k + 1)$. The rank at the chosen
  configuration is at least this, so \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally the converse holds as well, by the same count. Only the
  ``if'' half is formalized, as for \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Contraction at a core of planar deficiency zero}
\label{sec:main-component-contract}

This subsection proves the contraction step at a core whose planar
deficiency vanishes. Let $W \subseteq V(G)$ and $r \in W$. The
\emph{core} is the induced subgraph $H = G[W]$, and $G/H$ is the
contraction of $H$ to the body $r$ (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}): it
keeps every edge of $G$ not inside $W$, with its end in $W$ moved to $r$.
A body outside $W$ adjacent to a body of $W$ is an \emph{attachment};
$G/H$ is simple exactly when no attachment has two neighbours in $W$.
The step is Katoh and Tanigawa's contraction
case~\cite[\S6.2, Lemma~6.3]{katohTanigawa2011}, read on configurations.
A configuration of $G$ is reached along one curve, on which the core
shrinks towards the picture point of $r$. At the end of the curve every
body of $W$ sits at the point of $r$ in a configuration of $G/H$, as in
the placement of KT eq.~(6.7). The heights along the curve are the
solutions of a rescaled lifting system whose kernel does not jump at the
end of the curve, and the rank is bounded through the block-triangular
shape of KT eq.~(6.3): the rows of the core, together with the rows of
the other edges with the columns of $W$ deleted.

\begin{lemma}[The core and the contraction satisfy the standing hypotheses]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-standing}
  \lean{Graph.isX0Graph_induce_of_deficiency_two_eq_zero, Graph.isX0Graph_rigidContract_induce,
        Graph.twoEdgeConnected_rigidContract_induce, Graph.rigidContract_vertexSet_ncard}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-standing, def:cut-edges-2ec, def:rigid-contraction,
        def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and be $2$-edge-connected. Let
  $W \subsetneq V(G)$ with $|W| \ge 2$, such that no attachment has two
  neighbours in $W$, and let $G/H$ be the contraction of $H = G[W]$ to a
  body $r \in W$. Then $G/H$ is simple, satisfies the standing hypotheses
  and is $2$-edge-connected, and $|V(G/H)| = |V(G)| - |W| + 1$. If
  moreover $\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) = 0$, then $H$ satisfies the
  standing hypotheses.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-zero-connected, lem:reduction-measure}
  A graph of deficiency zero is connected, and of minimum degree at least
  two when it has at least two bodies
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-connected}); this gives the standing
  hypotheses at $H$ when $\operatorname{def}_2(H) = 0$. An edge of $G/H$
  is an edge of $G$ not inside $W$.
  Two such edges with the same ends in $G/H$ would give an attachment two
  neighbours in $W$, or two parallel edges of $G$, so $G/H$ is simple. A
  path of $G$ maps to a walk of $G/H$, so $G/H$ is connected, and a cut of
  $G/H$ pulls back to a cut of $G$ crossed by the same edges, so $G/H$ is
  $2$-edge-connected. At $r$, the $2$-edge-connectivity of $G$ gives two
  edges leaving $W$, and their outside ends are distinct attachments: an
  attachment with both would have two neighbours in $W$, or two parallel
  edges to one. At a body outside $W$ the contraction is injective on the
  closed neighbourhood. So every closed neighbourhood of $G/H$ has at
  least three members, and $G/H$ has minimum degree at least two. The
  contraction sends $W$ to $r$ and fixes every other body, which gives the
  body count (as in \cref{lem:reduction-measure}).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The contraction curve and the rescaled lifting system]
  \label{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system}
  \lean{Graph.contractLiftingMatrix, CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.contractPicture,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.contractHeight}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system, def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $W \subseteq V(G)$, $r \in W$, and let $q$ be a planar picture. For
  $t \in K$ the \emph{contraction curve} $q(t)$ keeps each body outside
  $W$ at $q$ and puts each $c \in W$ at $(1 - t)\,q_r + t\,q_c$. The
  \emph{rescaled lifting system} $M(t)$ is the lifting system with
  weights (\cref{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system}) whose weight
  $\omega(v, w)$ is $(x_w - (1 - t)\,x_r,\ y_w - (1 - t)\,y_r,\ t)$ when
  $w \notin W$ and the closed neighbourhood of $v$ meets $W$, and
  $(x_w, y_w, 1)$ otherwise. The \emph{heights} $z(t)$ of a solution
  $(z, h)$ of $M(t)$ are $t\,z_w$ at $w \in W$ and $z_w$ elsewhere.
\end{definition}

So $q(1) = q$, and $q(0)$ puts all of $W$ at $q_r$. The entries of
$M(t)$ are polynomials in $t$. At $t \neq 0$ the core heights are
magnified by $1/t$ and the planes through bodies meeting $W$ are read
in the magnified frame; at $t = 0$ the core keeps the picture $q$ on $W$,
while the rest of $G$ sees it at $q_r$.

\begin{lemma}[The rescaled system off the end of the curve]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel}
  \lean{Graph.contractHeight_mem_liftingSpace, Graph.exists_contractHeight_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  At every $t$, the heights of a solution of $M(t)$ lie in
  $L_G(q(t))$. At $t \neq 0$ every height of $L_G(q(t))$ is the heights
  of a solution of $M(t)$, so $\dim \ker M(t) \ge \dim L_G(q(t))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  At a body $v$ whose closed neighbourhood meets $W$, the heights $z(t)$
  on that neighbourhood agree with the affine function of the picture
  $q(t)$ with coefficients
  $(h_{v,1},\ h_{v,2},\ t\,h_{v,3} - (1 - t)(h_{v,1} x_r + h_{v,2} y_r))$;
  at the other bodies they agree with $h_v$. At $t \neq 0$ the
  substitution inverts: divide the core heights, and the corrected
  constant coefficients, by $t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A core of planar deficiency zero has one plane]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane}
  \lean{Graph.exists_core_plane}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system, lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  Let $H = G[W]$, and let $q$ be admissible for $H$ with
  $L_H(q) = \mathrm{Aff}(q)$. At every $t$, a solution $(z, h)$ of $M(t)$
  has one plane on the core: there is $g \in K^3$ with
  $z_w = g \cdot (x_w, y_w, 1)$ for every $w \in W$ and $h_c = g$ for
  every $c \in W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The rows at a core body $c$ and a core neighbour of $c$ have the weight
  $(x_w, y_w, 1)$ at every $t$, and, $H$ being induced, the closed
  neighbourhood of $c$ in $H$ is its closed neighbourhood in $G$ met
  with $W$. So the restriction of $z$ to $W$ lies in
  $L_H(q) = \mathrm{Aff}(q)$, with the planes $h_c$. Three members of
  the closed neighbourhood of $c$ in $H$ have independent picture points,
  and they pin $h_c$ to the one plane.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rescaled system at the end of the curve]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-limit}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_ker_contractLiftingMatrix_zero_le,
        Graph.exists_mem_ker_contractLiftingMatrix_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system, lem:pencil-contract-core-plane,
        def:rigid-contraction}
  Let $r \in W \subseteq V(G)$ and $H = G[W]$, and let $q$ be a picture.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $q$ is admissible for $H$ with $L_H(q) = \mathrm{Aff}(q)$,
      as in \cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane}, and admissible for
      $G/H$, then $\dim \ker M(0) \le \dim L_{G/H}(q)$.
    \item If no attachment has two neighbours in $W$, then every
      $z \in L_{G/H}(q)$ with $z_r = 0$ agrees off $W$ with the heights
      of a solution of $M(0)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  At $t = 0$ the rows at a core body $c$ and an attachment $u$ of $c$
  say that $u$ lies on the plane parallel to that of $c$ through the point
  $(q_r, 0)$; the rows at an attachment $u$ and a body outside $W$ say
  that the plane of $u$ passes through $(q_r, 0)$; and the constant
  coefficient of an attachment occurs only in its rows at its neighbours
  in $W$.

  For (1), send a solution to the heights $z_w + z_r$ at the bodies $w$
  outside $W$ and $z_r$ at $r$. By \cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane}
  the core has one plane $g$, which passes through $(q_r, z_r)$ and, after
  the shift, through every attachment; so the image lies in
  $L_{G/H}(q)$, with the planes at $r$ and at the attachments through the
  point of $r$. If the image vanishes, $g$ annihilates the homogeneous
  picture points of the closed neighbourhood of $r$ in $G/H$, three of
  which are independent, so $g = 0$; in the same way every plane outside
  $W$ vanishes, the constant coefficient of an attachment by its row at a
  neighbour in $W$. So the map is injective.

  For (2), put the core on the plane of $z$ at $r$, which passes through
  $(q_r, 0)$ and every attachment, as KT set every panel of the core to
  the panel of the contracted body in the proof of KT Claim~6.4
  \cite[p.~675]{katohTanigawa2011}, and give each body outside $W$ its
  plane of $z$. The constant coefficient of an attachment is fixed by its
  one neighbour in $W$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The core is rigid along the curve]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-core-rank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_induce_contractHeight}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane, lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel}
  In the setting of \cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane}, let $H$ be
  connected, and let $q(t)$ be admissible for $H$ with
  $\dim L_H(q(t)) = 3$. At the configuration $(q(t), z(t))$ of a solution
  of $M(t)$, the rank of $H$ is $6(|W| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift, cor:pencil-flat-x0}
  By \cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane} the core heights of $z(t)$ are
  $t\,g \cdot (x_w, y_w, 1)$, an affine function of the picture $q(t)$ on
  $W$. The rank of $H$ reads only the bodies of $H$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}) and does not change when a globally
  affine height is subtracted (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}), so it
  is the rank of the flat configuration of $H$ over $q(t)$, which is
  $6(|W| - 1)$ since $\dim L_H(q(t)) = 3$ (\cref{cor:pencil-flat-x0}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The surviving rows near the collapsed placement]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankPolynomial_rigidContract_induce_proj}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigid-contraction, lem:block-rank-contract}
  Let $r \in W \subseteq V(G)$ and $H = G[W]$, and let $n$ be panel
  normals at which every hinge of $G/H$ is nonzero and
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G/H, n) \ge N$. There is a polynomial $Q$ in the
  panel normals of $G$, nonzero at the \emph{collapsed placement} (every
  body of $W$ at the normal $n_r$, every other body at its own), such
  that at every $p$ with $Q(p) \neq 0$ the rows of $G \setminus E(H)$ at
  $p$, with the columns of $W$ deleted, have rank at least $N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:claim-6-4, lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter,
        lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval}
  At the collapsed placement each row of $G \setminus E(H)$, with the
  columns of $W$ deleted, is the corresponding row of $G/H$ with the
  column of $r$ deleted (as in \cref{lem:claim-6-4}), and deleting the
  one column of $r$ loses no rank
  (\cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}). So there are $N$ such
  rows, independent at the collapsed placement, and
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval} gives a polynomial nonzero there
  off whose zero set they stay independent. This is the comparison of
  KT eqs.~(6.5) and (6.9)~\cite[pp.~674--675]{katohTanigawa2011}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Contraction at a core of planar deficiency zero]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-contract-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_rigidContract}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing, def:cut-edges-2ec,
        def:rigid-contraction, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and be
  $2$-edge-connected. Let $W \subsetneq V(G)$ with $|W| \ge 2$ and
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) = 0$, such that no attachment has two
  neighbours in $W$, and let $G/H$ be the contraction of $H = G[W]$ to a
  body of $W$. If the general configuration of $G/H$ attains, then the
  general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-contract-standing, lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel,
        lem:pencil-contract-limit, lem:pencil-contract-core-rank,
        lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank, lem:block-rank-contract,
        thm:pencil-jj-equality, lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency, lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift,
        lem:deficiency-antitone, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  By \cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing}, $H$ and $G/H$ satisfy the
  standing hypotheses and $G/H$ is simple; $H$ is rigid, since
  $\operatorname{def}_3(H) \le \operatorname{def}_2(H) = 0$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-antitone}). Take one picture $q$ generic for
  $G/H$ and for $H$ and main for $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). It is admissible for $G/H$
  with an attaining height, main for $G/H$ with
  $\dim L_{G/H}(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G/H)$, and main for $H$
  with $\dim L_H(q) = 3$ (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}, at $G/H$ and at
  $H$), so $L_H(q) = \mathrm{Aff}(q)$. Subtracting the constant height at
  $r$ does not change the rank of $G/H$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}), so $G/H$ has an attaining height
  $z'$ with $z'_r = 0$, which agrees off $W$ with the heights of a
  solution $x_0$ of $M(0)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit}).

  The kernel does not jump at the end of the curve: at every $t \neq 0$
  with $q(t)$ admissible for $G$,
  \[
    \dim \ker M(0) \le \dim L_{G/H}(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G/H)
    = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) \le \dim L_G(q(t)) \le \dim \ker M(t),
  \]
  by \cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit}, the conservation of the
  deficiency (\cref{lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency}, at $n = 2$), the
  flat lower bound (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}) and
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel}. So the polynomial section of
  the kernel through $x_0$ (as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}, with the parameter $t$) solves $M(t)$
  off the zero set of one polynomial in $t$, and its heights $z(t)$ lie
  in $L_G(q(t))$.

  At $t = 0$ the configuration $(q(0), z(0))$ is the collapsed placement
  of $(q, z')$. Take $t$ off the zero sets of $t$, of that polynomial, of
  the main-picture polynomial of $G$ and the polynomial of
  \cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality} at $H$ along the curve, both nonzero at
  $t = 1$ where $q(1) = q$, and of the polynomial of
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank} along the curve, nonzero at
  $t = 0$. Then $q(t)$ is a main picture of $G$, the core has rank
  $6(|W| - 1)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-rank}; it is flat along the
  curve, so no attainment at $H$ is needed), and the rows of the
  other edges, with the columns of $W$ deleted, have rank at least
  $\operatorname{tgt}(G/H)$. By \cref{lem:block-rank-contract} the rank of
  $G$ at $(q(t), z(t))$ is at least
  $6(|W| - 1) + \operatorname{tgt}(G/H) = \operatorname{tgt}(G)$, since
  $|V(G/H)| = |V(G)| - |W| + 1$ and $G/H$ has the deficiency of $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency}, at $n = 3$). So
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally more holds. The contraction step needs only that $H$ is
  rigid and $G/H$ simple, together with two conditions at one picture:
  the heights of $G$ restrict onto $L_H$, and the kernel of $M(0)$ is no
  larger than the kernels near it. Both hold at a core of planar
  deficiency zero, the case proved above; the first forces
  $\operatorname{def}_2(H) \le \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ in general. At
  such a core, moreover, $\ker M(0)$ is the space of heights of $G/H$
  vanishing at $r$ plus one free constant, and Jackson and Jord\'an's
  equality at $G$ follows from those at $H$ and $G/H$. Only the bound and
  the containment of \cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit} are formalized,
  which is all the theorem uses. At a rigid core whose planar
  deficiencies add, both conditions are obtained for the solutions of
  $M(0)$ at one picture, and the step is
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-contract-additive}; the general form of the step is
  not formalized.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ears and cycles}
\label{sec:main-component-chain}

This subsection proves that a cycle attains, and the two ear steps that
need nothing of the smaller graph beyond attaining. Let $V_1 \subseteq
V(G)$. An \emph{ear} of $G$ on $V_1$ is a path
$a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ ($k \ge 1$) with $a, b \in V_1$ and
distinct \emph{interior bodies} $x_1, \dots, x_k$ outside $V_1$, such
that $V(G) = V_1 \cup \{x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ and every edge of $G$ off the
path lies inside $V_1$. The ear is \emph{open} if $a \neq b$, and then
$a$ and $b$ may be adjacent, and \emph{closed} if $a = b$. Along an open
ear the rank grows by $5(k + 1) + \dim(\rho + \Lambda) - 6$
(\cref{lem:block-rank-ear}), where $\rho$ is the space of relative
screws of $a, b$ in $G[V_1]$ (\cref{def:relative-screws}) and $\Lambda$
is the span of the hinges of the ear, and the deficiency grows by at
least $k - 5$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}); only this lower bound is
needed, since one configuration of rank at least the target suffices
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}). An ear with at least five interior
bodies has at least six hinges, and at a general configuration they span
the whole screw space, so the rank grows by at least the increase of the
target. One exhibited configuration certifies the span: a closed hexagon
whose joins are independent over every field, reached at a picture on
which $a$ and $b$ coincide. It is not admissible, but its heights are
heights of $G$ at every admissible picture, because the interior heights
of an ear are free, and the independence then passes to the general
picture and the general height. A cycle is an edge together with an open
ear on its two ends, and all its closed neighbourhoods have three
members; a closed ear makes its end a cut vertex whose other side is a
cycle.

\begin{lemma}[Three points impose nothing]
  \label{lem:pencil-three-points}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.exists_dotProduct_of_ncard_closedNbhd_le_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ be admissible for $G$, and let $v$ be a body of $G$ whose
  closed neighbourhood has at most three members. Then every height
  agrees with an affine function of the picture on the closed
  neighbourhood of $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By admissibility three members of the closed neighbourhood of $v$
  have linearly independent homogeneous picture points, so they are all
  its members, and any three values at them are taken by exactly one
  affine function.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The heights of an open ear]
  \label{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}
  \lean{Graph.mem_liftingSpace_of_ear,
    Graph.earExtend_mem_liftingSpace}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture,
        lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  Let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ be an open ear of $G$ on $V_1$ with
  $k \ge 2$, and let $q$ be admissible for $G$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A height lies in $L_G(q)$ as soon as it agrees with an affine
      function of the picture on the closed neighbourhood in $G$ of
      every body of $V_1$.
    \item Every $z^1 \in L_{G[V_1]}(q)$ is the restriction to $V_1$
      (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}) of a height $z \in L_G(q)$
      taking any prescribed values at $x_2, \dots, x_{k-1}$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-three-points}
  The closed neighbourhood of an interior body consists of the body and
  its two neighbours on the path, and every height is affine on it
  (\cref{lem:pencil-three-points}); this gives (1). For (2), the closed
  neighbourhood in $G$ of a body of $V_1$ is its closed neighbourhood in
  $G[V_1]$, together with $x_1$ at $a$ and $x_k$ at $b$. Take affine
  functions $h_a$ and $h_b$ agreeing with $z^1$ on the closed
  neighbourhoods of $a$ and $b$ in $G[V_1]$, and set
  $z_{x_1} = h_a(q_{x_1})$ and $z_{x_k} = h_b(q_{x_k})$, which is
  possible since $k \ge 2$ makes $x_1 \neq x_k$; then (1) applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Joins in flat coordinates]
  \label{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pointJoin,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.flatScrewEquiv_pointJoin,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.linearIndependent_pointJoin_of_flat}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-flat-split, def:extensor}
  For $p = (p_x, p_y, p_z, p_w) \in K^4$ write
  $\bar p = (p_x, p_y, p_w) \in K^3$. There is a linear isomorphism of
  the screw space $\Lambda^2 K^4$ onto $K^3 \times K^3$, adapted to the
  splitting of \cref{lem:pencil-flat-split}, that sends the join
  $p \wedge p'$ of $p, p' \in K^4$ to
  $\bigl(p_z\,\bar p' - p'_z\,\bar p,\ \bar p \times \bar p'\bigr)$. In
  particular, a family of joins is linearly independent as soon as the
  family of these vectors is.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Expand $p \wedge p'$ in the basis of $W'$ and $W_\Pi$ used in the
  proof of \cref{lem:pencil-flat-split}: the coefficients on $W_\Pi$
  are the $2 \times 2$ minors of $\bar p$ and $\bar p'$, and those on
  $W'$ pair the heights with the planar parts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independence of joins is an open condition]
  \label{lem:pencil-join-independence-open}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_mvPolynomial_linearIndependent_pointJoin_picture,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_mvPolynomial_linearIndependent_pointJoin_heights}
  \leanok

  Let $(u_i, w_i)_{i \in I}$ be a finite family of pairs of bodies, and
  write $p$ for the points of a configuration $(q, z)$. If the joins
  $p_{u_i} \wedge p_{w_i}$ are linearly independent at $(q_0, z)$, there
  is a polynomial $P$ in the picture, nonzero at $q_0$, such that they
  are linearly independent at $(q, z)$ whenever $P(q) \neq 0$. Likewise,
  if they are independent at $(q, z_0)$, there is a polynomial $R$ in the
  heights, nonzero at $z_0$, such that they are independent at $(q, z)$
  whenever $R(z) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  By \cref{lem:pencil-join-flat} the coordinates of each join are
  polynomials in the picture at fixed heights, and in the heights at a
  fixed picture. A finite family of vectors is linearly independent
  exactly when some maximal minor of its coordinate matrix is nonzero;
  take for $P$, or $R$, one such minor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent joins span the hinges]
  \label{lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.span_supportExtensor_eq_top_of_linearIndependent,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.card_le_finrank_of_linearIndependent_pointJoin}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:panel-support-extensor}
  Let $p$ be the points of a configuration of $G$, and let
  $g_1, \dots, g_m$ be edges of $G$, with ends $u_i, w_i$, whose joins
  $p_{u_i} \wedge p_{w_i}$ are linearly independent. Then the hinges at
  $g_1, \dots, g_m$ of the panel-hinge framework defining the rank of
  $p$ (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}) span a space of dimension at least
  $m$. In particular, if $m = 6$, the hinges at any set of edges
  containing $g_1, \dots, g_6$ span the whole screw space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank, lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}
  The hinge at an edge $uw$ spans the line of the meet of the planes
  $p_u^{\perp}$ and $p_w^{\perp}$, and the polarity, an invertible
  linear map of the screw space, carries the join $p_u \wedge p_w$ to
  that meet (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). So the meets at
  $g_1, \dots, g_m$ are independent and lie in the span of the hinges
  there. The screw space has dimension six.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A cycle]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-cycle}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_cycle}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be a cycle on $m \ge 3$ bodies:
  $V(G) = \{a, x_1, \dots, x_k, b\}$ with these bodies distinct and
  $k = m - 2$, and the edges of $G$ are those of the path
  $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ and one edge $ab$. Then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-three-points, lem:pencil-join-independence-open,
        lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates, lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span,
        lem:block-rank-ear, lem:block-rank-path, lem:relative-screws-path,
        def:D-deficiency, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Every closed neighbourhood of $G$ has three members, so at an
  admissible picture $q$ every height lies in $L_G(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-three-points}). Put the bodies at the points of
  \cref{lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates}: for $m \le 5$ the bodies
  $a, x_1, \dots, x_k, b$ at $Y_0, \dots, Y_{m-1}$, so that the $m$
  edges carry the joins of the closed $m$-gon; for $m \ge 6$ the bodies
  $a, x_1, x_2, x_3$ at $Y_0, \dots, Y_3$, the bodies $x_4, \dots, x_k$
  at $Y_4$ and $b$ at $Y_5$, so that the edges $a x_1$, $x_1 x_2$,
  $x_2 x_3$, $x_3 x_4$, $x_k b$ and $ba$ carry the joins of the closed
  hexagon. Let $z$ be the heights of these points, and take a main
  picture $q$ of $G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}) at which
  these $\min(m, 6)$ joins stay independent
  (\cref{lem:pencil-join-independence-open}).

  The cycle is the edge $ab$ with the open ear
  $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on $\{a, b\}$, and every hinge is nonzero
  at the admissible picture $q$. The edge has rank $5$ and relative screws
  spanned by its hinge (\cref{lem:block-rank-path} and
  \cref{lem:relative-screws-path}, at $k = 0$), so by
  \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} the rank at $(q, z)$ is
  $5 + 5(k + 1) + \lambda - 6 = 5m - 6 + \lambda$, where
  $\lambda \ge \min(m, 6)$ is the dimension of the span of all the
  hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span}). The partition into single
  bodies has value $6(m - 1) - 5m = m - 6$
  (\cref{def:D-deficiency}), so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge \max(0, m - 6)$ and the target is at
  most $\min(6m - 6, 5m) \le 5m - 6 + \lambda$. So
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An open ear with at least five interior bodies]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_openEar}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), with an open ear
  $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on $V_1$ with $k \ge 5$, whose ends $a$
  and $b$ may be adjacent. If the general configuration of $G[V_1]$
  attains, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-ear-fibre, lem:pencil-join-independence-open,
        lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates, lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span,
        lem:block-rank-ear, lem:deficiency-ear, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Let $q^*$ put $a$ and $b$ at $(1, 0)$, the bodies $x_1, x_2, x_3$ at
  $(0, -1)$, $(0, 0)$, $(-1, 0)$, the bodies $x_4, \dots, x_{k-1}$ at
  $(0, 0)$ and $x_k$ at $(-1, -1)$, and let $z^*$ be $1$ at $x_2$ and
  $x_3$ and $0$ elsewhere. The points of $a, x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4, x_k$
  are then $Y_0, \dots, Y_5$ (\cref{lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates}),
  and $x_{k-1}$ is at $Y_4$ too, so the six ear edges $a x_1$, $x_1 x_2$,
  $x_2 x_3$, $x_3 x_4$, $x_{k-1} x_k$ and $x_k b$, distinct since
  $k \ge 5$, carry the joins of the closed hexagon. The picture $q^*$ is
  not admissible; it only makes nonzero the polynomial $P$ of
  \cref{lem:pencil-join-independence-open} at the heights $z^*$.

  Take a picture $q$ off the zero sets of $P$, of the polynomial of the
  attainment at $G[V_1]$, and of the main-picture polynomial of $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). The height $z^*$ vanishes on
  $V_1$ and at $x_1$ and $x_k$, so it lies in $L_G(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}(1)), and the six joins are independent at
  $(q, z^*)$. The heights of $L_G(q)$ restricting to attaining heights of
  $G[V_1]$ contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}(2), \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}),
  and so do the heights at which the six joins stay independent
  (\cref{lem:pencil-join-independence-open}, in the heights); the two
  meet (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). At such a height $z$
  the rank of $G[V_1]$ is its target (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}), and
  the hinges of the ear span the screw space
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span}), so $\rho + \Lambda$ is the whole
  screw space and \cref{lem:block-rank-ear}, all hinges being nonzero at
  an admissible picture, adds $5(k + 1)$ to the rank. The deficiency
  grows by at least $k - 5$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}) and the number of
  bodies by $k$, so the target grows by at most $5(k + 1)$, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The informal argument proves the span of the hinges of an open ear for
  every position of its ends: whatever the points and planes of $a$ and
  $b$, with $p_a \neq p_b$, a general placement of the interior bodies
  has $\min(k + 1, 6)$ independent hinges, shown by one exhibited
  placement for each projective type of the ends. The proof above needs
  no such uniformity. The independence of six fixed joins is one
  polynomial condition in the picture and the heights, so a single point
  at which it holds suffices, and that point may be degenerate: the
  picture $q^*$ puts $a$ and $b$ at one point and is not admissible. What
  the point must satisfy is that its heights $z^*$ are heights of $G$ at
  the general picture, and this is where the interior heights of an ear
  enter: $z^*$ vanishes on $V_1$ and at $x_1$ and $x_k$, and is nonzero
  only at $x_2$ and $x_3$, which lie in no closed neighbourhood of a body
  of $V_1$ and whose own closed neighbourhoods have three members. This
  needs $k \ge 4$, and six distinct ear edges need $k \ge 5$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A closed ear]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-closed-ear}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_closedEar}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with a
  closed ear $c - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - c$ on $V_1$ with $k \ge 2$. If the
  general configuration of $G[V_1]$ attains, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-x0-cut, thm:pencil-x0-cycle}
  The body $c$ is a cut vertex: the sides $V_1$ and
  $V_2 = \{c, x_1, \dots, x_k\}$ meet in $c$, and every edge of $G$ lies
  inside one of them. As $G$ has no loop, $G[V_2]$ is the cycle
  $c - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - c$ on $k + 1 \ge 3$ bodies, which attains by
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cycle}, with the edge $x_1 c$ in the role of $ab$.
  So \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut} applies; no span of the hinges of a closed
  ear is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Graphs built from a cycle by ears]
  \label{rem:pencil-x0-ear-class}
  By induction on the number of ears, the three theorems above show that
  every graph obtained from a cycle by successively adding closed ears
  and open ears with at least five interior bodies has an attaining
  general configuration: each graph along the way satisfies the standing
  hypotheses, and removing its last ear leaves the subgraph induced on
  the other bodies. This class statement is not formalized separately.
  The structural coverage runs a strong induction on the number of
  bodies, and each of its ear cases applies
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear} or \cref{thm:pencil-x0-closed-ear}
  directly. For instance, the $\theta$-graph whose two branch bodies are
  joined by paths of one, two and six edges is a triangle with an open
  ear of five interior bodies on two adjacent bodies.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Open ears with two, three or four interior bodies}
\label{sec:main-component-short}

This subsection proves the ear steps for an open ear
$a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on $V_1$ with $k = 2, 3, 4$, whose ends may
be adjacent. With $\rho$ and $\Lambda$ as in
\cref{sec:main-component-chain}, the rank grows along the ear by
$5(k + 1) + \dim(\rho + \Lambda) - 6$ (\cref{lem:block-rank-ear}) and the
target by $6k - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) + \operatorname{def}(\tilde
G[V_1])$. So a configuration over a main picture of $G$, with nonzero
hinges, at which the rank of $G[V_1]$ is its target, has at least the
target rank of $G$ as soon as
\[
  \dim(\rho + \Lambda) \;\ge\; k + 1 + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])
  - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G), \qquad (\star)
\]
and then the general configuration of $G$ attains
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}). With two interior bodies, three
independent hinges suffice when the deficiency of $G$ is at least that of
$G[V_1]$, and the three joins of the ear are independent at a suitable
flat configuration.
With three or four interior bodies no bound on the deficiency is assumed.
Besides $G[V_1]$, the step then uses $G$ with its second interior body
$x_2$ suppressed, and the count is taken against it: putting $x_2$ back on a
line through one of its neighbours raises $\dim(\rho + \Lambda)$ by one
unless the span already contains every line through that neighbour
(\cref{lem:pencil-insertion}). If it contains every line through $x_1$
and every line through $x_3$, then with four interior bodies it contains
the six edges of a tetrahedron. With three, either some relative screw
pairs to a nonzero value (\cref{def:pencil-line-pairing}) with some line
joining the planes of the two ends, and then, for a general ear, with
the line $x_1 x_3$, so that it lies outside the span of the lines through
$x_1$ and the lines through $x_3$ (\cref{lem:pencil-two-stars}); or every
relative screw pairs to zero with every such line, and then the whole
span has dimension at most three (\cref{lem:pencil-bilinear}). The data on
$V_1$ are fixed first, from one configuration at which both smaller
graphs attain, and the data of the ear second
(\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}).

By \cref{lem:pencil-join-flat} every $c \in \Lambda^2 K^4$ has flat
coordinates $(\sigma(c), \tau(c)) \in K^3 \times K^3$, with
$\sigma(p \wedge p') = p_z\,\bar p' - p'_z\,\bar p$ and
$\tau(p \wedge p') = \bar p \times \bar p'$.

\begin{definition}[The pairing of lines]
  \label{def:pencil-line-pairing}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.kleinLin}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  The \emph{pairing} of $c, c' \in \Lambda^2 K^4$ is
  \[
    \langle c, c' \rangle = \sigma(c) \cdot \tau(c') + \sigma(c') \cdot \tau(c).
  \]
\end{definition}

The pairing is bilinear and symmetric. The \emph{star} of a point
$y \in K^4$ is the span $\operatorname{star}(y)$ of the joins
$y \wedge u$, $u \in K^4$, the lines through $y$.

\begin{lemma}[The pairing of two joins]
  \label{lem:pencil-line-pairing-join}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.klein_pointJoin_pointJoin,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eq_zero_of_kleinLin_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-line-pairing, def:extensor}
  For $p, q, r, s \in K^4$,
  $\langle p \wedge q, r \wedge s \rangle = \det(p, q, r, s)$; in
  particular two lines through a common point pair to zero. The pairing
  is nondegenerate: if $\langle c, c' \rangle = 0$ for every $c'$, then
  $c = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  Expand both sides in the coordinates of the four points, with the flat
  coordinates of \cref{lem:pencil-join-flat}. For nondegeneracy, pair $c$
  with the six screws whose flat coordinates are a unit vector and zero:
  they read off the coordinates of $\tau(c)$ and of $\sigma(c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The joins of four independent points]
  \label{lem:pencil-tetrahedron}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.linearIndependent_pointJoin_tetra,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.span_pointJoin_tetra_eq_top}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor}
  If $p_0, p_1, p_2, p_3 \in K^4$ are linearly independent, the six joins
  $p_i \wedge p_j$, $i < j$, are linearly independent, so they span
  $\Lambda^2 K^4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-line-pairing-join}
  Pair a vanishing combination $\sum c_{ij}\, p_i \wedge p_j = 0$ with the
  opposite edge $p_k \wedge p_l$. By \cref{lem:pencil-line-pairing-join}
  the pairing of two edges is $\pm \det(p_0, p_1, p_2, p_3) \neq 0$ when
  they are opposite and $0$ when they share a point, so every $c_{ij}$
  vanishes. The space $\Lambda^2 K^4$ has dimension six.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The lines through two points]
  \label{lem:pencil-two-stars}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.star,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.star_sup_star_le_ker_klein,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.five_le_finrank_star_sup_star}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-line-pairing, def:extensor}
  For $y, y' \in K^4$, every element $c$ of
  $\operatorname{star}(y) + \operatorname{star}(y')$ has
  $\langle c, y \wedge y' \rangle = 0$. If $y$ and $y'$ are linearly
  independent, then
  $\dim\bigl(\operatorname{star}(y) + \operatorname{star}(y')\bigr) \ge 5$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-line-pairing-join, lem:pencil-tetrahedron}
  A line through $y$ or through $y'$ meets the line $y y'$, so the
  pairing vanishes (\cref{lem:pencil-line-pairing-join}). Extend $y, y'$
  to a basis $y, y', e, e'$ of $K^4$: five of the six joins of
  \cref{lem:pencil-tetrahedron}, all but $e \wedge e'$, lie in the two
  stars.
\end{proof}

For $h \in K^3$ write $\hat\pi_h = \{(u_1, u_2, h \cdot u, u_3) : u \in
K^3\} \subseteq K^4$ for the homogeneous points of the plane
$z = h_1 x + h_2 y + h_3$, and $\Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$ for the space of
screws $c$ with $\sigma(c) = -\, h \times \tau(c)$.

\begin{lemma}[The lines of a plane]
  \label{lem:pencil-plane-lines}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.planeLines,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pointJoin_liftPlane_mem_planeLines,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_planeLines_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  For $h \in K^3$, the join of any two points of $\hat\pi_h$ lies in
  $\Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$, and $\dim \Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h \le 3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The first claim is a computation in the flat coordinates of
  \cref{lem:pencil-join-flat}. On $\Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$ the coordinate
  $\sigma$ is determined by $\tau$, so $\tau$ is injective there.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Lines joining two planes]
  \label{lem:pencil-bilinear}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.klein_liftPlane,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_sup_le_three_of_klein}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-line-pairing, lem:pencil-plane-lines}
  Let $h, h' \in K^3$, and let $c \in \Lambda^2 K^4$ have
  $\langle c, u \wedge v \rangle = 0$ for every $u \in \hat\pi_h$ and
  every $v \in \hat\pi_{h'}$. If $h \neq h'$, then $c = 0$; if $h = h'$,
  then $c \in \Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$. Consequently, if every element of a
  subspace $\rho \subseteq \Lambda^2 K^4$ has this property, and
  $f_1, f_2, f_3 \in \Lambda^2 K^4$ lie in $\Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$ when
  $h = h'$, then $\dim\bigl(\rho + \operatorname{span}(f_1, f_2, f_3)\bigr)
  \le 3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-join-flat}
  Test the hypothesis on the nine joins of the lift of a unit vector
  $e_i$ of $K^3$ to $\hat\pi_h$ with the lift of $e_j$ to $\hat\pi_{h'}$.
  The equations at $(i, i)$, and the sums of those at $(i, j)$ and
  $(j, i)$, are linear in $\tau(c)$ with coefficients $h_i - h'_i$; when
  $h \neq h'$ some coefficient is nonzero, and they force $\tau(c) = 0$,
  after which the remaining equations give $\sigma(c) = 0$. When $h = h'$ the equations say
  $\sigma(c) = -\, h \times \tau(c)$. For the consequence, if $h \neq h'$
  then $\rho = 0$; if $h = h'$ then $\rho$ and the $f_i$ lie in
  $\Lambda^2 \hat\pi_h$, of dimension at most three
  (\cref{lem:pencil-plane-lines}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Putting a body back next to a neighbour]
  \label{lem:pencil-insertion}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_insertion_ge,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_insertion_gain}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor}
  Let $K$ be infinite, let $\rho \subseteq \Lambda^2 K^4$ be a subspace
  and $F$ a finite family in $\Lambda^2 K^4$, let $y, y', u \in K^4$, and
  let $W = \rho + \operatorname{span} F + K\,(y \wedge y')$. For $t \in K$
  write $W_t = \rho + \operatorname{span} F +
  \operatorname{span}\bigl(y \wedge (y + t u),\ (y + t u) \wedge y'\bigr)$.
  Then $\dim W_t \ge \dim W$ for some $t \neq 0$, and if
  $y \wedge u \notin W$, then $\dim W_t \ge \dim W + 1$ for some
  $t \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Write $R = \rho + \operatorname{span} F$. For $t \neq 0$,
  $y \wedge (y + t u) = t\,(y \wedge u)$ and
  $(y + t u) \wedge y' = v_t$ with $v_t = y \wedge y' + t\,(u \wedge y')$,
  so $W_t$ contains $R$, $y \wedge u$ and $v_t$. A vector $v$ outside a
  subspace $S$ has $v + t w \notin S$ for one of any two distinct nonzero
  values of $t$, since otherwise $S$ contains $w$ and then $v$; as $K$ is
  infinite, two such values exist.

  If $y \wedge y' \in R$, then $W = R \subseteq W_t$, and when
  $y \wedge u \notin W$, $\dim W_t \ge \dim\bigl(R + K\,(y \wedge u)\bigr)
  = \dim W + 1$ for every $t \neq 0$. Otherwise $\dim W = \dim R + 1$, and
  some $t \neq 0$ has $v_t \notin R$, so that
  $\dim W_t \ge \dim(R + K v_t) = \dim W$. If moreover
  $y \wedge u \notin W$, then $y \wedge y' \notin R + K\,(y \wedge u)$,
  since a relation $y \wedge y' = r + c\,(y \wedge u)$ with $r \in R$ has
  $c \neq 0$ and puts $y \wedge u$ in $W$. So some $t \neq 0$ has
  $v_t \notin R + K\,(y \wedge u)$, and $\dim W_t \ge \dim R + 2 =
  \dim W + 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An open ear over fixed base data]
  \label{lem:pencil-ear-data}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.earPicture, CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.earHeight,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.earConfig, CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eval_earPointPoly,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.earConfig_of_mem, Graph.earHeight_mem_liftingSpace,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eval_bind₁_earPicturePoly,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_mvPolynomial_le_finrank_ofNormals_bind,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_mvPolynomial_le_finrank_sup_span_pointJoin}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture, def:pencil-configuration,
        def:pencil-x0-attains}
  Let $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ be an open ear of $G$ on $V_1$ with $k \ge 2$. Fix \emph{base
  data}: a picture $q_1$, heights $z_1 \in L_{G[V_1]}(q_1)$, and $h_a, h_b \in K^3$ with
  $z_1(w) = h_a \cdot (q_1(w), 1)$ on the closed neighbourhood of $a$ in $G[V_1]$, and likewise $h_b$
  at $b$. The \emph{ear data} are a point $s$ giving every body a point of $K^2$ and a value in $K$.
  They give the picture $q(s)$, equal to $q_1$ on $V_1$ and to the points of $s$ elsewhere, and the
  height $z(s)$, equal to $z_1$ on $V_1$, to $h_a \cdot (q(s)_{x_1}, 1)$ at $x_1$, to
  $h_b \cdot (q(s)_{x_k}, 1)$ at $x_k$, to the value of $s$ at the other interior bodies, and to $0$
  elsewhere.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $q(s)$ is admissible for $G$, then $z(s) \in L_G(q(s))$. The points of the
      configuration $(q(s), z(s))$ are polynomial in $s$, and on $V_1$ they are those of
      $(q_1, z_1)$.
    \item If $P$ is a polynomial in the picture, $s \mapsto P(q(s))$ is a polynomial in $s$.
    \item Let the points of a configuration be polynomial in a parameter $s$. If, at one parameter,
      every hinge of a graph $H$ is nonzero and the rank is at least $r$, there is a polynomial in
      $s$, nonzero there, such that the rank is at least $r$ wherever it does not vanish. If, at one
      parameter, $\dim(\rho + \operatorname{span} F) \ge n$ for a fixed subspace
      $\rho \subseteq \Lambda^2 K^4$ and a family $F$ of joins of points of the configuration,
      the same holds for this bound.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-ear-fibre, lem:pencil-picture-local, lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank,
        lem:pencil-join-flat}
  For (1), the lifting space of $G[V_1]$ and the closed neighbourhoods of $a$ and $b$ in $G[V_1]$
  read the picture only on $V_1$, where $q(s)$ is $q_1$ (\cref{lem:pencil-picture-local}). So
  $z(s)$ agrees with an affine function of the picture on the closed neighbourhood in $G$ of every
  body of $V_1$: with $h_a$ at $a$, since the neighbourhood is that in $G[V_1]$ together with $x_1$;
  with $h_b$ at $b$, likewise with $x_k$, which differs from $x_1$ since $k \ge 2$; and with the
  affine function of $z_1$ at any other body of $V_1$, whose closed neighbourhood lies in $V_1$.
  It vanishes off $V(G)$, so \cref{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}(1) applies. Every coordinate of a point
  of the configuration is a constant, a coordinate of $s$, or, at $x_1$ and $x_k$, affine in the
  picture coordinates of $s$. For (2), substitute these coordinates into $P$. For (3), substitute
  the points into the polynomial of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, which asks for the
  nonzero hinges. For the span, take a basis of $\rho$ together with $F$: the coordinates of its
  members are polynomial in $s$ (\cref{lem:pencil-join-flat}), $n$ of them are linearly independent
  at the given parameter, and they stay independent wherever a maximal minor of their coordinate
  matrix, nonzero there, does not vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An open ear with two interior bodies]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_openEar_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), with an open ear $a - x_1 - x_2 - b$
  on $V_1$ whose ends $a$ and $b$ may be adjacent, and suppose
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:D-deficiency}, at $n = 3$). If the general configuration of
  $G[V_1]$ attains, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-ear-fibre, lem:pencil-join-independence-open,
        lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates, lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span,
        lem:block-rank-ear, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Let $q^*$ put $a$, $x_1$, $x_2$ and $b$ at $(0, 0)$, $(-1, -1)$,
  $(1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$, and every other body at $(0, 0)$. At the
  heights $0$ the points of $a, x_1, x_2, b$ are then $Y_4, Y_5, Y_0, Y_1$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-chain-span-certificates}), so the three ear edges
  $a x_1$, $x_1 x_2$ and $x_2 b$ carry the joins $Y_4 \wedge Y_5$,
  $Y_5 \wedge Y_0$ and $Y_0 \wedge Y_1$ of the closed hexagon, which are
  linearly independent. The picture $q^*$ need not be admissible: it
  only shows that the polynomial $P$ of
  \cref{lem:pencil-join-independence-open}, at the heights $0$, is
  nonzero.

  Take a picture $q$ off the zero sets of $P$, of the polynomial of the
  attainment at $G[V_1]$, and of the main-picture polynomial of $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). The heights $0$ lie in the
  linear space $L_G(q)$, and the three joins are independent at $(q, 0)$.
  The heights of $L_G(q)$ restricting to attaining heights of $G[V_1]$
  contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}(2),
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}), and so do the heights at which the
  three joins stay independent (\cref{lem:pencil-join-independence-open},
  in the heights); the two meet (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}).
  At such a height the rank of $G[V_1]$ is its target
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}), every hinge is nonzero at the admissible
  picture $q$, and $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge 3$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-hinge-span}). This is $(\star)$ at $k = 2$ by the
  hypothesis on the deficiencies, so \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  At every admissible picture the three joins are already independent at
  the heights $0$: admissibility at $x_1$ and at $x_2$ keeps
  $a, x_1, x_2$ and $x_1, x_2, b$ off a line, so the lines $a x_1$,
  $x_1 x_2$ and $x_2 b$ of the plane $z = 0$ are not concurrent. The proof
  above does not use this. As in \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear}, one
  picture at which the joins are independent suffices, and it need not be
  admissible.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[An open ear with four interior bodies]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_openEar_four}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:graph-operations}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with
  an open ear $a - x_1 - x_2 - x_3 - x_4 - b$ on $V_1$ whose ends $a$ and
  $b$ may be adjacent. Let $G''$ be $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed: the
  splitting-off $G_{x_2}^{x_1 x_3}$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose
  new edge $x_1 x_3$ carries the label of the deleted edge $x_1 x_2$. If
  the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and of $G''$ attain, then the
  general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:block-rank-ear, lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-ear-fibre, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-picture-local,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-ear-data, lem:pencil-insertion, lem:pencil-tetrahedron,
        lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse, lem:deficiency-ear}
  Write $f = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$, and compute ranks with the
  joins as hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). $G''$ has
  the open ear $a - x_1 - x_3 - x_4 - b$ on $V_1$, and the same
  $G[V_1]$.

  \emph{Base data.} Take a picture $q^*$ off the zero sets of the
  main-picture polynomial of $G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open})
  and of the polynomials of the attainment at $G[V_1]$ and at $G''$, and
  heights $z^* \in L_{G''}(q^*)$ at which $G''$ attains and whose
  restriction attains for $G[V_1]$: both sets contain nonempty
  Zariski-open subsets of $L_{G''}(q^*)$, by \cref{lem:pencil-ear-fibre}(2)
  for the ear of $G''$ and \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}, and they
  meet (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). Fix the base data
  $(q_1, z_1)$, the restriction of $(q^*, z^*)$ to $V_1$, at which the rank
  of $G[V_1]$ is its target, and take for $h_a$ and $h_b$ the affine
  functions with which $z^*$ agrees on the closed neighbourhoods of $a$
  and $b$ in $G''$; they agree with $z_1$ on those in $G[V_1]$, as
  \cref{lem:pencil-ear-data} asks. Write $\hat\pi_a$ and $\hat\pi_b$ for
  the homogeneous points of their planes, which contain $p_a$ and the
  point of $x_1$, and $p_b$ and the point of $x_4$, at every configuration
  of ear data of $G$ or $G''$ with these base data. Since $x_1$ and $x_4$
  lie in those neighbourhoods, $(q^*, z^*)$ is the configuration of one
  ear datum $s^*$.
  The rank of $G[V_1]$ and its relative screws $\rho$ read only the hinges
  on its links, which the points on $V_1$ fix
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}, \cref{lem:pencil-picture-local}), so both
  are the same at every ear datum, in the frameworks of $G$ and of $G''$
  (\texttt{Graph.exists\_earBase\_splitOff}).

  \emph{The configuration of $G''$.} $G''$ does not read the coordinates
  of $x_2$ in the ear data. Each of three conditions on the ear data holds
  off the zero set of a polynomial that is nonzero at one ear datum
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(2),(3)): the rank of $G''$ is at least its
  target, at $s^*$, where the hinges of $G''$ are nonzero; the picture is
  admissible for $G''$, at $s^*$; and the six joins of the points of $x_1$,
  $x_3$, $x_4$ and $b$ span $\Lambda^2 K^4$. For the last, with $b$ at the
  picture $q_b$ and the height $z_b$, put $x_1$ over $q_b + (0, 1)$ and
  $x_4$ over $q_b + (1, 0)$ on their planes, and $x_3$ over $q_b$ at the
  height $z_b + 1$: the four points are linearly independent, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-tetrahedron} applies. Take ear data $y$ off all three
  zero sets, with points $y_1$, $y_3$, $y_4$. By \cref{lem:block-rank-ear}
  at $G''$,
  $W = \rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y_1,\ y_1 \wedge y_3,\
  y_3 \wedge y_4,\ y_4 \wedge p_b)$ has
  $\dim W \ge 4 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G'')$.

  \emph{Putting $x_2$ back.} Keep the other bodies of $y$ and give $x_2$ an
  affine point $x$; since $x_2$ is a middle body of the ear of $G$, this is
  ear data of $G$ with the base data. Every line through an affine point
  $y'$ is $y' \wedge u$ with $u$ of last coordinate $0$, since
  $y' \wedge v = y' \wedge (v - v_4 y')$. If $W$ does not contain every
  line through $y_1$, then $y_1 \wedge u \notin W$ for such a $u$, and for
  some $t \neq 0$ the point $x = y_1 + t u$ has
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \dim W + 1$ (\cref{lem:pencil-insertion});
  likewise at $y_3$. If $W$ contains both stars, it contains $y_1 \wedge
  y_3$, $y_1 \wedge y_4$, $y_1 \wedge p_b$, $y_3 \wedge y_4$,
  $y_3 \wedge p_b$ and $y_4 \wedge p_b$, the six edges of a tetrahedron,
  which span $\Lambda^2 K^4$ by the choice of $y$; then take $x = y_1$. The hinge $y_1 \wedge x$
  vanishes, but $\rho + \Lambda$ contains $W$. Either way
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \min(\dim W + 1, 6)$
  (\texttt{exists\_insertion\_four}).

  \emph{The configuration of $G$.} By \cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(3), whose
  bound on $\dim(\rho + \operatorname{span} F)$ asks for no nonzero hinge,
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \min(\dim W + 1, 6)$ is a nonzero polynomial
  condition on the ear data of $G$, and so is the main-picture condition
  at the ear pictures, nonzero at $s^*$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(2)).
  At a common non-root the picture is main for $G$, so the hinges are
  nonzero, the heights lie in $L_G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(1)), and
  the rank of $G[V_1]$ is its target.

  \emph{The count.} $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse}) and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge f + k - 5$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}),
  so $\min(\dim W + 1, 6) \ge 5 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, which
  is $(\star)$ at $k = 4$, and \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The informal argument takes $y$ with $y_1, y_3, y_4, p_b$ off a plane,
  a determinant affine in the free height of $y_3$, and when
  $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$ it keeps $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \dim W$ by the
  half of \cref{lem:pencil-insertion} that does not lower the span, at
  all but finitely many $t$. The proof above needs neither. The spanning
  of the six joins is certified at one exhibited ear datum, and when
  $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$ the body $x_2$ is put at the point of $x_1$: the
  configuration it builds only shows that the bound on
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda)$ is a nonzero polynomial, so it may have a
  vanishing hinge, and the configuration at which $G$ attains is taken
  afterwards, off that polynomial. Nor is the uniqueness of $h_a$ and
  $h_b$ used.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[An open ear with three interior bodies]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-three}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_openEar_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:graph-operations}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with
  an open ear $a - x_1 - x_2 - x_3 - b$ on $V_1$ whose ends $a$ and $b$
  may be adjacent. Let $G''$ be $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed: the
  splitting-off $G_{x_2}^{x_1 x_3}$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose
  new edge $x_1 x_3$ carries the label of the deleted edge $x_1 x_2$. If
  the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and of $G''$ attain, then the
  general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:block-rank-ear, lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-ear-fibre, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-picture-local,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-ear-data, lem:pencil-insertion, lem:pencil-two-stars,
        lem:pencil-bilinear, lem:pencil-plane-lines,
        lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse, lem:deficiency-ear}
  Write $f = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$. $G''$ has the open ear
  $a - x_1 - x_3 - b$ on $V_1$, and the same $G[V_1]$. Fix the base data
  $(q_1, z_1)$, the affine functions $h_a$ and $h_b$, the ear datum $s^*$
  and the relative screws $\rho$ as in the proof of
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four}
  (\texttt{Graph.exists\_earBase\_splitOff}). At every configuration of
  ear data, $p_a$ and the point $y_1$ of $x_1$ lie in $\hat\pi_a$, and the
  point $y_3$ of $x_3$ and $p_b$ lie in $\hat\pi_b$.

  \emph{The case split on $\rho$}, made before the ear data are chosen. In
  the first case $\langle c, u \wedge v \rangle = 0$ for every $c \in \rho$,
  $u \in \hat\pi_a$ and $v \in \hat\pi_b$. In the second, some $c \in \rho$
  pairs to a nonzero value with some $u_0 \wedge v_0$. The map
  $(s, s') \mapsto \langle c, \hat s \wedge \hat s' \rangle$, with
  $\hat s \in \hat\pi_a$ and $\hat s' \in \hat\pi_b$ the lifts of
  $s, s' \in K^3$, is bilinear, and $(0, 0, 1)$, $(1, 0, 1)$, $(0, 1, 1)$
  span $K^3$, so it is nonzero at a pair of affine points
  (\texttt{exists\_klein\_liftPlane\_affine\_ne\_zero}). Let $c^\perp$ be
  the hyperplane of screws pairing to zero with $c$. The condition
  $\langle c, y_1 \wedge y_3 \rangle \neq 0$ says
  $\dim\bigl(c^\perp + K\,(y_1 \wedge y_3)\bigr) = 6$, so by
  \cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(3) it holds off the zero set of a polynomial
  in the ear data that is nonzero at the ear datum putting $x_1$ and $x_3$
  over that affine pair.

  \emph{The configuration of $G''$.} Take ear data $y$ off the zero sets
  of the polynomials of \cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(2),(3) for these
  conditions: the rank of $G''$ is at least its target, and the picture is
  admissible for $G''$, both at $s^*$; in the second case, also
  $\langle c, y_1 \wedge y_3 \rangle \neq 0$. By \cref{lem:block-rank-ear}
  at $G''$,
  $W = \rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y_1,\ y_1 \wedge y_3,\
  y_3 \wedge p_b)$ has
  $\dim W \ge 3 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G'')$. The picture is
  admissible and $x_1 x_3$ is an edge of $G''$, so the pictures of $y_1$
  and $y_3$ differ and $y_1$, $y_3$ are linearly independent. The two
  stars then span at least five dimensions and pair to zero with
  $y_1 \wedge y_3$ (\cref{lem:pencil-two-stars}).

  \emph{$W$ contains both stars only if $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$.} In the first
  case $\dim W \le 3$ (\cref{lem:pencil-bilinear}; when the two planes
  coincide, the three joins are lines of that plane,
  \cref{lem:pencil-plane-lines}), so $W$ does not contain both stars
  (\texttt{not\_star\_sup\_star\_le\_of\_klein}). In the second, $c \in W$
  lies outside $\operatorname{star}(y_1) + \operatorname{star}(y_3)$, so if
  $W$ contained both stars it would have dimension six
  (\texttt{eq\_top\_of\_star\_sup\_star\_le}).

  \emph{Putting $x_2$ back, and the configuration of $G$}, as in the proof
  of \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four}. Some affine point $x$ for $x_2$,
  on a line through $y_1$ or $y_3$ that is not in $W$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-insertion}), or at $y_1$ when $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$, has
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \min(\dim W + 1, 6)$, where
  $\Lambda = \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y_1,\ y_1 \wedge x,\
  x \wedge y_3,\ y_3 \wedge p_b)$ (\texttt{exists\_insertion\_three}).
  This bound and the main-picture condition are nonzero polynomial
  conditions on the ear data of $G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(2),(3)).
  At a common non-root the picture is main for $G$, so the hinges are
  nonzero, the heights lie in $L_G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(1)), and
  the rank of $G[V_1]$ is its target.

  \emph{The count.} $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse}) and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge f + k - 5 = f - 2$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}), so
  $\min(\dim W + 1, 6) \ge 4 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, which is
  $(\star)$ at $k = 3$, and \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The steps with three and four interior bodies count against $G''$, not
  against $G[V_1]$ alone. Counting against $G[V_1]$ alone needs two
  further inputs: a lower bound on $\dim \rho$ at a general configuration
  of $G[V_1]$, from a further induction through an ear with two interior
  bodies, and a property of the span of a general ear, proved case by
  case over the projective types of the two ends, each a point on a
  plane. Counting against $G''$ needs neither. $G''$ bounds $\dim W$
  directly, and putting $x_2$ back gains a dimension unless $W$ contains
  every line through $x_1$ and every line through $x_3$, in which case
  $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$: with four interior bodies by the tetrahedron, and
  with three by the split on $\rho$, in whose first case the only
  question about the ends is whether their planes coincide
  (\cref{lem:pencil-bilinear}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$\theta$-graphs]
  \label{rem:pencil-x0-theta}
  A $\theta$-graph has two branch bodies joined by three internally
  disjoint paths, of $p_1 \le p_2 \le p_3$ edges, and it is simple when
  $p_2 \ge 2$. Its general configuration attains, and the structural
  coverage (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}) needs no separate case for
  it: a $\theta$-graph is reached by the flat step where its two
  deficiencies agree, by contraction where it has a planar-rigid set, and
  otherwise at a usable chain (\cref{def:pencil-x0-usable-chain}). One
  covering by the steps alone runs along its longest path. The two
  shorter paths form a cycle $C$
  on $s = p_1 + p_2$ bodies, which attains (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-cycle}),
  and the longest is an open ear on $V(C)$ with $k = p_3 - 1$ interior
  bodies, whose ends are adjacent when $p_1 = 1$. For $k \ge 5$,
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear} applies. For $k = 3, 4$,
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-three} and
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four} apply, since suppressing $x_2$ leaves
  the smaller $\theta$-graph with paths of $p_1$, $p_2$ and $p_3 - 1$
  edges. For $k = 2$, $s \le 6$ and the cycle has deficiency zero, so
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two} applies. The remaining simple
  $\theta$-graphs are $K_4 - e$ and $K_{2,3}$. Both of their deficiencies
  vanish, so at a general picture $\dim L(q) = 3$
  (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}), and \cref{cor:pencil-flat-attains}
  applies.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Open ears with one or two interior bodies at non-adjacent ends}
\label{sec:main-component-orbit}

This subsection proves the ear steps for an open ear $a - x_1 - b$ on
$V_1$ with one interior body, and for an open ear $a - x_1 - x_2 - b$ with
two and, unlike \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two}, no bound on the
deficiency, both when $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent. With $\rho$ and
$\Lambda$ as in \cref{sec:main-component-chain}, the aim is the
inequality $(\star)$ of \cref{sec:main-component-short}.
Both steps assume
\[
  \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \;\le\;
  \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])
\]
(\cref{def:deficiency-merged}, at $n = 2$): every partition of $V_1$
keeping $a$ and $b$ in one part falls at least two below the planar
deficiency of $G[V_1]$. Adding the edge $ab$ to $G[V_1]$ then lowers the
planar deficiency by at least two (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-merged}),
so at a general picture its lifting space is at least two smaller than
that of $G[V_1]$ (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality},
\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}). On the solutions $(z, h)$ of the
lifting system of $G[V_1]$ the two values of $h_a - h_b$ at the pictures of
$a$ and $b$ are then independent linear functionals
(\cref{lem:pencil-flag-genericity}), so at a general solution the point of
$b$ lies off the plane at $a$ and the point of $a$ off the plane at $b$.

With one interior body, a height of $G$ restricts to a height of $G[V_1]$
whose affine functions $h_a$ and $h_b$ at $a$ and $b$ agree at the picture
of $x_1$. The picture of $x_1$ is therefore chosen together with the
heights on $V_1$, as a point of their incidence
(\cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-incidence}, \cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-base}). The
two joins at $x_1$ are independent at every admissible picture, which is
$(\star)$ when the deficiency does not drop.

With two interior bodies, the step also uses $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed,
which has the one-body ear $a - x_1 - b$, and the count is taken against
it. At its configuration from \cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-base}, moving $x_1$
in the plane at $a$ and putting $x_2$ back in the plane at $b$ raises
$\dim(\rho + \Lambda)$ by one unless it is already the whole screw space
(\cref{lem:pencil-insertion-two}).

\begin{lemma}[The incidence functionals at the two ends are independent]
  \label{lem:pencil-flag-genericity}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.planeDiff,
        Graph.two_le_finrank_map_planeDiff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system, def:pencil-lifting-space,
        def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $G'$ and $H$ be graphs on the same bodies, and $a, b$ two bodies of
  $G'$, such that every closed neighbourhood in $H$ lies in the closed
  neighbourhood of the same body in $G'$, except that the one of $a$ may
  also contain $b$ and the one of $b$ may also contain $a$. Let $q$ be
  admissible for $G'$, with $\dim L_H(q) + 2 \le \dim L_{G'}(q)$. Then on
  the solutions $(z, h)$ of the lifting system of $G'$ at $q$
  (\cref{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system}), the two linear functionals
  \[
    (z, h) \mapsto (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_a, 1), \qquad
    (z, h) \mapsto (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_b, 1)
  \]
  are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  At a common zero $(z, h)$ of the two functionals, $h_a$ and $h_b$ take
  the same values at $q_a$ and at $q_b$. As $z_a = h_a \cdot (q_a, 1)$ and
  $z_b = h_b \cdot (q_b, 1)$, the height $z$ agrees with $h_a$ on the
  closed neighbourhood of $a$ in $G'$ together with $b$, and with $h_b$ on
  that of $b$ together with $a$; so $z \in L_H(q)$. At a picture
  admissible for $G'$ a solution is determined by its heights, and the
  solutions form a space of dimension $\dim L_{G'}(q)$ (the proof of
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}). So the common zeros of the two
  functionals form a space of dimension at most
  $\dim L_H(q) \le \dim L_{G'}(q) - 2$, and the two are independent.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-flag-genericity}
  \texttt{planeDiff a b} is the difference map $(z, h) \mapsto h_a - h_b$
  on the lifting system's ambient coordinates, read at $q_a$ or $q_b$ to
  give the two functionals in the statement.
  \texttt{Graph.two\_le\_finrank\_map\_planeDiff} is the independence
  claim itself, phrased as: the pair of functionals, viewed as one map
  into $K^2$, has rank two on the solutions of the lifting system of
  $G'$ at $q$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[An incidence meets two open conditions]
  \label{lem:pencil-one-ear-incidence}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_incidence}
  \leanok
  Let $K$ be infinite, let $L$ be a linear subspace of $K^\sigma$ for a
  finite index set $\sigma$, and let $D \colon K^\sigma \to K^3$ be linear
  with $\dim D(L) \ge 2$; read $D(z)$ as the affine function
  $q \mapsto D(z) \cdot (q, 1)$ on $K^2$. Let $A$ be a polynomial in the
  coordinates of $\sigma$, nonzero somewhere on $L$, and $B$ a nonzero
  polynomial on $K^2$. Then there are $z \in L$ and $q \in K^2$ with
  \[
    D(z) \cdot (q, 1) = 0, \qquad A(z) \neq 0, \qquad B(q) \neq 0.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}
  The constant affine functions form a line, so some $z_1 \in L$ has
  $D(z_1)$ nonconstant, and some $z_0 \in L$ has both $A(z_0) \neq 0$ and
  $D(z_0)$ nonconstant, since one of the first two coefficients of $D(z)$
  is a linear polynomial in $z$ nonzero at $z_1$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). As $\dim D(L) \ge 2$, some
  $u \in L$ has $D(u)$ not a multiple of $D(z_0)$. For $q \in K^2$ put
  $w(q) = D(u)(q)\, z_0 - D(z_0)(q)\, u \in L$; then $D(w(q))$ vanishes at
  $q$. An affine function vanishing on the zero line of the nonconstant
  $D(z_0)$ is a multiple of it, so some $q_0$ on that line has
  $D(u)(q_0) \neq 0$, and there $w(q_0) = D(u)(q_0)\, z_0$. So
  $(q, s) \mapsto A(s\, w(q))$ is a polynomial, nonzero at $q_0$ and
  $s = D(u)(q_0)^{-1}$, and a non-root $(q, s)$ of its product with $B$
  gives $z = s\, w(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The base configuration of a one-body ear]
  \label{lem:pencil-one-ear-base}

  $B(q) \neq 0$ and a polynomial $A$ in the coordinates of the lifting

  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok

  (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose new edge $ab$ carries the label of
  the edge $ax$. It is $G'$ together with the edge $ab$, simple since $a$
  and $b$ are not adjacent, and $\operatorname{def}_2(H') + 2 \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G')$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-merged}, at
  $D = 3$).

  Take a picture $q'$ off the zero sets of $B$, of the polynomial of the
  attainment at $G'$, and of the polynomial of Jackson and Jord\'an's
  equality at $H'$ (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}; the closed
  neighbourhoods of $H'$ contain those of $G'$, which have three members
  at a picture admissible for $G'$). Then
  $\dim L_{H'}(q') = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(H') \le \dim L_{G'}(q') - 2$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}), so on the solutions of the
  lifting system of $G'$ at $q'$ the linear map $(z, h) \mapsto h_a - h_b$

  \cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-incidence} to it, with $A$ the product of the
  two functionals of \cref{lem:pencil-flag-genericity} and the polynomial
  in the heights of the attainment at $G'$, which is nonzero at some
  solution (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}), and with $B$ read as

  elsewhere, which is nonzero at $q'_x$. This gives a solution $(z', h')$

  Let $q$ be $q'$ with $x$ moved to $q_x$. Then $B(q) \neq 0$, and $G'$

  The heights $z'$ on $V_1$ and $h'_a \cdot (q_x, 1) = h'_b \cdot (q_x, 1)$
  at $x$, with the coefficient vectors $h'$ on $V_1$ and, at $x$, that of
  the affine function through the three points of its closed neighbourhood

  of $H$ at $q$: the closed neighbourhoods of $a$ and $b$ in $H$ are those
  in $G'$ with $x$ added. Read $A$ on the solutions for $H$ through their
  restriction to $V_1$ (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}). It is nonzero
  at this solution, and wherever it is nonzero both functionals are
  nonzero and the heights attain for $G'$ (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Putting the second body back between the two end planes]
  \label{lem:pencil-insertion-two}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_insertion_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, lem:pencil-join-flat}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $\pi_a$ and $\pi_b$ be the planes
  $p_z = \ell_a \cdot \bar p$ and $p_z = \ell_b \cdot \bar p$ of $K^4$, for
  $\ell_a, \ell_b \in K^3$ (with $\bar p$ as in \cref{lem:pencil-join-flat}).
  Let $p_a \in \pi_a$, $p_b \in \pi_b$ and $y \in \pi_a \cap \pi_b$ be
  affine points, of last coordinate $1$, with $p_a \notin \pi_b$ and
  $p_b \notin \pi_a$. Then for every subspace $\rho \subseteq \Lambda^2 K^4$
  there are affine points $x_1 \in \pi_a$ and $x_2 \in \pi_b$ with
  \[
    \dim\bigl(\rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge x_1,\ x_1 \wedge x_2,\
      x_2 \wedge p_b)\bigr)
    \;\ge\; \min\bigl(\dim(\rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y,\
      y \wedge p_b)) + 1,\ 6\bigr).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-tetrahedron, lem:pencil-insertion}
  Write $W = \rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y, y \wedge p_b)$. If
  $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$, take $x_1 = x_2 = y$. Otherwise note that
  $\pi_a \neq \pi_b$, as $p_b \notin \pi_a$, so the two planes meet in a
  line through $y$; let $u_0$ be a direction of it, of last coordinate
  $0$. The points $p_a$, $y$, $u_0$, $p_b$ are linearly independent: both
  plane equations vanish at $y$ and $u_0$, the equation of $\pi_a$ vanishes
  at $p_a$ but not at $p_b$ and that of $\pi_b$ the other way round, and
  $y$ is affine while $u_0$ is not. Their six joins span $\Lambda^2 K^4$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-tetrahedron}), and $W$ contains $p_a \wedge y$ and
  $y \wedge p_b$, so one of $y \wedge u_0$, $u_0 \wedge p_b$,
  $p_a \wedge u_0$, $p_a \wedge p_b$ lies outside $W$.

  Put $x_1 = y + t u$ and $x_2 = y + \sigma (p_b - y)$, with $\sigma \neq 1$
  and $u$ a direction in $\pi_a$ of last coordinate $0$. For $t \neq 0$,
  the span of $\rho$ and the three joins contains $y \wedge p_b$ (a nonzero
  multiple of $x_2 \wedge p_b$), $u \wedge x_2$, and
  $p_a \wedge y + t\,(p_a \wedge u)$.
  So if $u \wedge x_2 \notin W$, some $t \neq 0$ gives dimension at least
  $\dim W + 1$, since a vector outside a subspace stays outside it after
  adding $t$ times another for some $t \neq 0$ (as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:pencil-insertion}). It remains to choose $u$ and $\sigma$ with
  $u \wedge x_2 \notin W$. If $y \wedge u_0 \notin W$, take $u = u_0$ and
  $\sigma = 0$. Otherwise, for $\sigma \notin \{0, 1\}$, the join
  $u \wedge x_2$ is modulo $W$ a nonzero multiple of $u_0 \wedge p_b$ when
  $u = u_0$, and of $p_a \wedge u_0$ for the same curve with the two planes
  exchanged. For $u = p_a - y$ and $\sigma \notin \{0, 1\}$ it is modulo
  $W$ a nonzero multiple of $p_a \wedge p_b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An open ear with two interior bodies at non-adjacent ends]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two-orbit}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_openEar_two_of_splitOff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:graph-operations, def:deficiency-merged}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with
  an open ear $a - x_1 - x_2 - b$ on $V_1$ whose ends $a$ and $b$ are not
  adjacent, and suppose $\operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$ (\cref{def:deficiency-merged}, at
  $n = 2$). Let $G_1$ be $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed: the splitting-off
  $G_{x_2}^{x_1 b}$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose new edge $x_1 b$
  carries the label of the deleted edge $x_1 x_2$. If the general
  configurations of $G[V_1]$ and of $G_1$ attain, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-one-ear-base, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection, lem:block-rank-ear,
        lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank, lem:pencil-insertion-two,
        lem:pencil-ear-data, lem:pencil-rank-congr,
        lem:pencil-picture-local, lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse,
        lem:deficiency-ear, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Write $f = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$, and compute ranks with the
  joins as hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). $G_1$ has
  the open ear $a - x_1 - b$ on $V_1$ and the same $G[V_1]$; it is simple,
  and its ends are not adjacent.

  \emph{Base data.} Apply \cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-base} to $G_1$, with
  $B$ the product of the polynomial of the attainment at $G_1$ and the
  polynomial of \cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open} for $G$. At the
  picture $q$ it gives, the solutions of the lifting system of $G_1$ at
  which $G_1$ attains contain a nonempty Zariski-open set too, and the two
  meet (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). Take a solution in both,
  with heights $z$; write $\pi_a$ and $\pi_b$ for the planes of its
  coefficient vectors at $a$ and $b$, and $y$ for the point of $x_1$. Then
  $p_b \notin \pi_a$, $p_a \notin \pi_b$ and $y \in \pi_a \cap \pi_b$, and
  the ranks of $G[V_1]$ and of $G_1$ are their targets, so by
  \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} at $G_1$,
  $s = \dim\bigl(\rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y,\ y \wedge
  p_b)\bigr) = 2 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G_1)$.

  \emph{Putting $x_2$ back.} By \cref{lem:pencil-insertion-two} there are
  affine points $y_1 \in \pi_a$ and $y_2 \in \pi_b$ with
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \min(s + 1, 6)$, where
  $\Lambda = \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y_1,\ y_1 \wedge y_2,\
  y_2 \wedge p_b)$.

  \emph{The configuration of $G$.} Take as base data the restriction of
  $(q, z)$ to $V_1$, with the affine functions of $\pi_a$ and $\pi_b$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}); the points $y_1$, $y_2$ at $x_1$, $x_2$ are
  the configuration of one ear datum of $G$. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(3) the bound
  $\dim(\rho + \Lambda) \ge \min(s + 1, 6)$ is a nonzero polynomial
  condition on the ear data, and so is the main-picture condition of $G$,
  nonzero at the ear datum with picture $q$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(2)).
  At a common non-root the picture is main for $G$, so the hinges are
  nonzero, the heights lie in $L_G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-ear-data}(1)), and
  the rank of $G[V_1]$ and $\rho$ are those of the base data
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}, \cref{lem:pencil-picture-local}).

  \emph{The count.} $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G_1) \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse}) and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge f - 3$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}), so
  $\min(s + 1, 6) \ge 3 + f - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, which is
  $(\star)$ at $k = 2$, and \cref{lem:block-rank-ear} and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} apply.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The step with two interior bodies counts against $G_1$, not against
  $G[V_1]$ alone. Counting against $G[V_1]$ alone means describing the
  subspaces $\rho$ at which a general ear gains too little, by a dimension
  count over the placements of the ear, carried out case by case over the
  projective types of the two ends. Counting against $G_1$ needs no such
  description. Its base configuration puts the point $y$ of $x_1$ on the
  line $\pi_a \cap \pi_b$, with each end's point off the other end's plane.
  With $u_0$ a direction of that line, the six joins of $p_a, y, u_0, p_b$
  span $\Lambda^2 K^4$, and
  $W = \rho + \operatorname{span}(p_a \wedge y,\ y \wedge p_b)$ holds two
  of them, so unless $W = \Lambda^2 K^4$ one of the other four lies outside
  $W$, and the matching curve gains a dimension
  (\cref{lem:pencil-insertion-two}). The only property of the ends used is
  that each point lies off the other plane, which the hypothesis on the
  merged deficiency gives at a general configuration, where the map
  $(z, h) \mapsto h_a - h_b$ has rank at least two
  (\cref{lem:pencil-flag-genericity}). In both steps the
  picture of $x_1$ is chosen together with the heights on $V_1$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-one-ear-base}), since a height of the one-body ear
  graph restricts to one of $G[V_1]$ whose affine functions at $a$ and $b$
  agree at the picture of $x_1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Splitting off a body of degree two}
\label{sec:main-component-splitoff}

This subsection proves the split-off step at a body $x$ of degree two
whose neighbours $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent, that is, at an open ear
$a - x - b$ on $V_1$ with one interior body. Let $G''$ be $G$ with $x$
suppressed: the splitting-off $G_x^{ab}$ (\cref{def:graph-operations})
whose new edge $ab$ carries the label of the edge $ax$; it is $G[V_1]$
together with the edge $ab$. The step assumes
\[
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]; a, b) + 5 \;\le\;
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])
\]
(\cref{def:deficiency-merged}, at $n = 3$): every partition of $V_1$
keeping $a$ and $b$ in one part falls at least five below the deficiency
of $G[V_1]$. The deficiency of $G''$ is then at least one below that of
$G$, and the general configuration of $G$ attains once that of $G''$
does. The argument transfers the splitting-off case of Jackson and
Jord\'an's proof of their pin-collinear
theorem~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin}, in which a vertex of degree two
with non-adjacent neighbours is split off and the smaller graph is
realized by induction.

At a configuration of $G''$, put the point of $x$ on the line through
the points of $a$ and $b$, as $p_x = (1 - s) p_a + s p_b$ with
$s \neq 0, 1$. The hinges at $ax$ and $xb$ are then nonzero multiples of
the hinge at $ab$, and the rank of $G$ is that of $G''$ plus five
(\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-special-rank}). This configuration is not
over an admissible picture of $G$, since the closed neighbourhood
$\{a, x, b\}$ of $x$ is pictured on a line. So the picture of $x$ moves
along a line from $(1 - s) q_a + s q_b$ to a general point, and the
heights along a line of solutions of the lifting system of $G[V_1]$
(\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-flexes}, \cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}).
For all but finitely many parameters of this curve the rank is at least
its value at the special position (\cref{lem:pencil-curve-limit}), which
is the form in which the argument uses the lower semicontinuity of the
rank, and the picture is a main picture of $G$.

\begin{lemma}[The rank along a polynomial curve of normals]
  \label{lem:pencil-curve-limit}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finite_setOf_finrank_lt_of_curve}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:panel-support-extensor,
        def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ be a graph with finitely many bodies and edges and an endpoint
  selector, and let $t \mapsto n(t) = (n_v(t))$ be panel normals whose
  coordinates are polynomials in $t \in K$, such that the hinge at every
  edge of $G$ is nonzero at $t = 0$. Then for all but finitely many
  $t \in K$, the rank of the rigidity matrix of the panel-hinge framework
  on $G$ with normals $n(t)$ is at least its value at $t = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}
  By \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} at $n(0)$ there is a
  polynomial $Q$ in the normal coordinates, nonzero at $n(0)$, such that
  at all normals off the zero set of $Q$ the rank is at least its value
  at $n(0)$. Substituting the curve gives a polynomial $t \mapsto Q(n(t))$
  in one variable, nonzero at $t = 0$. It has finitely many roots, and
  off them the rank bound holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rank at the special position]
  \label{lem:pencil-splitoff-special-rank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_splitOff_special,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_eq_add_of_motions}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-operations, def:panel-hinge-framework,
        def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ have an open ear $a - x - b$ on $V_1$ with one interior body,
  and let $G''$ be the splitting-off $G_x^{ab}$
  (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose new edge $ab$ carries the label of
  the edge $ax$. Let $(n_v)$ be panel normals in $K^{k+2}$ with $n_a$ and
  $n_b$ linearly independent and
  \[
    n_x = (1 - s)\, n_a + s\, n_b, \qquad s \neq 0, 1.
  \]
  Then the rank of the rigidity matrix of the panel-hinge framework on
  $G$ with these normals is that of $G''$ plus $D - 1$, where
  $D = \binom{k+2}{2}$ is the dimension of the screw space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:hinge-constraint,
        lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  The panel support extensor is bilinear and alternating
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), so up to sign the hinges at $ax$
  and $xb$ are $s\,C$ and $(1 - s)\,C$, where $C \neq 0$ is the hinge at
  $ab$ in $G''$; every other edge has the same hinge in $G$ and in $G''$.
  A screw assignment $S$ is therefore a motion of $G$ exactly when it is a
  motion of $G''$ with $S_x - S_a \in K C$: in one direction
  $S_a - S_b$ is the difference of $S_x - S_b$ and $S_x - S_a$, in the
  other $S_x - S_b = (S_x - S_a) + (S_a - S_b)$. As $x$ is not a body of
  $G''$, the map $S \mapsto S_x - S_a$ takes the motions of $G''$ onto the
  screw space, and the motions of $G$ are the preimage of the line $K C$,
  of codimension $D - 1$. The row spans are the annihilators of the motion
  spaces (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}), so the ranks
  differ by $D - 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[End planes that all meet over the line of the ends coincide]
  \label{lem:pencil-splitoff-flexes}
  \lean{Graph.planeDiff_eq_zero_of_splitOff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-operations, def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system,
        def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $G$ have an open ear $a - x - b$ on $V_1$ with one interior body,
  let $G' = G[V_1]$, and let $G''$ be the splitting-off $G_x^{ab}$ whose
  new edge $ab$ carries the label of the edge $ax$. Let $q$ be admissible
  for $G$ and for $G''$, with $\dim L_{G''}(q) \le \dim L_G(q)$. If every
  solution $(z, h)$ of the lifting system of $G'$ at $q$
  (\cref{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system}) has
  \[
    (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_a, 1) = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_b, 1) = 0,
  \]
  then every such solution has $h_a = h_b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness, lem:pencil-three-points}
  The flexes of $G'$ are the solutions of its lifting system, not the
  heights $L_{G'}(q)$: $G'$ may have bodies of degree one (it is a path
  when $G$ is a cycle), and there the heights do not determine the planes
  $h_a$ and $h_b$. Suppose some solution $y_0$ has $h_a \neq h_b$. The
  affine function $h_a - h_b$ vanishes at $q_a$ and $q_b$, and the
  homogeneous picture points of the closed neighbourhood $\{a, x, b\}$ of
  $x$ in $G$ are linearly independent, as $q$ is admissible for $G$. So
  the functional $\varphi(z, h) = (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_x, 1)$ is nonzero
  at $y_0$.

  By the hypothesis, every solution for $G'$ is one for $G''$: the closed
  neighbourhoods of $a$ and of $b$ in $G''$ are those in $G'$ with $b$,
  respectively $a$, added, and $h_a$ takes the value
  $z_b = h_b \cdot (q_b, 1)$ at $q_b$, and $h_b$ the value $z_a$ at $q_a$.
  Restriction to $V_1$ sends the solutions for $G$ at $q$ into the
  solutions for $G'$ on which $\varphi$ vanishes, since the closed
  neighbourhoods of $a$ and $b$ in $G$ contain $x$, and it is injective: a
  solution for $G$ vanishing on $V_1$ has $z_x = h_a \cdot (q_x, 1) = 0$,
  and then $h_x = 0$, since $h_x \cdot (q_w, 1) = z_w = 0$ at the three
  independent points of $\{a, x, b\}$ (as in
  \cref{lem:pencil-three-points}). So the solutions for $G$ form a space
  of dimension less than that of the solutions for $G'$, which is at most
  that of the solutions for $G''$. At a picture admissible for a graph
  the solutions form a space of dimension $\dim L(q)$ (the proof of
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}), so $\dim L_G(q) < \dim L_{G''}(q)$,
  a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A line of solutions through the special position]
  \label{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_mem_forall_add_smul_eq_zero,
    Graph.mem_liftingSpace_oneEar, Graph.ker_liftingMatrix_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-weighted-lifting-system, def:pencil-lifting-space,
        def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $L$ be a subspace of a vector space over $K$, let
      $\varphi_0$ and $\psi$ be linear functionals, and let $y_0 \in L$
      with $\varphi_0(y_0) = 0$. Suppose that $\varphi_0$ is nonzero
      somewhere on $L$, or that $\varphi_0$ and $\psi$ both vanish on $L$.
      Then some $w \in L$ has
      $\varphi_0(y_0 + t w) + t\, \psi(y_0 + t w) = 0$ for every
      $t \in K$.
    \item Let $G$ have an open ear $a - x - b$ on $V_1$ with one interior
      body, and let $q$ be admissible for $G$. If a solution $(z, h)$ of
      the lifting system of $G[V_1]$ at $q$ has
      $(h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_x, 1) = 0$, then the height that agrees with
      $z$ away from $x$ and takes the value $h_a \cdot (q_x, 1)$ at $x$
      lies in $L_G(q)$.
    \item The solutions of the lifting system of a graph $H$ at a picture
      depend only on the picture at the bodies of $H$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-three-points, lem:pencil-picture-local}
  (1) If $\varphi_0(W) \neq 0$ for some $W \in L$, take
  $w = \varphi_0(W)^{-1}\bigl(\psi(W)\, y_0 - \psi(y_0)\, W\bigr)$; then
  $\varphi_0(y_0 + t w) = -t\,\psi(y_0)$ and
  $\psi(y_0 + t w) = \psi(y_0)$. Otherwise take $w = 0$. The line
  $y_0 + t w$ is the curve
  $y_0 - t\,\psi(y_0)\bigl(\varphi_0(W) + t\,\psi(W)\bigr)^{-1} W$ of the
  informal argument multiplied by $1 + t\,\psi(W)/\varphi_0(W)$, a scalar
  equal to $1$ at $t = 0$, so it has no denominators to clear.

  (2) At a body $v$ of $V_1$ the closed neighbourhood in $G$ is that in
  $G[V_1]$, with $x$ added when $v$ is $a$ or $b$, and the height agrees
  with $h_v$ there, at $x$ because $h_a$ and $h_b$ agree at $(q_x, 1)$.
  The closed neighbourhood of $x$ is $\{a, x, b\}$, on which every height
  agrees with an affine function (\cref{lem:pencil-three-points}).

  (3) Each condition of the lifting system of $H$ reads the picture at a
  member of the closed neighbourhood of a body of $H$, and these are
  bodies of $H$ (as in \cref{lem:pencil-picture-local}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Splitting off a body of degree two]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-splitoff}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_splitOff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:graph-operations, def:D-deficiency, def:deficiency-merged}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), with an open ear $a - x - b$ on $V_1$
  with one interior body whose ends $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent. Let
  $G''$ be $G$ with $x$ suppressed: the splitting-off $G_x^{ab}$
  (\cref{def:graph-operations}) whose new edge $ab$ carries the label of
  the edge $ax$. Suppose $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]; a, b) + 5 \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$ (\cref{def:deficiency-merged},
  \cref{def:D-deficiency}, at $n = 3$). If the general configuration of
  $G''$ attains, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:splitoff-deficiency-merged, lem:deficiency-ear,
        lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse, thm:pencil-jj-equality,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-curve-limit,
        lem:pencil-splitoff-special-rank, lem:pencil-splitoff-flexes,
        lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}
  \emph{The counts.} By \cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-merged} at $D = 6$,
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') + 5 \le
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1])$, and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) + 4$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear}, at $k = 1$), so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') + 1 \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
  Also $\operatorname{def}_2(G'') \le \operatorname{def}_2(G)$
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency-reuse}, at $n = 2$).

  \emph{The picture.} $G''$ is simple, as $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent.
  Take a picture $q$ off the zero sets of the polynomial of the attainment
  at $G''$, of the polynomial of Jackson and Jord\'an's equality at $G''$
  (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}; the closed neighbourhoods of $G''$ have
  three members at a picture admissible for $G''$), and of the
  main-picture polynomial of $G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}).
  Then $q$ is admissible for $G''$ and a main picture of $G$, some
  $z'' \in L_{G''}(q)$ gives $G''$ its target rank, and
  $\dim L_{G''}(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G'') \le \dim L_G(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}). Let $y_0 = (z'', h)$ be a
  solution of the lifting system of $G''$ at $q$ with heights $z''$. It
  is a solution for $G[V_1]$, and $h_a - h_b$ vanishes at $(q_a, 1)$ and
  $(q_b, 1)$, since $ab$ is an edge of $G''$.

  \emph{The special position.} If some solution for $G[V_1]$ has
  $h_a - h_b$ nonzero at $(q_a, 1)$ or at $(q_b, 1)$, then for all but at
  most one $s$ it is nonzero at $(1 - s)(q_a, 1) + s\,(q_b, 1)$; otherwise
  every solution for $G[V_1]$ has $h_a = h_b$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-flexes}). Fix $s \neq 0, 1$ accordingly, put
  $q^0_x = (1 - s) q_a + s\, q_b$, and let
  $\varphi_0(z, h) = (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q^0_x, 1)$ and
  $\psi(z, h) = (h_a - h_b) \cdot (q_x - q^0_x, 0)$. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}(1) some solution $w$ for $G[V_1]$ gives
  $\varphi_0(y(t)) + t\,\psi(y(t)) = 0$ for $y(t) = y_0 + t w$.

  \emph{The curve.} Let $q(t)$ be $q$ with $x$ moved to
  $q^0_x + t\,(q_x - q^0_x)$, so that $q(1) = q$. As $q(t)$ agrees with
  $q$ on $V_1$, $y(t)$ is a solution for $G[V_1]$ at $q(t)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}(3)), and the identity above says that
  its $h_a - h_b$ vanishes at $(q(t)_x, 1)$. Let $z(t)$ be the height of
  $y(t)$ away from $x$, with the value $h_a \cdot (q(t)_x, 1)$ at $x$. The
  points of the configuration $(q(t), z(t))$ are polynomials in $t$. At
  $t = 0$ they are at the special position: $p_x = (1 - s) p_a + s\, p_b$,
  since $h_a \cdot (q_b, 1) = z''_b$, and $p_a$, $p_b$ are independent, as
  $q_a \neq q_b$. Every hinge of $G$ is nonzero at $t = 0$: $q^0_x$
  differs from $q_a$ and $q_b$ as $s \neq 0, 1$, and every other edge is an
  edge of $G''$. So at $t = 0$ the rank of $G$ is that of $G''$ at
  $(q, z'')$ plus five (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-special-rank},
  \cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}). The picture $q(0)$ is not admissible for
  $G$, so \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} does not apply there.

  \emph{A good parameter.} By \cref{lem:pencil-curve-limit} the rank of
  $G$ at $(q(t), z(t))$ is at least its value at $t = 0$ for all but
  finitely many $t$. The main-picture polynomial of $G$ at $q(t)$ is a
  polynomial in $t$, nonzero at $t = 1$, so $q(t)$ is a main picture of
  $G$ for all but finitely many $t$. Take $t$ outside both
  finite sets. Then $z(t) \in L_G(q(t))$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}(2)), and $(q(t), z(t))$ has rank at
  least $6(|V_1| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') + 5 \ge
  6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, as $|V(G)| = |V_1| + 1$.
  So \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The special configuration has the rank the step needs, but it is not
  over an admissible picture of $G$. The informal argument places it on
  the main component as a limit. It moves the point of $x$ off the line
  $q_a q_b$ in an arbitrary direction, keeps the moving picture
  admissible, and $\dim L_G$ minimal by a formula for the dimension of
  the solutions along the curve, lifts the curve by a
  rational family of solutions, and then uses the lower semicontinuity of
  the rank on the irreducible main component. The proof above uses no
  geometry of the main component. Its curve ends at the general picture
  itself, $q(1) = q$, so the main-picture condition along it is one
  polynomial in $t$, nonzero at $t = 1$, and the dimension of the
  solutions is compared only at $q$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-flexes}). The condition that makes the
  moving solution a height of $G$, that $h_a - h_b$ vanish at
  $(q(t)_x, 1)$, is the vanishing of the pencil $\varphi_0 + t\,\psi$ of
  functionals. With $s$ chosen as in the proof, the solutions for
  $G[V_1]$ contain a line $y_0 + t w$ with
  $(\varphi_0 + t\,\psi)(y_0 + t w) = 0$ for every $t$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-splitoff-curve}(1)), so the rational family is a
  polynomial one. The lower semicontinuity is taken
  along the one curve of normals (\cref{lem:pencil-curve-limit}): off
  finitely many $t$ the rank of $G$ is at least its value at the special
  position, and one such configuration over a main picture suffices
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
  Write $\delta = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]) -
  \operatorname{def}(\tilde G[V_1]; a, b)$. The informal argument gives
  more: whatever $\delta$, the general configuration of $G$ has rank at
  least one below its target, and it attains when $\delta \ge 5$. Only the
  case $\delta \ge 5$ is formalized. The structural coverage uses a
  one-body split-off only there; when $\delta = 0$ it uses the ear step
  with one interior body (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-one}), and it uses
  no one-body ear with $1 \le \delta \le 4$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Contraction at an additive core}
\label{sec:main-component-contract-additive}

This subsection proves the contraction step at a rigid core whose planar
deficiency need not vanish. Let $W \subsetneq V(G)$ and $r \in W$, and
let $H = G[W]$ and $G/H$ be as in \cref{sec:main-component-contract}.
The step assumes that $\operatorname{def}(\tilde H) = 0$ and that the
planar deficiencies add,
\[
  \operatorname{def}_2(H) + \operatorname{def}_2(G/H) \;\le\;
  \operatorname{def}_2(G),
\]
and it asks that the general configurations of both $H$ and $G/H$
attain. It is again Katoh and Tanigawa's contraction
case~\cite[\S6.2, Lemma~6.3]{katohTanigawa2011}, read on configurations
along the contraction curve and the rescaled lifting system
(\cref{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system}). The end of the curve is as
at a core of planar deficiency zero: a height of $G/H$ vanishing at $r$
agrees off $W$ with the heights of a solution of $M(0)$
(\cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit}), and the rows of the edges outside
$H$, with the columns of $W$ deleted, are bounded below near the
collapsed placement (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank}).

Two parts of that argument change, because the core is no longer flat.
The kernel of $M(0)$ is bounded above through the core heights of its
solutions (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound}), and below by
$3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, since for all but finitely many $t$ the
kernel of $M(t)$ is no larger than that of $M(0)$ and, at $t \neq 0$,
at least as large as $L_G(q(t))$. Under additivity, with Jackson and
Jord\'an's equality at $H$ and at $G/H$, the two bounds meet, and the
core heights of the solutions of $M(0)$ are then all of $L_H(q)$. So
the attainment at $H$, read on the core heights, and the bound on the
rows of the edges outside $H$ are two open conditions that meet inside
$\ker M(0)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}).
And the rank of the core along the curve is no longer its flat rank: at
$t \neq 0$ the configuration of $H$ is the image of one over the fixed
picture $q$ under an invertible linear map of $K^4$
(\cref{lem:pencil-rank-collineation},
\cref{lem:pencil-contract-magnified-rank}), and there the general
configuration of $H$ attains.

\begin{lemma}[A collineation of the normals preserves the rank]
  \label{lem:pencil-rank-collineation}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_linearEquiv}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ be a graph with an endpoint selector, and let $g$ be an
  invertible linear map of $K^4$. If panel normals $p$ and $p'$ in $K^4$
  satisfy $p'_w = g(p_w)$ at every body $w$ of $G$, then the rigidity
  matrices of the panel-hinge frameworks on $G$ with normals $p$ and
  with normals $p'$ have the same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular, lem:screw-map-rows,
        lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  The hinge at an edge $uv$ with normals $p$ is the image of the line
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the polarity
  (\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}), so the rigidity matrix with
  normals $p$ has the rank of the body-hinge framework whose hinge at
  $uv$ is $p_u \wedge p_v$ (\cref{lem:screw-map-rows}, as in the proof
  of \cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}), and likewise for $p'$.
  Both ends of an edge of $G$ are bodies of $G$, so there
  $p'_u \wedge p'_v = g(p_u) \wedge g(p_v)$ is the image of
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the invertible linear map of the screw space
  induced by $g$, and \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} applies once more. This
  is the argument of \cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}, for any
  invertible linear map.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The kernel at the end of the curve, through the core heights]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound}
  \lean{Graph.liftingRestrict_mem_liftingSpace_induce_of_contract,
        Graph.finrank_ker_contractLiftingMatrix_zero_add_three_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system, def:pencil-lifting-space,
        def:pencil-admissible-picture, def:rigid-contraction}
  Let $r \in W \subseteq V(G)$ and $H = G[W]$, let $q$ be a picture, and
  let $\rho$ send a vector $(z, h)$ of unknowns of the rescaled lifting
  system to the height that agrees with $z$ on $W$ and vanishes
  elsewhere.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item At every $t$, $\rho$ maps the solutions of $M(t)$ into
      $L_H(q)$.
    \item If $q$ is admissible for $H$ and for $G/H$, then
      $\dim \ker M(0) + 3 \le \dim \rho(\ker M(0)) + \dim L_{G/H}(q)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-contract-limit, lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  (1) The rows at a core body $c$ and a member of its closed
  neighbourhood in $H$ have the weight $(x_w, y_w, 1)$ at every $t$, as
  in the proof of \cref{lem:pencil-contract-core-plane}.

  (2) By rank--nullity it suffices that the solutions of $M(0)$ with
  $\rho = 0$ form a space $N$ with $\dim N + 3 \le \dim L_{G/H}(q)$. A
  solution in $N$ has zero heights on $W$, so by the rows of (1) its
  plane at every core body is zero: three members of each closed
  neighbourhood in $H$ have independent picture points. The map of the
  proof of \cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit}, which sends a solution to
  the heights $z_w + z_r$ at the bodies $w$ outside $W$ and $z_r$ at $r$,
  then takes $N$ injectively into $L_{G/H}(q)$, as it does there for a
  core with one plane, here the zero plane. Its image vanishes on the
  closed neighbourhood of $r$ in $G/H$, which consists of $r$ and the
  attachments: $z_r = 0$, and the row at a core body $c$ and an
  attachment $u$ of $c$ gives
  $z_u = h_c \cdot (x_u - x_r, y_u - y_r, 0) = 0$. Three members of that
  closed neighbourhood have independent picture points, so the only
  affine height vanishing there is zero, and the image meets
  $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ only in zero. As $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ is
  three-dimensional and lies in $L_{G/H}(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}), this gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rank of the core along the curve]
  \label{lem:pencil-contract-magnified-rank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_induce_contractHeight_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-contract-lifting-system, def:pencil-x0-attains}
  Let $r \in W$ and $H = G[W]$, let $q$ be a picture and $t \in K$
  nonzero, and let $(z, h)$ be any vector of unknowns of the rescaled
  lifting system. Then the rank of $H$ at the configuration
  $(q(t), z(t))$ equals its rank at $(q, z)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-rank-collineation}
  At a body $c$ of $W$ the point of $(q(t), z(t))$ is
  $((1 - t)\,x_r + t\,x_c,\ (1 - t)\,y_r + t\,y_c,\ t\,z_c,\ 1)$, the
  image of $(x_c, y_c, z_c, 1)$ under the linear map
  \[
    (\xi, \eta, \zeta, \omega) \mapsto
    (t\,\xi + (1 - t)\,x_r\,\omega,\ t\,\eta + (1 - t)\,y_r\,\omega,\
     t\,\zeta,\ \omega)
  \]
  of $K^4$, which is invertible as $t \neq 0$. So
  \cref{lem:pencil-rank-collineation} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Contraction at an additive core]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-contract-additive}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_additiveContract}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing, def:cut-edges-2ec,
        def:rigid-contraction, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and be
  $2$-edge-connected. Let $W \subsetneq V(G)$ with $|W| \ge 2$ and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[W]) = 0$, such that no attachment has two
  neighbours in $W$, and let $G/H$ be the contraction of $H = G[W]$ to a
  body of $W$. Suppose
  $\operatorname{def}_2(H) + \operatorname{def}_2(G/H) \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. If the general configurations of $H$ and of
  $G/H$ attain, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-contract-standing-rigid, lem:pencil-contract-standing,
        lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency, thm:pencil-jj-equality,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness, lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency,
        lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel,
        lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound, lem:pencil-contract-limit,
        lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-contract-magnified-rank, lem:pencil-rank-congr,
        lem:block-rank-contract}
  \emph{The pieces.} $H$ satisfies the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing-rigid}, at $n = 3$), and $G/H$ is
  simple and satisfies them (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing}). As $H$
  is rigid, $G/H$ has the deficiency of $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency}, at $n = 3$). Take one
  picture $q$ generic for the attainment and for Jackson and Jord\'an's
  equality (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}) at $H$ and at $G/H$, and main
  for $G$ (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). Then $q$ is
  admissible for $H$ and for $G/H$, with
  $\dim L_H(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(H)$ and
  $\dim L_{G/H}(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G/H)$, and both have
  attaining heights; that of $G/H$ may be taken to vanish at $r$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}). Call it $z'$.

  \emph{The kernel at the end of the curve.} The polynomial section of
  the kernel through the zero solution at $t = 0$ (as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}, with the parameter $t$) shows that
  $\dim \ker M(t) \le \dim \ker M(0)$ for all but finitely many $t$. At
  such a $t \neq 0$ with $q(t)$ a main picture of $G$,
  \[
    3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) \le \dim L_G(q(t)) \le \dim \ker M(t)
    \le \dim \ker M(0)
  \]
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency},
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel}). By
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound}, Jackson and Jord\'an's
  equality at $H$ and $G/H$, and additivity,
  \[
    \dim \ker M(0) + 3 \le \dim \rho(\ker M(0)) + \dim L_{G/H}(q)
    \le \dim L_H(q) + \dim L_{G/H}(q) \le 6 + \operatorname{def}_2(G).
  \]
  So $\dim \ker M(0) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, and
  $\rho(\ker M(0)) = L_H(q)$: the core heights of the solutions of $M(0)$
  are all of $L_H(q)$.

  \emph{Two open conditions.} The polynomial of the attainment at $H$,
  applied to $\rho$, is nonzero somewhere on $\ker M(0)$, at a preimage
  of an attaining height of $H$. The polynomial of
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-degenerate-rank}, for the rank of $G/H$ at
  $(q, z')$ and applied to the configuration $(q(0), z(0))$ of a
  solution, is nonzero at the solution that agrees with $z'$ off $W$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-limit}), whose configuration at $t = 0$ is
  the collapsed placement of $(q, z')$. So some $x_0 \in \ker M(0)$ makes
  both nonzero (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}).

  \emph{The curve.} At every $t \neq 0$ with $q(t)$ a main picture of
  $G$ the kernel does not jump:
  $\dim \ker M(0) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) \le \dim L_G(q(t)) \le
  \dim \ker M(t)$. So the polynomial section of the kernel through $x_0$
  solves $M(t)$ off the zero set of one polynomial in $t$, and its
  heights $z(t)$ lie in $L_G(q(t))$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-lifting-kernel}). Take $t$ off the zero sets
  of $t$, of that polynomial, of the main-picture polynomial of $G$ along
  the curve (nonzero at $t = 1$, where $q(1) = q$), and of the two open
  conditions along the section (nonzero at $t = 0$). Then $\rho$ of the
  solution at $t$ is an attaining height of $H$ over the fixed picture
  $q$ (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound}(1)), so the core has rank
  $6(|W| - 1)$ at $(q(t), z(t))$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-magnified-rank},
  \cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}), and the rows of the other edges, with
  the columns of $W$ deleted, have rank at least
  $\operatorname{tgt}(G/H)$. By \cref{lem:block-rank-contract} the rank of
  $G$ at $(q(t), z(t))$ is at least
  $6(|W| - 1) + \operatorname{tgt}(G/H) = \operatorname{tgt}(G)$, since
  $|V(G/H)| = |V(G)| - |W| + 1$ and $G/H$ has the deficiency of $G$. So
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The informal argument reaches the rank of the core otherwise. It
  shows that at a general picture the heights of $G$ restrict onto those
  of $H$, which under additivity follows from Jackson and Jord\'an's
  equality at $H$ and at $G/H$, and it deduces that the core is rigid at
  the general configuration of $G$ because restriction to the core is
  then dominant. The proof above needs neither. The two bounds on
  $\ker M(0)$ give the corresponding statement for the solutions of
  $M(0)$ at the one picture $q$: their core heights are all of $L_H(q)$.
  So the attaining heights of $H$ over $q$, a nonempty open subset of
  $L_H(q)$, pull back to a nonempty open subset of $\ker M(0)$, which
  meets the one on which the rows of the other edges keep their rank, and
  along the polynomial section through a common point both conditions
  hold off finitely many $t$. The core heights of the solution at $t$ lie
  in $L_H(q)$ at every $t$, since the rows of $M(t)$ between bodies of the
  core do not depend on $t$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-kernel-bound}(1)), and the configuration of
  $H$ over $q(t)$ is the image of the one over $q$ with those heights
  under an invertible linear map of $K^4$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-magnified-rank}). So the rank of the
  core is read over the fixed picture $q$, where the general
  configuration of $H$ attains, and never at a general configuration of
  $G$.

  Informally the hypothesis is additivity, since the reverse inequality
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) \le \operatorname{def}_2(H) +
  \operatorname{def}_2(G/H)$ holds for every $W$; only the inequality
  stated is used, and the reverse is not formalized. At a core of planar
  deficiency zero the hypothesis is the conservation of the planar
  deficiency (\cref{lem:pencil-contraction-deficiency}, at $n = 2$), and
  the general configuration of $H$ attains (\cref{cor:pencil-jj-flat}),
  so \cref{thm:pencil-x0-contract-rigid} is the case
  $\operatorname{def}_2(H) = 0$; it is proved separately, along the flat
  core and with no attainment at $H$. The structural coverage supplies
  additivity at its cores from a partition condition on $G$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The deficiency of a sparse graph}
\label{sec:main-component-sparse}

This subsection proves the partition estimates the structural coverage
(\cref{sec:main-component-coverage}) uses. Let $D$ be the dimension of
\cref{def:D-deficiency} at some $n$. For $X \subseteq V(G)$ let $e(X)$
be the number of edges of $G$ joining two distinct bodies of $X$, and
let
\[
  s_D(X) \;=\; D\,(|X| - 1) \;-\; (D - 1)\,e(X),
\]
the value of the partition of $G[X]$ into singletons; write $s = s_3$.
A set $W$ of at least two bodies is \emph{rigid} if
$\operatorname{def}_3(G[W]) = 0$, and \emph{planar-rigid} if
$\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) = 0$. The graph $G$ is \emph{sparse} if
$s(X) \ge 1$, that is $2e(X) \le 3|X| - 4$, for every $X \subseteq
V(G)$ with $|X| \ge 2$; a graph with no planar-rigid set is sparse
(\cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}). For bodies $a$ and $b$,
$\operatorname{def}_n(G; a, b)$ is the merged deficiency of
\cref{def:deficiency-merged}.

Two estimates carry the rest. A partition of a graph whose sets have
nonnegative singleton values falls below the singletons by at least the
singleton value of any one of its parts
(\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}); and merging the parts that meet
a set of deficiency zero does not lower the value of a partition
(\cref{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}). From them come the rigidity of a
tight set, the bound that a maximal rigid set places on the singleton
values above it, the additivity of the planar deficiency at such a set,
and the two estimates at the ends of a chain that the ear and split-off
steps ask for.

\begin{lemma}[The singletons bound the partitions of a sparse graph]
  \label{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef_add_partitionDef_induce_id_le, Graph.partitionDef_le_partitionDef_id}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $P$ be a partition of $V(G)$ with a part $Z$, and suppose
  $s_D(Y) \ge 0$ for every nonempty $Y \subseteq V(G) \setminus Z$.
  Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) + s_D(Z) \;\le\; s_D(V(G)).
  \]
  In particular, if $s_D(Y) \ge 0$ for every nonempty $Y \subseteq
  V(G)$, then $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \le s_D(V(G))$ for every
  partition $P$ of $V(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  Both $P$ and the partition into singletons are separated by $Z$, so
  each splits at $Z$ with the same cut term
  (\cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}). On $G[Z]$ the partition $P$ has
  one part and value zero, and the singletons have value $s_D(Z)$. The
  second statement follows by induction on the number of bodies, since
  $s_D(Z) \ge 0$ and the rest is the statement at $G[V(G) \setminus
  Z]$. The first follows from one such split and the second statement
  at $G[V(G) \setminus Z]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Adding one body]
  \label{lem:deficiency-add-body}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef_induce_insert, Graph.deficiency_induce_insert_eq_zero,
        Graph.partitionDef_two_induce_insert_id}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $X \subseteq V(G)$ and $u \notin X$, and let $P$ be a partition of
  $X \cup \{u\}$. Let $t$ be the number of edges of $G$ from $u$ to a
  body of $X$ in a part of $P$ other than the part of $u$, and let
  $\epsilon = 1$ if $u$ is alone in its part and $\epsilon = 0$
  otherwise. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[X \cup \{u\}]}(P) \;=\;
    \operatorname{def}_{\tilde G[X]}(P|_X) + \epsilon D - (D - 1)\,t.
  \]
  Consequently, if $D \ge 3$, $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[W]) = 0$ and
  a body $u \notin W$ is adjacent to two distinct bodies of $W$, then
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[W \cup \{u\}]) = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The number of parts grows by one exactly when $u$ is alone, and the
  crossing edges gain the $t$ edges at $u$. For the consequence, let $P$
  partition $W \cup \{u\}$. If $u$ shares a part, the value of $P$ is at
  most that of $P|_W$, which is at most zero. If $u$ is alone, both of
  its edges into $W$ cross, and the value is at most
  $0 + D - 2(D - 1) < 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A set of nonpositive singleton value contains a set of deficiency zero]
  \label{lem:deficiency-sparse}
  \lean{Graph.exists_deficiency_induce_eq_zero_of_partitionDef_id_nonpos,
        Graph.one_le_partitionDef_induce_id}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $X \subseteq V(G)$ with $|X| \ge 2$ and $s_D(X) \le 0$. Then some
  $Y \subseteq X$ with $|Y| \ge 2$ has $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[Y])
  = 0$. Hence, if no set of at least two bodies has deficiency zero,
  then $s_D(X) \ge 1$ for every $X$ with $|X| \ge 2$; at $n = 2$, a
  graph with no planar-rigid set is sparse.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}
  Take $Y \subseteq X$ minimal with $|Y| \ge 2$ and $s_D(Y) \le 0$.
  Every nonempty proper subset of $Y$ then has nonnegative singleton
  value, a singleton having value zero. A partition of $Y$ other than
  $\{Y\}$ has a part $Z \subsetneq Y$, and its value is at most $s_D(Y)
  - s_D(Z) \le 0$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}); the partition
  $\{Y\}$ has value zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Merging along a set of deficiency zero]
  \label{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.exists_partitionDef_le_mergeOn, Graph.deficiencyMerged_le_deficiency,
        Graph.deficiencyMerged_eq_deficiency_of_mem}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:deficiency-merged}
  Let $D \ge 1$ and let $W \subseteq V(G)$ be nonempty with
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G[W]) = 0$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For every partition $P$ of $V(G)$, some partition $P'$ coarser
      than $P$, with $W$ inside one of its parts, has
      $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) \le \operatorname{def}_{\tilde
      G}(P')$.
    \item For all bodies $a$ and $b$, $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G; a, b)
      \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, with equality if $a, b \in W$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:partitionDef-merge}
  (1) Let $P'$ merge the parts of $P$ that meet $W$ into one part. If $m$
  parts of $P$ meet $W$ and $e$ edges join distinct ones among them,
  merging them lowers the value by $D(m - 1) - (D - 1)e$
  (\cref{lem:partitionDef-merge}). The $e$ edges include the edges of
  $G[W]$ crossing $P|_W$, so this quantity is at most the value of
  $P|_W$ in $G[W]$, which is at most zero.

  (2) The merged deficiency is a maximum over fewer partitions. For the
  equality, merge a partition attaining $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  along $W$ by (1); the result has $a$ and $b$ in one part.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A tight set is rigid]
  \label{lem:deficiency-tight-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_three_induce_eq_zero_of_tight}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $X \subseteq V(G)$ with $|X| \ge 3$, and suppose $s(Z) \ge 1$ for
  every $Z \subseteq X$ with $|Z| \ge 2$. If $s(X) \le 1$, then
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G[X]) = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}
  Write $v_2(P) = 3(|P| - 1) - 2d(P)$ and $v_3(P) = 6(|P| - 1) - 5d(P)$
  for the two values of a partition $P$ of $X$, so that $v_3(P) =
  2v_2(P) - d(P)$. If $P$ has a part $Z$ with $2 \le |Z| < |X|$, then
  $v_2(P) \le s(X) - s(Z) \le 0$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}), so $v_3(P) \le 0$; the
  partition $\{X\}$ has $v_3 = 0$. For the partition into singletons,
  $v_2 = s(X) \le 1$ gives $2e(X) \ge 3|X| - 4 \ge 5$, so $e(X) \ge 3$
  and $v_3 = 2s(X) - e(X) < 0$.

  The informal argument places a tight set inside a maximal rigid set
  through the partition of the graph into its maximal rigid sets; the
  direct count above needs no such partition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A maximal rigid set bounds the singleton values above it]
  \label{lem:deficiency-core-bound}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_three_induce_eq_zero_of_le, Graph.partitionDef_induce_id_le_of_maximal}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be sparse.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $W \subseteq Y \subseteq V(G)$ with $W$ nonempty and
      $\operatorname{def}_3(G[W]) = 0$. If $s(Y) \le s(W)$, and
      $s(W) \le s(Q)$ for every $Q$ with $W \subseteq Q \subsetneq Y$,
      then $\operatorname{def}_3(G[Y]) = 0$.
    \item Let $W \subseteq V(G)$ be nonempty with
      $\operatorname{def}_3(G[W]) = 0$, and let $X_0$ be empty or a
      single body outside $W$ that sends at most two edges into every
      set avoiding it and at most one into $W$. Suppose $W$ is maximal
      among the sets $W' \supseteq W$ in $V(G) \setminus X_0$ with
      $\operatorname{def}_3(G[W']) = 0$. Then $s(W) \le s(X)$ for every
      $X$ with $W \subseteq X \subseteq V(G)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid, lem:deficiency-singleton-bound,
        lem:deficiency-add-body}
  (1) Let $P$ be a partition of $Y$, and let $P'$ be a partition coarser
  than $P$ with $W$ inside one part $Q$ and $v_3(P) \le v_3(P')$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}), in the notation of the proof of
  \cref{lem:deficiency-tight-rigid}. If $Q = Y$ then $v_3(P') = 0$.
  Otherwise $v_2(P') \le s(Y) - s(Q) \le 0$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}), so $v_3(P') \le 2v_2(P') \le
  0$.

  (2) Suppose not, and take $X \subseteq V(G)$ with $W \subseteq X$ and
  $s(X) < s(W)$, of least size. Then $X \ne W$, and $s(W) \le s(Q)$ for
  every $Q \supseteq W$ with fewer bodies than $X$. If $X$ avoids $X_0$,
  then (1) at $Y = X$ makes $G[X]$ rigid, against the maximality of $W$.
  Otherwise $X_0 = \{x\}$ with $x \in X$; put $Y = X \setminus \{x\}$,
  which contains $W$. Adding $x$ to $Y$ changes the singleton value by
  $3 - 2t$, where $t \le 2$ is the number of edges from $x$ into $Y$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}), and $t \le 1$ if $Y = W$. So $Y \ne
  W$, since otherwise $s(X) \ge s(W) + 1$. Then $s(Y) \ge s(W)$, as $Y$
  has fewer bodies than $X$, while $s(X) \ge s(Y) - 1$; so $s(X) = s(W)
  - 1$ and $s(Y) = s(W)$. Now (1) at $Y$ makes $G[Y]$ rigid, a set
  avoiding $X_0$ that strictly contains $W$, against the maximality of
  $W$.

  The informal argument derives this bound from the partition of $G$
  into its maximal rigid sets, whose quotient has no rigid set of two or
  more members; the minimal counterexample above needs no such partition
  and treats a maximal rigid set of $G$ and of $G - x$ alike.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The planar deficiency adds at a core]
  \label{lem:deficiency-additive-core}
  \lean{Graph.partitionDef_map, Graph.deficiency_induce_add_deficiency_rigidContract_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:rigid-contraction}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For a map $c$ of the bodies, the value of a partition of the
      image graph $c(G)$ equals the value of its pull-back along $c$ to
      a partition of $V(G)$.
    \item Let $G$ be sparse, let $r \in W \subseteq V(G)$, and suppose
      $s(W) \le s(X)$ for every $X$ with $W \subseteq X \subseteq V(G)$.
      Let $G/G[W]$ collapse $W$ to $r$ (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}).
      Then
      \[
        \operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) + \operatorname{def}_2(G/G[W]) \;\le\;
        \operatorname{def}_2(G).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}
  (1) The parts and the crossing edges correspond under $c$.

  (2) By \cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound} and sparsity,
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) \le s(W)$, and $s(V(G)) \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ is the value of the singletons. A partition
  of $G/G[W]$ pulls back by (1) to a partition of $V(G)$ with $W$
  inside one part $Z$, at which the deleted edges of $G[W]$ cross
  nothing; so its value is at most $s(V(G)) - s(Z) \le s(V(G)) - s(W)$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}). Adding the three bounds gives
  the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The deficiencies at a body of degree two]
  \label{lem:deficiency-one-body-chain}
  \lean{Graph.not_adj_and_deficiencyMerged_two_add_two_le, Graph.deficiencyMerged_three_add_five_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:deficiency-merged}
  Let $V_1 \subseteq V(G)$ and $x \in V(G) \setminus V_1$, and let
  $a \neq b$ in $V_1$ be joined to $x$ by edges.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $G$ is sparse, then $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent, and
      $\operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \le
      \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$.
    \item If no set of at least two bodies containing $x$ is rigid, then
      $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) + 5 \le
      \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound, lem:deficiency-add-body,
        lem:cut-edge-decomposition}
  (1) An edge $ab$ would give the triangle $\{x, a, b\}$ singleton value
  zero. Let a partition of $V_1$ with $a$ and $b$ together attain the
  merged deficiency, and let $Z$ be the part of $a$ and $b$. Then the
  merged deficiency plus $s(Z)$ is at most $s(V_1) \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}).
  Adding $x$ to $Z$ lowers the singleton value by at least one, as $x$
  has two edges into $Z$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}), and $s(Z \cup
  \{x\}) \ge 1$; so $s(Z) \ge 2$.

  (2) Suppose otherwise, and let $P$ attain the merged deficiency $m$,
  with $Q$ the part of $a$ and $b$. Refining $Q$ by a partition $R$ of
  $Q$, relabelled apart from the other parts of $P$, changes the value
  of $P$ by the value of $R$ in $G[Q]$
  (\cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}). Since
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]) \le m + 4$, every such $R$ has value at
  most $4$ in $G[Q]$, and at most $0$ if it keeps $a$ and $b$ together.
  Then $G[Q \cup \{x\}]$ is rigid (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}): with
  $x$ alone, both of its edges cross and the value drops by $10 - 6 =
  4$, to at most $0$; with $x$ in a part, each edge from $x$ to another
  part costs $5$, and when $a$ and $b$ are apart at least one does. This
  contradicts the hypothesis.

  The informal argument reads the merged deficiency off the partition of
  $G - x$ into its maximal rigid sets; the refinement above needs no such
  partition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A non-adjacent pair outside every rigid set]
  \label{lem:deficiency-two-body-chain}
  \lean{Graph.deficiencyMerged_two_add_two_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:deficiency-merged}
  Let $G$ be sparse, and let $a \neq b$ be bodies of $G$ that are not
  adjacent, with $\operatorname{def}_3(G; a, b) \neq
  \operatorname{def}_3(G)$. Then $\operatorname{def}_2(G; a, b) + 2 \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound, lem:deficiency-add-body,
        lem:deficiency-tight-rigid, lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}
  Otherwise the part $Z$ of $a$ and $b$ in a partition attaining the
  planar merged deficiency has $s(Z) \le 1$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}). The pair $\{a, b\}$ has
  singleton value $3$, as $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}), so $|Z| \ge 3$, and $Z$ is rigid
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-tight-rigid}). Then $\operatorname{def}_3(G; a,
  b) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The structural coverage}
\label{sec:main-component-coverage}

This subsection completes the strong induction on the number of bodies.
Call a graph \emph{smaller than} $G$ if it satisfies the standing
hypotheses (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}) and has fewer bodies than
$G$. One step of the induction is recorded as a single property
(\cref{def:pencil-x0-reduces}): the structural hypotheses of one of the
preceding steps, with a property $\mathcal P$ at every graph that step
consumes. The induction needs only that every graph satisfying the
standing hypotheses reduces to the graphs smaller than it
(\cref{thm:pencil-x0-reduction-attains}).

That every such graph does reduce is a case analysis
(\cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}). A graph with a bridge reduces along a
chain of bridges, and a 2-edge-connected graph with a cut vertex at the
cut vertex (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-cut-reduces}). A 2-connected graph whose
bodies all have degree two is a cycle
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-cycle-reduces}). Where the two deficiencies agree,
the flat step applies (\cref{cor:pencil-jj-flat}); otherwise
$\operatorname{def}_2(G) > 0$, and a graph with a planar-rigid set
reduces by contraction at a maximal one
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-planar-rigid-reduces}). What remains is a
2-connected sparse graph that is not a cycle
(\cref{sec:main-component-sparse}). There a \emph{chain} is a maximal
path of bodies of degree two between bodies of degree at least three
(\cref{def:pencil-x0-chain}), and it is \emph{usable} if one of the ear
or split-off steps applies to it (\cref{def:pencil-x0-usable-chain}).
Either some chain is usable (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-chain-reduces}), or the
graph has a rigid core at which the planar deficiencies add
(\cref{thm:pencil-x0-theorem-s}), where the contraction step at an
additive core applies.

\begin{definition}[One step of the induction]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-reduces}
  \lean{Graph.IsOpenEar, Graph.X0Reduces}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-standing, def:D-deficiency, def:deficiency-merged,
        def:graph-operations, def:rigid-contraction, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $\mathcal P$ be a property of graphs, and let open ears be as in
  \cref{sec:main-component-chain}. The graph $G$ \emph{reduces to
  $\mathcal P$} if one of the following holds.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$.
    \item $G$ has a cut vertex with sides $V_1$ and $V_2$ as in
      \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut}, and $G[V_1]$ and $G[V_2]$ satisfy
      $\mathcal P$.
    \item $G$ has a chain of bridges between $V_1$ and $V_2$ as in
      \cref{thm:pencil-x0-bridge}, and $G[V_1]$ and $G[V_2]$ satisfy
      $\mathcal P$.
    \item $G$ is a cycle, given as an edge $ab$ and a path from $a$ to
      $b$ through its other bodies, as in \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cycle}.
    \item $G$ has an open ear $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on $V_1$,
      $G[V_1]$ satisfies $\mathcal P$, and one of the following holds:
      $k \ge 5$; $k \in \{3, 4\}$ and $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed
      satisfies $\mathcal P$; $k = 2$ and $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])
      \le \operatorname{def}_3(G)$; $k = 2$, $a$ and $b$ are not
      adjacent, $\operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \le
      \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$ and $G$ with $x_2$ suppressed
      satisfies $\mathcal P$; or $k = 1$, $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent,
      $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]) \le \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ and
      $\operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \le
      \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$.
    \item $G$ has an open ear $a - x - b$ on $V_1$ whose ends are not
      adjacent, $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) + 5 \le
      \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$, and $G$ with $x$ suppressed
      satisfies $\mathcal P$.
    \item $G$ is 2-edge-connected, and there are $W \subsetneq V(G)$
      with $|W| \ge 2$, no body outside $W$ adjacent to two bodies of
      $W$, and $r \in W$, such that $G/G[W]$ (collapsing $W$ to $r$)
      satisfies $\mathcal P$ and either $\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) =
      0$, or $\operatorname{def}_3(G[W]) = 0$,
      $\operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) + \operatorname{def}_2(G/G[W]) \le
      \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ and $G[W]$ satisfies $\mathcal P$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Here suppressing an interior body $x_i$ is the splitting-off
  $G_{x_i}^{x_{i-1} x_{i+1}}$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}, with $x_0 =
  a$ and $x_{k+1} = b$) whose new edge carries the label of the edge
  $x_{i-1} x_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The induction on the number of bodies]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-reduction-attains}
  \lean{Graph.X0Reduces.x0Attains, Graph.X0Attains.of_isX0Graph_of_x0Reduces}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces, def:pencil-x0-attains,
        def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and reduce to
      $\mathcal P$. If the general configuration of every graph
      satisfying $\mathcal P$ attains, then the general configuration of
      $G$ attains.
    \item If every graph satisfying the standing hypotheses reduces to
      the graphs smaller than it, then the general configuration of
      every graph satisfying the standing hypotheses attains.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:pencil-jj-flat, thm:pencil-x0-cut, thm:pencil-x0-bridge,
        thm:pencil-x0-cycle, thm:pencil-x0-open-ear,
        thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four, thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-three,
        thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two, thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two-orbit,
        thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-one, thm:pencil-x0-splitoff,
        thm:pencil-x0-contract-rigid, thm:pencil-x0-contract-additive}
  (1) Each clause of \cref{def:pencil-x0-reduces} is the hypothesis of
  one step: the flat step (\cref{cor:pencil-jj-flat}), the cut vertex
  and the chain of bridges (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-bridge}), the cycle (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-cycle}),
  the open ears (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-four},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-three},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-two-orbit},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-open-ear-one}), the split-off
  (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-splitoff}) and the two contractions
  (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-contract-rigid},
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-contract-additive}). Each consumes attainment
  exactly at the graphs the clause requires to satisfy $\mathcal P$.

  (2) Strong induction on the number of bodies, by (1) with $\mathcal P$
  the property of being smaller than $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Chains]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-chain}
  \lean{Graph.IsChain}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces}
  A \emph{chain} of $G$ is an open ear $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on
  $V_1$ with $k \ge 1$ whose interior bodies all have degree two in $G$
  and whose ends have degree at least three.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Every body of degree two lies on a chain]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-chain-exists}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.exists_isChain}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-chain, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with $G - v$ connected for
  every body $v$ and some body of degree other than two. Then every body
  of degree two is an interior body of a chain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Extend a path through the body along bodies of degree two as far as
  possible. An interior body has both of its neighbours on the path, so
  if an end had degree two its other neighbour would either extend the
  path or be the other end. In the second case the path and that edge
  form a cycle joined to the rest of $G$ at one body at most, which is
  then a cut vertex, or the cycle is all of $G$, whose bodies would all
  have degree two. So both ends have degree at least three.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The standing hypotheses at the smaller graphs of a chain]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-chain-standing}
  \lean{Graph.IsChain.isX0Graph_induce, Graph.IsX0Graph.splitOff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-chain, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:graph-operations}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $G - v$ is connected for every body $v$, then $G[V_1]$
      satisfies the standing hypotheses for every chain on $V_1$.
    \item Let a body $v$ have exactly two edges, to distinct bodies $u$
      and $w$ that are not adjacent. Then the splitting-off $G_v^{uw}$,
      whose new edge carries the label of the edge $vu$, satisfies the
      standing hypotheses.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) The ends of the chain keep at least two neighbours in $V_1$ and
  every other body of $V_1$ keeps all of them. A nonempty proper subset
  of $V_1$ left by no edge of $G[V_1]$ would give one of $G - x_1$,
  adding $x_2, \dots, x_k$ to whichever of it and its complement in
  $V_1$ contains $b$.

  (2) The new edge is not parallel to an old one, since $u$ and $w$ are
  not adjacent, and $u$ and $w$ each exchange the edge to $v$ for the
  new edge. A set of bodies left by no edge of $G_v^{uw}$ contains both
  or neither of $u$ and $w$, and then, with $v$ added in the first case,
  it is left by no edge of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A graph of degree two everywhere is a cycle]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-cycle-reduces}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Reduces_of_forall_degree_eq_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with every body of degree
  two. Then $G$ reduces to the graphs smaller than it.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By the cycle clause. Extend a path through a body as far as possible
  along bodies of degree two. As every body has degree two, it stops
  only when its ends are adjacent, and the path with that edge is
  closed under adjacency, hence all of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Graphs that are not 2-connected]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-cut-reduces}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Reduces_of_not_twoEdgeConnected,
    Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Reduces_of_not_connected}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $G$ is not 2-edge-connected, then $G$ reduces to the graphs
      smaller than it.
    \item If $G$ is 2-edge-connected and $G - v$ is disconnected for some
      body $v$, then $G$ reduces to the graphs smaller than it.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) By the clause of a chain of bridges. As $G$ is connected but not
  2-edge-connected, some nonempty proper set $V'$ of bodies is left by
  exactly one edge; its sides are $V'$ and $V(G) \setminus V'$. Grow a
  path from that edge, keeping the two sides: an end of degree two has
  its other edge inside its own side, since that edge is not the one
  leaving $V'$, so the path extends into that side, which loses the
  end. The path stops when both ends have degree at least three. Each
  side is then connected, since its end is its only body with a
  neighbour outside it; the end loses exactly one edge, so it keeps
  degree at least two, and every other body keeps all its neighbours.

  (2) By the cut-vertex clause at $v$. As $G - v$ is disconnected, it
  has a nonempty set $C$ of bodies, closed under adjacency in $G - v$
  and not all of its bodies; the sides are $C \cup \{v\}$ and
  $V(G) \setminus C$. The body $v$ sends at least two edges into each
  side, since a single one would be a bridge, and every other body
  keeps all its neighbours. Each side is connected, since $v$ is its
  only body with a neighbour outside it.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Usable chains]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-usable-chain}
  \lean{Graph.ChainUsable}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-chain, def:deficiency-merged}
  A chain $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$ on $V_1$ is \emph{usable} if
  $k \ge 3$; or $k = 2$ and either $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) =
  \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$, or $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent and
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1]; a, b) + 2 \le
  \operatorname{def}_2(G[V_1])$; or $k = 1$ and either
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) = \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$ or
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) + 5 \le
  \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A usable chain gives a reduction]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-chain-reduces}
  \lean{Graph.IsChain.x0Reduces_of_chainUsable}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-usable-chain, def:pencil-x0-reduces}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and be sparse, with $G - v$
  connected for every body $v$. If some chain of $G$ is usable, then $G$
  reduces to the graphs smaller than it.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-chain-standing, lem:deficiency-ear-merge,
        lem:deficiency-one-body-chain}
  By the ear clause or the split-off clause at the usable chain, on
  $V_1$. $G[V_1]$ satisfies the standing hypotheses, and so do the graphs
  with an interior body suppressed (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-chain-standing}):
  the two neighbours of the suppressed body are not adjacent, because
  one of them is an interior body or, when $k = 1$, by sparsity
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-one-body-chain}). Where
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]; a, b) = \operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$,
  some partition attaining $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1])$ has $a$ and
  $b$ in one part, so $\operatorname{def}_3(G[V_1]) \le
  \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear-merge}). At $k = 1$
  sparsity also gives the planar bound of the ear step
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-one-body-chain}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Contraction at a maximal planar-rigid set]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-planar-rigid-reduces}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Reduces_of_deficiency_two_rigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses and be 2-edge-connected, with
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) > 0$ and a planar-rigid set. Then $G$
  reduces to the graphs smaller than it.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-add-body, lem:pencil-contract-standing}
  By the contraction clause at a maximal planar-rigid set $W$, which is
  proper, since $\operatorname{def}_2(G) > 0$. A body outside $W$ with
  two neighbours in $W$ would enlarge it (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body},
  at $n = 2$), so no such body exists, and $G/G[W]$ is smaller than $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Counting bodies of degree two]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-sparse-count}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.exists_degree_eq_two_notMem,
        Graph.IsX0Graph.partitionDef_three_induce_diff_nonpos}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-standing, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item If $G$ is sparse, then at least four bodies of $G$ have degree
      two.
    \item If no two bodies of degree two are adjacent, then
      $s_6(V(G) \setminus \{x\}) \le 0$ for every body $x$ of degree
      two.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) Sparsity at $V(G)$ gives $2|E(G)| \le 3|V(G)| - 4$, so the sum of
  $3 - \deg v$ over the bodies is at least $4$; each term is at most
  one, and positive only at degree two.

  (2) With $d$ bodies of degree two, their $2d$ edges are distinct, so
  $|E(G)| \ge 2d$, while $2|E(G)| \ge 2d + 3(|V(G)| - d)$. Hence
  $5|E(G)| \ge 6|V(G)|$, and removing $x$ removes one body and two
  edges.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A core when no chain is usable]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-theorem-s}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.exists_additiveCore}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-usable-chain, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:rigid-contraction}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, with $G - v$ connected for
  every body $v$, some body of degree other than two, and no
  planar-rigid set. If no chain of $G$ is usable, then there are $W
  \subsetneq V(G)$ with $|W| \ge 2$ and $\operatorname{def}_3(G[W]) =
  0$, with no body outside $W$ adjacent to two bodies of $W$, and $r \in
  W$ such that
  \[
    \operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) + \operatorname{def}_2(G/G[W]) \;\le\;
    \operatorname{def}_2(G).
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-sparse, lem:pencil-x0-sparse-count,
        lem:pencil-x0-chain-exists, lem:deficiency-one-body-chain,
        lem:deficiency-two-body-chain, lem:deficiency-tight-rigid,
        lem:deficiency-add-body, lem:deficiency-merge-rigid,
        lem:deficiency-core-bound, lem:deficiency-additive-core}
  $G$ is sparse (\cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}), so it has bodies of
  degree two (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-sparse-count}), each on a chain
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-chain-exists}), and every chain has at most two
  interior bodies. A chain with two has adjacent ends, since otherwise
  it would be usable (\cref{lem:deficiency-two-body-chain}), and then
  the chain and its ends form a 4-cycle, which is tight and so rigid
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-tight-rigid}). The body of a chain with one lies
  in a rigid set (\cref{lem:deficiency-one-body-chain}).

  If $\operatorname{def}_3(G) > 0$, one chain gives a rigid set, and a
  maximal rigid set $W$ containing it is proper. A body outside $W$ has
  at most one neighbour in $W$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}), and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-core-bound} with $X_0$ empty and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-additive-core} give the inequality.

  If $\operatorname{def}_3(G) = 0$, take a body $x$ of degree two and a
  rigid set avoiding it. If two bodies of degree two are adjacent, they
  lie on a chain with two interior bodies, whose 4-cycle is rigid, and
  since at least four bodies have degree two
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-sparse-count}), one of them, $x$, lies off it.
  Otherwise take any such $x$ and a rigid set in $V(G) \setminus \{x\}$,
  which exists since $s_6(V(G) \setminus \{x\}) \le 0$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-sparse-count}, \cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}).
  Let $W$ be a maximal rigid set in $V(G) \setminus \{x\}$ containing
  the rigid set taken. The body $x$ has at most one
  neighbour in $W$: a neighbour of degree two would have only one
  neighbour in $W$, too few for a rigid set; and two neighbours of
  degree at least three would be the ends of a chain $a - x - b$ whose
  merged deficiency equals its deficiency
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}), which would be usable. Every
  other body outside $W$ has at most one neighbour in $W$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body}), and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-core-bound} with $X_0 = \{x\}$ and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-additive-core} give the inequality.

  The informal statement adds $1 \le \operatorname{def}_2(G[W]) <
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, which the contraction step does not need,
  and excludes $\theta$-graphs, which the proof above does not use.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The structural coverage]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Reduces, Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Attains,
        Graph.X0Attains.of_twoEdgeConnected}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-reduces, def:pencil-x0-standing,
        def:pencil-x0-attains, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  Every graph satisfying the standing hypotheses reduces to the graphs
  smaller than it. Consequently, for $K$ infinite, the general
  configuration of every graph satisfying the standing hypotheses
  attains, and in particular that of every simple 2-edge-connected graph
  on at least three bodies.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-x0-reduction-attains, lem:pencil-x0-cut-reduces,
        lem:pencil-x0-cycle-reduces, lem:pencil-x0-planar-rigid-reduces,
        lem:pencil-x0-chain-exists, lem:pencil-x0-chain-reduces,
        thm:pencil-x0-theorem-s, lem:deficiency-sparse,
        lem:deficiency-antitone, lem:pencil-contract-standing,
        lem:pencil-contract-standing-rigid}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses. If $G$ is not
  2-edge-connected, or has a cut vertex, it reduces by
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-cut-reduces}; if all of its bodies have degree
  two, by \cref{lem:pencil-x0-cycle-reduces}. If
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ it reduces by the
  flat clause; otherwise $\operatorname{def}_2(G) > 0$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-antitone}), and a planar-rigid set gives a
  reduction by \cref{lem:pencil-x0-planar-rigid-reduces}. In the
  remaining case $G$ is sparse (\cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}). A usable
  chain gives a reduction (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-chain-reduces}); if there
  is none, \cref{thm:pencil-x0-theorem-s} gives a core $W$, at which
  $G[W]$ and $G/G[W]$ are smaller than $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing-rigid},
  \cref{lem:pencil-contract-standing}), so $G$ reduces by the
  contraction clause at $W$. The rest is
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-reduction-attains}; a simple 2-edge-connected
  graph on at least three bodies is connected with minimum degree at
  least two (\cref{def:cut-edges-2ec}).

  The informal case analysis sets $\theta$-graphs apart; here they fall
  under the cases above (\cref{rem:pencil-x0-theta}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two main-component statements}
\label{sec:main-component-statements}

This subsection proves the two main-component statements
(\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}), the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:pencil-conditional-realization-main-component}. The distinct
statement follows from the covering theorem directly: one attaining
configuration is an adjacent-distinct realization. The generic statement
does not follow fibre by fibre. At a graph with two hubs and three common
neighbours, $K_{2,3}$ the smallest, no configuration of the main component
over a general picture is nondegenerate
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-two-hubs-obstruction}), and the generic realization
lies on another component. It is proved inside the induction of the pencil
reduction (\cref{thm:pencil-reduction}), whose hypothesis is the
conditioned pair at every smaller graph. At a graph with no planar-rigid
set of two or more bodies, the general configuration is nondegenerate
(\cref{thm:pencil-x0-base-generic}). Otherwise a minimal planar-rigid set
gives a body of degree two at hub ends across which the
deficiency does not drop, or a pendant triangle
(\cref{lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear}), and the generic realization of the
smaller graph extends across it (\cref{lem:pencil-generic-one-ear},
\cref{lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle}). The informal argument runs a
separate induction over every graph satisfying the standing hypotheses; run
inside the pencil reduction, the graphs that are not two-edge-connected are
the cut-edge case (\cref{lem:pencil-cut-case}), and the pendant triangle at
a body of degree three, which needs a bridge, never arises.

\begin{lemma}[The distinct main-component statement]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.x0Dist}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-component-statements, def:pencil-distinct-motive}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field. Every simple two-edge-connected
  multigraph on at least three bodies has a pencil realization at the
  deficiency rank with adjacent concurrency points distinct: the distinct
  main-component statement (\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements})
  holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-x0-coverage, lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct,
        def:pencil-x0-standing, def:cut-edges-2ec}
  A simple two-edge-connected multigraph on at least three bodies is
  connected with minimum degree at least two (\cref{def:cut-edges-2ec}),
  so it satisfies the standing hypotheses (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing})
  and its general configuration attains (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}).
  One attaining configuration is such a realization
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}). No hypothesis on the number of
  edge labels is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Feasibility bounds the hub neighbourhoods]
  \label{lem:pencil-feasible-hub-conditions}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PencilNondegFeasible.hub_conditions}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate}
  If a finite multigraph is nondegeneracy-feasible
  (\cref{def:pencil-nondegenerate}), then every closed
  hub-neighbourhood has at most three members, and no triangle contains
  two pencil hubs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  At a nondegenerate realization the normals of a closed
  hub-neighbourhood of $v$ are independent and all orthogonal to the
  nonzero point of $v$, so there are at most three of them. At a triangle
  with two hubs $y, z$, their normals are independent and the three
  triangle points lie in their two-dimensional common orthogonal
  complement; the third body's conjunct (independent points if it is not a
  hub, a third independent normal if it is) cannot hold there.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three bodies carry no proper rigid subgraph]
  \label{lem:pencil-three-bodies-no-rigid}
  \lean{Graph.noRigid_of_simple_of_ncard_eq_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigid-subgraph}
  A simple multigraph on exactly three bodies has no proper rigid
  subgraph (\cref{def:rigid-subgraph}) at $n = 3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A proper rigid subgraph has exactly two bodies, and a rigid graph on
  two bodies has at least two edges across the cut separating them, which
  simplicity forbids.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two hubs with three common neighbours]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-two-hubs-obstruction}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.not_isNondegPencilRealization_of_two_hubs_three_common,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.not_isNondeg_pencilConfigPoint_of_two_hubs_three_common}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate, def:pencil-configuration}
  Let $a \ne b$ be pencil hubs of $G$ with three common neighbours $x, y,
  z$. No pencil realization at which the points of $x$, $y$ and $z$ are
  linearly independent is nondegenerate. In particular, over a planar
  picture at which $q_x, q_y, q_z$ are not collinear, no height gives a
  nondegenerate realization with the configuration points.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  At a nondegenerate realization the normals of $a$ and $b$ are
  independent, both lying in the closed hub-neighbourhood of $x$. Both
  annihilate the points of $x$, $y$ and $z$, through the cross-incidence
  along each of the six links, so three independent points would lie in a
  two-dimensional space. The configuration points over non-collinear
  picture points are independent, whatever the heights.
\end{proof}

A polynomial witnessing that the general configuration attains
(\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}) stays a witness when it is multiplied by the
determinant of the homogeneous picture points of $x$, $y$ and $z$, nonzero at
the picture $(0,0), (1,0), (0,1)$, so its good pictures can all be taken with
the three points non-collinear. At $K_{2,3}$, which is
nondegeneracy-feasible, every main picture is of that kind, since collinear
degree-two bodies enlarge the lifting space.

\begin{definition}[A new body joined to two bodies]
  \label{def:pencil-two-ear-graph}
  \lean{Graph.addTwoEar}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}
  For distinct bodies $u, w$ of $G$, the graph $G_{uw}$ is $G$ together
  with one new body $x$ and two new edges, joining $x$ to $u$ and to $w$.
  Its bodies and edges lie in larger types than $G$'s, as the edges of
  \cref{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges} do, so no label of $G$ is used.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The planes of two bodies separate without a planar-rigid set]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-planes-separate}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_planes_separate}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and $G$ simple, with at least three members in
  every closed neighbourhood, and with $\operatorname{def}_2(G[Y]) \ne 0$
  for every set $Y$ of at least two bodies. Let $u \ne w$ be bodies of
  $G$. There is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such
  that every picture $q$ with $P(q) \ne 0$ has some height $z \in L(q)$
  and, at every body, an affine function carrying $z$ on its closed
  neighbourhood, with the functions at $u$ and $w$ different.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-two-ear-graph, thm:pencil-jj-equality,
        lem:deficiency-sparse, lem:deficiency-singleton-bound}
  A height of $G$ whose planes at $u$ and $w$ agree extends to $G_{uw}$
  (\cref{def:pencil-two-ear-graph}), with the new body on that common
  plane, injectively. For a partition of the bodies of $G_{uw}$, the new
  body alone costs $1$, in a part other than those of $u$ and $w$ costs
  $4$, and in the part of $u$ costs $2$ when $w$ lies elsewhere. In the
  remaining case $u$ and $w$ share a part $X$ of at least two bodies, and
  refining $X$ into singletons raises the value by the singleton value of
  $X$, at least one by \cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}. So
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G_{uw}) \le \operatorname{def}_2(G) - 1$.
  Jackson and Jord\'an's equality (\cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}) at
  $G_{uw}$ and at $G$, at a picture general for both, gives that the
  heights with equal planes at $u$ and $w$ form a subspace of dimension at
  most $2 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, less than
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Fixing the new body's picture
  at one non-root of the polynomial of $G_{uw}$ turns that polynomial into
  a nonzero polynomial in the picture coordinates of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The hub normals of a configuration]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-conjunct-three}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.isNondeg_pencilConfig_of_planeDiff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate, def:pencil-configuration,
        def:pencil-admissible-picture, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph with every closed hub-neighbourhood of at
  most three members. Let $q$ be an admissible picture at which no hub
  and two of its hub neighbours have collinear picture points, and let
  $z \in L(q)$. Suppose that the planes of the two ends of every edge
  joining two hubs differ, and that the planes of any two hubs adjacent to
  one body that is not a hub differ. Then the configuration $(q, z)$, with
  the plane normal of an admissible triple at every body, is a
  nondegenerate pencil realization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization, lem:pencil-condition-linear}
  The configuration is a pencil realization with adjacent points distinct
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}). A non-hub's closed
  neighbourhood has at most three members, and admissibility puts three
  with non-collinear picture points in it, so its points are independent.
  Each normal is a nonzero multiple of $(h_0, h_1, -1, h_2)$ for the plane
  $z = h_0 x + h_1 y + h_2$ (\cref{lem:pencil-condition-linear}), so
  independence of normals is affine independence of planes. At a hub $v$
  with one hub neighbour this is the first condition. With two, $w_1$ and
  $w_2$, the difference $d_i$ of the planes of $w_i$ and $v$ is nonzero
  and vanishes at $q_v$ and $q_{w_i}$, so $d_2$ does not vanish at
  $q_{w_1}$, nor $d_1$ at $q_{w_2}$, since the three points are not
  collinear; evaluating a relation between $d_1$ and $d_2$ at $q_{w_1}$
  and at $q_{w_2}$ shows the two differences independent. The closed
  hub-neighbourhood of a body that is not a hub consists of at most two
  of its neighbours, and two is the second condition. Neither the
  standing hypotheses nor nondegeneracy-feasibility is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The general configuration is nondegenerate without a planar-rigid set]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-base-generic}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_forall_deficiency_two_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-generic-motive, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses, be
  nondegeneracy-feasible, and have $\operatorname{def}_2(G[Y]) \ne 0$ for
  every set $Y$ of at least two bodies. Then $G$ has a generic pencil
  realization (\cref{def:pencil-generic-motive}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-x0-coverage, lem:pencil-x0-planes-separate,
        lem:pencil-x0-conjunct-three, lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-feasible-hub-conditions, lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  Every closed hub-neighbourhood has at most three members
  (\cref{lem:pencil-feasible-hub-conditions}). Take a picture $q$ off the
  zero sets of the attaining polynomial of \cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage},
  which makes $q$ admissible, of the determinant of the homogeneous
  picture points of every three distinct bodies, and of the polynomial of
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-planes-separate} at every two distinct bodies. Over
  $q$, on the heights with their planes, the attaining heights contain the
  non-roots of one polynomial that has a non-root, and for each pair some
  coordinate of the difference of the two planes is nonzero at the point
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-planes-separate} gives. One point is a non-root of
  all of them, by induction on their number from
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}, which meets two.
  \Cref{lem:pencil-x0-conjunct-three} applies there, and the attaining
  polynomial gives the rank (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Steering a realization of a subgraph]
  \label{lem:pencil-generic-steer}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_pencilSeed_of_nondeg_of_selectors,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_coord_linearIndependent_pencilChartPoint_of_other_nonhub,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_isNondegPencilRealization_restrict_of_demoted,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_isNondegPencilRealization_steer}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate, def:pencil-generic-motive}
  Let $K$ be infinite.
  \textup{(a)} Let $G$ be simple and nondegeneracy-feasible and $H$ a
  subgraph of $G$. Suppose that at every pencil hub $h$ of $G$ that is a
  body but not a hub of $H$, every neighbour of $h$ in $G$ that is not its
  neighbour in $H$ is not a hub of $G$. Then $G$ has a nondegenerate
  realization whose restriction to $H$ is nondegenerate; so $H$ is
  nondegeneracy-feasible, with normals independent on the closed
  hub-neighbourhood in $G$ of every body of $G$.
  \textup{(b)} Let $H$ be loopless with at least one body, with a generic
  pencil realization and a nondegenerate realization with normals $n'$ and
  points $p'$. Let $S_1, \dots, S_k \subseteq V(H)$ be sets on which $n'$
  is independent, and $(u_1, w_1), \dots, (u_m, w_m)$ pairs of bodies of
  $H$ with $p'_{u_j} \cdot n'_{w_j} \neq 0$, where every member of every
  $S_i$, and every $w_j$, is a hub of $H$ or has exactly three closed
  neighbours in $H$. Then $H$ has a generic pencil realization whose
  normals are independent on every $S_i$, with $p_{u_j} \cdot n_{w_j} \neq
  0$ for every $j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Points and hub normals are polynomial in the coordinates of a chart, and
  every nondegenerate realization is a chart point up to per-body scalars,
  for every correct choice of selectors: at each body the selected normals,
  or at a body that is not a hub the selected points of its closed
  neighbours, are
  independent and orthogonal to its point, or normal, and completing them
  to a basis of that orthogonal complement fills the free slots.

  \textup{(a)} Reseed a nondegenerate realization of $G$, and choose one
  chart point that also makes independent the points of the closed
  neighbourhood in $H$ of every hub $h$ of $G$ that stops being a hub. If
  $h$ has two neighbours $u$, $w$ in $H$, this holds at a chart point built
  from standard basis vectors, since the other neighbours of $h$ are not
  hubs; with at most one, the closed neighbourhood is a body or an edge of
  $G$, independent already. Restriction keeps every condition except the
  point condition at those hubs, which is the chosen one; the normal
  condition is $G$'s.

  \textup{(b)} Reseed the generic realization, and the second one in the
  same chart. The rank at the first, and each condition at the second, are
  nonzero polynomials in the chart coordinates, and a common non-root gives
  the realization, at the rank since the target bounds the rank at every
  realization (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). The conditions read normals only at hubs and at bodies
  with three closed neighbours, whose normals do not depend on the free
  slots filled after that choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The generic realization across a one-body ear]
  \label{lem:pencil-generic-one-ear}
  \lean{Graph.IsOpenEar.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_isNondegPencilRealization,
        Graph.IsOpenEar.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_one}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-generic-motive, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and $G$ simple and nondegeneracy-feasible, with a
  body $x$ of degree two whose neighbours $a$ and $b$ are pencil hubs, and
  with $\operatorname{def}_3(G - x) \le \operatorname{def}_3(G)$. If $G - x$
  has a generic pencil realization whenever it is nondegeneracy-feasible,
  then $G$ has a generic pencil realization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-generic-steer, lem:block-rank-ear,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  An end that stops being a hub in $G - x$ loses only the neighbour $x$,
  which is not a hub, so \cref{lem:pencil-generic-steer}(a) applies with
  $H = G - x$: $G$ has a nondegenerate realization whose restriction is one
  of $G - x$, and $G - x$ has a generic realization. At the realization of
  $G$, $n_a$ and $n_b$ are independent, since $a$ and $b$ lie in the closed
  hub-neighbourhood of $x$, and not both $p_a \perp n_b$ and
  $p_b \perp n_a$: otherwise the independent points of $x$, $a$, $b$ would
  lie in the two-dimensional common orthogonal complement of $n_a$ and
  $n_b$. Such an end keeps exactly two neighbours, so it has three closed
  neighbours in $G - x$, and \cref{lem:pencil-generic-steer}(b), with the
  closed hub-neighbourhoods in $G$ of the bodies of $G - x$ and $\{a, b\}$
  as the sets and whichever of $p_a \cdot n_b$, $p_b \cdot n_a$ is nonzero,
  gives a generic realization of $G - x$ with the same three properties.

  Then $p_a, p_b$ are independent and the line $n_a^\perp \cap n_b^\perp$
  is not the line $p_a p_b$, so a point $p_x$ on the first off the second
  gives a nondegenerate realization of $G$, the new hinges $p_a \wedge p_x$
  and $p_x \wedge p_b$ independent. By the rank addition along an open ear
  (\cref{lem:block-rank-ear}) the rank rises by at least six, which is at
  least $G$'s target by the deficiency hypothesis; the target bounds the
  rank at every realization (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The generic realization across a pendant triangle]
  \label{lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_closedEar_two,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_closedEar_two_of_isNondegPencilRealization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-generic-motive}
  Let $K$ be infinite and $G$ simple and nondegeneracy-feasible, with two
  adjacent bodies $x_1, x_2$ of degree two, both joined to a body $c$ of
  degree at least four. If $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$ has a generic pencil
  realization whenever it is nondegeneracy-feasible, then $G$ has a
  generic pencil realization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-generic-steer, lem:block-rank-cut-vertex,
        lem:cycle-realization-rigid, lem:deficiency-cut-vertex,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Only $c$ can stop being a hub in $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$; it then has degree
  four, loses the two neighbours $x_1$, $x_2$, which are not hubs, and has
  three closed neighbours there. So \cref{lem:pencil-generic-steer}(a)
  makes $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$ nondegeneracy-feasible, with normals independent
  on the closed hub-neighbourhood in $G$ of each of its bodies, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-generic-steer}(b), with those sets, gives a generic
  realization of $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$ with the same property. Put $p_{x_1}$,
  $p_{x_2}$ in the plane of $c$ with $p_c, p_{x_1}, p_{x_2}$ independent,
  and give $x_1$ and $x_2$ the plane of $c$. This is a nondegenerate
  realization of $G$: the closed hub-neighbourhood in $G$ of a body of
  $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$ contains neither $x_i$, which are not hubs, and that of
  $x_i$ is $\{c\}$; the closed neighbourhood of $x_i$ carries the three
  independent points; and a body of $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$ that is not a hub of
  $G$ is not $c$, so its closed neighbourhood is unchanged and it is not a
  hub of $G - \{x_1, x_2\}$.

  The triangle's three hinges are three of the six joins of a basis, hence
  independent, so the triangle is rigid (\cref{lem:cycle-realization-rigid})
  of rank twelve, and ranks add at the cut vertex $c$
  (\cref{lem:block-rank-cut-vertex}). The deficiencies add at $c$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-cut-vertex}), the triangle's being nonnegative, so
  the rank reaches $G$'s target, which bounds it
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A minimal planar-rigid set has a good ear]
  \label{lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.exists_oneEar_or_pendantTriangle, Graph.exists_eq_triple_of_minimal,
        Graph.exists_closedEar_two_of_triangle}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-standing, def:D-deficiency, def:cut-edges-2ec,
        def:pencil-nondegenerate}
  Let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing})
  and be two-edge-connected on at least four bodies, with every closed
  hub-neighbourhood of at most three members and no triangle containing
  two pencil hubs, and suppose $\operatorname{def}_2(G[Y]) = 0$ for some
  set $Y$ of at least two bodies. Then $G$ has a body $y$ of degree two
  whose neighbours are pencil hubs with $\operatorname{def}_3(G - y) \le
  \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, or two adjacent bodies of degree two with a
  common neighbour of degree at least four.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:deficiency-sparse, lem:deficiency-add-body,
        lem:deficiency-tight-rigid, lem:deficiency-merge-rigid,
        lem:deficiency-ear-merge}
  Take a planar-rigid set $W_0$ with as few bodies as possible. Then
  $s(W_0) \le 0$, every $Z \subsetneq W_0$ with $|Z| \ge 2$ has
  $s(Z) \ge 1$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-sparse}), and $|W_0| \ge 3$, since
  two bodies of a simple graph have singleton value at least one. Adding a
  body $u$ to a set changes its singleton value by $3 - 2t$, with $t$ the
  number of edges from $u$ into the set (\cref{lem:deficiency-add-body});
  so every body of $W_0$ has at least two neighbours in $W_0$.

  Suppose two adjacent bodies $y$ and $u$ of $W_0$ each have at most one
  neighbour in $Z = W_0 \setminus \{y, u\}$. Putting $y$ and then $u$ back
  into $Z$ does not lower the singleton value: $y$ adds at least $3 - 2$,
  and $u$, with at most two edges into $Z \cup \{y\}$, at least $3 - 4$. So
  $s(Z) \le s(W_0) \le 0$ and $|Z| \le 1$. Hence $W_0 = \{y, u, w\}$, and
  $s(W_0) \le 0$ makes it a triangle. If every body of $W_0$ is a hub,
  each has at most two hub neighbours, its closed hub-neighbourhood having
  at most three members, so this applies at any edge of $G[W_0]$ and gives
  a triangle of hubs, which is excluded. So some $y \in W_0$ has degree
  two, and both of its neighbours $a$ and $b$ lie in $W_0$.

  If $a$, say, is not a hub, it has degree two too, and the same argument
  at the edge $ya$ gives a pendant triangle $\{y, a, w\}$. Its third body
  $w$ has degree at least four: at least two edges cross the triangle
  (\cref{def:cut-edges-2ec}), and all of them are at $w$. If $a$ and $b$
  are hubs, $X = W_0 \setminus \{y\}$ has $s(X) \le s(W_0) + 1 \le 1$, and
  $|X| \ge 3$, since $X = \{a, b\}$ with $s(X) \le 1$ would need the edge
  $ab$, and $\{y, a, b\}$ would be a triangle with two hubs. So $X$ is rigid
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-tight-rigid}). Then $a$ and $b$ lie in the rigid set
  $X$ of $G - y$, so $\operatorname{def}_3(G - y; a, b) =
  \operatorname{def}_3(G - y)$ (\cref{lem:deficiency-merge-rigid}), and $y$
  joins the part of $a$ and $b$ without changing the value
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-ear-merge}), which gives
  $\operatorname{def}_3(G - y) \le \operatorname{def}_3(G)$.

  The informal argument settles the case of hubs by $G[W_0]$ being a cycle,
  walks the maximal chain of bodies of degree two through $y$, and obtains
  the rigidity of $W_0 \setminus \{y\}$ from its connectivity and its
  bridges. The count at one edge above needs no cycle and no chain, the
  tight-set lemma needs no bridge, and the minimality of $W_0$ enters only
  through the singleton values of its proper subsets.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The generic conjunct from the induction hypothesis]
  \label{thm:pencil-generic-step}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.hasGenericPencilRealization_of_IH}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-generic-motive, def:pencil-conditioned-pair}
  Let $K$ be infinite and $G$ simple and two-edge-connected on at least
  three bodies, nondegeneracy-feasible, and suppose every multigraph on
  fewer bodies, at least one, satisfies the conditioned pair at $n = 3$
  (\cref{def:pencil-conditioned-pair}). Then $G$ has a generic pencil
  realization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-standing, thm:pencil-x0-base-generic,
        lem:pencil-three-bodies-no-rigid,
        lem:pencil-feasible-hub-conditions, lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear,
        lem:pencil-generic-one-ear, lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle}
  $G$ satisfies the standing hypotheses. Without a planar-rigid set of at
  least two bodies, \cref{thm:pencil-x0-base-generic} applies. On three
  bodies $G$ is a triangle, with no proper rigid subgraph
  (\cref{lem:pencil-three-bodies-no-rigid}), and the direct construction
  on three bodies that the split case of
  \cref{thm:pencil-conditional-realization-pair} uses applies. Otherwise
  \cref{lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear} applies, with
  \cref{lem:pencil-feasible-hub-conditions}, and gives a body of degree two
  or a pendant triangle. The graph with it removed is simple, nonempty and
  smaller, so the hypothesis gives its conditioned pair, whose generic
  conjunct \cref{lem:pencil-generic-one-ear} or
  \cref{lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle} extends to $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The conditioned pair at every nonempty multigraph]
  \label{thm:pencil-conditioned-pair-nonempty}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilPair_of_nonempty,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilPair_of_IH}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-conditioned-pair}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field. Every multigraph with at least one body
  satisfies the conditioned pair at $n = 3$
  (\cref{def:pencil-conditioned-pair}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-reduction, thm:pencil-generic-step,
        lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement, lem:pencil-cut-case,
        lem:pencil-nonsimple-case}
  Apply \cref{thm:pencil-reduction}, with the loop, base and cut-edge cases
  as in \cref{thm:pencil-conditional-realization-pair}. The contraction and
  split cases take one argument: at a loopless multigraph on at least three
  bodies, the cut-edge case (\cref{lem:pencil-cut-case}) if it is not
  two-edge-connected; if it is two-edge-connected and simple,
  \cref{thm:pencil-generic-step} and \cref{lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement}
  give the generic and distinct conjuncts and the bare one follows; if it
  is not simple, \cref{lem:pencil-nonsimple-case}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The general point of the main component attains]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-generic-attains}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph.x0Attains, CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.x0Dist,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.x0Gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-distinct-motive,
        def:pencil-generic-motive, def:D-deficiency,
        def:pencil-main-component-statements}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field. For every simple connected multigraph $G$
  of minimum degree at least two, the general configuration of $G$ attains
  the rank $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}; \cref{def:D-deficiency}, at $n = 3$).
  The two main-component statements
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}) hold: every simple
  two-edge-connected multigraph on at least three bodies has a pencil
  realization at the deficiency rank with adjacent points distinct
  (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive}), and, wherever a nondegenerate
  realization exists at all, a generic one
  (\cref{def:pencil-generic-motive}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-x0-coverage, lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement,
        lem:pencil-x0-two-hubs-obstruction,
        thm:pencil-conditioned-pair-nonempty}
  The first claim is \cref{thm:pencil-x0-coverage}. Granting it, the
  distinct statement needs one attaining configuration
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-distinct-statement}). The generic statement does
  not follow fibre by fibre: at a graph with two hubs and three common
  neighbours, no configuration over a picture where the three are not
  collinear is nondegenerate (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-two-hubs-obstruction}).
  It is proved inside the induction of \cref{thm:pencil-reduction}
  instead (\cref{thm:pencil-generic-step}). At a graph with no
  planar-rigid set of two or more bodies the general configuration is
  nondegenerate (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-base-generic}). Otherwise a minimal
  planar-rigid set gives a one-body ear at hub ends across which the
  deficiency does not drop, or a pendant triangle
  (\cref{lem:pencil-rigid-good-ear}), and the generic realization of the
  smaller graph extends across it (\cref{lem:pencil-generic-one-ear},
  \cref{lem:pencil-generic-pendant-triangle}). So every simple
  two-edge-connected graph satisfies the conditioned pair
  (\cref{thm:pencil-conditioned-pair-nonempty}), whose generic conjunct
  is the generic statement.
\end{proof}
