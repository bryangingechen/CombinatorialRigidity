\section{The main component of the pencil configuration space}
\label{sec:main-component}

\Cref{sec:pencil-main-component-route} reduced the hinge-pencil question
to the two main-component statements
(\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}): every simple
two-edge-connected multigraph has a pencil realization at the deficiency
rank with adjacent concurrency points distinct, and a generic one
whenever a nondegenerate realization exists at all. This section opens
the geometry those statements are proved from. Over a fixed planar
drawing of the bodies, the pencil realizations of a graph form a linear
space of \emph{heights}; the drawings for which that space is smallest
form a dense open set, over which the realizations assemble into an
irreducible family whose general member attains the deficiency rank. A
single realization at that rank forces the general one, because the
rank is lower semicontinuous. The first subsection introduces these
objects and proves that one-witness principle; the second computes the
rank of a flat configuration exactly; the third derives from the planar
rank theorem the equality of Jackson and Jord\'an on which the remaining
steps rest. The ear, split-off, contraction and cut steps and the
structural coverage build on them; the last subsection states the
passage back to the two statements.

\subsection{Planar pictures, the lifting space, and the main component}
\label{sec:main-component-carrier}

Throughout, $G$ is a finite simple connected multigraph of minimum
degree at least two and $K$ is an infinite field. This subsection
introduces planar pictures and their lifting spaces, reads every
configuration over an admissible picture as a pencil realization, and
proves that one configuration at the deficiency rank over a main
picture forces the general one (\cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}).

\begin{definition}[Admissible planar picture]
  \label{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-nondegenerate}
  A \emph{planar picture} of $G$ is a map $q \colon V(G) \to K^2$,
  written $q_v = (x_v, y_v)$; its \emph{homogeneous picture points} are
  the vectors $(x_v, y_v, 1) \in K^3$. The picture is \emph{admissible}
  if $q_u \neq q_v$ for every edge $uv$ and, for every body $v$, three
  members of the closed neighbourhood of $v$
  (\cref{def:pencil-nondegenerate}) have linearly independent homogeneous
  picture points, that is, the picture points over the closed
  neighbourhood are not collinear.
\end{definition}

Admissibility is a finite conjunction of non-vanishing conditions, one
coordinate difference for each edge and one $3 \times 3$ determinant for
each body, so the admissible pictures form a Zariski-open subset of
$K^{2|V(G)|}$. It is nonempty, since $K$ is infinite and every closed
neighbourhood has at least three members by the degree hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[The lifting space]
  \label{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  \lean{Graph.liftingSpace, Graph.affineLifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $q$ be a planar picture of $G$. A \emph{height} is a map
  $z \colon V(G) \to K$. The \emph{lifting space} $L(q)$ is the set of
  heights $z$ such that, for every body $v$, the restriction of $z$ to
  the closed neighbourhood of $v$ agrees with an affine function of the
  picture there: there are $a, b, c \in K$ with
  $z_w = a x_w + b y_w + c$ for every $w$ in that neighbourhood. The
  space $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ of \emph{globally affine} heights is the
  subspace of heights $z_w = \alpha x_w + \beta y_w + \gamma$ for a
  single triple $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) \in K^3$, the same at every
  body.
\end{definition}

The conditions are linear in $z$, so $L(q)$ is a linear subspace of
$K^{V(G)}$.

\begin{lemma}[The lifting space contains the affine heights]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  \lean{Graph.affineLifts_le_liftingSpace, Graph.finrank_affineLifts,
        Graph.three_le_finrank_liftingSpace}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  $\mathrm{Aff}(q) \subseteq L(q)$ at every picture $q$. At an
  admissible picture, provided $G$ has at least one body,
  $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$ is three-dimensional; in particular
  $\dim L(q) \geq 3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A single triple $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ already restricts to an
  affine function of the picture on every closed neighbourhood, giving
  the containment with no hypothesis on $q$. At an admissible picture,
  some body's closed neighbourhood carries three members with linearly
  independent homogeneous picture points, so the coefficients
  $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)$ are pinned down by their values there: the
  linear map from coefficients to heights is injective, hence an
  isomorphism onto its image $\mathrm{Aff}(q)$. The dimension bound
  follows from the containment by monotonicity of dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-lifting-space}
  A height is a function on the whole ambient set of bodies that
  vanishes off $V(G)$, so that for a graph not spanning the body set
  the dimension of $L(q)$ is still that of a space of heights on
  $V(G)$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{definition}[Configurations and their planes]
  \label{def:pencil-configuration}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigPoint,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicture,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicture_ne_zero_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilNormalOfPicturePoly}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  A planar picture $q$ and a height $z$ give the \emph{configuration}
  $p = (q, z)$ of homogeneous points
  $p_v = (x_v, y_v, z_v, 1) \in K^4$. Given a choice of three bodies
  $a_v, b_v, c_v$ at every body $v$, the \emph{plane normal} of $p$ at
  $v$ is the vector $n_v \in K^4$ with
  $n_v \cdot w = \det[p_{a_v}, p_{b_v}, p_{c_v}, w]$ for every
  $w \in K^4$.
\end{definition}

The plane normal is polynomial in the picture coordinates and, at a
fixed picture, in the height coordinates; it is orthogonal to the
three chosen points, and nonzero exactly when they are linearly
independent. When the three chosen bodies lie in the closed
neighbourhood of $v$ with non-collinear picture points and
$z \in L(q)$, it is, up to a nonzero scalar
(\cref{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}), the normal of the
plane containing the points of the whole closed neighbourhood
(\cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}), whichever three are
chosen.

\begin{lemma}[The plane of a selector contains the closed neighbourhood]
  \label{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.dotProduct_pencilNormalOfPicture_eq_zero_of_mem_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-configuration, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ be a picture, $z \in L(q)$, and $v$ a body of $G$ whose
  three chosen bodies $a_v, b_v, c_v$ lie in the closed neighbourhood
  of $v$. Then the plane normal $n_v$ is orthogonal to $p_w$ for every
  $w$ in the closed neighbourhood of $v$, not only the three chosen
  bodies.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Membership in $L(q)$ gives, at $v$, coefficients $h \in K^3$
  expressing every point of the closed neighbourhood as the image,
  under one fixed linear map $K^3 \to K^4$ built from $h$, of its
  homogeneous picture point. Four images under one linear map of four
  vectors of the $3$-dimensional $K^3$ are always linearly dependent,
  so the determinant defining $n_v \cdot p_w$ vanishes for every $w$ in
  the neighbourhood, including the three chosen bodies themselves.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The plane normal is independent of the selector, up to a scalar]
  \label{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_smul_pencilNormalOfPicture_eq_of_mem_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  Let $q$ be admissible, $z \in L(q)$, and $v$ a body of $G$. For two
  choices of three bodies at $v$, each with non-collinear picture
  points and lying in the closed neighbourhood of $v$, the two
  resulting plane normals differ by a nonzero scalar.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Both normals are nonzero, since a plane normal vanishes exactly when
  its three defining points are dependent, and here both triples are
  independent. Fix the second choice's three points: the first normal
  is orthogonal to them by \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}
  (they lie in the closed neighbourhood), and the second normal is
  orthogonal to them by its own defining orthogonality. Those three
  points are independent, so their common perp in $K^4$ is
  one-dimensional; two nonzero vectors of a one-dimensional space are
  scalar multiples of one another.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The pencil condition is linear in the heights]
  \label{lem:pencil-condition-linear}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.mem_liftingSpace_of_coplanar,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.exists_smul_eq_interpolant}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-configuration,
        def:pencil-panel-realization}
  Let $q$ be admissible and $z$ a height. The configuration $p = (q, z)$
  has every closed neighbourhood coplanar if and only if $z \in L(q)$.
  Every such configuration is a pencil realization
  (\cref{def:pencil-panel-realization}): adjacent bodies carry distinct
  points, and each body's plane is unique and non-vertical.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd,
        lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar,
        lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}
  If $z \in L(q)$, admissibility gives at $v$ three closed-neighbourhood
  members with non-collinear picture points, whose plane normal is
  nonzero and, by \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd},
  orthogonal to the whole closed neighbourhood: the neighbourhood is
  coplanar. Conversely, if some nonzero normal annihilates the whole
  closed neighbourhood of $v$, evaluating it at those three non-collinear
  members forces its height coefficient nonzero --- else it would already
  annihilate three independent picture points --- and solving for $z_w$
  exhibits $z$ as an affine function of the picture on the neighbourhood,
  giving $z \in L(q)$.

  Adjacent points are distinct because $q_u \neq q_v$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}). If $z_w = ax_w + by_w
  + c$ on the neighbourhood of $v$, its points lie on the plane
  $\zeta = a\xi + b\eta + c$; by the argument of
  \cref{lem:pencil-selector-independent-scalar}, any nonzero normal of the
  neighbourhood is a nonzero scalar multiple of $(a, b, -1, c)$, so its
  height coefficient is nonzero (the plane is non-vertical) and it is
  determined by $a, b, c$ up to scale (the plane is unique).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Main pictures]
  \label{def:pencil-main-picture}
  \lean{Graph.IsMainPicture}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $\ell_0$ be the least value of $\dim L(q)$ over admissible
  pictures $q$ of $G$. A \emph{main picture} of $G$ is an admissible
  picture $q$ with $\dim L(q) = \ell_0$, and $U$ denotes the set of main
  pictures.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The general configuration attains]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-attains}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-configuration, def:panel-hinge-framework,
        def:rigidity-matrix, def:D-deficiency}
  The \emph{rank} of a configuration $p = (q, z)$ of $G$ is the rank of
  the rigidity matrix (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}) of the panel-hinge
  framework on $G$ whose panel normal at each body $v$ is $p_v$
  (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). The \emph{general configuration of
  $G$ attains} if there is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture
  coordinates such that every picture $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is
  admissible and the heights $z \in L(q)$ at which $(q, z)$ has rank
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency},
  at $n = 3$) contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $L(q)$.
\end{definition}

The hinge of that framework at an edge $uv$ is the meet of the planes
$p_u^{\perp}$ and $p_v^{\perp}$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}),
the image of the line $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the complement
isomorphism, and it is nonzero whenever $q_u \neq q_v$. An invertible
linear map of the screw space applied to every hinge leaves the rank
unchanged (\cref{lem:screw-map-rows}), so the rank of a configuration
is also the rank of the body-hinge framework with hinges
$p_u \wedge p_v$, the framework of a pencil realization. Taking the
plane normals $n_v$ as panel normals instead would not give this rank:
where two adjacent planes coincide the meet is zero, and a zero hinge
allows no relative motion of its two bodies.

\Cref{def:pencil-x0-attains} makes sense over any field, but only over
an infinite field does $P \neq 0$ make the good pictures a nonempty
Zariski-open set: over a finite field a nonzero polynomial can vanish at
every point, and the condition then holds vacuously.

\begin{lemma}[The rank of a configuration is its point-join rank]
  \label{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_span_rigidityRows_pencilConfigFramework,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigFramework,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.pencilConfigFramework_supportExtensor_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:body-hinge-framework, def:extensor,
        lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular, lem:screw-map-rows}
  Let $p = (q, z)$ be a configuration of $G$, and let $F_p$ be the
  body-hinge framework on $G$ (\cref{def:body-hinge-framework}) whose
  hinge at each edge $uv$ is the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  (\cref{def:extensor}). Then the rigidity matrix of $F_p$ has the rank
  of $p$ (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). If $q$ is admissible, every
  hinge of $F_p$ is nonzero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The hinge at $uv$ of the panel-hinge framework defining the rank of
  $p$ is the meet of the planes $p_u^{\perp}$ and $p_v^{\perp}$, the
  image of $p_u \wedge p_v$ under the polarity
  (\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}), an invertible linear map of
  the screw space over every field; by \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} the two
  rigidity matrices have the same rank. Over an admissible picture
  adjacent picture points differ, so the points $(x_u, y_u, z_u, 1)$
  and $(x_v, y_v, z_v, 1)$ are linearly independent and their join is
  nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  A body-hinge framework carries a hinge for every label of the ambient
  set of edge labels, including labels that are not edges of $G$. The
  formal framework $F_p$ carries there one fixed nonzero line, which no
  rigidity row reads, so that every hinge is nonzero as a pencil
  realization requires.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A configuration over an admissible picture is a pencil realization]
  \label{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization}
  \lean{Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.hasDistinctPencilRealization,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.hasPencilPanelRealization_pencilConfigFramework}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd,
        def:pencil-panel-realization, def:pencil-distinct-motive}
  Let $q$ be an admissible picture of $G$, $z \in L(q)$ and
  $p = (q, z)$. Choose at every body $v$ three members of its closed
  neighbourhood with non-collinear picture points, and let $n_v$ be the
  resulting plane normal (\cref{def:pencil-configuration}). Then the
  framework $F_p$ of \cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}, with
  panel normals $n_v$ and concurrency points $p_v$, is a pencil
  realization of $G$ (\cref{def:pencil-panel-realization}) whose
  adjacent points are linearly independent. If moreover $p$ has rank
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, then $G$ has an
  adjacent-distinct pencil realization (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive},
  at $n = 3$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The three chosen configuration points at $v$ are independent, since
  their picture points are, so $n_v \neq 0$. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-selector-plane-contains-nbhd}, $n_v$ is orthogonal
  to $p_w$ for every $w$ in the closed neighbourhood of $v$.

  For an edge $uv$ both $u$ and $v$ lie in the closed neighbourhoods of
  $u$ and of $v$, so $p_u$ and $p_v$ are orthogonal to $n_u$ and to
  $n_v$: the line $p_u \wedge p_v$ lies in both panels, and it passes
  through both points by construction. Taking $w = v$ gives the
  incidence $p_v \cdot n_v = 0$, and $p_v \neq 0$ since its last
  coordinate is $1$. Adjacent points are independent because adjacent
  picture points differ, and every hinge is nonzero
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). The rank of $F_p$ is the
  rank of $p$ by the same lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Scaling and affine shifts of the heights preserve the rank]
  \label{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_smul_add_affineLifts}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank, def:pencil-lifting-space,
        lem:screw-map-rows}
  Let $q$ be a picture of $G$, $z$ a height, $t \in K$ nonzero and
  $a \in \mathrm{Aff}(q)$. Then the configurations $(q, z)$ and
  $(q, tz + a)$ have the same rank (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Write $a_w = \alpha x_w + \beta y_w + \gamma$ on $V(G)$. The linear
  map $(\xi, \eta, \zeta, \omega) \mapsto
  (\xi, \eta, t\zeta + \alpha\xi + \beta\eta + \gamma\omega, \omega)$ of
  $K^4$ is invertible, since $t \neq 0$, and carries the point of
  $(q, z)$ at every body to the point of $(q, tz + a)$ there. It induces
  an invertible linear map of the screw space taking each line
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ to the line through the two image points, so by
  \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} the two frameworks of
  \cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank} have rigidity matrices of the
  same rank, and that lemma converts both to the rank of a
  configuration.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The hinges are affine in the heights]
  \label{rem:pencil-hinge-affine}
  Fix a picture $q$. For an edge $uv$ the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  (\cref{def:extensor}) is an affine function of the height $z$: each of
  its Grassmann coordinates is a $2 \times 2$ minor of the two points
  $(x_u, y_u, z_u, 1)$ and $(x_v, y_v, z_v, 1)$, and the minors reading
  the height coordinate are linear in $z$, the others constant in $z$.
  Consequently the augmented motion matrix at $(q, z)$, whose unknowns
  are a screw $m_v$ for every body and a scalar $\omega_e$ for every
  edge, subject to $m_u - m_v = \omega_e (p_u \wedge p_v)$ at each edge
  $e = uv$, is a picture-only matrix plus one linear in $z$ and supported
  on the $\omega$ columns; when every hinge is nonzero its rank is
  $|E(G)|$ plus the rank of the rigidity matrix of the pencil
  realization. Only one consequence of this is used here, the invariance
  of the rank under scaling and affine shifts of the heights, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-rank-scale-shift} proves it directly.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[One attaining configuration forces the general one]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_exists,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.exists_mvPolynomial,
        Graph.IsAdmissiblePicture.supportExtensor_ne_zero,
        Graph.liftingMatrix, Graph.map_ker_liftingMatrix,
        Graph.finrank_ker_liftingMatrix,
        Matrix.exists_mvPolynomial_section_mulVec_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture, def:pencil-x0-attains,
        def:panel-support-extensor, lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Let $G$ have at least one body, let $q_0$ be a main picture of $G$
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}) and $z_0 \in L(q_0)$, and suppose the
  configuration $(q_0, z_0)$ has rank at least
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). Then the general configuration of $G$
  attains.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Admissibility is the non-vanishing of finitely many polynomials in the
  picture, one coordinate difference for each pair of adjacent bodies and
  one $3 \times 3$ determinant for each body, so some polynomial nonzero
  at $q_0$ has only admissible non-roots. Next, $L(q)$ is the projection
  to the heights of the kernel of a linear system $M(q)$ in the heights
  $z$ and one coefficient vector $h_v \in K^3$ per body, requiring
  $z_w = h_v \cdot (x_w, y_w, 1)$ on the closed neighbourhood of $v$ and
  vanishing off $V(G)$; its entries are affine in $q$. Over an admissible
  picture the projection is injective, because three non-collinear
  picture points in each closed neighbourhood determine $h_v$, so there
  the kernel has dimension $\dim L(q)$, least at the main picture $q_0$.

  Lift $z_0$ to a kernel vector $x_0$ at $q_0$. Take a projection $\pi$
  onto $\ker M(q_0)$ and a left inverse $H$ of the stacked matrix
  $[M(q_0); \pi]$ (the rows of $M(q_0)$ above those of $\pi$), which is
  injective; then $S(q) = H\,[M(q); \pi]$ is a square matrix polynomial
  in $q$ with $S(q_0) = 1$. At an admissible
  picture $q$ with $\det S(q) \neq 0$ the stack is injective, so $\pi$
  embeds $\ker M(q)$ into $\ker M(q_0)$, onto because $\dim \ker M(q)$ is
  at least $\dim \ker M(q_0)$. The preimage $x$ of $x_0$ has
  $S(q) x = x_0$, and by Cramer's rule
  $\operatorname{adj} S(q)\, x_0 = \det S(q)\, x$ lies in the kernel: a
  section of the kernel, polynomial in $q$, passing through $x_0$ at
  $q_0$. Its height part $Z(q)$ lies in $L(q)$.

  The rank of a configuration is the rank of the panel-hinge framework
  with the configuration points as panel normals. Its hinges are nonzero
  over an admissible picture (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), since
  two points $(x, y, z, 1)$ over distinct picture points are
  independent. So \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} gives a
  polynomial $Q$ in the configuration coordinates, nonzero at
  $(q_0, z_0)$, whose non-vanishing forces the target rank as a lower
  bound. Take $P$ to be the admissibility polynomial times $\det S(q)$
  times $Q$ at the configuration $(q, Z(q))$, and, for a picture $q$ with
  $P(q) \neq 0$, take $R = Q$ at $(q, \cdot)$. Then $P(q_0) \neq 0$, the
  height $Z(q) \in L(q)$ has $R(Z(q)) \neq 0$, and at every $z \in L(q)$
  with $R(z) \neq 0$ the rank is at least the target and, every hinge
  being nonzero, at most the target
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The main pictures are nonempty and Zariski-open]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isAdmissiblePicture, Graph.exists_isMainPicture,
        Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_isMainPicture}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture}
  There is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that
  every picture $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is a main picture. In particular
  the set $U$ of main pictures is nonempty and Zariski-open.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  On the moment curve $v \mapsto (t_v, t_v^2)$ at pairwise distinct
  parameters $t_v$, adjacent bodies lie at distinct points, and any three
  members of a closed neighbourhood, which has at least three by the
  degree hypothesis, lie at non-collinear points, since three points of a
  parabola at distinct parameters never are collinear: this picture is
  admissible.
  Among admissible pictures the natural number $\dim L(q)$ therefore
  attains a least value at some admissible $q_0$, which is a main
  picture. The semicontinuity argument of \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness},
  run at the trivial kernel vector $0$ in place of a witness height, gives
  a second polynomial, nonzero at $q_0$, off whose zero set $\dim L(q)$
  does not exceed $\dim L(q_0)$; off the product of that polynomial with
  the one witnessing admissibility near $q_0$, $q$ is admissible with
  $\dim L(q) \le \dim L(q_0)$, hence a main picture.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}
  In place of the standing hypotheses on $G$, the formal statements
  assume the two consequences the construction uses: $G$ has no loops,
  and every closed neighbourhood has at least three members.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The general configuration attaining gives an adjacent-distinct realization]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.hasDistinctPencilRealization}

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok

  \cref{def:pencil-x0-attains} has a non-root $q$, which is admissible,

  member $z$. Apply \cref{lem:pencil-config-distinct-realization} to
  $(q, z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two open conditions on a fibre meet]
  \label{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}
  \lean{MvPolynomial.exists_mem_eval_ne_zero₂}
  \leanok
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $L$ be a linear subspace of $K^\sigma$
  for an index set $\sigma$. If two polynomials in the coordinates of
  $\sigma$ are each nonzero somewhere on $L$, some single point of $L$
  makes both nonzero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Restrict both polynomials to the line through the two witnessing
  points of $L$, an affine substitution of one variable for the
  coordinates of $\sigma$ turning each into a univariate polynomial.
  Each restriction is nonzero, since it agrees with the corresponding
  hypothesis at one of the line's two ends, so their product is a
  nonzero univariate polynomial over the infinite field $K$ and has a
  non-root there. The corresponding point of the line lies in $L$,
  since a linear subspace is closed under it, and both original
  polynomials are nonzero there.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The main component]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_isMainPicture,
        MvPolynomial.exists_mem_eval_ne_zero₂,
        Graph.x0Attains_of_exists,
        Graph.X0Attains.hasDistinctPencilRealization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-main-picture, def:pencil-x0-attains,
        def:pencil-distinct-motive, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $G$ have at least one body, no loops, and
  at least three members in every closed neighbourhood. Then the main
  pictures of $G$ (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}) contain the non-roots
  of a nonzero polynomial in the picture coordinates. Over any picture
  $q$, two polynomials in the heights that are each nonzero somewhere on
  $L(q)$ are nonzero together somewhere on $L(q)$. Moreover, if a single
  configuration $(q, z)$ with $q$ a main picture and $z \in L(q)$ has
  rank at least $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, then the
  general configuration of $G$ attains (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}),
  and $G$ has an adjacent-distinct pencil realization
  (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive}, at $n = 3$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The first claim is \cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}, and the
  second is \cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection} applied to the
  linear subspace $L(q)$ of the heights. For the third, $L(q)$ is at
  every picture the height projection of the kernel of the lifting system
  (\texttt{Graph.map\_ker\_liftingMatrix}), so a Cramer section of that
  system through the witness, composed with a rank polynomial nonzero at
  the witness, shows that the general configuration attains
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}); an attaining general configuration
  gives the realization (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The geometry of the main component]
  \label{rem:pencil-x0-main-component}
  Over the set $U$ of main pictures the dimension of $L(q)$ is constant,
  equal to $\ell_0$, and $L(q)$ is the height projection of the kernel of
  a matrix polynomial in $q$, so the pairs $(q, z)$ with $q \in U$ and
  $z \in L(q)$ form a vector bundle over $U$. Its closure $X_0$, the
  \emph{main component} of the pencil configuration space, is
  irreducible of dimension $2|V(G)| + \ell_0$, and it contains every
  flat configuration $(q, 0)$ with $q \in U$. The statements of this
  subsection never form $X_0$ itself: in them, ``general'' means off the
  zero set of a nonzero polynomial in the picture coordinates and, fibre
  by fibre, off the zero set of a nonzero polynomial in the heights
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}). The irreducibility of $X_0$ enters only
  through the fibre statement of \cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}, by
  which two polynomials each nonzero somewhere on one fibre $L(q)$ are
  nonzero there together, and through products of picture polynomials;
  the dimension of $X_0$ is not used.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The flat rank}
\label{sec:main-component-flat}

This subsection computes the rank of a \emph{flat} configuration
$(q, 0)$, all heights zero, over an admissible picture $q$, and derives
from it the case of the induction in which the two deficiencies agree.
Every hinge of a flat configuration lies in the plane $z = 0$, so the
screw space splits into a part the edges glue together and a planar
part. The planar part's motions are the families of affine functions on
$K^2$ that agree at both ends of every edge, and the map evaluating each
at its own picture point identifies them with the lifting space. So the
flat rank is exactly $6|V(G)| - 3 - \dim L(q)$
(\cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank}), and a partition count on the planar part
bounds $\dim L(q)$ below by $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$
(\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}). This is an instance of the
classical correspondence between the motions of a flat panel structure
and the polyhedra over it (Crapo--Whiteley~\cite{crapoWhiteley1982},
Example~4.4, for the tetrahedron), with $L(q)$ the space of liftings of
the picture in the sense of scene analysis
(Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1996}, \S 8.3); the proofs below are
self-contained. Throughout, $\operatorname{def}_2$ and
$\operatorname{def}_3$ are the deficiencies of \cref{def:D-deficiency}
at $n = 2$ and $n = 3$ ($D = 3$ and $D = 6$).

\begin{definition}[The lifting planes]
  \label{def:pencil-lifting-planes}
  \lean{Graph.liftingPlanes}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $q$ be a planar picture of $G$, and identify a vector
  $h \in K^3$ with the affine function $(x, y) \mapsto h \cdot (x, y, 1)$
  on $K^2$. The space of \emph{lifting planes} $F(q)$ is the set of
  families $(h_v)_{v \in V(G)}$ of affine functions with
  $(h_u - h_v)(q_u) = (h_u - h_v)(q_v) = 0$ for every edge $uv$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The lifting planes and the lifting space]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_liftingPlanes}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, def:pencil-lifting-space}
  Let $q$ be admissible. Then $\dim F(q) = \dim L(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Define $\Phi(h)_w = h_w(q_w)$. For a neighbour $u$ of $v$,
  $h_u(q_u) = h_v(q_u)$, so $\Phi(h)$ restricts on the closed
  neighbourhood of $v$ to the affine function $h_v$, and
  $\Phi(h) \in L(q)$. Conversely, for $z \in L(q)$ let $h_v$ be an affine
  function agreeing with $z$ on the closed neighbourhood of $v$; both
  ends of an edge $uv$ lie in both closed neighbourhoods, so
  $(h_v)_v \in F(q)$, and $\Phi$ maps it to $z$. If $\Phi(h) = 0$, then
  $h_v$ vanishes at every point of the closed neighbourhood of $v$,
  three of which are not collinear, so $h_v = 0$. Hence $\Phi$ is an
  isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-lifting-planes}
  The families of lifting planes range over the whole ambient set of
  bodies and are unconstrained off $V(G)$, as the infinitesimal motions
  of every framework are. Each body off $V(G)$ adds three to
  $\dim F(q)$, and six to the dimension of the motions in
  \cref{lem:pencil-flat-split}; these terms cancel in every rank.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The lifting planes are the motions of a plane framework]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_pencilPicturePoint,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $q$ be admissible, and let $P_q$ be the panel-hinge framework in
  the plane on $G$ whose panel normal at each body $v$ is
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}, at $n = 2$). Then
  the infinitesimal motions of $P_q$ form a space isomorphic to $F(q)$,
  and $\operatorname{rank} R(P_q) = 3|V(G)| - \dim L(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor}
  The screw space of the plane is $K^3$. The hinge of $P_q$ at an edge
  $uv$ is the meet of the lines with normals $(x_u, y_u, 1)$ and
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), a nonzero vector
  orthogonal to both, since $q_u \ne q_v$. So a relative screw is a
  multiple of the hinge exactly when it is orthogonal to both normals,
  which is the condition defining $F(q)$. The rank follows from
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim} by rank--nullity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The flat lower bound on the lifting space]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  \lean{Graph.three_add_deficiency_le_finrank_liftingSpace}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ have at least one body and let $q$ be admissible. Then
  $\dim L(q) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, where
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \max_P\,[3(|P| - 1) - 2d(P)]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  By \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions} and \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}
  it suffices to bound the motions of $P_q$ below by
  $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Its bodies are $3$-dimensional and each
  hinge imposes two conditions. For a partition $P$, the part-wise
  constant assignments form a $3|P|$-dimensional space, on which the
  edges inside a part impose nothing and each crossing edge at most two
  conditions; this is the relative deficiency bound
  \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}, at $D = 3$, and gives at least
  $3|P| - 2d(P) = 3 + [3(|P| - 1) - 2d(P)]$ motions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The flat split]
  \label{lem:pencil-flat-split}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.flatScrewEquiv,
        Graph.finrank_linkConstants,
        Graph.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_pointJoinFramework_flat}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-planes, lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}
  Let $G$ be connected, let $q$ be admissible, and let $F_{(q,0)}$ be the
  body-hinge framework whose hinge at each edge $uv$ is the line
  $p_u \wedge p_v$ of the flat configuration $p = (q, 0)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}). Then its infinitesimal
  motions form a space of dimension $3 + \dim F(q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, def:hinge-constraint}
  At $z = 0$ every hinge $p_u \wedge p_v$ lies in
  $W_\Pi = \operatorname{span}(e_{01}, e_{03}, e_{13})$, the Pl\"ucker
  coordinates of lines in the plane $z = 0$. Split
  $\Lambda^2 K^4 = W' \oplus W_\Pi$ with
  $W' = \operatorname{span}(e_{02}, e_{12}, e_{23})$ and write a screw as
  $m_v = (m'_v, m^\Pi_v)$. The hinge condition
  $m_u - m_v \in \operatorname{span}(p_u \wedge p_v)$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) splits into $m'_u = m'_v$ and
  $m^\Pi_u - m^\Pi_v \in \operatorname{span}(p_u \wedge p_v)$. Since $G$
  is connected, $m'$ is constant, a $3$-dimensional contribution.
  Identifying $W_\Pi = \Lambda^2 \hat\Pi$, $\hat\Pi = \operatorname{span}
  (e_0, e_1, e_3)$, with the affine functions on $K^2$ by
  $a \wedge b \mapsto \det(a, b, \cdot)$, the line $p_u \wedge p_v$
  becomes the affine functions vanishing at $q_u$ and $q_v$, so the
  planar part's solutions are $F(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The flat rank]
  \label{thm:pencil-flat-rank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_le,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_eq_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-lifting-space, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be connected and let $q$ be admissible. Then the rank
  $\operatorname{rank} R(q, 0)$ of the flat configuration
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}) is exactly
  $6|V(G)| - 3 - \dim L(q)$; hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(q, 0) \le 6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_2(G)$,
  with equality if and only if $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-flat-split, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim,
        lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  The rank of $(q, 0)$ is that of $F_{(q,0)}$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-config-point-join-rank}), which is $6|V(G)|$ minus
  the dimension $3 + \dim F(q) = 3 + \dim L(q)$ of its motions
  (\cref{lem:pencil-flat-split} and \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-dim}). The
  bound and its equality case then follow from
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The flat configuration attains]
  \label{cor:pencil-flat-attains}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_liftingSpace_eq_three_add_deficiency_three_iff,
        Graph.x0Attains_of_finrank_liftingSpace_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-flat-rank, def:pencil-x0-attains}
  Let $G$ be connected and let $q$ be admissible. The flat configuration
  at $q$ attains $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ if and only if
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, if and only if equality holds
  in \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} at $q$ and
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$. If
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency, lem:deficiency-antitone,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness, def:pencil-main-picture}
  The first equivalence is the exact rank of \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank}. By
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} and
  \cref{lem:deficiency-antitone},
  $\dim L(q) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) \ge 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$,
  which gives the second, and shows that under
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$ every admissible picture
  has $\dim L \ge \dim L(q)$: $q$ is a main picture
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-picture}), and the flat configuration, at rank
  at least the target, is a witness for \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A flat main component attains]
  \label{cor:pencil-flat-x0}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_finrank_liftingSpace_eq_three,
        Graph.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_flat_of_finrank_eq_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:pencil-flat-attains, lem:pencil-lifting-space-affine}
  Let $G$ be connected, and suppose that $\dim L(q) = 3$ at some
  admissible picture $q$, so that $\ell_0 = 3$ and the main component is
  flat. Then $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G) = 0$, the
  flat configuration at every admissible $q$ with $\dim L(q) = 3$ has
  rank $6(|V(G)| - 1)$, and the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-flat-rank, lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency,
        lem:deficiency-antitone}
  \Cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} gives
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) \le 0$, so both deficiencies vanish
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-antitone}; both are nonnegative). Then
  \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank} gives the rank $6|V(G)| - 6$, and
  \cref{cor:pencil-flat-attains} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Jackson and Jord\'an's equality}
\label{sec:main-component-jj}

By \cref{thm:pencil-flat-rank} the flat configuration reaches the
$\operatorname{def}_2$ bound exactly when $\dim L(q) = 3 +
\operatorname{def}_2(G)$. That this equality holds at the general
picture is the form in which the argument uses Jackson and Jord\'an's
pin-collinear theorem~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin}, at $G$ itself and at
the smaller graphs of the contraction, chain and split-off steps. This
subsection derives it over every infinite field from the planar rank
theorem, Katoh and Tanigawa's rank formula~\cite{katohTanigawa2011} at
$d = 2$ (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows}), read in the chart
$(x_v, y_v, 1)$ of the plane framework of
\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}. The graph need not be
connected and need not span the set of bodies.

\begin{lemma}[Rescaling the panel normals]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-rescale}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.panelSupportExtensor_smul_right,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ofNormals_smul,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_smul,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_smul}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:panel-support-extensor,
        def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $(n_v)$ be panel normals and $(c_v)$ nonzero scalars. At every
  edge $e$ with recorded ends $u, v$, the panel-hinge framework on $G$
  with normals $c_v n_v$ has the hinge $c_u c_v\, C(e)$, where $C(e)$ is
  its hinge at the normals $n_v$. So the two frameworks have the same
  infinitesimal motions and rigidity matrices of the same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  The support extensor is linear in each of its two normals
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), and a nonzero multiple of a hinge
  spans the same line, which is all the hinge constraint reads
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). Equal motion spaces give equal row
  spans (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The planar rank theorem in the chart]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-chart}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_le_finrank_span_rigidityRows_pencilPicturePoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-rows, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be simple with at least two bodies.
  There is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that
  at every picture $q$ with $P(q) \ne 0$ the plane framework $P_q$ of
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}, with panel normal
  $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ at each body, has
  $\operatorname{rank} R(P_q) \ge 3(|V(G)| - 1) -
  \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank, def:panel-general-position,
        lem:general-position-support-nonzero, lem:pencil-jj-rescale}
  \Cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows} at $n = 2$ ($D = 3$) gives a realization
  of $G$ at rank $3(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Its rank
  polynomial (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}), a polynomial
  whose non-roots are in general position
  (\cref{def:panel-general-position}), and the product of the third
  coordinates of the normals have a common non-root $n$, since $K$ is
  infinite. Dividing each normal $n_v$ by its third coordinate gives the
  normals $(x_v, y_v, 1)$ of a picture $q'$, at the same rank and with
  nonzero hinges (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-rescale},
  \cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). The rank polynomial at
  $q'$, composed with the chart $q \mapsto (x_v, y_v, 1)$, is nonzero at
  $q'$; it is the required $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relabelling the edges]
  \label{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}
  \lean{Graph.embedEdges, Graph.vertexSet_embedEdges, Graph.embedEdges_isLink,
        Graph.edgeSet_embedEdges, Graph.closedNbhd_embedEdges, Graph.Simple.embedEdges,

  Relabel the edges of $G$ injectively into a larger set of labels.
  This keeps $G$ simple if it is simple, and it changes neither the
  lifting spaces $L(q)$, nor the admissible and the main pictures, nor
  $\operatorname{def}_n(G)$ for any $n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The bodies, the pairs of adjacent bodies and hence the closed
  neighbourhoods are unchanged, and a partition crosses exactly the
  images of the edges it crossed before.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:pencil-jj-embed-edges}
  The formal planar rank theorem is stated over a fixed type of edge
  labels with a supply of spare labels (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}),
  and \cref{lem:pencil-jj-chart} carries that supply. Jackson and
  Jord\'an's equality below mentions no edge label, so it first relabels
  the edges into a label set with room to spare
  (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}); it carries no hypothesis on the

\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[Jackson and Jord\'an's equality]
  \label{thm:pencil-jj-equality}
  \lean{Graph.exists_mvPolynomial_finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.IsMainPicture.finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.exists_isMainPicture_finrank_liftingSpace_eq,
        Graph.closedNbhd_subset_vertexSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-main-picture,
        def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be simple, with at least one body and
  at least three members in every closed neighbourhood. There is a
  nonzero polynomial $P$ in the picture coordinates such that every
  picture $q$ with $P(q) \ne 0$ is a main picture with
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$. Consequently
  $\ell_0 = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$: every main picture has
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-jj-chart, lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions,
        lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency}
  Relabel the edges (\cref{lem:pencil-jj-embed-edges}). The main
  pictures contain the non-roots of a nonzero polynomial
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}), and \cref{lem:pencil-jj-chart}
  gives a second, since $G$ has at least three bodies (a closed
  neighbourhood of a body lies in $V(G)$); let $P$ be their product. At a
  non-root $q$ the rank of
  the plane framework is $3|V(G)| - \dim L(q)$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-planes-motions}), so
  $\dim L(q) \le 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-space-deficiency} gives the reverse. A main
  picture has $\dim L(q)$ at most its value at such a non-root, and at
  least $3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G)$ by the same lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The general configuration attains when the two deficiencies agree]
  \label{cor:pencil-jj-flat}
  \lean{Graph.x0Attains_of_deficiency_two_eq_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ be connected and simple, with at least
  three members in every closed neighbourhood. If
  $\operatorname{def}_2(G) = \operatorname{def}_3(G)$, then the general
  configuration of $G$ attains (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:pencil-jj-equality, cor:pencil-flat-attains}
  \Cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality} gives an admissible picture with
  $\dim L(q) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_2(G) = 3 + \operatorname{def}_3(G)$,
  and \cref{cor:pencil-flat-attains} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cut vertices and bridges}
\label{sec:main-component-cut}

The steps of the induction share one shape. Each deduces that the
general configuration of $G$ attains (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains})
from the same conclusion at smaller graphs on the same bodies and edge
labels. It picks one picture generic for the smaller graphs and main
for $G$, and it finds the heights inside the single lifting space
$L_G(q)$, where two nonempty Zariski-open conditions always meet
(\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). One configuration at the
target rank then suffices (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}). This
subsection states the standing hypotheses and the restriction of
heights to a subgraph, and proves the first two steps. At a cut vertex
and along a chain of bridges the graph is a fibre product of two
smaller graphs, and attaining passes from the two pieces to the whole.

\begin{definition}[The standing hypotheses]
  \label{def:pencil-x0-standing}
  \lean{Graph.IsX0Graph, Graph.three_le_ncard_closedNbhd,
    Graph.IsX0Graph.three_le_ncard_closedNbhd}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  A graph $G$ satisfies the \emph{standing hypotheses} if it is finite,
  simple and connected, with minimum degree at least two. Then every
  closed neighbourhood of $G$ has at least three members: a body of
  degree $d$ in a simple graph has $d$ distinct neighbours.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Heights restrict to a subgraph]
  \label{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  \lean{Graph.liftingRestrict, Graph.liftingRestrict_apply,
    Graph.liftingRestrict_mem_liftingSpace,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.pencilConfigPoint_liftingRestrict,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.restrictPoly,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eval_restrictPoly}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-lifting-space, def:pencil-configuration}
  Let $G' \le G$ and let $q$ be any picture. The height that agrees with
  $z \in L_G(q)$ on $V(G')$ and vanishes elsewhere lies in
  $L_{G'}(q)$, and its configuration points agree with those of $z$ at
  the bodies of $G'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A closed neighbourhood in $G'$ lies in the corresponding closed
  neighbourhood in $G$, where $z$ agrees with an affine function of the
  picture.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rank reads only the bodies of the graph]
  \label{lem:pencil-rank-congr}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  The rank of the rigidity matrix of the panel-hinge framework on $G$
  depends only on the panel normals at the bodies of $G$, and not on
  which end of each edge is recorded first.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:hinge-constraint,
        lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  The hinge at an edge is built from the normals at its two ends, and
  swapping them negates it (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}); the
  hinge constraint reads only the line it spans
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). So the motion spaces agree, and equal
  motion spaces give equal row spans
  (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The heights at a cut vertex]
  \label{lem:pencil-cut-fibre}
  \lean{Graph.exists_liftingRestrict_eq_of_cutVertex}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}
  Let $G = G_1 \cup G_2$ with $V(G_1) \cap V(G_2) = \{v\}$ and disjoint
  edge sets. At every picture $q$, both restriction maps
  $L_G(q) \to L_{G_i}(q)$ (\cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}) are onto.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Given $z^1 \in L_{G_1}(q)$, extend it across $V(G_2)$ by the affine
  function of its closed neighbourhood at $v$. Every closed
  neighbourhood of $G$ other than $N_G[v]$ lies in one side, and
  $N_G[v] = N_1[v] \cup N_2[v]$ carries that affine function on both, so
  the extension lies in $L_G(q)$ and restricts to $z^1$. The other side
  is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally more holds: at every $q$ admissible for $G_1$ and $G_2$,
  $L_G(q)$ is the set of pairs $(z^1, z^2) \in L_{G_1} \times L_{G_2}$
  whose planes at $v$ coincide, so $\dim L_G(q) = \dim L_{G_1} +
  \dim L_{G_2} - 3$. Only the surjectivity is formalized, which is all
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut} uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A cut vertex]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-cut}
  \lean{Graph.X0Attains.of_cutVertex}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-standing}), with $V(G) = V_1 \cup V_2$,
  $V_1 \cap V_2 = \{v\}$ and every edge of $G$ inside $V_1$ or inside
  $V_2$. If the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and $G[V_2]$ attain,
  then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:pencil-cut-fibre, lem:block-rank-cut-vertex,
        lem:deficiency-cut-vertex, lem:pencil-lifting-restrict,
        lem:pencil-rank-congr, lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection,
        lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  Take a picture $q$ generic for both sides and main for $G$
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open}). The attaining heights of
  each side contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset of $L_{G[V_i]}(q)$.
  Since the restrictions are onto (\cref{lem:pencil-cut-fibre},
  \cref{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict}), their preimages are nonempty
  Zariski-open subsets of $L_G(q)$, and they meet
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection}). At such a height $z$ the
  rank of each side is its target (\cref{lem:pencil-rank-congr}), the
  ranks add (\cref{lem:block-rank-cut-vertex}), and so do the
  deficiencies (\cref{lem:deficiency-cut-vertex}), while the body counts
  overlap in one. So $(q, z)$ has rank at least the target of $G$, and
  \cref{lem:pencil-x0-one-witness} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally the converse holds as well: at a general configuration of
  $G$ the ranks add and each is at most its target, so if $G$ attains,
  both sides attain. Only the ``if'' half is formalized, the half the
  induction uses; the ``only if'' half is a tracked item of the
  formalization, not a node of this chapter.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The heights along a chain of bridges]
  \label{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre}
  \uses{lem:pencil-lifting-restrict, def:pencil-admissible-picture}
  Let $G$ be $G_1 \sqcup G_2$ plus a path $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  ($k \ge 0$ new vertices), with $a \in V(G_1)$ and $b \in V(G_2)$. At
  every picture $q$ admissible for $G$, both restriction maps
  $L_G(q) \to L_{G_i}(q)$ are onto.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The path bodies have degree two, and their closed neighbourhoods are
  three non-collinear points, so they impose nothing. The body $a$ adds
  $z_{x_1} = h_a(q_{x_1})$ and $b$ adds $z_{x_k} = h_b(q_{x_k})$ (for
  $k = 0$, read $x_1 = b$ and $x_k = a$). Given $z^1 \in L_{G_1}(q)$,
  put an affine function $g$ on $V(G_2)$: for $k \ge 2$ take $g = 0$,
  the two end conditions fix $z_{x_1}$ and $z_{x_k}$, and the $k - 2$
  middle heights are free; for $k = 1$ take $g$ with $g(q_x) =
  h_a(q_x)$; for $k = 0$ take $g$ with $g(q_a) = z^1_a$ and $g(q_b) =
  h_a(q_b)$, which exists because $q_a \neq q_b$. The other restriction
  is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally more holds: at every $q$ admissible for $G$, $G_1$ and
  $G_2$, $\dim L_G(q) = \dim L_{G_1} + \dim L_{G_2} + c_k$, with
  $c_0 = -2$, $c_1 = -1$, and $c_k = k - 2$ for $k \ge 2$. Only the
  surjectivity is formalized, which is all \cref{thm:pencil-x0-bridge}
  uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[A chain of bridges]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-bridge}
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, def:pencil-x0-standing}
  Let $K$ be infinite and let $G$ satisfy the standing hypotheses. Let
  $V(G)$ be the disjoint union of $V_1$, $V_2$ and path bodies
  $x_1, \dots, x_k$ ($k \ge 0$), with a path $a - x_1 - \cdots - x_k - b$
  from $a \in V_1$ to $b \in V_2$, every other edge of $G$ lying inside
  $V_1$ or inside $V_2$. If the general configurations of $G[V_1]$ and
  $G[V_2]$ attain, then the general configuration of $G$ attains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre, lem:block-rank-cut, lem:cut-edge-decomposition,
        lem:pencil-lifting-restrict, lem:pencil-rank-congr,
        lem:pencil-x0-fibre-intersection, lem:pencil-x0-main-picture-open,
        lem:pencil-x0-one-witness}
  As for \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut}, with \cref{lem:pencil-bridge-fibre}
  for the heights. Each of the $k + 1$ bridges adds $D - 1 = 5$ to the
  rank (\cref{lem:block-rank-cut}, all hinges being nonzero at an
  admissible picture) and $1$ to the deficiency
  (\cref{lem:cut-edge-decomposition}), and the $k$ path bodies add $6k$
  to the body term, so the targets satisfy
  $\operatorname{tgt}(G) = \operatorname{tgt}(G_1) +
  \operatorname{tgt}(G_2) + 5(k + 1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Informally the converse holds as well, by the same count. Only the
  ``if'' half is formalized; the ``only if'' half is a tracked item of
  the formalization, as for \cref{thm:pencil-x0-cut}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The passage to the two main-component statements}
\label{sec:main-component-statements}

The theorem below has two parts. The first is the conclusion of a strong
induction on the number of bodies, through the ear, split-off,
contraction and cut steps, which the structural coverage completes. The
second derives from the first the two main-component statements
(\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}), the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:pencil-conditional-realization-main-component}.

\begin{theorem}[The general point of the main component attains]
  \label{thm:pencil-x0-generic-attains}
  \uses{def:pencil-x0-attains, lem:pencil-x0-one-witness,
        lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct, thm:pencil-x0-main-component,
        def:pencil-distinct-motive, def:pencil-generic-motive,
        def:D-deficiency, def:pencil-main-component-statements}
  For every simple connected multigraph $G$ of minimum degree at least
  two, the general configuration of $G$ attains the rank
  $6(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:pencil-x0-attains}; \cref{def:D-deficiency}, at $n = 3$).
  Granting this at every such
  graph, the two main-component statements
  (\cref{def:pencil-main-component-statements}) follow: the general
  member of $X_0$ is a pencil realization at the deficiency rank with
  adjacent points distinct (\cref{def:pencil-distinct-motive}), and,
  wherever a nondegenerate realization exists at all, a generic one
  (\cref{def:pencil-generic-motive}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \uses{thm:pencil-jj-equality, cor:pencil-jj-flat}
  By \cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component} it suffices, for the first
  claim, to exhibit one configuration over a main picture attaining the
  target rank. That runs a strong induction on the number of bodies
  through ear, split-off, contraction and cut steps, whose flat inputs
  use Jackson and Jord\'an's equality in the form
  \cref{thm:pencil-jj-equality}, derived from the planar rank theorem
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows} in place of their pin-collinear
  theorem~\cite{jacksonJordan2008pin}; where the two deficiencies agree
  it is \cref{cor:pencil-jj-flat}. Granting the first claim, the
  distinct statement needs a single attaining configuration
  (\cref{lem:pencil-x0-attains-distinct}). The generic statement holds on
  the dense open subset of a fibre $L(q)$ where a nondegenerate
  realization survives: the attaining heights and the heights carrying a
  nondegenerate realization are each nonempty and Zariski-open in $L(q)$,
  so they meet by the fibre statement of
  \cref{thm:pencil-x0-main-component}.
\end{proof}
