\section{The molecule rank formula (KT Corollary~5.7)}
\label{sec:molecule-application}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph of minimum degree at least two. In this
chapter we prove KT Corollary~5.7, the rank formula
\[
  r(G^2) \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
\]
where $r = r_3$ is the generic rank in $\R^3$ (\cref{def:genericRank})
and $G^2$ is the square of $G$ (\cref{def:square-graph}). The deficiency
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is that of \cref{def:D-deficiency} at
$D = 6$, with $\tilde G = 5 \cdot G$. Jackson and Jord\'an conjectured
the formula and proved the inequality
$\le$~\cite[Conjecture~2.2 and Theorem~4.1]{jacksonJordan2008}. Their
Conjecture~2.1 is the bar-joint form of the molecular conjecture of Tay
and Whiteley~\cite{tayWhiteley1984}, and they proved that the formula is
equivalent to it (their Theorem~4.3). Katoh and Tanigawa deduce the
formula from their Theorem~5.6, through Whiteley's equivalence between
molecular and bar-joint realizations and Jackson and Jord\'an's
Theorem~4.3 (KT p.~671).

We derive both inequalities from KT Theorem~5.6, through the equalities
between dimensions of motion spaces that \cref{sec:molecule-modelling}
proves. The opening of that chapter outlines the argument and compares
it with Jackson and Jord\'an's.
\Cref{sec:molecule-application-domination} shows that the rank of a
graph at any placement is at most its generic rank.
\Cref{sec:molecule-application-carrier} passes from the simple graph $G$
to a multigraph, on which the formalization states the deficiency and
KT Theorem~5.6. \Cref{sec:molecule-application-formula} proves the two
inequalities and the formula, and derives from it when the square of a
graph is generically rigid.

\subsection{The generic rank bounds the rank at every placement}
\label{sec:molecule-application-domination}

At a placement that is generic for row independence, the rank of the
rigidity map of a graph equals its generic rank
(\cref{lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}). The lower bound compares the
two at the centres of a molecular realization, which need not be
generic.

\begin{lemma}[The rank at a placement is at most the generic rank]
  \label{lem:square-rank-le-genericRank}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.finrank_span_rigidityRow_le_genericRank,
    SimpleGraph.finrank_range_rigidityMap_le_genericRank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRank, lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}
  Let $H$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, and let $p$ be a placement of
  $V$ in $\R^d$. Then
  \[
    \dim \operatorname{span} \{\, r_e \mid e \in E(H) \,\} \;\le\; r_d(H),
  \]
  where $r_e$ is the rigidity row of $K_V$ at $p$ indexed by $e$
  (\cref{def:rigidityRow}). Equivalently,
  $\operatorname{rank} R(H, p) \le r_d(H)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk, lem:span-range-rigidityRow}
  The linear rigidity matroid $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ is represented by the
  extended rigidity rows $\tilde r_e$ at $p$
  (\cref{def:linearRigidityMatroid}), and $\tilde r_e = r_e$ for every
  edge $e$ of $K_V$. In a represented matroid, the vectors of a set span
  a space whose dimension is the rank of the set
  (\cref{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}). So the span has dimension
  equal to the rank of $E(H)$ in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$. A set that is
  independent in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}). It is therefore
  independent in $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$
  (\cref{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}). Hence the rank of $E(H)$
  in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ is at most its rank $r_d(H)$ in
  $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$.

  The rank of $R(H, p)$ is the dimension of the same span. Indeed,
  $R(H, p)$ has the same rank as its transpose, whose image is spanned by
  the rigidity rows of $H$ at $p$ (\cref{lem:span-range-rigidityRow}).
  For an edge $uv$ of $H$, the row of $H$ at $uv$ is
  $x \mapsto \langle p_u - p_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which is also the row $r_{uv}$ of
  $K_V$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pin states the bound for the span, and the second the bound
  for $\operatorname{rank} R(H, p)$, which the proof of
  \cref{lem:molecule-rank-lower-bound} uses.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Simple graphs as multigraphs}
\label{sec:molecule-application-carrier}

Katoh and Tanigawa state their Theorem~5.6 for multigraphs, and the
deficiency is defined for a multigraph (\cref{def:D-deficiency}). We
regard the simple graph $G$ as a multigraph. In the formalization this
step is the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}[A simple graph as a multigraph]
  \label{lem:molecule-graph-carrier}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.shadowGraph, SimpleGraph.shadowGraph_simple,
    SimpleGraph.shadowGraph_vertexSet, SimpleGraph.shadowGraph_isLink_iff,
    SimpleGraph.card_shadowLabel_lt}
  \leanok
  Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite set $V$. Then there is a simple
  multigraph $G'$ with vertex set $V$, whose edges are drawn from a set
  of more than $6(|V| - 1)$ labels, in which two distinct vertices are
  joined by an edge if and only if they are adjacent in $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Take as labels the $|V|(|V| + 1)/2$ unordered pairs of elements of
  $V$ and $6(|V| - 1) + 1$ further labels, more than $6(|V| - 1)$ in
  all. For each edge $uv$ of $G$, let $G'$ have one edge joining $u$ and
  $v$, labelled by the pair $\{u, v\}$. Distinct edges of $G'$ have
  distinct labels, hence distinct pairs of ends, and no edge is a loop,
  as $G$ has none. So $G'$ is simple, and its edges join exactly the
  adjacent pairs of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  In the formalization, simple graphs and multigraphs are different
  objects. The square and the generic rank are defined for simple graphs,
  and the deficiency and the frameworks of \cref{sec:molecule-modelling}
  for multigraphs, whose edges are drawn from a fixed set of labels
  (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}). The pinned form of
  \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular} asks for more than $6(|V| - 1)$
  labels (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}), and the pairs alone are too
  few when $2 \le |V| \le 9$. The first pin is $G'$, the next three are
  its simplicity, its vertex set and its edges, and the fifth is the
  bound on its labels. The pinned forms of the three results below
  state the deficiency of $G'$, and apply the results of
  \cref{sec:molecule-modelling} to $G'$.
\end{fmlnote}

A partition of $V$ separates as many edges of $G'$ as of $G$, since the
edges of $G'$ join exactly the adjacent pairs of $G$. So $G'$ and $G$
have the same deficiency. It is the deficiency $\operatorname{def}(G)$ of
Jackson and Jord\'an~\cite[\S2]{jacksonJordan2008}, the largest value of
$6(|P| - 1) - 5\,e_G(P)$ over the partitions $P$ of $V$, where $e_G(P)$
is the number of edges of $G$ that join different parts of $P$. We write
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ for it, as Katoh and Tanigawa do.

\subsection{The two inequalities and the formula}
\label{sec:molecule-application-formula}

Both inequalities pass from molecular frameworks on $G$ to bar-joint
frameworks on $G^2$ by the square-graph dictionary
(\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint}). The dictionary applies at
placements in general position up to order four, and it needs every
vertex of $G$ to have degree at least two.

\begin{lemma}[The lower bound]
  \label{lem:molecule-rank-lower-bound}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_rank_lower_bound}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular, thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint,
    lem:square-rank-le-genericRank, lem:molecule-graph-carrier}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph of minimum degree at least two on a finite,
  nonempty vertex set $V$. Then
  $3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le r(G^2)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:square-graph, lem:span-range-rigidityRow}
  Regard $G$ as a multigraph, as in \cref{lem:molecule-graph-carrier}. By
  \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular} there is a placement
  $c \colon V \to \R^3$ in general position up to order four such that
  the molecular framework on $(G, c)$ has
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) = 6 + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. By the
  square-graph dictionary (\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint}),
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G^2, c) \;=\; 3|V| - \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)
    \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  The generic rank $r(G^2)$ is at least this rank
  (\cref{lem:square-rank-le-genericRank}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The upper bound; Jackson and Jord\'an]
  \label{lem:molecule-rank-upper-bound}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_rank_upper_bound}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:exists-generic-general-position, lem:genericRank-eq-finrank-span,
    thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint, lem:molecule-graph-carrier}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph of minimum degree at least two on a finite,
  nonempty vertex set $V$. Then
  $r(G^2) \le 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:square-graph, def:hinge-concurrent, lem:deficiency-rank-bound,
    lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}
  Regard $G$ as a multigraph, as in \cref{lem:molecule-graph-carrier}.
  Let $p \colon V \to \R^3$ be a placement that is generic for row
  independence and in general position up to order four
  (\cref{lem:exists-generic-general-position}). As $p$ is generic, the
  rank of $R(G^2, p)$ is $r(G^2)$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}). So the square-graph
  dictionary (\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint}) gives
  \[
    r(G^2) \;=\; 3|V| - \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, p).
  \]
  As $p$ is in general position, the centres $p(v)$ are distinct, so the
  hinges of the molecular framework on $(G, p)$ are nondegenerate. Hence
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, p) \ge 6 + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-rank-bound}), and the bound follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned form of the dictionary takes the molecular framework with an
  endpoint selector that assigns each edge its two ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). The pinned form of the
  bound of \cref{lem:deficiency-rank-bound} asks for a nonzero hinge
  at every label, not only at every edge. The pinned lemma builds a selector with both properties. It
  assigns each edge of the multigraph of \cref{lem:molecule-graph-carrier}
  its two ends, and every other label one fixed pair of adjacent vertices
  of $G$. So every label has two distinct ends, and as $p$ is injective,
  a nonzero hinge.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{corollary}[The molecule rank formula; Jackson and Jord\'an,
  KT Corollary~5.7]
  \label{cor:molecule-rank-formula}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_rank_formula}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:molecule-rank-lower-bound, lem:molecule-rank-upper-bound,
    def:genericRank, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph of minimum degree at least two on a finite,
  nonempty vertex set $V$. Then
  \[
    r(G^2) \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  where $r$ is the rank function of the generic rigidity matroid
  $\mathcal{R}_3(V)$ (\cref{def:genericRank}), and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the deficiency of
  \cref{def:D-deficiency} at $D = 6$, with $\tilde G = 5 \cdot G$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Combine \cref{lem:molecule-rank-lower-bound} and
  \cref{lem:molecule-rank-upper-bound}.
\end{proof}

The formula decides when the square of a graph is rigid. As
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$, it gives $r(G^2) \le 3|V| - 6$,
with equality if and only if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, that is,
if and only if $5 \cdot G$ contains six edge-disjoint spanning trees
(\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}).

A placement $p$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$ if and only if
$\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, p) \ge 3|V| - 6$
(\cref{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}). This rank is at most $r(G^2)$
(\cref{lem:square-rank-le-genericRank}). At a placement that is generic
for row independence it equals $r(G^2)$
(\cref{lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}). So $G^2$ is generically rigid
in $\R^3$ (\cref{def:isGenericallyRigid}) if and only if $5 \cdot G$
contains six edge-disjoint spanning trees. This is Jackson and Jord\'an's
Conjecture~2.1~\cite{jacksonJordan2008}. Katoh and Tanigawa note it as a
corollary of their work (KT \S7). When $5 \cdot G$ contains six
edge-disjoint spanning trees, every placement that is generic for row
independence is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$
(\cref{cor:molecule-generic-rigid}).
