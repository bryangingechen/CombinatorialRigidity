\section{Frameworks and infinitesimal rigidity}
\label{sec:frameworks}

This section defines the rigidity of a graph placed in Euclidean space,
and proves the facts about it that Laman's theorem
(\cref{sec:laman-theorem}) uses. We follow the presentation in
Jord\'an's survey~\cite{jordan2016} (\S 1.2). A framework places each
vertex of a graph at a point of $\R^{d}$. Its infinitesimal motions
assign a velocity to each vertex so that no edge changes length to
first order. They form the kernel of a linear map, the rigidity map.
The translations and rotations of $\R^{d}$ give the trivial
infinitesimal motions, and a framework is infinitesimally rigid when it
has no others. A graph is generically rigid when some framework of it
is infinitesimally rigid.

After the definitions we bound the rank of the rigidity map by the
number of edges. We show that rigidity is preserved by adding edges and
by isomorphisms, and that infinitesimal rigidity survives a small
change of the framework. We then prove that a graph on $n$ vertices
that is generically rigid in $\R^{d}$ has at least $dn - d(d+1)/2$
edges. The section ends with the complete graph $K_2$, which is
generically rigid in every dimension and is the base case of the
induction in \cref{sec:laman-theorem}.

Throughout, $G$ is a graph on a set $V$, which is finite where a
statement says so, and $d \in \N$. We equip $\R^{d}$ with its
standard inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$. As in
\cref{sec:henneberg}, we write $uv$ for the edge with endpoints $u$ and
$v$.

\subsection{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[Framework]
  \label{def:framework}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Framework}
  \leanok
  A \emph{framework} on $V$ in $\R^{d}$ is a map $p : V \to \R^{d}$,
  which places each vertex $v$ at the point $p_v$. The frameworks on
  $V$ in $\R^{d}$ form the real vector space $(\R^{d})^{V}$.
\end{definition}

Jord\'an's framework is the pair $(G, p)$ of a graph and such a map.
Here a framework is the map alone, and the graph enters each notion
below as a separate argument. We also call a framework a
\emph{placement} of $V$.

\begin{lemma}[Dimension of the space of frameworks]
  \label{lem:framework-finrank}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Framework.finrank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework}
  Let $V$ be finite. Then $\dim_{\R} (\R^{d})^{V} = d\,|V|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The space $(\R^{d})^{V}$ is a product of $|V|$ copies of $\R^{d}$,
  each of dimension $d$.
\end{proof}

Let $p$ be a framework, and let each vertex $v$ move with velocity
$p'_v$, along the line $t \mapsto p_v + t\,p'_v$. The squared length
of an edge $uv$ then has derivative
$2 \langle p_u - p_v,\, p'_u - p'_v \rangle$ at $t = 0$. The rigidity
map records these derivatives, without the factor $2$.

\begin{definition}[Rigidity map]
  \label{def:rigidityMap}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.RigidityMap}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework}
  Let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$. The \emph{rigidity map}
  of $G$ at $p$ is the linear map
  $R(G, p) : (\R^{d})^{V} \to \R^{E(G)}$ given by
  \[
    R(G, p)(p')(uv) \;=\; \langle p_u - p_v,\; p'_u - p'_v \rangle
  \]
  for $p' \in (\R^{d})^{V}$ and each edge $uv$ of $G$.
\end{definition}
The right-hand side is unchanged when $u$ and $v$ are exchanged, since
both of its factors change sign, so it defines a value at the edge
$uv$. It is linear in $p'$. In the standard bases, the matrix of
$R(G, p)$ is Jord\'an's rigidity matrix (p.~36). The kernel of
$R(G, p)$ is the space of \emph{infinitesimal motions} of $G$ at $p$.

We record the defining formula as a lemma, which later proofs cite
when they evaluate the rigidity map at an edge.

\begin{lemma}[The rigidity map at an edge]
  \label{lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.rigidityMap_apply}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityMap}
  Let $uv$ be an edge of $G$, and let $p$ and $p'$ be frameworks on $V$
  in $\R^{d}$. Then
  \[
    R(G, p)(p')(uv) \;=\; \langle p_u - p_v,\; p'_u - p'_v \rangle.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Immediate from the definition.
\end{proof}

Each translation and each infinitesimal rotation of $\R^{d}$ gives an
infinitesimal motion of $G$ at $p$, whatever the graph $G$ is.
Together they give the motions $p'_v = A p_v + b$, for a
skew-symmetric $d \times d$ matrix $A$ and a vector $b \in \R^{d}$.
These are the \emph{trivial} infinitesimal motions (Jord\'an, p.~36;
\cref{sec:trivial-motions}). The pairs
$(A, b)$ form a space of dimension $d(d+1)/2$, so the trivial motions
form a space of dimension at most $d(d+1)/2$. Equality holds when the
points $p_v$ affinely span $\R^{d}$
(\cref{lem:trivialMotions-finrank-ge-of-affinelySpanning}). A framework
is infinitesimally rigid when all of its infinitesimal motions are
trivial. We state this as a bound on the dimension of the kernel, the
form that rank--nullity arguments use.

\begin{definition}[Infinitesimally rigid framework]
  \label{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsInfinitesimallyRigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityMap}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$. We
  say that $p$ is \emph{infinitesimally rigid} for $G$ if
  \[
    \dim_{\R} \ker R(G, p) \;\le\; \frac{d(d+1)}{2}.
  \]
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:infinitesimal-rigidity}
  When the points $p_v$ affinely span $\R^{d}$, the kernel contains the
  trivial motions, a space of dimension $d(d+1)/2$, so the definition
  says that every infinitesimal motion is trivial. In general, by
  rank--nullity and \cref{lem:framework-finrank}, it says that
  $R(G, p)$ has rank at least $d|V| - d(d+1)/2$. Jord\'an (pp.~36--37)
  asks instead that the rank attain the bound $S(|V|, d)$ of his
  Lemma~1.2.1. This bound is $d|V| - d(d+1)/2$ when $|V| \ge d + 2$
  and $|V|(|V| - 1)/2$ when $|V| \le d + 1$; the two expressions agree
  at $|V| = d$ and at $|V| = d + 1$. The rank never exceeds
  $S(|V|, d)$, so the two definitions agree when $|V| \ge d$. They
  also agree when $|V| \le 1$, where both hold for every $p$. In
  particular they agree for every $V$ in the plane, the case of Laman's
  theorem. When $2 \le |V| \le d - 1$, which needs
  $d \ge 3$, the definition above is weaker. For instance, two vertices
  with no edge between them, placed anywhere in $\R^{3}$, satisfy it,
  since the kernel has dimension $6$; yet the two vertices can move
  apart.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{definition}[Generically rigid graph]
  \label{def:isGenericallyRigid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  Let $V$ be finite. The graph $G$ is \emph{generically rigid} in
  dimension $d$ if some framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$ is infinitesimally
  rigid for $G$.
\end{definition}
Defining generic rigidity through a single framework avoids generic
frameworks, those whose coordinates are algebraically independent over
$\Q$. Jord\'an (p.~38) calls $G$ rigid in $\R^{d}$ when its generic
frameworks are rigid: every continuous motion of the vertices that
keeps each edge length fixed comes from an isometry of $\R^{d}$. For
$|V| \ge d$, where infinitesimal rigidity has Jord\'an's meaning
(\cref{fmlnote:infinitesimal-rigidity}), the two notions agree, for
two reasons. At a generic framework, rigidity and infinitesimal
rigidity are equivalent, by a theorem of Asimow and
Roth~\cite{asimowRoth1978}. And the rigidity map has its largest rank
at a generic framework, since its entries are polynomials in the
coordinates with integer coefficients (Jord\'an, pp.~38--39).

\begin{definition}[Generically rigid graph, injective form]
  \label{def:isGenericallyRigidInj}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigidInj}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  Let $V$ be finite. The graph $G$ is \emph{injectively generically
  rigid} in dimension $d$ if some injective framework on $V$ in
  $\R^{d}$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G$.
\end{definition}
An injectively generically rigid graph is generically rigid. The
induction that proves Laman's theorem
(\cref{thm:isLaman-isGenericallyRigid-two}) keeps an injective
framework at each step, and weakens to generic rigidity at the end. Its
step for each Henneberg move places the new vertex using that the
move's vertices $a$ and $b$ lie at distinct points
(\cref{sec:henneberg-rigidity}). In positive dimension the two notions
agree, since an infinitesimally rigid framework can be moved slightly
to an injective one (\cref{sec:rigidity-openness}).

\subsection{Rank and kernel bounds}

We compare the kernels of the rigidity maps of two graphs, one of which
contains the edges of the other, and we bound the rank of the rigidity
map by the number of edges. The first gives the monotonicity results
below, and the second gives the edge-count bound.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel monotonicity]
  \label{lem:rigidityMap-ker-mono}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.rigidityMap_ker_mono}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityMap}
  Let $G'$ be a graph on $V$ with $E(G) \subseteq E(G')$, and let $p$
  be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$. Then
  \[
    \ker R(G', p) \;\subseteq\; \ker R(G, p).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $p' \in \ker R(G', p)$. At each edge of $G$, the value of
  $R(G, p)(p')$ equals the value of $R(G', p)(p')$ at the same edge of
  $G'$, which is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rank is bounded by edge count]
  \label{lem:rigidityMap-finrank-range-le}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.rigidityMap_finrank_range_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityMap}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$.
  Then the rank of $R(G, p)$ is at most $|E(G)|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The range of $R(G, p)$ lies in $\R^{E(G)}$, which has dimension
  $|E(G)|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Graph monotonicity}

Adding edges to a graph preserves each form of rigidity, at the same
framework.

\begin{lemma}[Infinitesimal rigidity is monotone]
  \label{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-mono}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsInfinitesimallyRigid.mono}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid, lem:rigidityMap-ker-mono}
  Let $V$ be finite, let $G'$ be a graph on $V$ with
  $E(G) \subseteq E(G')$, and let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in
  $\R^{d}$. If $p$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G$, then $p$ is
  infinitesimally rigid for $G'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The kernel of $R(G', p)$ lies in that of $R(G, p)$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-ker-mono}), so its dimension is at most
  $d(d+1)/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic rigidity is monotone]
  \label{lem:isGenericallyRigid-mono}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigid.mono}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigid, lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-mono}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $G'$ be a graph on $V$ with
  $E(G) \subseteq E(G')$. If $G$ is generically rigid in dimension $d$,
  then so is $G'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A framework that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$ is infinitesimally
  rigid for $G'$ (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-mono}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Injective generic rigidity is monotone]
  \label{lem:isGenericallyRigidInj-mono}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigidInj.mono}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigidInj, lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-mono}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $G'$ be a graph on $V$ with
  $E(G) \subseteq E(G')$. If $G$ is injectively generically rigid in
  dimension $d$, then so is $G'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  An injective framework that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$ is
  infinitesimally rigid for $G'$ (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-mono}),
  and it is still injective.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transport along graph isomorphism}
\label{sec:rigidity-iso-transport}

Rigidity is invariant under isomorphism, as sparsity is
(\cref{lem:isSparse-iso}). The induction in \cref{sec:laman-theorem}
uses this for graphs that are identified only up to isomorphism, such
as a Laman graph on two vertices and $K_2$.

\begin{lemma}[Infinitesimal rigidity transports along graph isomorphism]
  \label{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsInfinitesimallyRigid.iso}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  Let $V$ and $W$ be finite, let $\varphi$ be an isomorphism from $G$
  to a graph $H$ on $W$, and let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$.
  If $p$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G$, then $p \circ \varphi^{-1}$
  is infinitesimally rigid for $H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Composition with $\varphi$ is a linear isomorphism from
  $(\R^{d})^{W}$ to $(\R^{d})^{V}$, sending $q'$ to $q' \circ \varphi$.
  Let $uv$ be an edge of $G$, so that $\varphi(u)\varphi(v)$ is an edge
  of $H$. The value of $R(H, p \circ \varphi^{-1})(q')$ at
  $\varphi(u)\varphi(v)$ is
  $\langle p_u - p_v,\, q'_{\varphi(u)} - q'_{\varphi(v)} \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which is the value of
  $R(G, p)(q' \circ \varphi)$ at $uv$. Since $\varphi$ maps the edges
  of $G$ onto those of $H$, the vector $q'$ is an infinitesimal motion
  of $H$ at $p \circ \varphi^{-1}$ if and only if
  $q' \circ \varphi$ is an infinitesimal motion of $G$ at $p$. So
  composition with $\varphi$ maps the one kernel isomorphically onto
  the other, and the two kernels have the same dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic rigidity transports along graph isomorphism]
  \label{lem:isGenericallyRigid-iso}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigid.iso}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigid, lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}
  Let $V$ and $W$ be finite, and let $\varphi$ be an isomorphism from
  $G$ to a graph $H$ on $W$. If $G$ is generically rigid in dimension
  $d$, then so is $H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  If $p$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G$, then $p \circ \varphi^{-1}$
  is infinitesimally rigid for $H$ (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Injective generic rigidity transports along graph isomorphism]
  \label{lem:isGenericallyRigidInj-iso}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigidInj.iso}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigidInj, lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}
  Let $V$ and $W$ be finite, and let $\varphi$ be an isomorphism from
  $G$ to a graph $H$ on $W$. If $G$ is injectively generically rigid
  in dimension $d$, then so is $H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  If $p$ is injective and infinitesimally rigid for $G$, then
  $p \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is infinitesimally rigid for $H$
  (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}). It is injective, since
  $\varphi^{-1}$ is a bijection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Openness of infinitesimal rigidity}
\label{sec:rigidity-openness}

Infinitesimal rigidity is an open condition on the framework: every
framework close enough to an infinitesimally rigid one is again
infinitesimally rigid. So an infinitesimally rigid framework can be
moved slightly, while staying infinitesimally rigid, to satisfy a
further condition that holds on a dense open set of frameworks. The
Type~II step of the Henneberg induction
(\cref{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two}) moves a point in this
way, off the line through two others.

\begin{lemma}[Infinitesimal rigidity is an open condition on the placement]
  \label{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-eventually}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsInfinitesimallyRigid.eventually}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid, lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $p_0$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$
  that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$. Then every framework in some
  neighbourhood of $p_0$ in $(\R^{d})^{V}$ is infinitesimally rigid for
  $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $r$ be the rank of $R(G, p_0)$, and choose
  $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_r \in (\R^{d})^{V}$ whose images under
  $R(G, p_0)$ form a basis of its range. The entry of
  $R(G, p)(\xi_i)$ at an edge $uv$ is the inner product of $p_u - p_v$
  with $(\xi_i)_u - (\xi_i)_v$ (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), so
  $R(G, p)(\xi_i)$ depends continuously on $p$. Linear independence of
  $r$ vectors in the finite-dimensional space $\R^{E(G)}$ is an open
  condition. So for every $p$ in some neighbourhood of $p_0$ the
  vectors $R(G, p)(\xi_1), \dots, R(G, p)(\xi_r)$ are linearly
  independent, and $R(G, p)$ has rank at least $r$.

  For such $p$, rank--nullity in $(\R^{d})^{V}$ shows that the kernel
  of $R(G, p)$ has dimension at most that of the kernel of
  $R(G, p_0)$, which is at most $d(d+1)/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The edge-count bound}

Rank--nullity gives a lower bound on the number of edges of a
generically rigid graph. In the plane it says that a graph on $V$ that
is generically rigid has at least $2|V| - 3$ edges, as many as a Laman
graph on $V$ (\cref{lem:isGenericallyRigid-card-mul-le-two}).

\begin{theorem}[Edge-count bound for generically rigid graphs]
  \label{thm:isGenericallyRigid-card-mul-le}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericallyRigid.card_mul_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigid, lem:framework-finrank,
    lem:rigidityMap-finrank-range-le}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $G$ be generically rigid in dimension $d$.
  Then
  \[
    d\,|V| \;\le\; |E(G)| + \frac{d(d+1)}{2}.
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $p$ be a framework that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$. By
  rank--nullity, $\dim (\R^{d})^{V}$ is the rank of $R(G, p)$ plus the
  dimension of its kernel. Here $\dim (\R^{d})^{V} = d\,|V|$
  (\cref{lem:framework-finrank}), the rank is at most $|E(G)|$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-finrank-range-le}), and the kernel has
  dimension at most $d(d+1)/2$, since $p$ is infinitesimally rigid.
  Together these give the bound.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The bound is stated in additive form because the formal statement
  counts in the natural numbers, in which $d\,|V| - d(d+1)/2$ would be
  truncated at $0$.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Worked example: \texorpdfstring{$K_2$}{K2} is generically rigid in every dimension}

The complete graph $K_2$ on two vertices is the base case of the
induction in \cref{sec:laman-theorem}, which needs it in the injective
form.

\begin{theorem}[$K_2$ is injectively generically rigid in every positive dimension]
  \label{thm:top-fin-two-isGenericallyRigidInj}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.top_fin_two_isGenericallyRigidInj}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isGenericallyRigidInj, lem:framework-finrank,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $d \ge 1$. Then the complete graph $K_2$ on two vertices is
  injectively generically rigid in dimension $d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $u$ and $v$ be the two vertices, and let $e$ be the first
  standard basis vector of $\R^{d}$. The framework $p$ with $p_u = 0$
  and $p_v = e$ is injective. The velocities $p'_u = e$ and $p'_v = 0$
  give
  $R(K_2, p)(p')(uv) = \langle 0 - e,\, e - 0 \rangle = -1$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), so $R(K_2, p)$ has rank at least
  $1$. The space of frameworks has dimension $2d$
  (\cref{lem:framework-finrank}), so by rank--nullity the kernel has
  dimension at most $2d - 1$. Finally $2d - 1 \le d(d+1)/2$, since the
  difference is $(d - 1)(d - 2)/2 \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$K_2$ is generically rigid in every dimension]
  \label{thm:top-fin-two-isGenericallyRigid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.top_fin_two_isGenericallyRigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:top-fin-two-isGenericallyRigidInj, def:isGenericallyRigid}
  Let $d \in \N$. Then the complete graph $K_2$ on two vertices is
  generically rigid in dimension $d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For $d \ge 1$ this follows from
  \cref{thm:top-fin-two-isGenericallyRigidInj}. For $d = 0$ the space
  of frameworks is $\{0\}$, so the kernel of the rigidity map has
  dimension $0 = d(d+1)/2$.
\end{proof}
