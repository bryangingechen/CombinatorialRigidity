\section{Body-bar Tay theorem}
\label{sec:body-bar}

This chapter proves \textbf{Tay's theorem}~\cite{tay1984}: for
$n \ge 2$ and $d = n(n+1)/2$, a multigraph $G$ on $b$ bodies
is the underlying graph of an infinitesimally rigid independent
body-bar framework in $\mathbb{R}^n$ if and only if
$|E| = d(b - 1)$ and every $m$-body sub-multigraph has at most
$d(m - 1)$ edges; equivalently, by the
Tutte--Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961} tree-packing
theorem, $G$ is the edge-disjoint union of $d$ spanning trees. The
abstract matroid-union results this argument uses are developed
separately in \cref{sec:matroid-union}.

The proof follows Whiteley's matroid-union
framing~\cite{whiteley1988} (a streamlining of Tay's original
induction-based proof~\cite{tay1984}, surveyed in modern form
in~\cite{whiteley1996}). Three ingredients:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The generic $k$-frame matroid on a multigraph is the
        $k$-fold union of the cycle matroid (Whiteley's
        Theorem~1).
  \item Edmonds' matroid-partition theorem~\cite{edmonds1965}
        characterizes independence in this union by the count
        $|E'| \le k|V'| - k$ on every non-empty subset.
        Tutte--Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961}
        follows as a corollary.
  \item Body-bar realizations are obtained by specializing
        two-extensor row coefficients to standard-basis
        Pl\"ucker coordinates (Whiteley's witness
        construction).
\end{enumerate}

Whiteley's full irreducible-variety lift (Proposition~6
in~\cite{whiteley1988}, lifting independence from the
indeterminate setting to ``almost all'' body-bar realizations) is
not pursued here. This chapter proves the existence-of-realization
form of Tay's theorem only: there exists a body-bar realization in
$\mathbb{R}^n$ that is infinitesimally rigid (resp. independent)
if and only if the count holds, witnessed by the standard-basis specialization.

\subsection{Sparsity and the union of cycle matroids}
\label{sec:body-bar-matroid-union}

Throughout this chapter, $G$ is a finite multigraph: two vertices may
be joined by several edges, and loops are allowed. We call its vertices
\emph{bodies} and its edges \emph{bars}, and write $V(G)$ and $E(G)$
for these sets. For a set $E'$ of bars, $V(E')$ is the set of bodies
incident with a bar of $E'$, and $G \restriction E'$ is the multigraph
with the bodies of $G$ and the bars of $E'$. In this subsection we
characterize the sets of bars that are unions of $k$ forests by a
count.

A set of bars is a \emph{forest} if it contains no cycle; a loop is a
cycle, and so are two bars with the same ends. The forests are the
independent sets of a matroid on $E(G)$, the \emph{cycle matroid} of
$G$~\cite[\S1.1]{oxley2011}. We write $r$ for its rank function. Let
$c(E')$ be the number of connected components of the multigraph with
bodies $V(E')$ and bars $E'$. Then~\cite[(1.3.8)]{oxley2011}
\[
  r(E') \;=\; |V(E')| - c(E').
\]

The union $\bigcup_j M_j$ of matroids $M_1, \dots, M_k$ on a common
finite set was introduced by Nash-Williams~\cite{nashWilliams1966}.
\Cref{sec:matroid-union} defines it as the matroid induced from their
direct sum by a bipartite graph (\cref{def:matroid-union}). A set is
independent in the union if and only if it is a union
$I_1 \cup \dots \cup I_k$ of sets $I_j$ independent in $M_j$
(\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}). For $k$ copies of one matroid $M$, the
rank of the union (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}) shows that a set
$X$ is independent in the union if and only if $|Y| \le k\, r_M(Y)$
for every $Y \subseteq X$. This is Edmonds' matroid-partition
theorem~\cite[Theorem~1]{edmonds1965}. We apply these results to $k$
copies of the cycle matroid of $G$, whose independent sets are the
unions of $k$ forests.

The count compares each non-empty set of bars with the bodies it meets.
For simple graphs, \cref{def:isSparse} instead bounds the number of
edges spanned by a set of vertices. Tay's theorem
(\cref{thm:tay-witness}) is about the case $k = \ell$.

\begin{definition}[Sparse and tight multigraphs]
  \label{def:graph-sparse}
  \lean{Graph.IsSparse, Graph.IsTight}
  \leanok
  Let $k$ and $\ell$ be natural numbers. A multigraph $G$ is
  \emph{$(k, \ell)$-sparse} if every non-empty set $E'$ of bars of $G$
  satisfies $|E'| \le k\,|V(E')| - \ell$. It is
  \emph{$(k, \ell)$-tight} if moreover $|E(G)| = k\,|V(G)| - \ell$.
\end{definition}

The next theorem is the part of Whiteley's
Corollary~3~\cite{whiteley1988} that we use, and we follow his proof.

\begin{theorem}[Independence in a union of cycle matroids;
  Whiteley Corollary~3]
  \label{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  \lean{Graph.unionPow_cycleMatroid_indep_iff_isSparse_restrict}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-sparse, thm:matroid-partition-rank,
    lem:union-indep-iff}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $k$ be a natural number, and let
  $E' \subseteq E(G)$. Then $E'$ is independent in the union of $k$
  copies of the cycle matroid of $G$ if and only if
  $G \restriction E'$ is $(k, k)$-sparse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:matroid-partition-rank, lem:union-indep-iff}
  By Edmonds' theorem (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank};
  \texttt{Matroid.\allowbreak{}Union\_\allowbreak{}pow\_\allowbreak{}indep\_\allowbreak{}iff\_\allowbreak{}count}),
  $E'$ is independent in the union if and only if $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$
  for every $Y \subseteq E'$. On the other hand, $G \restriction E'$ is
  $(k, k)$-sparse if and only if $|Y| \le k\,|V(Y)| - k$ for every
  non-empty $Y \subseteq E'$.

  Suppose that $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$ for every $Y \subseteq E'$, and let
  $Y \subseteq E'$ be non-empty. Then $c(Y) \ge 1$, so
  $r(Y) \le |V(Y)| - 1$ and $|Y| \le k\,|V(Y)| - k$.

  Conversely, let $G \restriction E'$ be $(k, k)$-sparse, and let
  $Y \subseteq E'$ be non-empty. Sparsity alone gives
  $|Y| \le k\,|V(Y)| - k$, which is weaker than $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$ when
  $c(Y) > 1$. So we apply it to each component. Let $c = c(Y)$, and let
  $Y_1, \dots, Y_c$ be the sets of bars of the components of the
  multigraph with bodies $V(Y)$ and bars $Y$. Each $Y_i$ is non-empty,
  as every body in $V(Y)$ meets a bar of $Y$. Its component is
  connected, so $r(Y_i) = |V(Y_i)| - 1$, and sparsity gives
  $|Y_i| \le k\,|V(Y_i)| - k = k\, r(Y_i)$.
  The sets $Y_i$ partition $Y$, and the sets $V(Y_i)$ partition
  $V(Y)$, so
  \[
    |Y| \;=\; \sum_{i} |Y_i| \;\le\; k \sum_{i} \bigl(|V(Y_i)| - 1\bigr)
    \;=\; k\,\bigl(|V(Y)| - c\bigr) \;=\; k\, r(Y).
  \]
\end{proof}

\subsection{The $k$-frame matroid as a union of cycle matroids}
\label{sec:body-bar-k-frame}

Whiteley represents the union of $k$ copies of the cycle matroid of $G$
by a matrix with indeterminate entries, the general $k$-frame
on $G$~\cite[Definition~2.2]{whiteley1988}. Its nonzero entries are
indeterminates, placed in the pattern of an incidence matrix of $G$
repeated $k$ times. In this subsection we prove Whiteley's Theorem~1:
the matroid of the rows of this matrix is the union of $k$ copies of the
cycle matroid (\cref{thm:k-frame-union-cycle}).

Fix an orientation of each bar. The \emph{signed incidence vector}
$a_e$ of a bar $e$ has one entry for each body: $1$ at the head of $e$,
$-1$ at its tail and $0$ elsewhere, so that $a_e = 0$ if $e$ is a loop.
Its entries are integers, so we may read it over any ring. Over every
field the vectors $a_e$ represent the cycle matroid: a set of bars is a
forest if and only if its signed incidence vectors are linearly
independent~\cite[Lemma~5.1.3]{oxley2011}. Reversing a bar changes the
sign of $a_e$, so nothing below depends on the orientation. For a
vector $w$ with one entry for each body and for
$j \in \{1, \dots, k\}$, we write $w^{(j)}$ for the vector with $k$
blocks that has $w$ in block $j$ and $0$ in the other blocks.

\begin{definition}[The $k$-frame matroid]
  \label{def:k-frame-matroid}
  \lean{Graph.kFrameMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-union}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph and let $k \ge 1$. Let $R = \Q[X_{e,j}]$ be
  the polynomial ring over $\Q$ in one indeterminate $X_{e,j}$ for each
  bar $e$ and each $j \in \{1, \dots, k\}$, and let $K$ be its field of
  fractions. The \emph{$k$-frame row} of a bar $e$ is
  \[
    \rho_e \;=\; \sum_{j = 1}^{k} X_{e,j}\, a_e^{(j)}
    \;=\; \bigl(X_{e,1}\, a_e, \dots, X_{e,k}\, a_e\bigr),
  \]
  where $a_e$ is the signed incidence vector of $e$.
  The \emph{$k$-frame matroid} $F(G, X)$ is the matroid on $E(G)$ in
  which a set of bars is independent if and only if their $k$-frame
  rows are linearly independent over $K$.
\end{definition}

Whiteley's matrix groups its columns by body. Grouped by block instead,
as in the proof of his Theorem~1, its rows are the rows $\rho_e$. By
\cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}, it suffices to show that a
set $E'$ of bars is independent in $F(G, X)$ if and only if
$G \restriction E'$ is $(k, k)$-sparse
(\cref{lem:k-frame-indep-iff-count}).

Suppose that the rows of $E'$ are linearly independent, and let
$Y \subseteq E'$. The rows of the bars in $Y$ lie in a subspace of
dimension $k\, r(Y)$, so $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$
(\cref{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest}). Conversely, suppose that
$G \restriction E'$ is $(k, k)$-sparse. Then $E'$ is the union of $k$
disjoint forests. Setting $X_{e,j} = 1$ when $e$ lies in the $j$-th
forest, and every other indeterminate to $0$, turns the rows of $E'$
into a block-diagonal matrix whose blocks are incidence matrices of
forests. Its rows are linearly independent, and hence so are the rows
of $E'$ (\cref{lem:k-frame-specialize-forest}).

The first direction rests on three facts of linear algebra. For a
subspace $W$ of a vector space $U$, we write $W^k$ for the subspace of
$U^k$ of the vectors whose $k$ blocks all lie in $W$.

\begin{lemma}[The $k$-frame rows lie in a product of incidence spans]
  \label{lem:k-frame-span-le-pi}
  \lean{Graph.span_kFrameRow_le_blockPiSpan, Graph.kFrameRow_mem_blockPiSpan,
    Graph.blockPiSpan}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid}
  Let $A$ be the span over $K$ of the signed incidence vectors of the
  bars of $G$. Then the $k$-frame row of every bar lies in $A^k$, and
  hence so does the span of all $k$-frame rows.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Block $j$ of $\rho_e$ is $X_{e,j}\, a_e$, which lies in $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The dimension of a product subspace]
  \label{lem:k-frame-pi-finrank}
  \lean{Graph.finrank_constPiSpan, Graph.constPiSpanEquiv}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-span-le-pi}
  Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional subspace of a vector space $U$ over
  $K$. Then $\dim W^k = k \dim W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Taking a vector of $W^k$ to its $k$ blocks is a linear isomorphism
  from $W^k$ onto the product of $k$ copies of $W$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The span of a represented set has dimension its rank]
  \label{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}
  \lean{Matroid.Rep.finrank_span_image_eq_rk,
    Graph.finrank_span_signedIncMatrix_eq_cycleMatroid_rk}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid}
  Let $M$ be a matroid of finite rank, represented over a field by
  vectors $v_e$: a set $I$ is independent in $M$ if and only if the
  vectors $v_e$, $e \in I$, are linearly independent. Then for every
  set $Y$ of elements of $M$, the span of $\{v_e : e \in Y\}$ has
  dimension $r_M(Y)$. In particular, for every set $Y$ of bars of $G$,
  the span over $K$ of $\{a_e : e \in Y\}$ has dimension $r(Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $I$ be a basis of $Y$ in $M$. For $e \in Y \setminus I$ the set
  $I \cup \{e\}$ is dependent, so $v_e$ lies in the span of
  $\{v_f : f \in I\}$. Hence this span is the span of
  $\{v_e : e \in Y\}$. The vectors $v_f$, $f \in I$, are linearly
  independent, so it has dimension $|I| = r_M(Y)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent $k$-frame rows satisfy the count]
  \label{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest}
  \lean{Graph.forest_count_of_linearIndepOn_kFrameRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, lem:k-frame-span-le-pi, lem:k-frame-pi-finrank,
    lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}
  Let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set of bars whose $k$-frame rows are
  linearly independent over $K$. Then $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$ for every
  $Y \subseteq E'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, lem:k-frame-span-le-pi, lem:k-frame-pi-finrank,
    lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}
  Let $Y \subseteq E'$, and let $A_Y$ be the span over $K$ of
  $\{a_e : e \in Y\}$. The $|Y|$ rows $\rho_e$ with $e \in Y$ are
  linearly independent. They lie in $A_Y^k$, by the argument of
  \cref{lem:k-frame-span-le-pi} with $Y$ in place of $E(G)$
  (\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}span\_\allowbreak{}kFrameRowOn\_\allowbreak{}le\_\allowbreak{}blockPiSpanOn}).
  By \cref{lem:k-frame-pi-finrank} and
  \cref{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk},
  $\dim A_Y^k = k \dim A_Y = k\, r(Y)$. Hence $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$.

  Whiteley proves this direction from a nonzero minor of order $|E'|$.
  Some monomial of the minor is nonzero, and setting its indeterminates
  to $1$ and the others to $0$ exhibits $E'$ as a union of $k$ forests.
  The dimension bound gives the count without expanding the
  determinant.
\end{proof}

Substituting numbers for the indeterminates is a ring homomorphism on
$R$, but it is not defined on all of $K$. So for the converse we work
over $R$, which contains the entries of every $k$-frame row.

\begin{lemma}[A forest packing gives independent block vectors]
  \label{lem:k-frame-specialize-li}
  \lean{Graph.specRow_linearIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid}
  Let $F_1, \dots, F_k$ be forests of $G$. Then the vectors
  $a_e^{(j)}$, one for each pair $(j, e)$ with $e \in F_j$, are
  linearly independent over $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For each $j$, the vectors $a_e$ with $e \in F_j$ are linearly
  independent, because $F_j$ is a forest. A linear relation among the
  vectors $a_e^{(j)}$ is, block by block, a linear relation among the
  vectors $a_e$ with $e \in F_j$ for each $j$, so it is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independence over $K$ and over $R$]
  \label{lem:k-frame-li-over-poly-ring}
  \lean{Graph.linearIndepOn_kFrameRow_iff_over_polyRing}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid}
  Let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set of bars. Then the $k$-frame rows of
  the bars in $E'$ are linearly independent over $K$ if and only if they
  are linearly independent over $R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A nontrivial linear relation over $R$ is one over $K$. Conversely,
  multiplying a nontrivial linear relation over $K$ by a common
  denominator of its coefficients gives one over $R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A $(k, k)$-sparse set of bars is a union of $k$ forests]
  \label{lem:k-frame-forest-packing-of-sparse}
  \lean{Graph.exists_forestPacking_cover_of_isSparse_restrict}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  Let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set of bars such that $G \restriction E'$
  is $(k, k)$-sparse. Then $E' = F_1 \cup \dots \cup F_k$ for some
  forests $F_1, \dots, F_k$ of $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}, $E'$ is independent in
  the union of $k$ copies of the cycle matroid. By
  \cref{lem:union-indep-iff}, it is the union of $k$ sets independent in
  the cycle matroid, that is, of $k$ forests.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Specializing the $k$-frame rows on a forest packing]
  \label{lem:k-frame-specialize-identity}
  \lean{Graph.forestEval_kFrameRowR_eq_single, Graph.kFrameRowR, Graph.forestEval}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, lem:k-frame-specialize-li}
  Let $F_1, \dots, F_k$ be pairwise disjoint forests of $G$, and let
  $\varphi \colon R \to \Q$ be the ring homomorphism with
  $\varphi(X_{e,j}) = 1$ if $e \in F_j$ and $\varphi(X_{e,j}) = 0$
  otherwise. Let $e \in F_{j_0}$. Then applying $\varphi$ to each entry
  of $\rho_e$ gives $a_e^{(j_0)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Block $j$ of $\rho_e$ is $X_{e,j}\, a_e$. The entries of $a_e$ are
  $0$ and $\pm 1$, so $\varphi$ maps this block to
  $\varphi(X_{e,j})\, a_e$. Here $\varphi(X_{e,j_0}) = 1$. For
  $j \ne j_0$ we have $e \notin F_j$, as the forests are disjoint, so
  $\varphi(X_{e,j}) = 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization defines the $k$-frame row twice, with entries in
  $R$ (\texttt{Graph.kFrameRowR}) and with entries in $K$, and proves
  that the second is the image of the first under the inclusion of $R$
  in $K$
  (\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}kFrameRow\_\allowbreak{}eq\_\allowbreak{}map\_\allowbreak{}kFrameRowR}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse sets of bars have independent $k$-frame rows]
  \label{lem:k-frame-specialize-forest}
  \lean{Graph.linearIndepOn_kFrameRow_of_isSparse_restrict}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, lem:k-frame-specialize-li, lem:k-frame-li-over-poly-ring,
    lem:k-frame-forest-packing-of-sparse, lem:k-frame-specialize-identity}
  Let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set of bars such that $G \restriction E'$
  is $(k, k)$-sparse. Then the $k$-frame rows of the bars in $E'$ are
  linearly independent over $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid}
  We follow the converse half of Whiteley's
  proof~\cite[Theorem~1]{whiteley1988}. By
  \cref{lem:k-frame-forest-packing-of-sparse}, $E'$ is the union of
  forests $F_1, \dots, F_k$. We may take them pairwise disjoint, by
  replacing each $F_j$ with $F_j \setminus (F_1 \cup \dots \cup F_{j-1})$,
  since a subset of a forest is a forest. Let $\varphi$ be the
  homomorphism of \cref{lem:k-frame-specialize-identity}. It maps the
  row of each bar $e \in F_j$ to $a_e^{(j)}$. These vectors are
  linearly independent over $K$ (\cref{lem:k-frame-specialize-li}), and
  their entries are rational, so they are linearly independent over
  $\Q$.

  Let $P$ be the matrix over $R$ whose rows are the $k$-frame rows of
  the bars in $E'$. The matrix $\varphi(P)$ has linearly independent
  rows, so it has a nonzero minor of order $|E'|$. The same minor of $P$
  is a polynomial, and $\varphi$ maps it to that nonzero minor of
  $\varphi(P)$. Hence it is nonzero, and the rows of $P$ are linearly
  independent over $R$
  (\texttt{Matrix.\allowbreak{}linearIndependent\_\allowbreak{}rows\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}specialized\_\allowbreak{}submatrix\_\allowbreak{}det\_\allowbreak{}ne\_\allowbreak{}zero}).
  By \cref{lem:k-frame-li-over-poly-ring}, they are linearly independent
  over $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independence in the $k$-frame matroid by the count]
  \label{lem:k-frame-indep-iff-count}
  \lean{Graph.kFrameMatroid_indep_iff_isSparse_restrict}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest,
    lem:k-frame-specialize-forest, thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  Let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set of bars. Then $E'$ is independent in
  $F(G, X)$ if and only if $G \restriction E'$ is $(k, k)$-sparse.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest, lem:k-frame-specialize-forest}
  The set $E'$ is independent in $F(G, X)$ if and only if its $k$-frame
  rows are linearly independent over $K$. If they are, then
  $|Y| \le k\, r(Y)$ for every $Y \subseteq E'$
  (\cref{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest}). So $E'$ is independent
  in the union of $k$ copies of the cycle matroid, by Edmonds' theorem
  (\cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}). Then $G \restriction E'$ is
  $(k, k)$-sparse by \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}. The
  converse is \cref{lem:k-frame-specialize-forest}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The $k$-frame matroid is a union of cycle matroids;
  Whiteley Theorem~1]
  \label{thm:k-frame-union-cycle}
  \lean{Graph.kFrameMatroid_eq_unionPow_cycleMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-frame-matroid, def:matroid-union,
    lem:k-frame-indep-iff-count, thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph and let $k \ge 1$. Then the $k$-frame matroid
  $F(G, X)$ is the union of $k$ copies of the cycle matroid of $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-indep-iff-count, thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  Both matroids have ground set $E(G)$, so it suffices to show that they
  have the same independent sets. By \cref{lem:k-frame-indep-iff-count},
  a set $E' \subseteq E(G)$ is independent in $F(G, X)$ if and only if
  $G \restriction E'$ is $(k, k)$-sparse. By
  \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}, the same holds in the
  union.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:k-frame-union-restriction}
  In the formalization the bars of $G$ form a subset $E(G)$ of a type
  of edge labels, and the union of the cycle matroids is a matroid on
  all the labels, in which a label outside $E(G)$ is a loop. This is how
  the formalization treats unions of matroids on different ground sets
  (\cref{def:matroid-union}). The formal theorem therefore states that
  $F(G, X)$ is the restriction of that union to $E(G)$.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Tree-packing as a corollary}
\label{sec:body-bar-tree-packing}

Applied to all the bars of $G$,
\cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse} gives the tree-packing
theorem of Tutte and Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961},
and for a connected tight multigraph the forests are spanning trees.

\begin{theorem}[Tutte--Nash-Williams tree-packing]
  \label{thm:tutte-nash-williams}
  \lean{Graph.tutte_nash_williams}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-sparse, thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  A multigraph $G$ is the edge-disjoint union of $k$ forests if and
  only if $G$ is $(k, k)$-sparse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse, lem:union-indep-iff}
  Edge-disjoint $k$-forest decomposition is exactly independence of
  the whole edge set in the cycle-matroid union
  (\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}, with the forests as the per-copy
  independent sets), which \cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}
  identifies with $(k, k)$-sparsity. The disjointness is free: an
  acyclic cover disjointifies (subsets of forests are forests), and a
  disjoint cover is a cover.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Spanning-tree refinement]
  \label{cor:k-spanning-trees}
  \lean{Graph.isSpanningTreePacking_of_isTight}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-sparse, thm:tutte-nash-williams}
  If $G$ is connected and $(k, k)$-tight then the $k$ edge-disjoint
  forests of \cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams} are spanning trees. This
  is the ``union of $d$ spanning trees'' form of Tay's theorem
  (Whiteley Theorem~13).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:tutte-nash-williams}
  Connectivity gives $r(G.\mathrm{cycleMatroid}) = |V| - 1$, so the
  tight global equality $|E| = k|V| - k$ reads as $|E| = k \cdot r$.
  The $k$ forests cover $E$ disjointly, so $\sum_{i} |F_i| = |E| = k
  \cdot r$, while each forest is independent and hence $|F_i| \le r$;
  equality of the sum with $k \cdot r$ forces $|F_i| = r$ for every
  $i$, so each $F_i$ is a maximal acyclic set, i.e.\ a spanning tree.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Body-bar frameworks and the rigidity matrix}
\label{sec:body-bar-framework}

Following Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1988} \S3 and Tay~\cite{tay1984}
\S5, a body-bar framework assigns each bar a two-extensor (Pl\"ucker)
coordinate in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d = n(n+1)/2$; the rigidity map is
the bar-constraint linear map analogous to the bar-joint rigidity
map of \cref{sec:frameworks}.

\begin{definition}[Body-bar framework]
  \label{def:body-bar-framework}
  \lean{Graph.bodyBarDim, Graph.BodyBarFramework}
  \leanok
  A \emph{body-bar framework} in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ on a multigraph
  $G$ pairs $G$ with a placement assigning each bar
  $e \in E(G)$ a two-extensor coordinate in
  $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d = n(n+1)/2$ (a point of the Pl\"ucker
  embedding of $\mathrm{Gr}(2, n+1)$, with degenerate coordinates
  permitted).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Body-bar rigidity map]
  \label{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.rigidityMap}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework}
  Fix an orientation of $G$, giving each bar $e$ an ordered pair of
  endpoints $(u, v)$. The \emph{rigidity map} of a body-bar framework
  is the linear map from body motions — a velocity $m(v) \in
  \mathbb{R}^{d}$ assigned to each body $v$ — to bar constraints (one
  real number per bar), sending $m$ to the family $e \mapsto
  \langle b_{e},\, m(u) - m(v)\rangle$, with one row per bar (the
  constraint row of Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1988} \S3; $b_{e}$ is $e$'s
  two-extensor coordinate). The orientation only fixes the sign of each
  row; the kernel and rank are orientation-independent.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Infinitesimal rigidity of a body-bar framework]
  \label{def:infinitesimally-rigid-body-bar}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.IsInfinitesimallyRigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}
  A body-bar framework on $b$ bodies is \emph{infinitesimally rigid}
  when its rigidity map has rank $d \cdot b - d$, i.e.\ its kernel is
  exactly the $d$-dimensional space of trivial screw motions ---
  the body-bar analogue of \cref{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Independence of a body-bar framework]
  \label{def:independent-body-bar}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.IsIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}
  A body-bar framework is \emph{independent} when its rigidity map has
  full row rank, $\mathrm{rank} = |E(G)|$ --- equivalently its bar
  constraints are linearly independent, with no redundant bars. The
  body-bar analogue of edge-set row independence
  (\cref{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Tay's theorem (existence-of-realization form)}
\label{sec:body-bar-tay}

The chapter's target, in Whiteley's witness form~\cite{whiteley1988}
(Theorem~8). We treat only the \emph{existence-of-realization} form:
there exists an independent (resp.\ isostatic) body-bar framework if and
only if the count holds, witnessed by the standard-basis specialization.
Whiteley's stronger ``almost all realizations are rigid'' phrasing,
via the irreducible-variety lift of Proposition~6
in~\cite{whiteley1988}, is not pursued here; the generic form is
instead obtained coordinate-wise, following Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}, in \cref{sec:generic-lift}.

\begin{theorem}[Tay's theorem, independent form; Whiteley Theorem~8]
  \label{thm:tay-witness}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.tay_witness}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:infinitesimally-rigid-body-bar,
    def:independent-body-bar, def:graph-sparse, thm:tutte-nash-williams,
    cor:k-spanning-trees}
  Let $n \ge 2$ and $d = n(n+1)/2$. A multigraph $G$ carries an
  independent body-bar framework in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ if and only if $G$ is
  $(d, d)$-sparse (equivalently, by \cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams},
  $G$ is the union of $d$ edge-disjoint forests). It carries an
  \emph{isostatic} (independent and infinitesimally rigid) body-bar
  framework if and only if $G$ is $(d, d)$-tight (equivalently, by
  \cref{cor:k-spanning-trees}, the union of $d$ spanning trees) ---
  the count $|E| = d(b - 1)$ of Tay~\cite{tay1984}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{prop:tay-witness-exists, lem:tay-isSparse-of-independent}
  The ($\Leftarrow$) directions are \cref{prop:tay-witness-exists};
  the ($\Rightarrow$) sparsity converse is
  \cref{lem:tay-isSparse-of-independent}, and the isostatic count
  follows by substituting independence ($\mathrm{rank} = |E|$) into the
  infinitesimal-rigidity equality $\mathrm{rank} + d = d \cdot b$ to get
  $|E| + d = d|V|$, i.e.\ $(d, d)$-tightness.
\end{proof}

The two halves are formalized separately: the witness ($\Leftarrow$)
direction as \cref{prop:tay-witness-exists}, and the converse
($\Rightarrow$, an independent body-bar framework forces
$(d, d)$-sparsity) as \cref{lem:tay-isSparse-of-independent}, the
body-bar analogue of the Lov\'asz--Yemini rank-upper-bound argument
(\cref{lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two}).

\begin{proposition}[Tay witness, existence direction]
  \label{prop:tay-witness-exists}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.exists_isIndependent_of_isSparse,
    Graph.BodyBarFramework.exists_isIsostatic_of_isTight}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:independent-body-bar,
    def:infinitesimally-rigid-body-bar, def:graph-sparse,
    thm:tutte-nash-williams}
  Let $d = n(n+1)/2$. If a multigraph $G$ is $(d, d)$-sparse then it
  carries an independent body-bar framework in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, and
  if $G$ is $(d, d)$-tight then it carries an isostatic one (the count
  $|E| = d(b - 1)$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-map-body-bar, thm:tutte-nash-williams}
  By \cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams}, $(d, d)$-sparsity gives a
  disjoint cover of $E(G)$ by $d$ forests; take the standard-basis
  framework $b_{e} = e_{j(e)}$ for the forest index $j(e)$ of each bar.
  The block-diagonal rank count makes this framework independent
  ($\mathrm{rank} = |E(G)|$). For the tight case, independence
  ($\mathrm{rank} = |E|$) together with the tightness equality
  $|E| + d = d|V|$ gives the infinitesimal-rigidity count
  $\mathrm{rank} + d = d \cdot b$ directly, since $b = |V(G)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Tay converse: independent $\Rightarrow$ sparse]
  \label{lem:tay-isSparse-of-independent}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.isSparse_of_isIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:independent-body-bar,
    def:rigidity-map-body-bar, def:graph-sparse, def:k-frame-matroid}
  Let $d = n(n+1)/2$. If a body-bar framework $F$ on $G$ is independent,
  then $G$ is $(d, d)$-sparse: every non-empty bar set $E' \subseteq E(G)$
  spanning $V'$ satisfies $|E'| + d \le d|V'|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest}
  The body-bar analogue of the Lov\'asz--Yemini rank-upper-bound
  (\cref{lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two}). Independence makes all
  $|E(G)|$ rigidity rows linearly independent, so on each $E'$ the $|E'|$
  rows span a subspace of dimension $|E'|$. The row for a bar $e$ is the
  inner product $m \mapsto \langle b_{e}, m_{u} - m_{v}\rangle$, a
  real-coefficient combination $\sum_{c} (b_{e})_{c}\cdot(\text{block-}c$
  incidence row of $e)$ across the $d$ coordinate blocks --- the real
  specialization of the indeterminate $k$-frame row of
  \cref{lem:k-frame-nonzero-monomial-forest}. So the rows on $E'$ lie in
  the block-diagonal product subspace of dimension $d \cdot r(E')$
  ($r$ the cycle-matroid rank), giving $|E'| \le d\,r(E')$. With
  $r(E') + 1 \le |V'|$ (a non-empty bar set spans at least one
  non-trivial component), $|E'| \le d(|V'| - 1)$, i.e.\ $|E'| + d \le d|V'|$.
\end{proof}
