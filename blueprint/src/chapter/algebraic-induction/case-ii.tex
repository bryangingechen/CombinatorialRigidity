\subsection{Case II: no proper rigid subgraph and \texorpdfstring{$k > 0$}{k > 0}}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseII}

This subsection proves Case~II of KT Theorem~5.5, in which the minimal
$k$-dof-graph $G$ is $2$-edge-connected, has at least three vertices
and no proper rigid subgraph, and $k > 0$ (KT \S6.3). Katoh and
Tanigawa split off a vertex $v$ of degree two, whose edges join it to
vertices $a$ and $b$. The graph $G_v^{ab}$ is a minimal
$(k-1)$-dof-graph with fewer vertices, simple by KT Lemma~6.7
(\cref{lem:splitoff-simple}), and the induction hypothesis realizes
it. They then put $v$ back, with the hinge of the edge $vb$
equal to that of the new edge $ab$, and the rank rises by $D - 1$ (KT
Lemma~6.8). Katoh and Tanigawa note that this is essentially the idea
of Whiteley's proof that the $1$-extension preserves the infinitesimal
rigidity of bar-joint frameworks~\cite[Theorem~2.2.2]{whiteley1996}
(KT p.~650).

The first two lemmas concern putting back the body $v$. The first pins
one body (\cref{lem:case-II-rank-lift}). The second says that the
infinitesimal motions of a framework on $G$ are constant on $V(G)$
exactly when they are constant on the other vertices and agree there
with their value at $v$ (\cref{lem:case-II}). The proof of KT
Lemma~6.8 (\cref{lem:case-II-realization}) counts rows instead, and
does not use them.

\begin{remark}[Katoh and Tanigawa's steps in Case~II and the formal proof]
  \label{rem:case-II-kt-steps}
  Two lemmas of this subsection and three of
  \cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative} belong to Katoh and
  Tanigawa's proof of KT Lemma~6.8, or state facts about it for
  infinitesimal motions. The formal proof of KT Theorem~5.5
  (\cref{thm:theorem-55}) does not take them. We keep them beside the
  argument they belong to, and say here what the formal proof does
  instead.

  \emph{Putting back $v$.} Let $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ be the realization that
  the induction hypothesis gives, of rank $D(|V(G)| - 2) - (k - 1)$ (KT
  eq.~(6.11), p.~678). Katoh and Tanigawa put $v$ back with the hinge
  of $vb$ equal to the hinge $q(ab)$ of the edge $ab$. This gives a
  realization $(G, p_1)$ (KT eq.~(6.12), p.~678). In the formal proof
  the panel of $v$ has the normal $q(a) + q(b)$, and the realization is
  $(G, q_0)$ (the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}). The first
  two lemmas of this subsection state facts about putting back $v$ for
  infinitesimal motions, which Katoh and Tanigawa do not use.
  \Cref{lem:case-II-rank-lift} reads KT Lemma~5.1 at a given rank:
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + k'$ exactly when the motions that vanish at $v$
  form a space of dimension $k'$. By its second assertion, replacing the
  normal of $v$ in a panel-hinge framework in which $v$ lies on no edge,
  as in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, does not change the dimension of this space.
  Every motion is constant on $V(G)$ exactly when it is constant on the
  other vertices and takes there its value at $v$ (\cref{lem:case-II}).

  The induction hypothesis gives the rank of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$. The last
  step of the formal proof starts from a lower bound on the rank of
  $R(G, q_0)$ (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). So the formal
  proof counts rows throughout, as Katoh and Tanigawa do, and it does not
  pass to motions. Besides, \cref{lem:case-II} concerns the rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$, at which every motion is constant on $V(G)$
  (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). In Case~II, where $k > 0$, the
  target rank is smaller.

  \emph{The rows of $G_v^{ab}$.} The edges of $G_v$ have the same hinges
  in $(G, p_1)$ as in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, so the two rigidity matrices
  have the same rows at these edges (KT eq.~(6.13), p.~679).
  \Cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows} states the consequence for any
  two frameworks: linearly independent rows of one, each of which is
  also a row of the other, are linearly independent rows of the other.
  The edge $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$ is not an edge of $G$, however, and its
  rows are not rows of $R(G, p_1)$. Katoh and Tanigawa add the columns
  of $v$ to those of $a$. The rows of $vb$ then have, outside the
  columns of $v$, the entries of the rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$
  (KT eqs.~(6.15)--(6.17), p.~679). The formal proof uses a row
  operation instead. At $q_0$ the hinges of $vb$, $va$ and $ab$ agree up
  to sign, so each row at $ab$ is the difference of a row at $vb$ and a
  row at $va$. Hence every row of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ lies in the row space
  of $R(G, q_0)$ (the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement}).

  The rank then adds, as in KT eq.~(6.16). Some $D - 1$ rows at $vb$ are
  linearly independent on the screw assignments that vanish off $v$,
  and the rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ vanish there
  (\cref{lem:rigidityRows-pinned-placement-rank-add}). The linearly
  independent rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ that this count takes may include
  rows at $ab$. So the formal proof does not use
  \cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows}.

  \emph{One row short.} For $k = 0$, the graph $G_v^{ab}$ is a minimal
  $0$-dof-graph, and the same count gives one row less than the target
  rank (KT p.~680). \Cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement} states this
  count from a realization of $G_v^{ab}$ whose motions are all constant
  on its vertex set, and its proof makes it first from a realization of
  any rank. For $k > 0$, the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}
  takes the count in this form. In Case~III,
  where $k = 0$, the formal proof obtains the full rank of each of its
  frameworks from the count of KT eq.~(6.29)
  (\cref{lem:case-III-rank-certification}).

  \emph{The rotation.} In $(G, p_1)$ the hinges at $v$ lie in the panel
  of $a$ (KT p.~678). Katoh and Tanigawa rotate the panel of $v$ slightly, which
  does not lower the rank, to make the realization nonparallel (KT
  Lemma~5.2, KT p.~679). For the rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$, two lemmas of
  \cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative} pass instead from
  linearly independent rows to a realization. Suppose that at some
  normals, at least $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ rows of the rigidity matrix are
  linearly independent. Then \cref{lem:relative-device-count} gives
  normals at which $\dim Z(G,q)$ is at most its value at that rank. \Cref{lem:realization-of-independent-rows} gives a realization
  of $G$ of that rank. Both hold at the given normals, by their proofs.
  Their formal counterparts pass to normals off the zeros of a minor of
  these rows (the notes after them).

  Neither lemma serves the formal proof. In Case~II the count gives
  only $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$ rows, with $k > 0$, and the lemmas say
  nothing of the position of the normals. The formal proof needs a
  generic full-rank realization: normals in general position, at the
  target rank (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). Instead of rotating a panel,
  it chooses all the normals at once. It keeps a minor of the rows as a
  polynomial in the normals, nonzero at $q_0$, off whose zeros the rank
  is at least $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$
  (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). At a common non-root of
  this polynomial and of the polynomial for general position, the
  normals are in general position and the rank is at least
  $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$ (the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}).
  As the hinges there are nondegenerate, the rank there is at most the
  target rank (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{remark}

By \cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}, a realization of $G$ whose
bodies are the vertices of $G$ is at the target rank
(\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) exactly when
$\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. The first assertion of
the next lemma restates KT Lemma~5.1 (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}) in
this form.

\begin{lemma}[Pinning one body]
  \label{lem:case-II-rank-lift}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_iff_finrank_pinnedMotions,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_withNormal_iff_finrank_pinnedMotions}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, def:framework-with-normal,
        lem:with-normal-preserves}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, let $v$ be a body, and let
  $k'$ be an integer. Then $\dim Z(G,p) = D + k'$ if and only if the
  space $\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ of the infinitesimal motions $S$ with
  $S(v) = 0$ has dimension $k'$ (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}). Let
  $(G,n)$ be a panel-hinge framework in which $v$ lies on no edge, and
  let $(G,n')$ be obtained by replacing the normal of $v$
  (\cref{def:framework-with-normal}). Then $\dim Z(G,n') = D + k'$ if
  and only if $\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ has dimension $k'$ in $(G,n)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:with-normal-preserves}
  By \cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex},
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \dim \operatorname{pinned}(v)$, which gives the
  first assertion. If $v$ lies on no edge of $G$, then $(G,n)$ and
  $(G,n')$ have the same subspace $\operatorname{pinned}(v)$
  (\cref{lem:with-normal-preserves}). So the second assertion is the
  first, applied to $(G,n')$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem is the assertion for body-hinge frameworks,
  and the second the one for panel-hinge frameworks. As in
  \cref{lem:with-normal-preserves}, the second assumes that the pair of
  bodies that the endpoint selector assigns to each edge of $G$ does
  not contain $v$ (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Putting back a body of degree two]
  \label{lem:case-II}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_insert_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph with $k > 0$, let $v$ be a vertex
  of $G$ of degree two, and let $t = V(G) \setminus \{v\}$. Then every
  infinitesimal motion of a body-hinge framework $(G,p)$ is constant on
  $V(G)$ if and only if every infinitesimal motion $S$ is constant on $t$
  and satisfies $S(v) = S(w)$ for every $w \in t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As $v$ is a vertex of $G$, we have $V(G) = t \cup \{v\}$. A screw
  assignment $S$ is constant on $t \cup \{v\}$ if and only if it is
  constant on $t$ and $S(v) = S(w)$ for every $w \in t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is the assertion for every body-hinge framework,
  every set $t$ of bodies and every body $v$, with $t \cup \{v\}$ in
  place of $V(G)$; the statement is its case $t = V(G) \setminus \{v\}$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Case~II: splitting off a vertex of degree two; KT Lemma~6.8]
  \label{lem:case-II-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_II_realization_all_k}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a
  $2$-edge-connected minimal $k$-dof-graph with $k > 0$, $|V(G)| \ge 3$
  and no proper rigid subgraph. Suppose that for every $k'$, every
  minimal $k'$-dof-graph with at least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$
  vertices has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization, and a generic
  full-rank realization if it is simple. Then $G$ has a generic
  full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), of rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:reduction-step-pos,
        lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank, lem:splitoff-simple,
        lem:reduction-measure, lem:simple-minimal-noRigid,
        lem:reducible-vertex, lem:general-position-support-nonzero,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound,
        lem:case-II-realization-placement,
        lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of KT Lemma~6.8
  (pp.~677--679). The graph $G$ is simple
  (\cref{lem:simple-minimal-noRigid}). As $d \ge 2$, we have $D \ge 3$,
  so $G$ has a vertex $v$ of degree two (\cref{lem:reducible-vertex};
  KT Lemma~4.6). Its edges $e_a$ and $e_b$ join it to vertices $a$ and
  $b$, and $a \neq b$ as $G$ is simple.

  Next, $G_v^{ab}$ is simple, by KT Lemma~6.7
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-simple}). If $|V(G)| \ge 4$, this is its last
  assertion. Let $|V(G)| = 3$. If an edge of $G$ joined $a$ and $b$, then
  this edge, $e_a$ and $e_b$ would be all the edges of the simple graph
  $G$. So $G$ would be a triangle, and a $0$-dof-graph by the first
  assertion of KT Lemma~6.7, contradicting $k > 0$. So no edge of $G$ joins $a$ and $b$, and $G_v^{ab}$ is simple,
  as in the proof of \cref{lem:splitoff-simple}.

  By KT Lemma~4.8(ii) (\cref{lem:reduction-step-pos}), $G_v^{ab}$ is a
  minimal $(k-1)$-dof-graph. Its vertices are the $|V(G)| - 1 \ge 2$
  vertices of $G$ other than $v$ (\cref{lem:reduction-measure}). So the
  induction hypothesis gives a generic full-rank realization
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, with
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) \;=\;
      D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr) - (k - 1),
  \]
  which is KT eq.~(6.11). Its normals are in general position, so the
  normals $n_a = q(a)$ and $n_b = q(b)$ are linearly independent.

  Katoh and Tanigawa keep the hinges of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at the edges of
  $G_v$. They give the edge $vb$ the hinge $q(ab)$ of the edge $ab$, and
  the edge $va$ a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace $L$ of the panel
  $\Pi(a)$ (KT eq.~(6.12)). With the panel $\Pi(a)$ also at $v$, this is
  a panel-hinge realization $(G, p_1)$ for every such $L$ (KT
  Claim~6.9). They add the columns of $v$ to those of $a$ (KT
  eqs.~(6.14) and~(6.15)). As $p_1(vb) = q(ab)$, the rows of $vb$ and of
  the edges of $G_v$ then have, outside the columns of $v$, the entries
  of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ (KT eqs.~(6.16) and~(6.17)). The rows of $va$
  vanish outside the columns of $v$, and they have rank $D - 1$ there.
  So
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G, p_1) \;\ge\;
      (D - 1) + \operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q)
    \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - k.
  \]
  Finally they rotate the panel of $v$ slightly, which does not lower
  the rank, to make the realization nonparallel (KT Lemma~5.2,
  KT p.~679).

  In a panel-hinge framework the hinge of an edge is the meet of the
  panels of its ends (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), while Katoh and
  Tanigawa ask only that it lie in both panels. For the hinge of $vb$
  to be $q(ab) = \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b)$, the panel $\Pi(v)$ must contain
  $\Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b)$. It must also differ from $\Pi(a)$, as otherwise
  the hinge of $va$ would be degenerate. Then the hinge
  $\Pi(v) \cap \Pi(a)$ of $va$ is again $\Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b)$, so that
  $L = q(ab)$.

  The normal $n_a + n_b$ gives such a panel, as $n_a$ and $n_b$ are
  linearly independent. Let $q_0$ be the normals with
  $q_0(v) = n_a + n_b$ and $q_0(w) = q(w)$ for every body $w \neq v$,
  and let $(G, q_0)$ be the panel-hinge framework on $G$ with these
  normals. As $C$ is bilinear and $C(n,n) = 0$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), the edges $vb$ and $va$ of
  $(G, q_0)$ have, up to sign, the hinge $C(n_a, n_b)$ of the edge $ab$
  of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$. Every other edge of $G$ is an edge of $G_v$, and
  so of $G_v^{ab}$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}). Its ends
  differ from $v$, so it has the same hinge in both frameworks, up to
  sign.

  We now count rows as Katoh and Tanigawa do, with a row operation in
  place of their column operation. The hinges of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ are
  nondegenerate, as its normals are in general position
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). So the count of
  \cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement}, with $t = 1$ and
  $N = D(|V(G)| - 2) - (k - 1)$, shows that the hinges of $(G, q_0)$
  are nondegenerate and that
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G, q_0) \;\ge\;
      (D - 1) + D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr) - (k - 1)
    \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - k.
  \]
  Its proof places every row of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ in the row space of
  $R(G, q_0)$, a row at $ab$ being the difference of a row at $vb$ and
  a row at $va$, and adds $D - 1$ rows at $vb$. These are linearly
  independent on the screw assignments that vanish off $v$, where the
  rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ vanish.
  For $k = 0$, the graph $G_v^{ab}$ would be a minimal $0$-dof-graph,
  and the same count would give one row less than the target rank (KT
  p.~680).
  Case~III supplies the missing row
  (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseIII}).

  The normals $q_0$ need not be in general position. Instead of
  rotating a panel, we choose all the normals at once, as in the proof
  of \cref{lem:case-I-realization} and the passage after it. Every hinge
  of $(G, q_0)$ is nonzero, and $R(G, q_0)$ has the rank above. So there
  is a polynomial $Q$ in the normals with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$ such that
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$ wherever
  $Q(q) \neq 0$. This is \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, and
  $Q$ is the minor by which the proof of \cref{lem:genericity-device}
  gives the lower semicontinuity of the rank. Let $P$
  be the polynomial of the proof of \cref{lem:case-I-realization}, so
  that the normals $q$ are in general position wherever $P(q) \neq 0$.
  As $K$ is infinite, the nonzero polynomial $QP$ has a non-root $q'$.

  The rank of $R(G, q')$ is then at least $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$, and the
  normals $q'$ are in general position. As $G$ has no loops, every hinge
  of $(G, q')$ is nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). So the rank is at most
  $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$ (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), and
  $(G, q')$ is a generic full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem also assumes that some edge label lies outside
  $E(G)$, the label of the new edge $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). It then lies outside $E(G_v)$, as the
  count of \cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement} asks
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}).
\end{fmlnote}
