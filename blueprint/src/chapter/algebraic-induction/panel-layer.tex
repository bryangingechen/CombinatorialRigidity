\subsection{Panel-hinge frameworks}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-panel}

As recalled in \cref{sec:body-hinge-tay}, Katoh and Tanigawa call a
body-hinge framework a panel-hinge framework if the hinges at each body
lie in a common hyperplane, the panel of the
body~\cite[p.~648]{katohTanigawa2011}. Their realizations of simple
graphs are built from the panels: each body $v$ has a panel $\Pi(v)$,
and the hinge of an edge $uv$ is the intersection
$\Pi(u) \cap \Pi(v)$~\cite[\S5.1]{katohTanigawa2011}. We build
panel-hinge frameworks in the same way, in homogeneous coordinates. A
panel is given by a normal vector in $K^{d+1}$. The hinge of $uv$ is
recorded by the meet of the two panels (\cref{def:meet}), which we
compute from their normals. The hinges at a body then lie in its panel by
construction, and the extensor of a hinge is nonzero exactly when the
normals at its ends are linearly independent.

This subsection defines these frameworks
(\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). It then gives a condition on the
normals under which every hinge between two distinct bodies has a nonzero
extensor (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). Normals on the moment curve
satisfy it (\cref{lem:moment-curve-general-position}).

\begin{definition}[Panel support extensor]
  \label{def:panel-support-extensor}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.panelSupportExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:join, def:meet-complement-iso, def:hinge-constraint}
  Let $n_1, n_2 \in K^{d+1}$. The \emph{panel support extensor} of $n_1$
  and $n_2$ is
  \[
    C(n_1, n_2) \;=\; *(n_1 \vee n_2)
    \;\in\; \textstyle\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1},
  \]
  the image of the join $n_1 \vee n_2$ (\cref{def:join}) under the
  complement isomorphism $*$ (\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}).
\end{definition}

\begin{fmlnote}
  As in \cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} and \cref{sec:molecular-meet},
  the formalization writes $d = k + 1$, so that normals lie in $K^{k+2}$
  and $C(n_1, n_2)$ lies in $\bigwedge^{k} K^{k+2}$. Several pinned
  theorems of this chapter also take the dimension that determines $D$ in
  the deficiency $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ as a separate parameter,
  with a hypothesis that forces it to equal $k + 1$.
\end{fmlnote}

The extensor $C(n_1, n_2)$ lies in the screw space
$\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ of \cref{def:hinge-constraint}, and
$C(n_2, n_1) = -C(n_1, n_2)$. It is nonzero if and only if $n_1$ and
$n_2$ are linearly independent, since $*$ is an isomorphism and
$n_1 \vee n_2 \neq 0$ exactly in that case (the remark after
\cref{def:extensor}). If they are, then $C(n_1, n_2)$ is the meet of the
hyperplanes $n_1^\perp$ and $n_2^\perp$ of $K^{d+1}$, a nonzero extensor
of their intersection, which has dimension $d - 1$ (the remarks after
\cref{def:meet}).

The panel support extensor describes the hinge of two panels. As in
\cref{sec:molecular-meet} for $d = 3$, the hyperplane
$\{x \in K^d : a \cdot x + a_0 = 0\}$ has the normal
$n = (a, a_0) \in K^{d+1}$, and a point $x$ lies on it exactly when
$\bar x \cdot n = 0$, where $\bar x = (x, 1)$
(\cref{def:homogeneous-coords}). Katoh and Tanigawa's panel
$\{x : x \cdot c(v) + 1 = 0\}$ has the normal
$(c(v), 1)$~\cite[\S5.1]{katohTanigawa2011}. Suppose that the hyperplanes
with normals $n_1$ and $n_2$ meet in a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine
subspace $H$, spanned by affinely independent points
$p_1, \dots, p_{d-1}$. The two hyperplanes are distinct, so $n_1$ and
$n_2$ are linearly independent. The vectors
$\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d-1}$ are linearly independent
(\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}) and orthogonal to $n_1$ and $n_2$. Let
$C(H) = \bar p_1 \vee \cdots \vee \bar p_{d-1}$ be the extensor of $H$
(\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}). It is a nonzero multiple of
$C(n_1, n_2)$
(\texttt{extensor\_\allowbreak{}join\_\allowbreak{}proportional\_\allowbreak{}complementIso\_\allowbreak{}meet}),
so the two give the same hinge constraint.

A panel-hinge framework takes the normals as its data.

\begin{definition}[Panel-hinge framework]
  \label{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:hinge-constraint}
  A \emph{panel-hinge framework} $(G, n)$ is a multigraph $G = (V, E)$
  together with a vector $n_v \in K^{d+1}$ for each body $v$, the normal
  of its panel $\Pi(v)$. Its \emph{body-hinge interpretation} is the
  body-hinge framework $(G, p)$ (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) whose hinge
  at an edge $e$ with ends $u$ and $v$ has the supporting extensor
  \[
    C(p(e)) \;=\; C(n_u, n_v)
  \]
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}).
\end{definition}

Since $C(n_v, n_u) = -C(n_u, n_v)$, the hinge constraint at $e$ does not
depend on the order of its ends. We identify a panel-hinge framework
with its body-hinge interpretation, and write $R(G,p)$ and $Z(G,p)$ for
its rigidity matrix and its space of infinitesimal motions
(\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}). The hinge at an edge $e$ of a body-hinge
framework is \emph{nondegenerate} if $C(p(e)) \neq 0$. In a panel-hinge
framework this means that the normals at the ends of $e$ are linearly
independent. For Katoh and Tanigawa a hinge is a $(d-2)$-dimensional
affine subspace, and its extensor is nonzero (the remark after
\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). Here a hinge may be degenerate. If
$C(p(e)) = 0$, the hinge constraint at $e$ is $S(u) = S(v)$, and it welds
the two bodies together.

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}
  In the formalization a multigraph records which bodies an edge joins as
  a relation. A panel-hinge framework therefore also records, for every
  edge label $e$, an ordered pair of bodies, and the hinge at $e$ is the
  panel support extensor of their normals. We call this record the
  \emph{endpoint selector}. The definition does not tie it to $G$: the
  pair at an edge of $G$ need not be the ends of the edge, and a label
  outside $E(G)$ also gets a pair, which does not affect $R(G,p)$. The
  frameworks of \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework} are those whose selector
  assigns each edge of $G$ its two ends, in either order. In the
  formalization this is a separate condition on the selector, which a
  result states as a hypothesis or as part of its conclusion. The
  endpoint choice of $G$ (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}) satisfies it.
  Likewise, a result that needs nondegenerate hinges assumes them. The
  molecular conjecture (\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}) assumes them at
  every edge label, on both of its sides.
\end{fmlnote}

A nondegenerate hinge at a body $v$ lies in the panel of $v$, since its
extensor $C(n_v, n_w)$ is an extensor of $n_v^\perp \cap n_w^\perp$. So
the body-hinge interpretation of a panel-hinge framework meets Katoh and
Tanigawa's condition~\cite[p.~648]{katohTanigawa2011}, and we use their
name for the body-hinge frameworks that arise in this way.

\begin{definition}[Hinge-coplanar body-hinge framework]
  \label{def:hinge-coplanar}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.IsHingeCoplanar,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isHingeCoplanar_toBodyHinge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:hinge-constraint}
  A body-hinge framework is \emph{hinge-coplanar} if it is the body-hinge
  interpretation of a panel-hinge framework.
\end{definition}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal predicate inherits the endpoint selector of
  \cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}. It holds for a body-hinge
  framework each of whose hinges is the panel support extensor of the
  normals of some two bodies, not necessarily the ends of its edge.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa's condition asks only that the hinges at each body lie
in a common hyperplane. It also admits two bodies with the same panel,
joined by any $(d-2)$-dimensional hinge in that panel. The meet of two
equal panels is zero, so under \cref{def:hinge-coplanar} the hinge
between two bodies with the same panel is degenerate. The containment form
of \cref{def:coplanar-panel-realization}, later in this chapter, admits
such a hinge.

A condition on the normals alone makes every hinge between two distinct
bodies nondegenerate.

\begin{definition}[Panel normals in general position]
  \label{def:panel-general-position}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.IsGeneralPosition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  The normals of a panel-hinge framework $(G, n)$ are in \emph{general
  position} if $n_u$ and $n_v$ are linearly independent for any two
  distinct bodies $u$ and $v$.
\end{definition}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a panel-hinge framework on a simple graph
nonparallel if the panels of any two distinct bodies meet in a
$(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace~\cite[\S5.1]{katohTanigawa2011}. Its
normals are then in general position, since any two of its panels are
distinct. The converse fails for two parallel panels, which meet only at
infinity. Katoh and Tanigawa avoid parallel panels by a projective
transformation, under which infinitesimal rigidity is
invariant~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{lemma}[General position gives nondegenerate hinges]
  \label{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ne_zero_of_isGeneralPosition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-general-position, def:panel-support-extensor}
  Let the normals of a panel-hinge framework $(G, n)$ be in general
  position. Then every edge of $G$ with two distinct ends has a
  nondegenerate hinge.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor}
  Let $e$ have ends $u \neq v$. Then $n_u$ and $n_v$ are linearly
  independent, so $C(p(e)) = C(n_u, n_v) \neq 0$ (the remark after
  \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moment-curve normals are in general position]
  \label{lem:moment-curve-general-position}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.isGeneralPosition_withMomentNormals,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.isGeneralPosition_ofParam}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-general-position}
  Let $(t_v)_{v \in V}$ be distinct elements of $K$, and let
  $n_v = (1, t_v, t_v^2, \dots, t_v^{d})$ be the point of the moment
  curve at $t_v$. Then the normals $n_v$ are in general position. In
  particular, if $K$ is infinite and $V$ is finite, then there are
  normals in general position.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $u \neq v$. The first two coordinates of $n_u$ and $n_v$ form the
  matrix with rows $(1, t_u)$ and $(1, t_v)$, whose determinant
  $t_v - t_u$ is not zero. So $n_u$ and $n_v$ are linearly independent.
  An infinite field has $|V|$ distinct elements for every finite $V$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The generic rank formula (KT Proposition 2.3)}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-prop11}

Katoh and Tanigawa's Proposition~2.3, which they credit to Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}, relates the rank of the rigidity matrix
of a generic realization of a multigraph $G$ to the deficiency
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ and to the rank of the matroid
$M(\tilde G)$~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011}. It has two halves of
different kinds. The matroidal half is KT eq.~(2.4),
$\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
which \cref{thm:def-eq-corank} proves. The analytic half is Jackson and
Jord\'an's rank formula
\[
  \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)
\]
at a generic realization $(G,p)$. We prove it for panel-hinge
realizations with nondegenerate hinges
(\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}).

The analytic half is in turn two inequalities of different kinds. The
upper bound on the rank holds at every realization with nondegenerate
hinges. The lower bound needs one realization that attains the rank, as a
generic realization has the largest rank. Jackson and Jord\'an build such
a realization from $D$ edge-disjoint forests in $\tilde G$ with
$D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ edges in
all~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{jacksonJordan2009}. We take it instead from KT
Theorem~5.5, which this chapter proves by induction
(\cref{thm:theorem-55}), as Katoh and Tanigawa do in their proof of
Theorem~5.6~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{proposition}[Generic rank equals $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$; KT Proposition 2.3]
  \label{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.rigidityMatrix_prop11}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:D-deficiency, thm:def-eq-corank, def:dof-generic, lem:genericity-device}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V \neq \emptyset$, and let
  $(G,p)$ be a generic panel-hinge realization of $G$
  (\cref{def:dof-generic}) whose hinges are all nondegenerate. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  This is Jackson and Jord\'an's rank
  formula~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{jacksonJordan2009}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound,
        lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}
  Let $k = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. Since
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D|V| - \dim Z(G,p)$
  (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}), the claim is that
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + k$.

  \emph{The upper bound on the rank.} By
  \cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}, $\dim Z(G,p) \ge D + k$. These
  motions come from a partition $P$ of $V$ with
  $\operatorname{def}_{\tilde G}(P) = k$. The screw assignments that are
  constant on each part of $P$ form a space of dimension $D|P|$. On this
  space, each edge that joins two parts imposes $D - 1$ linear conditions,
  because its hinge is nondegenerate, and the other edges impose none. So
  at least $D|P| - (D-1)\,d_G(P) = D + k$ dimensions of motions remain.

  \emph{The lower bound on the rank.} If $|V| = 1$, every edge of $G$ is a
  loop, whose rows vanish, and both sides of the formula are $0$. Let
  $|V| \ge 2$. We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of their
  Theorem~5.6 (KT p.~670). The graph $G$ has a spanning subgraph $G'$ that is a
  minimal $k$-dof-graph (\cref{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}). By
  KT Theorem~5.5 (\cref{thm:theorem-55}), $G'$ has a panel-hinge
  realization $(G', q)$ with
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G', q) = D(|V|-1) - k$, that is, with
  $\dim Z(G', q) = D + k$. Extend $q$ to a realization $(G, q)$ of $G$ by
  giving each edge of $G$ that is not in $G'$ any hinge. Adding edges can
  only shrink the space of motions (\cref{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le},
  below), so $\dim Z(G, q) \le D + k$. A generic realization has the
  largest rank over all realizations of $G$ (\cref{def:dof-generic}).
  Hence
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;\ge\; \operatorname{rank} R(G,q)
    \;\ge\; D(|V|-1) - k.
  \]
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization has no notion of a generic realization in the sense
  of \cref{def:dof-generic}. The pinned theorem is stated for a
  body-hinge framework whose supporting extensors are nonzero at every
  edge label. It assumes the lower bound on the rank,
  $\dim Z(G,p) \le D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and derives
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ from the upper bound.
  Its screw assignments are defined on the whole body set, so this is the
  rank formula when $G$ spans the body set. The theorems that apply it,
  such as \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}, take $G$ spanning and prove the
  lower bound on the rank for the realization they construct, by the
  argument of the proof above. For simple spanning graphs on at least two
  bodies, the formula at every generic realization is formalized for
  generic normals (\cref{thm:panel-generic-rank}). It is also formalized
  for generic hinge points (\cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank}).
\end{fmlnote}

At deficiency zero the formula reads
$\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D(|V|-1)$. So a generic realization with
nondegenerate hinges is infinitesimally rigid if and only if
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, by
\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}. By KT Proposition~2.2
(\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}), this holds if and only if
$\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees. This is Tay and
Whiteley's theorem in the form of KT Proposition~1.1
(\cref{sec:body-hinge-tay}), for the rigidity matrix of
\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}.

\subsection{The induction skeleton and base case}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-base}

KT Theorem~5.5 is proved by induction on $|V(G)|$, so its statement must be
strong enough to serve as its own inductive hypothesis. That hypothesis is
KT's realization hypothesis (KT eq.~(6.1)): a minimal $k$-dof-graph has a
panel-hinge realization at the deficiency rank. We record it in two forms, a
rank form (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) that composes through the induction and
the nondegenerate-hinge form KT actually uses (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}), then
state the two-vertex base case (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base}) and the
base-region trichotomy at which the induction terminates.

\begin{definition}[Realization hypothesis, rank form; KT eq.\ (6.1)]
  \label{def:rank-hypothesis}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.HasFullRankRealization,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.HasGenericFullRankRealization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:k-dof}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V \neq \emptyset$. A panel-hinge
  realization $(G,p)$ of $G$ (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}) is
  \emph{at the target rank} if
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  A \emph{generic full-rank realization} of $G$ is a panel-hinge
  realization at the target rank whose normals are in general position
  (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). For a minimal $k$-dof-graph,
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = k$ (\cref{def:k-dof}), and the target
  rank is the rank $D(|V| - 1) - k$ of KT eq.~(6.1).
\end{definition}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}
  In the formalization every framework has the same set of bodies, and a
  graph need not have all of them as vertices. So the hypothesis is
  stated for the rank (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). The first
  pinned predicate is the rank form at deficiency zero: a panel-hinge
  framework on $G$ whose infinitesimal motions are all constant on
  $V(G)$, that is, of rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$. Its endpoint selector is
  unconstrained (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), and no main
  result uses this predicate. The second pinned predicate is the generic
  full-rank realization, whose selector assigns each edge of $G$ its two
  ends.

  Katoh and Tanigawa call a nonparallel realization generic if the
  coordinates of the vectors $c(v)$ that define its panels are
  algebraically independent over $\Q$~\cite[p.~668]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  A generic realization has the largest rank of all nonparallel
  realizations of its graph, and their proof uses this for the
  realizations given by the induction hypothesis (KT footnote~6, p.~684).
  A generic full-rank realization asks only that the normals be in
  general position. Where the formal proof needs a realization given by
  the induction hypothesis to attain a rank, it chooses the normals off
  the zero set of the finitely many polynomials that this step tests, a
  product of determinants and rank polynomials
  (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}) fixed before the choice.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{remark}[Why the target is a rank equality]
  \label{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}
  The rows of $R(G,p)$ involve only the bodies of $G$. Let $W$ be a set
  of bodies that contains $V(G)$, and regard $(G,p)$ as a framework on
  $W$ in which the bodies outside $V(G)$ lie on no edge. Its rank does
  not change, and each body of $W \setminus V(G)$ adds $D$ dimensions to
  its space of motions. So the target rank does not depend on $W$, and
  it is equivalent to
  \[
    \dim Z(G,p) \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)
    + D\,|W \setminus V(G)|.
  \]
  For $W = V(G)$ this is $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
  as in the proof of \cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}. When
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, the target rank says that every
  infinitesimal motion is constant on $V(G)$.
\end{remark}

A panel-hinge realization at the target rank may have degenerate hinges.
If all the normals are equal, then every hinge is degenerate and welds
its two bodies together, so every connected $0$-dof-graph has a
panel-hinge realization at the target rank. Katoh and Tanigawa's hinges
are nondegenerate (the remark after \cref{def:hinge-constraint}). A
generic full-rank realization has a nondegenerate hinge at every edge
with two distinct ends (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). The
second form of the hypothesis asks for nondegenerate hinges directly,
each in the panels of the two ends of its edge.

\begin{definition}[Realization hypothesis, nondegenerate-hinge form; KT eq.\ (6.1)]
  \label{def:genuine-hinge-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.HasPanelRealization,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.ExtensorInPanel}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:panel-hinge-framework, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V \neq \emptyset$. An extensor
  \emph{lies in the panel} with normal $n \in K^{d+1}$ if it is
  $x_1 \vee \cdots \vee x_{d-1}$ for some $x_1, \dots, x_{d-1} \in n^\perp$.
  A \emph{nondegenerate-hinge panel realization} of $G$ is a body-hinge
  framework $(G,p)$ at the target rank
  $D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), together with a nonzero normal
  $n_v \in K^{d+1}$ for each body $v$, such that the hinge $C(p(e))$ at
  each edge $e$ with ends $u$ and $v$ is nonzero and lies in the panels
  with normals $n_u$ and $n_v$.
\end{definition}

This is the form of the hypothesis in Katoh and Tanigawa's proof. Their
panel-hinge frameworks are body-hinge frameworks whose hinges at each
body lie in a common hyperplane, the panel of the
body~\cite[p.~668]{katohTanigawa2011}, and their hinges are
nondegenerate. Here the hinge at an edge need not be the meet of the
two panels. When the two panels coincide, it can be any nonzero extensor
in their common panel, as in KT Lemma~5.3 (p.~669). A generic full-rank
realization gives a nondegenerate-hinge one.

\begin{lemma}[A generic full-rank realization has nondegenerate hinges]
  \label{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.hasPanelRealization_of_generic}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $d \ge 2$, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $|V| \ge 2$.
  If $G$ has a generic full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}),
  then $G$ has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a generic full-rank realization with normals $n_v$. It
  is at the target rank. Each normal $n_v$ is nonzero, since $|V| \ge 2$
  gives a body $w \neq v$, and $n_v$ and $n_w$ are linearly independent.
  Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u \neq v$. Its hinge $C(n_u, n_v)$ is
  nonzero (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). It is the meet
  of the hyperplanes $n_u^\perp$ and $n_v^\perp$, so it is
  $x_1 \vee \cdots \vee x_{d-1}$ for a basis $x_1, \dots, x_{d-1}$ of
  $n_u^\perp \cap n_v^\perp$ (the remark after
  \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). So it lies in both panels. A loop
  at a body $u$ gives no row of $R(G,p)$, since its constraint involves
  only $S(u) - S(u) = 0$. So we may replace its hinge by
  $x_1 \vee \cdots \vee x_{d-1}$ for any linearly independent
  $x_1, \dots, x_{d-1} \in n_u^\perp$, without changing the rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ has no loops, and it keeps every
  hinge of the generic realization. The theorems that apply it do so for
  simple graphs.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa's Theorem~5.5~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011} gives
a minimal $k$-dof-graph $G$ with $|V| \ge 2$ a panel-hinge realization
of rank $D(|V| - 1) - k$, nonparallel if $G$ is simple. We state it as
two claims: a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization of every such $G$,
and a generic full-rank realization of a simple one. For simple $G$ the
second gives the first (\cref{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}).

The second claim needs simplicity. Let $G$ have two vertices $u$ and
$v$, and two edges $e$ and $f$ that join them. Then $G$ is a
$0$-dof-graph (\cref{lem:two-vertex-zero-dof}), and its target rank is
$D$. In a panel-hinge realization of $G$ with $n_u$ and $n_v$ linearly
independent, the hinges at $e$ and $f$ are both $\pm C(n_u, n_v)$. So
the two edges impose the same $D - 1$ conditions, and the rank is
$D - 1$. Katoh and Tanigawa realize $G$ at rank $D$ with two coincident
panels and two distinct hinges in them (KT Lemma~5.3, p.~669). They
define nonparallel realizations only for simple graphs (KT \S5.1,
p.~668), and their induction hypothesis, KT eq.~(6.1), asks for a
nonparallel realization only of a simple graph.

The proof is by induction on $|V|$, as in KT \S6. It uses the base cases
later in this subsection, and Cases~I, II and~III, which
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseI},
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseII} and
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseIII} prove.

\begin{theorem}[Realization at the target rank; KT Theorem 5.5]
  \label{thm:theorem-55}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.theorem_55_minimalKDof_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization,
        thm:minimal-kdof-reduction-all-k,
        lem:theorem-55-base-producer, lem:theorem-55-base,
        lem:case-cut-edge-realization, lem:case-cut-edge-realization-gp,
        lem:case-I, lem:case-II, lem:case-I-realization, lem:case-I-dispatch,
        lem:case-II-realization, lem:case-III, lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a
  minimal $k$-dof-graph with $|V| \ge 2$. Then $G$ has a
  nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}), of rank $D(|V| - 1) - k$. If
  $G$ is simple, then $G$ also has a generic full-rank realization
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). In particular, a minimal $0$-dof-graph
  with at least two vertices has an infinitesimally rigid
  nondegenerate-hinge panel realization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:minimal-kdof-reduction-all-k, lem:theorem-55-base-producer,
        lem:theorem-55-base, lem:case-cut-edge-realization,
        lem:case-cut-edge-realization-gp, lem:case-I, lem:case-II,
        lem:case-I-realization, lem:case-I-dispatch, lem:case-II-realization,
        lem:case-III, lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower, lem:chain-data-extract}
  For a multigraph $H$, let $P(H)$ say that $H$ has a nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realization, and also a generic full-rank realization if $H$ is
  simple. We prove $P(G)$ for every minimal $k$-dof-graph $G$ with at
  least one vertex, for all $k$ at once, by induction on $|V(G)|$
  (\cref{thm:minimal-kdof-reduction-all-k}). The induction hypothesis is
  KT eq.~(6.1): $P(H)$ holds for every minimal $k'$-dof-graph $H$ with
  at least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices, for every $k'$.

  \emph{The base.} If $G$ has one or two vertices, then $P(G)$ holds
  (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base-producer}). A minimal $k$-dof-graph with
  two vertices has no edge, one edge, or two parallel edges, and the last
  case is KT Lemma~5.3 (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base}).

  \emph{The four cases.} Let $|V(G)| \ge 3$. We follow Katoh and
  Tanigawa's four cases (KT p.~671). If $G$ is not $2$-edge-connected,
  then KT Lemma~6.1 gives $P(G)$. \Cref{lem:case-cut-edge-realization}
  gives the nondegenerate-hinge realization, and
  \cref{lem:case-cut-edge-realization-gp} gives the generic one when $G$
  is simple. If $G$ has a proper rigid subgraph, then Case~I gives $P(G)$
  (KT \S6.2; \cref{lem:case-I-dispatch}, \cref{lem:case-I-realization}).
  It uses KT Lemma~6.2 when $G$ is not simple, KT Lemma~6.3 when $G$ is
  simple and has a proper rigid subgraph $H$ with $G/E(H)$ simple, and
  KT Lemma~6.5 otherwise.

  In the remaining two cases $G$ is $2$-edge-connected and has no proper
  rigid subgraph, so $G$ is simple (\cref{lem:simple-minimal-noRigid}).
  If $k > 0$, then Case~II gives a generic full-rank realization of $G$
  (KT Lemma~6.8; \cref{lem:case-II-realization}). If $k = 0$, then
  Case~III does (KT Lemmas~6.10 and~6.13; \cref{lem:case-III}). In both
  cases this realization is also a nondegenerate-hinge one
  (\cref{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}), so $P(G)$ holds.

  \emph{The deficiencies.} The induction hypothesis is used at
  deficiencies other than $k$, even when $k = 0$. KT Lemma~6.1 applies
  it to the two sides of a cut of at most one edge, at deficiencies
  smaller than $k$. Case~I applies it to a proper rigid subgraph $H$, a
  minimal $0$-dof-graph, and to the contraction $G/E(H)$, a minimal
  $k$-dof-graph. In Case~II, splitting off a vertex of degree two gives a
  minimal $(k-1)$-dof-graph. Case~III applies it to a minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph with $k' \le D - 2$ (KT Claim~6.11, eq.~(6.22);
  \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}). For general $d$, Case~III also
  distinguishes whether $G$ is a cycle on at most $d$ vertices or has a
  chain of length $d$ (KT Lemma~6.13; \cref{lem:chain-data-extract}). The
  case $d = 3$, $k = 0$ of the theorem is
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem also assumes that every minimal $k'$-dof-graph, for
  every $k'$, has an edge label outside its edge set
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{remark}[The fresh-edge-label convention]
  \label{rem:fresh-edge-supply}
  The formalization takes the bodies of every framework from one fixed
  set $W$, and the edges of every multigraph from one fixed set $L$ of
  labels (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}). Katoh and Tanigawa's
  argument has no analogue of $L$. An operation of the induction that
  adds an edge, such as splitting off a vertex, needs a label that the
  graph does not use. Every minimal $k$-dof-graph $G$ has one when $L$ is
  large enough. Indeed, every base of $M(\tilde G)$ meets the copies of
  each edge of $G$ (\cref{def:k-dof}), and it has
  $D(|V(G)| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ elements
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}). So $G$ has at most that many edges
  (\texttt{edgeSet\_\allowbreak{}ncard\_\allowbreak{}add\_\allowbreak{}deficiency\_\allowbreak{}le\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}isMinimalKDof}).
  As $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$, a label outside $E(G)$ exists
  whenever $D(|W| - 1) < |L|$
  (\texttt{freshEdgeSupply\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}card\_\allowbreak{}lt}).
  Minimality is needed here: a graph whose edges are loops at one body
  can use every label.

  The pinned theorems assume one of two forms of this condition, and the
  statements of this chapter omit it. The bound $D(|W| - 1) < |L|$ is
  assumed by \cref{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance}, \cref{thm:theorem-55-6},
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-d3}, \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture},
  \cref{cor:theorem-55-6-multigraph-d3} and
  \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}, and by the second theorem
  pinned on \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}. The supply itself, a
  label outside $E(H)$ for every minimal $k'$-dof-graph $H$ and every
  $k'$, is assumed by \cref{thm:theorem-55},
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}, \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows} and the
  first theorem pinned on \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}.
  \Cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-nonvacuous} gives an instance that meets
  the bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[KT Proposition 2.3 at deficiency zero from KT Theorem 5.5 ($d = 3$)]
  \label{cor:theorem-55-d3-spanning}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_deficiency_of_theorem_55_d3}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance, def:rank-hypothesis}

    \dim Z(G,p) \;=\; D \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance, prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}
  By \cref{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance}, $G$ has a generic full-rank
  realization $(G,p)$, of rank $D(|V| - 1)$. So
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D|V| - \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D$
  (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}). As $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$,
  this is the formula of KT Proposition~2.3 at $(G,p)$
  (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:endpoint-selector-totality}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ spans the set of bodies, and it
  takes the generic full-rank realization of
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-d3-instance} as a hypothesis. It derives the
  formula from the pinned theorem of \cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11},
  which asks for a nonzero hinge at every edge label. So it first changes
  the endpoint selector (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}) at
  each label outside $E(G)$ to a fixed pair of distinct bodies, which
  changes no hinge at an edge of $G$. Its conclusion is a panel-hinge
  framework on $G$ with $\dim Z = D$. It does not record that the normals
  are in general position, or that the selector assigns each edge of $G$
  its two ends. A framework whose hinges are all degenerate also meets
  this conclusion, since a $0$-dof-graph is connected.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa's Theorem~5.6 removes the minimality from KT
Theorem~5.5: every multigraph $G$ has a panel-hinge realization of rank
$D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}.
The lower bound on the rank in the proof of KT Proposition~2.3
(\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}) follows their proof of this theorem.
At deficiency zero the realization is infinitesimally rigid, as
\cref{cor:theorem-55-d3-spanning} records for a minimal $0$-dof-graph at
$d = 3$. Katoh and Tanigawa obtain the molecular conjecture from the
theorem in this way (KT p.~670; \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}).

We prove the theorem for simple graphs. In the panel-hinge frameworks of
\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}, a multigraph need not have a realization
at the target rank with nondegenerate hinges: the two-vertex double edge
has none (the paragraph before \cref{thm:theorem-55}). The multigraph form,
with hinges that need only lie in the panels, is
\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph} in
\cref{sec:molecular-coplanar-multigraph}. We state the theorem first for
panel-hinge realizations, whose hinges may be degenerate, and then with
nondegenerate hinges, as Katoh and Tanigawa's are
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}).

\begin{theorem}[Realization of a simple graph at the target rank; KT Theorem 5.6]
  \label{thm:theorem-55-6}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_of_theorem_55_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55, lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le,
        lem:subgraph-minimality, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a
  simple graph with $V \neq \emptyset$. Then $G$ has a panel-hinge
  realization $(G, p)$ at the target rank (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}),
  that is, with
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55, lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le,
        lem:subgraph-minimality, prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof (KT p.~670). Let
  $c = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. If $|V| = 1$, then $G$ has no edge,
  as it is simple, and $c = 0$. So every realization of $G$ has rank
  $0 = D(|V| - 1) - c$. Let $|V| \ge 2$.

  \emph{The realization.} By \cref{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph},
  $G$ has a spanning subgraph $G'$ that is a minimal $c$-dof-graph. It is
  simple, as a subgraph of $G$. By KT Theorem~5.5 (\cref{thm:theorem-55}),
  $G'$ has a generic full-rank realization $(G', q)$
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). Its normals $n_v$ are in general position,
  and $\operatorname{rank} R(G', q) = D(|V| - 1) - c$. Let $(G, p)$ be the
  panel-hinge framework on $G$ with the same normals. At each edge of $G'$
  it has the hinge of $(G', q)$. Katoh and Tanigawa first apply a
  projective transformation so that the panels of any two bodies meet in a
  $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace. Infinitesimal rigidity is invariant
  under such a transformation. They then give each edge of $G$
  outside $G'$ a hinge in the intersection of the panels of its ends. Here
  no transformation is needed. The normals are in general position, so the
  hinge $C(n_u, n_v)$ of every edge $uv$ of $G$ is nonzero
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}).

  \emph{The rank.} The restriction of $(G, p)$ to the edges of $G'$ is
  $(G', q)$, so $Z(G, p) \subseteq Z(G', q)$
  (\cref{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}, below). Hence
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G, p) \;\ge\; \operatorname{rank} R(G', q)
    \;=\; D(|V| - 1) - c.
  \]
  The hinges of $(G, p)$ are nondegenerate, so the upper bound on the rank
  in KT Proposition~2.3 holds at $(G, p)$
  (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}), and
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p) \le D(|V| - 1) - c$. So $(G, p)$ is at the
  target rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ spans the set $W$ of bodies, and it
  states the target rank as
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). It places no condition on the
  endpoint selector (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). The
  framework that it constructs gives each edge of $G$ outside $G'$ the
  hinge of one fixed pair of distinct bodies, which need not be the ends of
  the edge. The second pinned theorem of \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}
  records the ends. The pinned theorem also assumes $D(|W| - 1) < |L|$
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[KT Theorem 5.6 with nondegenerate hinges]
  \label{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_genuine_of_theorem_55_gen,

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6, lem:general-position-support-nonzero,
        lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}
  Let $(G, p)$ be the realization constructed in the proof of
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6}, from a spanning subgraph of $G$ that is a
  minimal $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$-dof-graph
  (\cref{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}). It is at the target rank,
  and its normals are in general position. Each edge of $G$ has two
  distinct ends, as $G$ is simple. So every hinge of $(G, p)$ is

\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem places no condition on the endpoint selector
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), as for
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6}. The second states that the selector assigns
  each edge of $G$ its two ends. Both assume that $G$ spans the set of
  bodies, state the target rank as
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and assume the supply
  of fresh labels (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). Both ask for a nonzero
  hinge at every edge label, not only at the edges of $G$
  (\cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}). A label outside $E(G)$ gets
  the hinge of two distinct bodies, which is why they assume $|V| \ge 2$.
\end{fmlnote}

The realization in the proof of \cref{thm:theorem-55-6} is a generic
full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). So the generic
conclusion of KT Theorem~5.5 holds for every simple graph with at least two
vertices, minimal or not. A later chapter uses its case $d = 2$ for a
graph whose bodies are part of a larger set (\cref{sec:main-component-jj}).
There the target rank is no longer equivalent to
$\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, since each body outside
the graph adds $D$ dimensions of motions
(\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). So the proof compares ranks rather
than motions.

\begin{theorem}[Generic full-rank realizations of simple graphs; KT Theorem 5.6]
  \label{thm:theorem-55-6-rows}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_genuine_recordsLinks_of_theorem_55_gen,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.hasGenericFullRankRealization_of_theorem_55_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine, def:rank-hypothesis, def:panel-general-position,
        lem:general-position-support-nonzero}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a
  simple graph with $|V| \ge 2$. Then $G$ has a generic full-rank
  realization $(G, p)$ (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}): its normals are in
  general position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}), and
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  In particular, every hinge of $(G, p)$ is nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55, lem:general-position-support-nonzero,
        lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq, lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound,
        lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}
  We use the realization of \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}. Let
  $c = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and let $G'$ be a spanning subgraph
  of $G$ that is a minimal $c$-dof-graph
  (\cref{lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}). Let $(G', q)$ be a generic
  full-rank realization of $G'$ (\cref{thm:theorem-55}), and let $(G, p)$
  be the panel-hinge framework on $G$ with the normals of $(G', q)$. These
  normals are in general position, so every hinge of $(G, p)$ is
  nondegenerate (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}).

  The restriction of $(G, p)$ to the edges of $G'$ has the hinges of
  $(G', q)$. So it has the same motions as $(G', q)$, and hence the same
  row space (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}). Its rows are
  among those of $R(G, p)$, so
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p) \ge \operatorname{rank} R(G', q) =
  D(|V| - 1) - c$. Conversely, the hinges of $(G, p)$ are nondegenerate, so
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p) \le D(|V| - 1) - c$, also when the screw
  assignments are defined on a larger set of bodies
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorems do not assume that $G$ has every body as a vertex.
  The first states its conclusion as a panel-hinge framework on $G$ with
  normals in general position, a selector that assigns each edge of $G$
  its two ends, a nonzero hinge at every edge label
  (\cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}), and the target rank. The
  second restates it as a generic full-rank realization
  (\cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}). Both assume the supply of fresh
  labels (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}).
\end{fmlnote}

We now prove the molecular conjecture of Tay and
Whiteley~\cite{tayWhiteley1984}, recalled in \cref{sec:body-hinge-tay},
for simple graphs. Katoh and Tanigawa state and prove it for multigraphs
(KT Conjecture~1.2, p.~648), and
\cref{fmlnote:molecular-conjecture-multigraph} explains the restriction.
On both sides the hinges are nondegenerate, as theirs are. A degenerate
hinge welds the two bodies of its edge together. So without this
condition, both sides would hold exactly when $G$ is connected.

\begin{theorem}[The molecular conjecture for simple graphs; KT Conjecture 1.2]
  \label{thm:molecular-conjecture}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.molecular_conjecture}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:dof-generic, def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a
  simple graph with $|V| \ge 2$. Then $G$ has an infinitesimally rigid
  body-hinge realization (\cref{def:dof-generic}) with nondegenerate hinges
  if and only if $G$ has an infinitesimally rigid panel-hinge realization
  (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}) with nondegenerate hinges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound, def:rank-hypothesis,
        def:D-deficiency}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa (KT p.~670). Suppose first that $G$ has an
  infinitesimally rigid panel-hinge realization with nondegenerate hinges.
  Its body-hinge interpretation (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}) is an
  infinitesimally rigid body-hinge realization of $G$ with the same
  hinges.

  Conversely, let $(G, p)$ be an infinitesimally rigid body-hinge
  realization of $G$ with nondegenerate hinges. As it is infinitesimally
  rigid, $\dim Z(G, p) = D$. As its hinges are nondegenerate,
  $\dim Z(G, p) \ge D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. Both facts are in
  \cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}. Hence
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le 0$, and so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ (\cref{def:D-deficiency}). By KT
  Theorem~5.6 with nondegenerate hinges (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}),
  $G$ has a panel-hinge realization with nondegenerate hinges at the target
  rank $D(|V| - 1)$ (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). It is infinitesimally
  rigid (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}
  Katoh and Tanigawa state Conjecture~1.2 for multigraphs (KT p.~648), and
  their panel-hinge frameworks ask only that the hinges at each body lie
  in a common hyperplane. The panel-hinge frameworks of
  \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework} take the hinge of each edge to be the
  meet of the panels of its ends, and for them the multigraph form of the
  theorem is false. The two-vertex double edge has an infinitesimally
  rigid body-hinge realization with nondegenerate hinges
  (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base}, below). In a panel-hinge realization of it
  with nondegenerate hinges, both hinges are $\pm C(n_u, n_v)$, and the
  rank is $D - 1$ (the paragraph before \cref{thm:theorem-55}). Katoh and
  Tanigawa realize it with two coincident panels and two distinct hinges
  in them (KT Lemma~5.3, p.~669). The multigraph form holds for hinges that
  need only lie in the panels (\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}).

  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ spans the set $W$ of bodies and that
  $D(|W| - 1) < |L|$ (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). Its panel side places
  no condition on the endpoint selector
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), so it asserts less than a
  panel-hinge realization of $G$. Both of its sides ask for a nonzero
  hinge at every edge label (\cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}).
\end{fmlnote}

By the proof, the two sides of \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture} hold if and
only if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, that is, if and only if
$\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees
(\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}). Katoh and Tanigawa state
their result in this form~\cite[p.~647]{katohTanigawa2011}.

\begin{remark}[Nondegenerate hinges at every edge label]
  \label{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}
  Let $W$ and $L$ be the sets of bodies and edge labels of
  \cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}. The pinned theorems of
  \cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}, \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine},
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-rows} and \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture} ask for
  a nonzero hinge $C(p(e))$ at every label $e \in L$, not only at the edges
  of $G$. So do the lower bound of \cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}
  and the predicate of \cref{def:coplanar-panel-realization}.
  A label outside $E(G)$ imposes no constraint. A body-hinge framework can
  give it any nonzero extensor. A panel-hinge framework gives it the meet
  of the panels of two bodies (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}),
  which is nonzero only when their normals are linearly independent.

  On one body every hinge of a panel-hinge framework is $C(n_v, n_v) = 0$.
  So, once $L$ is nonempty, the panel side of the pinned theorem of
  \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture} fails on one body. Its body side holds,
  since a single body is infinitesimally rigid whatever its hinges. That
  theorem therefore assumes $|V| \ge 2$. The statement of
  \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture} concerns only the edges of $G$, and a
  simple graph on one vertex has no edge, so both of its sides hold there.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An instance of the hypotheses]
  \label{rem:molecular-conjecture-nonvacuous}
  The hypotheses of the pinned theorem of \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}
  can all be met. Take $K = \R$, $d = 3$, two bodies and seven labels,
  and let $G$ have one edge, which joins the two bodies. Then $G$ is
  simple and spans the bodies, and $D(|W| - 1) = 6 < 7 = |L|$
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). The formalization applies the theorem to
  this instance
  (\texttt{molecular\_\allowbreak{}conjecture\_\allowbreak{}witness}).
\end{remark}

The base of the induction is the two-vertex double edge, the minimal
$0$-dof-graph. Realized by two hinges with linearly independent supporting
extensors (KT Lemma~5.3, \cref{lem:rank-parallel-full}) it attains the full rank
$D = D(|V(G)|-1)$ at $k = 0$, which in the $V(G)$-relative rank hypothesis
(\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) is rigidity on the two bodies.

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 base case ($|V(G)| = 2$)]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-base}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.theorem_55_base}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, lem:rank-parallel-full}
  Let $F$ be a body-hinge framework carrying two edges $e_1, e_2$, both joining
  the same two distinct bodies $u \ne v$, whose supporting extensors
  $C(p(e_1)), C(p(e_2))$ are linearly independent. Then every infinitesimal
  motion of $F$ is constant on $\{u, v\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-parallel-full}
  Both edges constrain the relative screw $S(u) - S(v)$ to the span of a
  supporting extensor. Their extensors being linearly independent, the
  parallel-hinges lemma (KT Lemma~5.3, \cref{lem:rank-parallel-full}) forces
  $S(u) = S(v)$ for every infinitesimal motion $S$. Every body of $\{u, v\}$ is
  $u$ or $v$, so $S$ is constant on $\{u, v\}$.
\end{proof}

The parallel-pair base case (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base-producer-parallel})
places two hinges in a single shared panel $\Pi(u) = \Pi(v) = n^{\perp}$
(writing $\Pi(v) = \mathrm{panel}(v)$ for body $v$'s hyperplane), with two
non-proportional supporting extensors (cf.\ KT Lemma~5.3, p.~670). The
following supplies that coincident-panel pair.

\begin{lemma}[Two independent extensors in a common panel]
  \label{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_linearIndependent_extensor_pair_perp}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, lem:extensor-independence}
  For any normal $n \in K^4$ there are points $p_0, p_1, q_0, q_1 \in
  n^{\perp} = \{x : x \cdot n = 0\}$ whose grade-$2$ extensors
  $p_0 \wedge p_1$ and $q_0 \wedge q_1$ in $\bigwedge^2 K^4$ are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:extensor-independence}
  The panel $n^{\perp}$ has dimension at least $3$ in $K^4$ (it is the kernel of the single
  pairing functional $x \mapsto x \cdot n$, so $\dim n^{\perp} \ge 4 - 1 = 3$, with equality
  unless $n = 0$). Pick three linearly independent vectors $a, b, c \in n^{\perp}$ and set
  $p = (a, b)$, $q = (a, c)$. The two wedges $a \wedge b$ and $a \wedge c$ are linearly
  independent: a relation $s\,(a \wedge b) + t\,(a \wedge c) = 0$, joined on the left with $c$
  (resp.\ $b$), kills the second (resp.\ first) term by the alternating property and leaves
  $s\,(c \wedge a \wedge b) = 0$ (resp.\ $t\,(b \wedge a \wedge c) = 0$); the surviving triple
  wedge is nonzero by independence of $\{a, b, c\}$, so $s = t = 0$. (This is the two-subset
  instance of the join-to-isolate technique behind \cref{lem:extensor-independence}.)
\end{proof}

The base region of the induction is the minimal-dof-graphs on at most two
bodies. By the trichotomy (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}) they fall into
three cases: the empty graph, a single edge, and a parallel edge pair. Each is
realized directly, and the dispatch (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base-producer})
assembles them.

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 base region ($|V(G)| \le 2$): the empty graph]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-base-producer-empty}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_empty,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_empty_gp}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization, lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}
  Let $G$ be a minimal-$k$-dof-graph on $1 \le |V(G)| \le 2$ bodies realizing the \emph{empty}
  case of the base trichotomy (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy} (i)): $E(G) = \emptyset$, and
  hence $\mathrm{def}(\tilde G) = D(|V(G)| - 1)$. Then $G$ has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}) at the target rank $D(|V(G)|-1) - \mathrm{def} = 0$, and
  also a \emph{generic} full-rank realization (\cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}) at that same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The bare construction is geometry-free: take the all-zero framework (every edge carries the
  zero supporting extensor) at a fixed nonzero panel normal. With no edges no hinge constraint
  is imposed, so the rigidity-row family is empty, its span is $\{0\}$, and the rank is $0$; the
  per-link conjunct is vacuous. The target rank vanishes because the empty-graph
  deficiency is exactly $D(|V(G)|-1)$ (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}). For the generic
  companion build the single-body / empty framework at a \emph{seed} --- an assignment of a
  normal parameter to each body ---
  chosen as a non-root of the \emph{general-position polynomial}, a fixed nonzero polynomial whose
  non-roots are exactly the assignments placing the panel normals in general position: the panel
  normals are then in general position, the rigidity-row family is
  still empty (rank $0$), and link-recording is vacuous.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 base region ($|V(G)| \le 2$): a single edge]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-base-producer-single}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_single_edge,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_single_edge_gp}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization, lem:two-vertex-trichotomy,
        lem:extensor-pair-in-panel, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ be a two-vertex minimal-$1$-dof-graph realizing the \emph{single-edge} case of the base
  trichotomy (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy} (ii)): $V(G) = \{x, y\}$ with $x \ne y$,
  $E(G) = \{e\}$ a single edge joining $x$ and $y$, and $\mathrm{def}(\tilde G) = 1$. Then $G$ has
  a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}) at the target rank
  $D(|V(G)|-1) - \mathrm{def} = D - 1 = 5$ (at $d = 3$, $D = 6$), and also a \emph{generic}
  full-rank realization (\cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}) at that same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel, def:rigidity-matrix}
  For the bare realization, place coincident panels $\Pi(x) = \Pi(y) = n^{\perp}$ at a fixed nonzero
  normal $n$ and assign the single hinge one nonzero supporting extensor inside the common panel
  (the first of the pair of \cref{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}; nonzero by its linear
  independence). The lone hinge's row block already \emph{is} the entire rigidity-row family
  (there is only one edge), and that block has rank exactly $D - 1$ (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix});
  so the rigidity-row span has rank $D - 1 = D(|V(G)|-1) - \mathrm{def}$, as required.
  Unlike the parallel-pair case the framework is not rigid ($\mathrm{def} = 1$): the rank
  is the single hinge-row count, not the full two-vertex base rank. The generic companion builds
  the panel framework from a normal assignment ($\mathtt{ofNormals}$) at a
  seed chosen as a non-root of the
  general-position polynomial, with the single edge's two distinct endpoints: general position
  forces the lone supporting extensor nonzero, and the same single hinge-row count gives rank
  $D - 1$.
\end{proof}

This is the one base case whose generic realization is not automatic. The
parallel-pair case carries the geometric content of the base region; the empty
and single-edge cases reduce to rank counts.

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 base region ($|V(G)| \le 2$): a parallel edge pair]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-base-producer-parallel}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_parallel_pair}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, lem:two-vertex-trichotomy, lem:theorem-55-base,
        lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}
  Let $G$ be a two-vertex minimal-$0$-dof-graph realizing the \emph{parallel-pair} case of the
  base trichotomy (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy} (iii)): $V(G) = \{x, y\}$ with $x \ne y$,
  $E(G) = \{e, f\}$ with $e \ne f$ two edges both joining $x$ and $y$, and $\mathrm{def}(\tilde G)
  = 0$. Then $G$ has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}) at the full target rank $D(|V(G)|-1) - \mathrm{def} =
  D \cdot 1 = 6$ (at $d = 3$, $D = 6$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:theorem-55-base, lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}
  Place \emph{coincident panels} $\Pi(x) = \Pi(y) = n^{\perp}$ at a fixed nonzero normal
  $n$ and assign the pair's two hinges two linearly independent supporting extensors inside the
  common panel $n^{\perp}$ (\cref{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}). Each link's supporting extensor is
  then nonzero and lies in both endpoint panels, verifying the per-link conjunct. The two
  independent extensors give the combined hinge-row blocks full rank $D$ on the relative
  screw $S(x) - S(y)$, so the two-vertex base case (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base}) makes the framework
  infinitesimally rigid on $\{x, y\} = V(G)$; the $V(G)$-relative rank bridge (rigid on $V(G)$ iff
  the rigidity-row span has rank $D(|V(G)|-1)$) turns that into the target rank equality,
  with $\mathrm{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 base region ($|V(G)| \le 2$): the trichotomy dispatch]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-base-producer}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_base_producer_gen,
        Graph.not_simple_of_isMinimalKDof_of_ncard_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization, lem:two-vertex-trichotomy,
        lem:theorem-55-base-producer-empty, lem:theorem-55-base-producer-single,
        lem:theorem-55-base-producer-parallel}
  Let $G$ be a minimal-$k$-dof-graph with $1 \le |V(G)| \le 2$. Then $G$ has a
  nondegenerate-hinge panel realization (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}) at the
  target rank $D(|V(G)|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, and, if $G$ is
  simple, a \emph{generic} full-rank realization (\cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}) at
  that rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:theorem-55-base-producer-empty, lem:theorem-55-base-producer-single,
        lem:theorem-55-base-producer-parallel}
  Dispatch on the base trichotomy (\cref{lem:two-vertex-trichotomy}). The unconditional
  bare conjunct comes from the three bare cases. For the simple conjunct, the empty case
  ($E(G) = \emptyset$) and the single-edge case ($\mathrm{def} = 1 > 0$) both admit a
  \emph{generic} full-rank realization, built from a normal assignment ($\mathtt{ofNormals}$)
  at a seed chosen as a non-root of the general-position
  polynomial, so the panel normals are in general position;
  the rank is $0$ (empty) resp.\ $D - 1$ (single-edge, the single hinge-row count).
  The single-edge case is the one base case where the simple conjunct is not automatic. In the
  parallel-pair case ($k = 0$) a two-vertex minimal-$0$-dof-graph cannot be simple: the
  single-vertex cut supplies two distinct parallel edges, both forced to link the same pair,
  which simplicity collapses, a contradiction
  (\texttt{Graph.not\_simple\_of\_isMinimalKDof\_of\_ncard\_two}); the $G$-simple antecedent is
  therefore vacuous.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The generic conjunct is the strengthened form of \cref{def:rank-hypothesis}
  (general-position, link-recording panel normals,
  \cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}); the
  unconditional conjunct is the bare nondegenerate-hinge form of
  \cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}.
\end{fmlnote}

This conditioned pair is the base-region instance, at $|V(G)| \le 2$, of the
all-deficiency induction hypothesis KT Theorem~5.5 is proved against (the base
region of the all-$k$ reduction, \cref{thm:minimal-kdof-reduction-all-k}).

The two-body base case has a three-body analogue: a triangle with independent
supporting extensors is rigid on its three bodies.

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem 5.5 triangle base ($|V(G)| = 3$)]
  \label{lem:theorem-55-triangle}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.theorem_55_triangle}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, lem:rank-parallel-full}
  If a body-hinge framework $F$ has three edges $e_1 : u$--$v$, $e_2 : v$--$w$,
  $e_3 : w$--$u$ forming a triangle whose three supporting extensors
  $C(p(e_1))$, $C(p(e_2))$, $C(p(e_3))$ are linearly independent, then every
  infinitesimal motion of $F$ is constant on $\{u, v, w\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-parallel-full}
  Each hinge constraint puts a relative screw $S(u) - S(v)$, $S(v) -
  S(w)$, $S(w) - S(u)$ in the one-dimensional span of its supporting
  extensor; the three differences telescope to $0$ (the cyclic shift is a
  bijection of $\{u,v,w\}$). Linear independence of the three extensors,
  extending the two-extensor argument of \cref{lem:rank-parallel-full} to three
  independent extensors, forces each difference to vanish, so
  $S(u) = S(v) = S(w)$, and constancy on $\{u,v,w\}$ follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic-seed existence for the triangle base]
  \label{lem:triangle-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_triangle_normals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, lem:panel-support-extensor-independence,
        lem:extensor-independence}
  When $k \ge 1$ (so $k+2 \ge 3$), there exist three vectors
  $n_0, n_1, n_2 \in K^{k+2}$ such that (1) each cyclic pair $(n_0,n_1)$,
  $(n_1,n_2)$, $(n_2,n_0)$ has a nonzero grade-$2$ join $n_i \wedge n_j \ne 0$,
  and (2) the cyclic supporting-extensor family $i \mapsto C(n_i, n_{i+1})$
  (indices mod $3$) is linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence, lem:extensor-independence}
  Take $n_i = e_i$, the standard basis vectors. By
  \cref{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence} it suffices to show the
  grade-$2$ join family is linearly independent. The three cyclic joins
  $e_0 \wedge e_1$, $e_1 \wedge e_2$, $e_2 \wedge e_0$ realize the three distinct
  $2$-subsets $\{0,1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, $\{0,2\}$, so up to sign they are three
  members of the $\Lambda^2$-basis family; indexed by distinct subsets, they
  inherit independence from the full basis family
  (\cref{lem:extensor-independence}). (The sign at the wrap edge,
  $e_2 \wedge e_0 = -\,e_0 \wedge e_2$, is \texttt{normalsJoin\_swap}.)
\end{proof}

The triangle seed generalizes to a cycle of any length (\cref{lem:triangle-normals}
is its $m = 3$ instance). Consecutive hinges of a panel cycle share a panel, so
a panel-hinge seed assigns one normal per body, which the free extensor pairs of
\cref{lem:exists-independent-panel-extensor} do not provide.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic-seed existence for a general cycle]
  \label{lem:cycle-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_cycle_normals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, lem:panel-support-extensor-independence,
        lem:extensor-independence}
  For $3 \le m \le k + 2$ there exist $m$ vectors $n_0, \dots, n_{m-1} \in K^{k+2}$
  such that (1) each cyclic pair $(n_i, n_{i+1})$ (indices mod $m$) has a nonzero
  grade-$2$ join $n_i \wedge n_{i+1} \ne 0$, and (2) the cyclic supporting-extensor
  family $i \mapsto C(n_i, n_{i+1})$ is linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence, lem:extensor-independence}
  Witness: the standard basis restricted along the inclusion of index sets,
  $n_i = e_i$ (this is where $m \le k + 2$ enters). The $m$ cyclic joins then
  realize the $m$ distinct $2$-subsets $\{i, i+1\}$ ($i < m-1$) and $\{0, m-1\}$ ---
  the edges of the cycle $C_m$ embedded in the index set. Via the grade-2
  antisymmetry at the single wrap edge, the cyclic join family equals unit scalars
  times the sorted subfamily of the $\Lambda^2$-basis family indexed by these
  $2$-subsets; the edge-to-subset index map is injective for $m \ge 3$ (two cyclic
  edges coincide only if $2 = 0$ in $\Z/m$), so the subfamily inherits independence
  from the basis family (\cref{lem:extensor-independence}), and unit scaling
  preserves independence. Each join is nonzero since consecutive indices are
  distinct; the extensor conclusion follows via
  \cref{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The framework-construction operation}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-withgraph}

Cases~I and~II each construct a realization of $G$ from a realization of a
different graph: Case~I from the contraction $G/E(H)$
(\cref{def:rigid-contraction}), Case~II from the splitting-off $G_v^{ab}$
(\cref{def:graph-operations}). Both need the same operation, which keeps the hinge
assignment fixed while changing the underlying multigraph. The supporting
extensors $C(p(e))$ and hinge-row blocks depend only on the hinge data, not on
which edges $G$ carries, so only the set of edges imposing a constraint
changes. The one fact needed from the operation is the graph-monotonicity of
the motion space: deleting edges (passing to a subgraph) can only enlarge the
null space $Z(G,p)$, the direction Cases~I and~II take toward the smaller
inductive graph. On a panel realization the operation keeps the panel normals
fixed, so it preserves coplanarity.

\begin{definition}[Framework on a new graph]
  \label{def:framework-with-graph}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.withGraph,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.withGraph,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_withGraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:panel-hinge-framework}
  For a body-hinge framework $F = (G, p)$ and any multigraph $G'$ on the same
  bodies, $F[G']$ is the framework with underlying multigraph $G'$ and the same
  hinge assignment $p$. Every supporting extensor $C(p(e))$, hinge-row block,
  and per-edge constraint is unchanged; only the set of edges imposing a
  constraint changes. The same operation applies to a panel-hinge framework
  $P$, swapping the multigraph while keeping the per-body panel normals and
  endpoint selector.
\end{definition}

The operation commutes with the body-hinge interpretation: writing $P^\flat$
for the body-hinge interpretation of a panel-hinge framework $P$, we have
$(P[G'])^\flat = (P^\flat)[G']$.

Case~II's $1$-extension re-inserts a body $v$ with a chosen panel, which a
second operation supplies.

\begin{definition}[Replacing one body's panel normal]
  \label{def:framework-with-normal}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.withNormal}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  For a panel-hinge framework $P$ and a body $v$, the framework $P[v \mapsto n]$
  replaces only $v$'s panel normal, leaving the multigraph, endpoint selector,
  and every other normal fixed.
\end{definition}

Each hinge whose endpoints avoid $v$ keeps its supporting extensor. Before $v$'s
two new edges are added it is unhinged (no edge of $G$ has $v$ as an endpoint),
as in the splitting-off $G_v^{ab}$, so replacing its normal leaves the inductive
realization untouched away from $v$ and preserves its motion count.

\begin{lemma}[Replacing an unhinged body's normal preserves the motion count]
  \label{lem:with-normal-preserves}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_withNormal_supportExtensor_of_ne,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_withNormal_infinitesimalMotions_eq,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_withNormal_pinnedMotions_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework-with-normal}
  When $v$ is unhinged, replacing $v$'s panel normal changes neither the null
  space $Z(G,p)$ nor any body-$w$-pinned motion subspace.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework-with-normal}
  A hinge whose endpoints avoid $v$ keeps its supporting extensor
  (\texttt{toBodyHinge\_withNormal\_supportExtensor\_of\_ne}), and when $v$ is
  unhinged every hinge avoids $v$; so the hinge-row family, and hence the null
  space and every pinned motion subspace, is unchanged
  (\texttt{toBodyHinge\_withNormal\_infinitesimalMotions\_eq},
  \texttt{toBodyHinge\_withNormal\_pinnedMotions\_eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Edge deletion enlarges the motion space]
  \label{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_le_withGraph_of_le,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_le_of_graph_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework-with-graph}
  If $G' \le G$ then $Z(G,p) \subseteq Z(G', p)$, equivalently
  $\dim Z(G,p) \le \dim Z(G', p)$, i.e.\
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G', p) \le \operatorname{rank} R(G, p)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework-with-graph}
  Each link of $G'$ is a link of $G$, and the matching supporting extensors
  agree, so every infinitesimal motion of $G$ meets every constraint of $G'$:
  $Z(G,p) \subseteq Z(G', p)$. Taking dimensions gives the rank form.
\end{proof}

This is the ``re-adding edges only grows the rank'' monotonicity that lifts a
realization of a minimal $k$-dof spanning subgraph to the whole multigraph, the
step \cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11} uses (cf.\ the fixed-graph
span-monotonicity of KT Lemma~5.2).


\subsection{The multigraph molecular conjecture in the hinge-coplanar model}
\label{sec:molecular-coplanar-multigraph}

Katoh--Tanigawa state both Theorem~5.6 and Conjecture~1.2 for multigraphs (KT
p.~648, p.~670). For the frameworks of \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework} the
multigraph equivalence is false
(\cref{fmlnote:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}): a hinge there \emph{is} the
meet of its endpoint panels, while KT require only that each hinge \emph{lie
in} the panel of each endpoint (KT \S5.1, p.~668), a freedom their
coincident-panel realizations use (KT Lemma~5.3, pp.~669--670). This
subsection states the panel side in KT's containment model and recovers both
results at full multigraph strength. The data needed are already those of the
nondegenerate-hinge realization form (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}): a
body-hinge framework together with panel normals under which every link's
supporting extensor lies in both endpoint panels.

\begin{definition}[Hinge-coplanar panel realization; KT \S5.1]
  \label{def:coplanar-panel-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.HasCoplanarPanelRealization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:genuine-hinge-realization}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph. A body-hinge framework $(G,p)$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) together with a panel-normal assignment
  $v \mapsto n_v \in K^{k+2}$ is a \emph{hinge-coplanar panel realization} of
  $G$ when every body of $V(G)$ has a nonzero normal, the supporting extensor
  of every edge label is nonzero, and every link's supporting extensor lies in
  the panels of both its endpoints: each endpoint's hyperplane $n_v^{\perp}$
  contains $k$ points spanning the extensor, as in
  \cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}.
\end{definition}

This is the containment form of hinge-coplanarity: all hinges at a body $v$
lie in the single hyperplane $n_v^{\perp}$, so each body may be replaced by a
rigid panel (KT p.~648). At two linearly independent normals the containment
forces the hinge to be the meet of the two panels, and the notion agrees with
\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}; at coincident panels it admits any
$(d-2)$-flat of the shared panel, exactly the freedom of KT Lemma~5.3 that the
meet model cannot express. The nondegeneracy condition ranges over the whole
edge-label type, as in \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}
(\cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}).

\begin{remark}[Multigraph conventions]
  \label{rem:coplanar-conventions}
  Two conventions below differ from KT, both in the direction of a stronger
  statement. First, KT's multigraphs carry parallel edges but no self-loops
  (KT \S2.5, p.~654); the statements below admit loops. A loop's hinge
  constrains the relative screw of a body against itself, so its rigidity
  rows vanish and neither side of the equivalence is affected; a realization
  places at each loop a nonzero extensor of the endpoint's panel. Second,
  KT's coplanarity condition constrains only the hinges incident to each
  body, and chooses the panel $\Pi_{G,p}(v)$ arbitrarily when it is not
  unique (KT \S5.1, p.~668); \cref{def:coplanar-panel-realization} assigns
  every body a nonzero normal, an equivalent packaging since nonzero normals
  always exist. As in \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}, panels and hinges
  through the hyperplane at infinity are admitted; KT reach these through
  projective invariance (KT p.~670).
\end{remark}

KT Theorem~5.6 in its full multigraph form extends the deficiency-rank
realization of \cref{thm:theorem-55-6} from simple spanning multigraphs to
all of them.

\begin{theorem}[The deficiency rank for every spanning multigraph; KT Theorem 5.6, \S5.2]
  \label{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_6_multigraph,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_6_multigraph_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:coplanar-panel-realization, thm:theorem-55,
        lem:subgraph-minimality, lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le,
        def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $G$ be a spanning multigraph on any nonempty body set --- parallel edges
  and loops admitted --- at any dimension $n \ge 2$ (equivalently
  $D = \binom{n+1}{2} \ge 3$) and over any infinite field $K$, of any
  characteristic. Then $G$ has a hinge-coplanar panel realization
  (\cref{def:coplanar-panel-realization}) attaining the deficiency rank
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  i.e.\ realizing the rank hypothesis (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) at
  $k' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55, lem:subgraph-minimality, lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le,
        prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11, def:genuine-hinge-realization,
        lem:minimal-kdof-spanning-subgraph}
  If $|V| = 1$ the single body is trivially rigid: $\operatorname{def}(\tilde
  G) = 0$ (every partition has a single part), and a hinge-coplanar realization
  is a constant nonzero normal together with a nonzero extensor of the single
  panel (\cref{rem:coplanar-single-body}). For $|V| \ge 2$ we follow KT's
  removal-and-extension argument (KT p.~670), in the
  containment model. Remove edges from $G$ to reach a minimal $k$-dof
  spanning subgraph $G' \le G$ with $V(G') = V(G)$ and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde{G'}) = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) =: c$, the
  deficiency-preserving removal given by the matroid argument behind
  \cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}; minimality makes $G'$ loopless, so every
  loop of $G$ is among the removed edges. Realize $G'$ at its own deficiency
  by KT Theorem~5.5 (\cref{thm:theorem-55}): its nondegenerate-hinge
  conclusion (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}) holds for every minimal
  $k$-dof multigraph, with no simplicity assumption, and provides a
  body-hinge framework $q$ on $G'$, panel normals, and the per-link
  containments, with $\dim Z(G',q) = D + c$.

  Extend $q$ to $G$: keep the supporting extensor of every $G'$-link, and
  give each remaining label whose edge links $u$ and $v$ in $G$ a nonzero
  extensor lying in both endpoint panels. Such an extensor always exists:
  for any two hyperplanes through the origin of $K^{k+2}$ the common
  perpendicular space has dimension at least $k$ by rank--nullity, so $k$
  linearly independent common points exist and their extensor is nonzero ---
  whether the panels are transversal, coincident, or the endpoints equal (a
  loop). This is the homogeneous counterpart of KT's projective move
  (\texttt{exists\_extensor\_in\_two\_panels\_grade}). Labels outside $E(G)$
  receive an arbitrary nonzero extensor. The result is a hinge-coplanar
  panel realization of $G$.

  The motion space of a framework depends only on the supporting extensors
  of its links, so the extension restricted to $G'$ has the motion space of
  $q$, and re-adding edges only shrinks it
  (\cref{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}):
  $\dim Z(G,p) \le \dim Z(G',q) = D + c$. Every hinge being nondegenerate,
  the KT Proposition~1.1 rank bridge (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11})
  yields the deficiency rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[KT Theorem 5.6 at $d = 3$, multigraph form]
  \label{cor:theorem-55-6-multigraph-d3}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.theorem_55_6_multigraph_d3}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $G$ be a spanning multigraph on any nonempty body set at dimension
  $d = 3$ ($D = 6$). Then $G$ has a hinge-coplanar panel realization attaining
  the deficiency rank
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = 6(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}
  Specialize \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph} to $n = 3$, where $D = 6$.
\end{proof}

The multigraph molecular conjecture follows exactly as in the simple case,
with \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph} in place of
\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}.

\begin{theorem}[The multigraph Molecular Conjecture; KT Conjecture 1.2]
  \label{thm:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecular_conjecture_multigraph}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:coplanar-panel-realization, def:dof-generic, def:hinge-constraint}
  Let $G$ be a spanning multigraph on any nonempty body set --- parallel edges
  and loops admitted --- at any dimension $n \ge 2$ (equivalently
  $3 \le D = \binom{n+1}{2}$) and over any infinite field $K$, of any
  characteristic. Then $G$ can be realized as an infinitesimally rigid
  \emph{body-hinge} framework with nondegenerate hinges in $K^{n}$ if and
  only if $G$ can be realized as an infinitesimally rigid
  \emph{hinge-coplanar panel-hinge} framework
  (\cref{def:coplanar-panel-realization}) in $K^{n}$. This is KT
  Conjecture~1.2, the \emph{Molecular Conjecture}, at full multigraph
  strength (KT p.~648), conjectured by Tay and
  Whiteley~\cite{tayWhiteley1984} and proved by
  Katoh--Tanigawa~\cite{katohTanigawa2011}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound,
        def:rank-hypothesis, def:D-deficiency}
  ($\Leftarrow$) A hinge-coplanar panel realization is in particular a
  body-hinge realization with nondegenerate hinges; this direction is
  elementary.

  ($\Rightarrow$) As in \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}, a nondegenerate
  rigid body-hinge realization forces $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$: the
  genericity-free lower bound
  $D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \dim Z(G,p)$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}) meets $\dim Z(G,p) = D$ from
  rigidity (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis} at $k' = 0$). The multigraph KT
  Theorem~5.6 (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}) at deficiency zero then
  produces a hinge-coplanar panel realization with $\dim Z(G,p) = D$, i.e.\
  infinitesimally rigid.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The single-body case in the containment model]
  \label{rem:coplanar-single-body}
  Unlike \cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}, the statements above carry no
  $\ge 2$-body hypothesis, matching KT Conjecture~1.2. In the meet model that
  hypothesis is essential (\cref{rem:molecular-conjecture-essential}: with one
  body every meet is degenerate, $\operatorname{panelSupportExtensor}(n,n) =
  0$). In the containment model it is not needed: on a single body $G$ is a
  subsingleton, so $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ and the framework is
  trivially rigid, and a hinge-coplanar realization is any constant nonzero
  normal $n_0$ paired with a nonzero extensor of the single panel $n_0^\perp$
  (\texttt{exists\_extensor\_in\_two\_panels\_grade}; a loop's hinge may be any
  such extensor). Both the deficiency-rank realization
  (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}) and the equivalence
  (\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}) therefore hold at $|V| = 1$.
\end{remark}
