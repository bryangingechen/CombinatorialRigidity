\subsection{The rank at generic normals}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-genericity}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a nonparallel panel-hinge realization generic if
the coordinates of the normals of its panels are algebraically
independent over $\Q$. The entries of the rigidity matrix are
polynomials in these coordinates, so a generic realization has the
largest rank of all nonparallel realizations of its
graph~\cite[\S5.1, p.~668]{katohTanigawa2011}. Jackson and Jord\'an made
this observation for panel-and-hinge
frameworks~\cite[\S7]{jacksonJordan2009}, and Katoh and Tanigawa refer to
them for it. Their proof uses it in KT Claim~6.4 (p.~675) and for the
realizations that the induction hypothesis gives (KT footnote~6,
p.~684). We replace
algebraic independence by the non-vanishing of polynomials in the
normals, as explained after \cref{lem:case-I-realization}.
\Cref{lem:genericity-device} states the resulting lower semicontinuity
of the rank. The proofs of KT Lemmas~6.3 and~6.8
(\cref{lem:case-I-realization}, \cref{lem:case-II-realization}) use it in
the form of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, in the next
subsection. Given normals at which the rank is at least $N$, that lemma
gives a polynomial in the normals that does not vanish there, off whose
zeros the rank is at least $N$. These proofs choose their normals off
the zeros of a product of such polynomials, and bound the rank from
above by \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}.

\begin{lemma}[Lower semicontinuity of the rank in the normals; KT \S5.1]
  \label{lem:genericity-device}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_good_realization,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_good_realization_const}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field and let $G$ be a multigraph. Regard the
  normals $q$ of the panel-hinge frameworks $(G,q)$ on a finite set $W$ of
  bodies as the points of $K^N$, where $N = (d+1)|W|$. Each entry of
  $R(G,q)$ is a polynomial of degree two in $q$, bilinear in the normals
  (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}, \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). Hence,
  for each $r$, the set of the $q$ with
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge r$ is Zariski-open; that is, the rank
  is lower semicontinuous in $q$. The rank is at least its value at a
  given $q_0$ on a dense Zariski-open set, and the largest rank is
  attained on such a set. Equivalently,
  $\dim Z(G,q) = D|W| - \operatorname{rank} R(G,q)$ is upper
  semicontinuous in $q$, and its least value is attained on a dense
  Zariski-open set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  We prove the lemma at the normals at which every hinge of $(G,q)$ is
  nondegenerate, the setting of Katoh and Tanigawa's nonparallel
  realizations. The remark after the proof shows that it fails at the
  others. These normals form a Zariski-open set $U$, as the coordinates
  of the hinge $C(q(u), q(w))$ of an edge with ends $u$ and $w$ are
  polynomials in $q$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). Choose the rows
  of $R(G,q)$ at each edge as in \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}.
  Their coordinates are polynomials of degree two in $q$, and at every
  $q \in U$ they span the row space of $R(G,q)$.

  So for $q \in U$, the rank of $R(G,q)$ is at least $r$ if and only if
  some $r \times r$ minor of the matrix of these rows does not vanish at
  $q$. Each
  minor $\Delta$ is a polynomial in $q$. Hence the set of the $q \in U$
  with $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge r$ is the intersection of $U$ with
  the union of the finitely many sets $\{\Delta \neq 0\}$, and it is
  Zariski-open.
  If $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q_0) \ge r$ at some $q_0 \in U$, then some
  minor $\Delta$ has $\Delta(q_0) \neq 0$. The rank is then at least $r$
  on the open set $U \cap \{\Delta \neq 0\}$, which contains $q_0$. As $K$
  is infinite, a nonzero polynomial has a non-root, and so has a product
  of nonzero polynomials. Hence any two nonempty Zariski-open subsets of
  $K^N$ meet, and each of them is dense. Taking for $q_0$ normals in $U$
  of the largest rank gives the assertion on the largest rank. The
  assertions on $Z(G,q)$ are the same, as
  $\dim Z(G,q) = D|W| - \operatorname{rank} R(G,q)$
  (\cref{def:rigidity-matrix}).
\end{proof}

The lemma needs nondegenerate hinges. If the normals at the ends $u$ and
$w$ of an edge are linearly dependent, its hinge is degenerate, the hinge
constraint welds $u$ and $w$ (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), and the
rows of the edge have rank $D$ rather than $D - 1$. So for the graph with
one edge $uw$, $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q)$ is $D$ where $q(u)$ and
$q(w)$ are linearly dependent and $D - 1$ elsewhere. The set where the
rank is $D$ is Zariski-closed and not open, and the largest rank is
attained only there. The rows of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
vanish at a degenerate hinge, and the rank of the matrix of these rows,
unlike that of $R(G,q)$, is lower semicontinuous at every $q$.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorems state neither the open sets nor the
  semicontinuity. The first applies to a family of frameworks whose rows
  have polynomial coordinates. If some $m$ of the rows are linearly
  independent at $q_0$, it gives normals $q$ with
  $m + \dim Z(G,q) \le D|W|$, a bound that $q_0$ already satisfies. The
  second is the case of a single framework. No main result uses either
  of them. The open set is that of
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, at normals with
  nondegenerate hinges.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Counting rows and motions}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative}

As in \cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}, let the frameworks on a
multigraph $G$ have a finite set $W \supseteq V(G)$ of bodies, those
outside $V(G)$ on no edge. Each body of $W \setminus V(G)$ adds $D$
dimensions to $Z(G,q)$, and every infinitesimal motion is constant on
$V(G)$ exactly when $\dim Z(G,q) = D(|W \setminus V(G)| + 1)$. The first
four lemmas count in this form. They compute the motions that vanish on
$V(G)$ (\cref{lem:relative-screw-split}), and pass from independent rows, or
from a bound on $\dim Z(G,q)$, to motions constant on $V(G)$. Then come
the independent rows that a bound on the rank gives
(\cref{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}), and the form of
\cref{lem:genericity-device} that the realization lemmas use
(\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}), with a superseded
application of it to the contraction of Case~I
(\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel}). The subsection ends with the
count of KT eq.~(6.12) when the graph obtained by splitting off a vertex
of degree two is rigid (\cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement}), and
the four lemmas on rows before it.

\begin{lemma}[The block pin of $V(G)$]
  \label{lem:relative-screw-split}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_pinnedMotionsOn_vertexSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:pinned-motions-on}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework whose screw assignments are
  defined on a finite set $W$ of bodies with $V(G) \subseteq W$. Then
  \[
    \dim Z_{V(G)}(G,p) \;=\; D\,|W \setminus V(G)|.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  A screw assignment that vanishes on $V(G)$ satisfies every hinge
  constraint, as both ends of every edge lie in $V(G)$. So the block pin
  $Z_{V(G)}(G,p)$ (\cref{def:pinned-motions-on}) consists of all the
  screw assignments that vanish on $V(G)$. Such an assignment takes an
  arbitrary value in $K^D$ at each body of $W \setminus V(G)$, so these
  assignments form a space of dimension $D|W \setminus V(G)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent rows bound the motions]
  \label{lem:relative-device-count}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_relative_full_count_ofParam}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:genericity-device, lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a multigraph with
  $V(G) \neq \emptyset$, whose panel-hinge frameworks have a finite set
  $W \supseteq V(G)$ of bodies. Suppose that at some normals $q_0$, some
  $m \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$ rows of $R(G,q_0)$, chosen as in
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}, are linearly independent. Then
  there are normals $q$ with
  \[
    \dim Z(G,q) \;\le\; D\,\bigl(|W \setminus V(G)| + 1\bigr).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The normals $q_0$ will do. The $m$ rows lie in the row space of
  $R(G,q_0)$, so
  \[
    \dim Z(G,q_0) \;=\; D|W| - \operatorname{rank} R(G,q_0) \;\le\;
    D|W| - D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr),
  \]
  and $|W| = |V(G)| + |W \setminus V(G)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem applies the genericity argument of
  \cref{lem:genericity-device} to the rows of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  (\texttt{exists\_\allowbreak{}good\_\allowbreak{}realization\_\allowbreak{}ofParam}),
  which gives normals $q$ with $m + \dim Z(G,q) \le D|W|$. It then
  substitutes $|W| = |V(G)| + |W \setminus V(G)|$, as above. It assumes
  that every edge label is an edge of $G$, whose ends the endpoint
  selector gives (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). No main
  result uses it.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Rigidity on $V(G)$ from a bound on the motions]
  \label{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_vertexSet_of_finrank_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:relative-screw-split, lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:case-I}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework whose screw assignments are
  defined on a finite set $W$ of bodies with
  $\emptyset \neq V(G) \subseteq W$. If
  \[
    \dim Z(G,p) \;\le\; D\,\bigl(|W \setminus V(G)| + 1\bigr),
  \]
  then every infinitesimal motion of $(G,p)$ is constant on $V(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Fix a body $v_0 \in V(G)$. A motion that vanishes on $V(G)$ vanishes at
  $v_0$, so $Z_{V(G)}(G,p) \subseteq \operatorname{pinned}(v_0)$. By KT
  Lemma~5.1 (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}), the hypothesis and
  \cref{lem:relative-screw-split},
  \[
    \dim \operatorname{pinned}(v_0) \;=\; \dim Z(G,p) - D \;\le\;
    D\,|W \setminus V(G)| \;=\; \dim Z_{V(G)}(G,p).
  \]
  So the two subspaces are equal: a motion that vanishes at $v_0$
  vanishes on $V(G)$. Every motion $S$ is the sum of the trivial motion
  with value $S(v_0)$ and a motion that vanishes at $v_0$
  (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}), so $S$ is constant on $V(G)$. This is
  the criterion in the note after \cref{lem:case-I}, with $s = \{v_0\}$
  and $t = V(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A rigid realization from independent rows]
  \label{lem:realization-of-independent-rows}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.hasFullRankRealization_of_independent_panelRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, lem:relative-device-count,
        lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count, lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a multigraph with
  $V(G) \neq \emptyset$. Suppose that at some normals $q_0$, some
  $m \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$ rows of $R(G,q_0)$, chosen as in
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}, are linearly independent. Then
  $G$ has a panel-hinge realization of rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$, that is, one
  whose infinitesimal motions are all constant on $V(G)$
  (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $W$ be the set of bodies. By \cref{lem:relative-device-count} and
  its proof, the normals $q = q_0$ satisfy
  $\dim Z(G,q) \le D(|W \setminus V(G)| + 1)$. By
  \cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}, every infinitesimal motion
  of $(G,q)$ is then constant on $V(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem takes $q$ from the pinned theorem of
  \cref{lem:relative-device-count}, under the same assumption on the
  endpoint selector. Its conclusion is the first predicate of
  \cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}. No main result uses it.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Independent rows from a bound on the rank]
  \label{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_independent_panelRow_subfamily_of_le_finrank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:rigidity-matrix,
        lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework whose hinges are nondegenerate.
  At each edge $e$ of $G$, with ends $u$ and $w$, take the linear forms
  $S \mapsto C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t$, where $C = C(p(e))$ and
  $x = S(u) - S(w)$, for all pairs of indices $t$ and $t'$, as in
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. If
  $N \le \operatorname{rank} R(G,p)$, then some $N$ of these forms are
  linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As the hinges are nondegenerate, the forms at each edge span the same
  space as the rows of $R(G,p)$ at that edge (the proof of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So all the forms span the row
  space of $R(G,p)$, whose dimension is
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \ge N$, and some $N$ of them are linearly
  independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem reads the ends of each edge from the endpoint
  selector (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), and indexes the
  forms by the edge and the pair of indices. The last step of the proof
  of \cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract} is the case of a
  framework rigid on $V(G)$, with $N = D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A rank bound off the zeros of a polynomial]
  \label{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankPolynomial_of_le_finrank_linking}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $q_0$ be normals at which every hinge
  of the panel-hinge framework $(G,q_0)$ is nondegenerate. If
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q_0) \ge N$, then there is a polynomial $Q$ in
  the normals with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$ such that
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge N$ at every $q$ with $Q(q) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals, lem:independent-rows-of-rank}
  Choose the rows of $R(G,q)$ at each edge as in
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. As the hinges of $(G,q_0)$ are
  nondegenerate, these rows span the row space of $R(G,q_0)$, so some $N$
  of them are linearly independent at $q_0$. The matrix of their
  coordinates then has an $N \times N$ minor that does not vanish at
  $q_0$. Its entries are polynomials in the normals, and we let $Q$ be
  this minor, as a polynomial in the normals.

  Wherever $Q(q) \neq 0$, the same $N$ rows are linearly independent at
  $q$. Each of them is a form $S \mapsto \varphi(S(u) - S(w))$ at an edge
  with ends $u$ and $w$, where $\varphi$ vanishes on the hinge of the
  edge at $q$ (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So it lies in the
  row space of $R(G,q)$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}), and
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge N$.
  This is the argument of \cref{lem:genericity-device}, with the minor
  kept.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is superseded and kept for the record; no other node
uses it. It gives the rank of the reoriented realization of $G/E(H)$
itself (\cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}) off the zeros
of a polynomial, for
\cref{lem:rigidityRows-splice-rank-add} with $\pi = \pi_{V(H)}$. At
normals in general position for $G$, however, an edge of
$G \setminus E(H)$ at a body of $V(H)$ has different hinges in $G$ and
in $G/E(H)$, so the second hypothesis of that lemma need not hold.
Case~I uses \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj} instead.

\begin{lemma}[The rank of the reoriented contraction off the zeros of a polynomial (superseded)]
  \label{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankPolynomial_of_IH_relabel_linking}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank, lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}
  Let $H$, $r$, $f$ and $G_1 = G/E(H)$, with their orientations, be as in
  \cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}. Suppose that $G_1$ has
  no loops and has a panel-hinge realization whose normals are in general
  position and whose rank is
  $N = D(|V(G_1)|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G_1)$. Then there is a
  nonzero polynomial $Q$ in the panel normals such that at every $q$ with
  $Q(q) \neq 0$, the panel-hinge framework on $G_1$ with normals $q$ and
  this orientation has rank at least $N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank, lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank,
    lem:general-position-support-nonzero}
  By \cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank} there are normals $n$
  in general position at which this framework has rank $N$. As $G_1$ has
  no loops, its hinges at $n$ are nonzero
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). So
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, at $n$ with the bound $N$,
  gives a polynomial $Q$ with $Q(n) \neq 0$, so that $Q \neq 0$, such that
  the rank is at least $N$ wherever $Q$ does not vanish.
\end{proof}

We turn to KT eq.~(6.12) when $k = 0$. Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $v$
be a vertex of $G$ of degree two whose edges join it to vertices $a$ and
$b$, and let $G_v$ and $G_v^{ab}$ be obtained from $G$ by removing $v$ and
by splitting off $v$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}). In Case~II, Katoh and
Tanigawa put $v$ back into a realization of $G_v^{ab}$, with the hinge of
$vb$ equal to that of $ab$, and the rank rises by $D - 1$
(\cref{lem:case-II-realization}). In Case~III, where $k = 0$, the graph
$G_v^{ab}$ is a minimal $0$-dof-graph (KT Lemma~4.8(i),
\cref{lem:reduction-step}), and the same count gives one row less than
the target rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (KT p.~680). Case~III supplies the
missing row (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseIII}).
\Cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement} states this count, and the
next four lemmas give its parts. In them, as in
\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, the rows of $R(G,q)$ at an
edge are the linear forms of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals},
indexed by the edge and a pair of coordinates. At a nondegenerate hinge
they span the rows of the edge.

\begin{lemma}[The $D - 1$ rows of a nondegenerate hinge]
  \label{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_independent_panelRow_of_edge,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_independent_panelRow_subfamily_of_edge}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:hinge-row-block, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $(G,q)$ be a panel-hinge framework, and let $e$ be an edge of $G$
  with distinct ends whose hinge is nondegenerate. Then some $D - 1$ of
  the rows of $R(G,q)$ at $e$ are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-row-block, lem:edge-rows-independent,
        lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $u$ and $w$ be the ends of $e$, and let $C \neq 0$ be its hinge.
  The linear forms on $K^D$ that vanish on $C$ form a space of dimension
  $D - 1$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). The map $S \mapsto S(u) - S(w)$
  takes the screw assignments onto $K^D$, as $u \neq w$. So composing
  with it gives a space of linear forms on the screw assignments of
  dimension $D - 1$ (\cref{lem:edge-rows-independent}). The rows of
  $R(G,q)$ at $e$ span this space (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}),
  and $D - 1$ of them are linearly independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem gives $D - 1$ linearly independent forms in
  the span of the rows at $e$, and the second gives $D - 1$ of the rows
  themselves. Both hold for every body-hinge framework, and read the ends
  of $e$ from the endpoint selector
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Independent rows of a rigid realization]
  \label{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_independent_panelRow_subfamily_of_rigidOn,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_of_isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_vertexSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix,
        lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:relative-screw-split,
        def:graph-operations}
  Let $G_v^{ab}$ be obtained from a multigraph $G$ by splitting off a
  vertex $v$ of degree two (\cref{def:graph-operations}), and let
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ be a panel-hinge framework with nondegenerate hinges,
  every infinitesimal motion of which is constant on $V(G_v^{ab})$. Then
  some $D(|V(G_v^{ab})| - 1)$ rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ are linearly
  independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:relative-screw-split,
        lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $W$ be the set of bodies, and let $V' = V(G_v^{ab})$, which contains
  the ends of the new edge $ab$. Fix a body $w \in V'$. A motion that
  vanishes at $w$ vanishes on $V'$, as it is constant there. So
  $\operatorname{pinned}(w) = Z_{V'}(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and by KT Lemma~5.1
  (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}) and \cref{lem:relative-screw-split},
  \[
    \dim Z(G_v^{ab}, q) \;=\; D + D\,|W \setminus V'|.
  \]
  Hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) = D|W| - \dim Z(G_v^{ab}, q)
  = D(|V'| - 1)$. As the hinges are nondegenerate, the rows of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ span its row space, and $D(|V'| - 1)$ of them are
  linearly independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem is the lemma for every body-hinge framework
  whose hinges are nondegenerate, with the endpoint selector giving the
  ends of each edge. It asks both at every edge label, so that every
  label is an edge. No main result uses it. The second pinned theorem is
  the dimension of $Z(G_v^{ab}, q)$ in the proof, for every body-hinge
  framework.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Rows shared by two frameworks]
  \label{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_independent_panelRow_transport}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Let $(G_1, q_1)$ and $(G_2, q_2)$ be panel-hinge frameworks. Index the
  rows of each rigidity matrix by an edge and a pair of coordinates, as
  in \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. Let $s_1$ be a set of indices at
  which the rows of $R(G_1, q_1)$ are linearly independent, and let
  $f \colon s_2 \to s_1$ be injective, such that the row of
  $R(G_2, q_2)$ at each $i \in s_2$ is the row of $R(G_1, q_1)$ at
  $f(i)$. Then the rows of $R(G_2, q_2)$ at the indices in $s_2$ are
  linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  These rows are the rows of $R(G_1, q_1)$ at the indices $f(i)$, which
  are distinct as $f$ is injective. A subfamily of a linearly independent
  family is linearly independent.

  The row at an index depends only on the ends of its edge and their
  normals (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So the hypothesis holds,
  with $f$ the identity, for indices at edges that $G_1$ and $G_2$ share
  with the same ends, when $q_1$ and $q_2$ agree at these ends. For
  instance, $G$ and $G_v^{ab}$ share the edges of $G_v$
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rows at $v$ and rows that vanish at $v$]
  \label{lem:case-II-placement-block-independent}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sum_pinned_block}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $(G,q)$ be a panel-hinge framework, and let $v$ be a body. Let
  $\rho_1, \dots, \rho_m$ be rows of $R(G,q)$ at edges with the end $v$,
  whose restrictions to the screw assignments that vanish off $v$ are
  linearly independent. Let $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_l$ be linearly
  independent rows of $R(G,q)$ at edges whose ends differ from $v$. Then
  $\rho_1, \dots, \rho_m, \sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_l$ are linearly
  independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Suppose that $\sum_i a_i \rho_i + \sum_j b_j \sigma_j = 0$, and let $S$
  be a screw assignment that vanishes off $v$. Each $\sigma_j$ is a row
  at an edge whose ends differ from $v$, so $\sigma_j(S) = 0$, and hence
  $\sum_i a_i \rho_i(S) = 0$. By the hypothesis on the $\rho_i$, every
  $a_i$ is $0$. Then $\sum_j b_j \sigma_j = 0$, and every $b_j$ is $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem holds for any two families of linear forms on the
  screw assignments, of which the second vanishes on the assignments that
  vanish off $v$. \Cref{lem:rigidityRows-pinned-placement-rank-add} uses it
  in this form.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[KT eq.~(6.12) when $G_v^{ab}$ is rigid: one row short]
  \label{lem:case-II-realization-placement}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_II_placement_eq612}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, lem:case-II-placement-new-rows,
        lem:case-II-placement-old-rows,
        lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
        lem:case-II-placement-block-independent}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $v$ be a vertex of $G$ of degree two whose
  edges join it to vertices $a$ and $b$, and let $G_v^{ab}$ be obtained by
  splitting off $v$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}). Let $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ be
  a panel-hinge framework with nondegenerate hinges, every infinitesimal
  motion of which is constant on $V(G_v^{ab})$. Let $t \neq 0$, and let
  $q_0$ be the normals with $q_0(v) = q(a) + t\,q(b)$ and
  $q_0(w) = q(w)$ for every body $w \neq v$. Then some
  $D(|V(G)| - 1) - 1$ rows of $R(G, q_0)$ are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows, lem:case-II-placement-old-rows,
        lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
        lem:case-II-placement-block-independent,
        lem:independent-rows-of-rank, lem:splitoff-edge-substitution,
        lem:with-normal-preserves}
  We follow the count in the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization},
  where $t = 1$. Let $n_a = q(a)$ and $n_b = q(b)$. The hinge
  $C = C(n_a, n_b)$ of the edge $ab$ of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ is nonzero. As
  $C$ is bilinear and $C(n,n) = 0$ (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}),
  \[
    C(n_a + t\,n_b,\, n_b) \;=\; C,
    \qquad
    C(n_a + t\,n_b,\, n_a) \;=\; -t\,C.
  \]
  So, up to sign, the edges $vb$ and $va$ of $(G, q_0)$ have the hinges
  $C$ and $-tC$, both nondegenerate as $t \neq 0$. Every other edge of
  $G$ is an edge of $G_v$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}). Its
  ends differ from $v$, so it has the same hinge in $(G, q_0)$ as in
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and this hinge is nondegenerate.

  The normals $q_0$ and $q$ differ only at $v$, which lies on no edge of
  $G_v^{ab}$. So $(G_v^{ab}, q_0)$ has the hinges and the infinitesimal
  motions of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ (\cref{lem:with-normal-preserves}). Its
  vertices are those of $G$ other than $v$
  (\cref{def:graph-operations}). By
  \cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}, some $D(|V(G)| - 2)$
  rows $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_l$ of $R(G_v^{ab}, q_0)$ are linearly
  independent. Each of them lies in the row space of $R(G, q_0)$. A row
  at an edge of $G_v$ is also a row of $R(G, q_0)$
  (\cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows}). A row at the edge $ab$ is, up
  to sign, a form $S \mapsto \varphi(S(a) - S(b))$, where $\varphi$ is a
  linear form on $K^D$ that vanishes on $C$. The forms
  $S \mapsto \varphi(S(v) - S(b))$ and $S \mapsto \varphi(S(v) - S(a))$
  lie in the row spaces of the edges $vb$ and $va$ of $(G, q_0)$, as
  $\varphi$ vanishes on their hinges, and
  \[
    \varphi\bigl(S(a) - S(b)\bigr) \;=\;
      \varphi\bigl(S(v) - S(b)\bigr) - \varphi\bigl(S(v) - S(a)\bigr).
  \]
  This is the row form of Katoh and Tanigawa's column operation, which
  adds the columns of $v$ to those of $a$ (KT eqs.~(6.14)--(6.16)).

  By \cref{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}, some $D - 1$ rows
  $\rho_1, \dots, \rho_{D-1}$ of $R(G, q_0)$ at $vb$ are linearly
  independent. Up to sign, they are the forms
  $S \mapsto \varphi_i(S(v) - S(b))$ for linearly independent forms
  $\varphi_i$ on $K^D$. On the screw assignments that vanish off $v$, they
  are the forms $S \mapsto \varphi_i(S(v))$, which are again linearly
  independent. The $\sigma_j$ vanish there, as no edge of $G_v^{ab}$ has
  $v$ as an end. So the $\rho_i$ and the $\sigma_j$ are linearly
  independent together, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-II-placement-block-independent}, and they lie in the row
  space of $R(G, q_0)$. Hence, as in
  \cref{lem:rigidityRows-pinned-placement-rank-add},
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G, q_0) \;\ge\;
      (D - 1) + D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr) \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - 1.
  \]
  As the hinges of $(G, q_0)$ are nondegenerate, its rows span its row
  space. So some $D(|V(G)| - 1) - 1$ of them are linearly independent.
  Katoh and Tanigawa note this count for $k = 0$ on
  p.~680~\cite{katohTanigawa2011}.
\end{proof}

The rows at $ab$ cannot be left out of this count when $|V(G)| \ge 3$
and $G$ has no proper rigid subgraph (\cref{def:rigid-subgraph}), as in
Case~III. Suppose that some $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at
the edges of $G_v$ were linearly independent. They are rows of
$R(G_v, q)$, so the realization $(G_v, q)$, whose hinges are
nondegenerate, would have rank at least $D(|V(G_v)| - 1)$. Then
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G_v) = 0$
(\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), and $G_v$, with its
$|V(G)| - 1 \ge 2$ vertices, would be a proper rigid subgraph of $G$.
This is why $v$ is put back with the hinge of $vb$ equal to that of
$ab$.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes a realization not of $G_v^{ab}$ but of a
  subgraph $G'$ of $G$ that does not contain $v$ and has $|V(G)| - 1$
  vertices, with nondegenerate hinges, every infinitesimal motion of
  which is constant on $V(G')$, and with $q(a)$ and $q(b)$ linearly
  independent. The rows of $G'$ are rows of $(G, q_0)$
  (\cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows}), so none of them has to be
  written through the edges $vb$ and $va$. The pinned theorem also states
  that the hinge of $va$ is nondegenerate, and it does not assume that $v$
  has degree two. By the argument above, $G'$ is a proper rigid subgraph
  of $G$ when $|V(G)| \ge 3$. So in Cases~II and~III, where $G$ has none,
  no realization meets its hypothesis. No main result uses it. The proof of
  \cref{lem:case-II-realization} makes the count for $k > 0$ as above.
\end{fmlnote}
