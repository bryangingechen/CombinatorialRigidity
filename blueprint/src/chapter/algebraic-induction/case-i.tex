\subsection{Case I: a proper rigid subgraph}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseI}

This subsection proves Case~I of KT Theorem~5.5, in which the minimal
$k$-dof-graph $G$ has at least three vertices and a proper rigid
subgraph $H$ (KT \S6.2). Katoh and Tanigawa contract $H$ to a single
vertex, realize $H$ and the contraction $G/E(H)$ by induction, and
combine the two realizations into one of $G$ whose rank is the sum of
their ranks (KT eq.~(6.3)). To obtain a nonparallel realization when $G$
is simple, they distinguish three cases: $G$ is not simple (KT
Lemma~6.2); $G$ is simple and some proper rigid subgraph has a simple
contraction (KT Lemma~6.3); and $G$ is simple and no proper rigid
subgraph has one (KT Lemma~6.5).

We first prove KT Lemma~5.4 on cycles, which Case~III uses, and define
the block pin, which pins all the bodies of a set at once. We then
follow KT \S6.2, which applies KT Lemma~3.5 to the contraction
(\cref{lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}): the rank count of KT
eq.~(6.3), with the block-triangular bound behind it, and its form for
infinitesimal motions; KT Claim~6.4, with the rank lemmas it needs; and
finally KT Lemmas~6.3, 6.2 and~6.5, with KT Claim~6.6 and the case
split that combines them (\cref{lem:case-I-all-k}).

\begin{remark}[Katoh and Tanigawa's steps and the formal proof]
  \label{rem:case-I-kt-steps}
  Several of the steps just listed follow Katoh and Tanigawa's argument,
  or restate it for infinitesimal motions, but the formal proof of KT
  Theorem~5.5 (\cref{thm:theorem-55}) does not take them. We keep them
  beside the argument they belong to, and say here what the formal proof
  does instead.

  \emph{Cycles.} KT Lemma~5.4 realizes a cycle on $m$ vertices for
  every $3 \le m \le D$ (KT p.~670). At most $D$ hinges can have
  linearly independent extensors
  (\cref{lem:cycle-realization-dim-bound}), and some $D$ panel support
  extensors are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:exists-independent-panel-extensor}). Katoh and Tanigawa
  apply KT Lemma~5.4 only to cycles on at most $d + 1$ vertices (the
  note after \cref{lem:cycle-realization}). The formal proof realizes these
  cycles with the normals of \cref{lem:cycle-normals}, and it uses
  neither of the two lemmas. For two bodies joined by two edges,
  \cref{lem:cycle-realization-base} restates \cref{lem:theorem-55-base}
  for panel-hinge frameworks. The formal proof applies the body-hinge
  form, for instance in KT Lemma~6.2
  (\cref{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple}).

  \emph{The rank count.} Katoh and Tanigawa argue with the ranks of
  rigidity matrices throughout KT \S6.2. The first diagonal block of
  KT eq.~(6.3) consists of the rows of the edges of $H$. The second
  consists of the rows of the other edges, with the columns of $V(H)$
  deleted. Katoh and Tanigawa read off that $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p)$
  is at least the sum of the ranks of the two blocks (KT
  pp.~673--675). \Cref{lem:case-I} states the count as an identity. Its
  proof obtains the reverse inequality from the block pin
  (\cref{lem:pinned-motions-on-rank-bound}). For $k = 0$, the count has
  a form for infinitesimal motions (\cref{lem:case-I-splice-seed}). Two
  more lemmas give properties of the block pin: deleting edges enlarges
  it (\cref{lem:pinned-motions-on-mono}), and it detects rigidity
  (\cref{lem:pinned-motions-on-rigid-iff}). The formal proof uses
  neither the identity nor these lemmas. Nor does it use the forms of
  the identity with the block pin (the note after \cref{lem:case-I}).

  Like Katoh and Tanigawa, the formal proof needs only the lower bound.
  No realization with nondegenerate hinges has a rank larger than the
  target rank $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). The formal proof obtains
  the lower bound by counting rows at one choice of normals. Some
  $D(|V(H)| - 1)$ rows of the edges of $H$ are linearly independent, and
  so are some $D(|V(G)| - |V(H)|) - k$ rows of the other edges after the
  columns of $V(H)$ are deleted. By the block form, all these rows
  together are linearly independent (the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-I-realization}). This count gives the rank at every
  $k$. The form for infinitesimal motions gives only rigidity, the case
  $k = 0$.

  \emph{KT Claim~6.4.} In KT Lemma~6.3 the hinges of the edges between
  $V(H)$ and the other bodies change (KT eq.~(6.6)). Katoh and Tanigawa
  compare the second block with its value when every body of $H$ is
  given the panel of the vertex to which $H$ is contracted. There it has
  the rank of the contraction. As the coefficients of their panels are
  algebraically independent, the second block has at least this rank
  (KT Claim~6.4 and eq.~(6.9), p.~675). For $k = 0$ and a simple
  contraction, \cref{lem:claim-6-4} gives this rank at some normals,
  and its proof takes these. At these normals the bodies of $H$ share
  one normal. So the hinges of the edges of $H$ are zero, and the
  normals are not in general position. The formal proof needs instead a
  nonzero polynomial in the normals, off whose zeros the second block
  has the rank of the contraction. Over an infinite field it has a
  common non-root with the polynomials for the rows of $H$ and for
  general position (the proof of \cref{lem:case-I-realization}). The
  polynomial is that of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}, at
  every $k$, and its proof takes the steps of \cref{lem:claim-6-4} at the
  same normals. It takes the place of Katoh and Tanigawa's algebraic
  independence (the passage after \cref{lem:case-I-realization}).
\end{remark}

KT Lemma~5.4 states that a cycle on $m$ vertices, for $3 \le m \le D$,
has an infinitesimally rigid nonparallel panel-hinge
realization~\cite[p.~670]{katohTanigawa2011}. Katoh and Tanigawa note
that a cycle can be analyzed directly from the definition of
infinitesimal motions. For the computation in dimension~$3$ they refer
to Crapo and Whiteley~\cite[Proposition~3.4]{crapoWhiteley1982} and to
Whiteley~\cite[Proposition~3]{whiteley1999}, and they note that it
works in every dimension. Crapo and Whiteley show that a cycle of $m$
panels and $m$ hinges in dimension~$3$ is infinitesimally rigid if and
only if the extensors of its hinges are linearly independent. We need
one direction: in every dimension, a cycle whose hinges have linearly
independent extensors is infinitesimally rigid
(\cref{lem:cycle-realization-rigid}). Since $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$
has dimension $D$, the hinges of a cycle on $m$ bodies can have linearly
independent extensors only if $m \le D$
(\cref{lem:cycle-realization-dim-bound}), the bound of KT Lemma~5.4.
For $m \le d + 1$, the normals of \cref{lem:cycle-normals} give a
generic full-rank realization of the cycle
(\cref{lem:cycle-realization}).

The case of two bodies joined by two edges is the double edge of KT
Lemma~5.3, which \cref{lem:theorem-55-base} treats for body-hinge
frameworks. The next lemma restates that lemma for panel-hinge
frameworks.

\begin{lemma}[Two bodies joined by two independent hinges]
  \label{lem:cycle-realization-base}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_rankHypothesis_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, lem:theorem-55-base}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a panel-hinge framework, and let $e_1$ and $e_2$ be
  edges of $G$ that both join the bodies $u \neq v$, with $C(p(e_1))$ and
  $C(p(e_2))$ linearly independent. Then every infinitesimal motion $S$
  of $(G,p)$ has $S(u) = S(v)$. In particular, if $u$ and $v$ are the
  only bodies, then $(G,p)$ is infinitesimally rigid, of rank $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:theorem-55-base, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}
  This is \cref{lem:theorem-55-base} for the body-hinge interpretation of
  $(G,p)$ (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). An infinitesimally rigid
  framework on two bodies has rank $D(2 - 1) = D$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[At most $D$ independent hinges]
  \label{lem:cycle-realization-dim-bound}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.card_le_screwDim_of_linearIndependent,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.card_le_screwDim_of_supportExtensor_linearIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a panel-hinge framework, and let $e_1, \dots, e_m$ be
  edges of $G$ with $C(p(e_1)), \dots, C(p(e_m))$ linearly independent.
  Then $m \le D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The extensors lie in $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$, which has dimension
  $\binom{d+1}{d-1} = D$ (the remark after
  \cref{lem:extensor-independence}).
\end{proof}

The bound of \cref{lem:cycle-realization-dim-bound} is attained by panel
support extensors (\cref{lem:exists-independent-panel-extensor}).

\begin{lemma}[Up to $D$ independent panel support extensors]
  \label{lem:exists-independent-panel-extensor}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_independent_panelSupportExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_independent_normalsJoin}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, lem:panel-support-extensor-independence}
  Let $m \le D$. Then there are $a_i, b_i \in K^{d+1}$ for
  $1 \le i \le m$ such that the extensors $C(a_i, b_i)$ are linearly
  independent, and so are the joins $a_i \vee b_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $e_0, \dots, e_d$ be the standard basis of $K^{d+1}$. The
  $\binom{d+1}{2} = D$ joins $e_s \vee e_t$ with $s < t$ are linearly
  independent (the remark after \cref{def:extensor}). Choose $m$ distinct
  pairs $s_i < t_i$, and let $a_i = e_{s_i}$ and $b_i = e_{t_i}$. Then
  the joins $a_i \vee b_i$ are linearly independent, and so are the
  extensors $C(a_i, b_i)$ (\cref{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence}).
\end{proof}

The pairs $(a_i, b_i)$ of \cref{lem:exists-independent-panel-extensor}
need not be the normals at the ends of the edges of a cycle, whose
consecutive hinges share a normal. For a cycle, the normals of
\cref{lem:cycle-normals} serve instead (\cref{lem:cycle-realization}).

\begin{lemma}[A cycle with independent hinges is rigid]
  \label{lem:cycle-realization-rigid}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.theorem_55_cycle}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:trivial-motions-rigid}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, let $m \ge 1$, and let
  $v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}$ be bodies, not necessarily distinct. Let
  $f_0, \dots, f_{m-1}$ be edges of $G$ such that $f_i$ joins $v_i$ and
  $v_{i+1}$, with indices modulo $m$, and suppose that
  $C(p(f_0)), \dots, C(p(f_{m-1}))$ are linearly independent. Then every
  infinitesimal motion $S$ of $(G,p)$ has
  $S(v_0) = S(v_1) = \cdots = S(v_{m-1})$. In particular, if
  $v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}$ are all the bodies, then $(G,p)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We argue as for KT Lemma~5.3 (\cref{lem:rank-parallel-full}) and
  \cref{lem:theorem-55-triangle}, which are the cases $m = 2$ and $m = 3$
  with distinct bodies. Let $S$ be an infinitesimal motion. By the hinge
  constraints (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}), there are scalars
  $\lambda_0, \dots, \lambda_{m-1}$ with
  $S(v_i) - S(v_{i+1}) = \lambda_i C(p(f_i))$ for each $i$.

  Since the indices are taken modulo $m$, the $m$ differences sum to
  zero. Hence $\sum_i \lambda_i C(p(f_i)) = 0$, and by the linear
  independence every $\lambda_i$ is zero. So $S(v_i) = S(v_{i+1})$ for
  every $i$. If $v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}$ are all the bodies, then $S$ is
  trivial (\cref{def:trivial-motions-rigid}).
\end{proof}

The formal realization of a cycle ends by choosing its normals once
more, in general position, without decreasing the rank (the note after
\cref{lem:cycle-realization}). Katoh and Tanigawa take steps of this
kind by KT Lemma~5.2~\cite[p.~669]{katohTanigawa2011}, a special case,
as they note, of a lemma of Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite[Lemma~7.1]{jacksonJordan2009}. It says that if a
panel-hinge realization of a simple graph has one pair of equal panels,
and every other pair of panels is nonparallel, then the graph has a
nonparallel realization whose rank is not smaller. They rotate one
panel of that pair slightly, and argue by continuity over $\R$. In the
proof of KT Lemma~6.10 they use it to turn realizations of full rank
whose panels are not nonparallel into nonparallel ones (KT p.~691).
The formalization takes such steps with polynomials in the normals in
place of the rotation: for an infinitesimally rigid realization by the
next lemma, and in general by the polynomial of
\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, as in the proof of
\cref{lem:case-II-realization}.

\begin{lemma}[A rigid realization gives a generic one]
  \label{lem:generic-realization-of-rigid}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.hasGenericFullRankRealization_of_rigidOn_ofNormals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a multigraph with
  $V(G) \neq \emptyset$ and $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. Suppose
  that some panel-hinge realization $(G, q_0)$ of $G$ has a
  nondegenerate hinge at every edge and is infinitesimally rigid on
  $V(G)$. Then $G$ has a generic full-rank realization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-general-position, lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
        lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count, lem:independent-rows-of-rank}
  The normals $q_0$ need not be in general position. As $(G, q_0)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid on $V(G)$ and its hinges are nondegenerate, some
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ rows of $R(G, q_0)$ are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}).
  As in the proof of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, there is
  a polynomial $Q$ in the normals with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$ such that the same
  rows are linearly independent at every $q$ with $Q(q) \neq 0$. Off the
  zero set of another nonzero polynomial, the normals are in general
  position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). As $K$ is infinite, some
  normals $q$ are a non-root of both.

  Then $R(G, q)$ has $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ linearly independent rows. Let $W$
  be the set of bodies. The screw assignments form a space of dimension
  $D|W|$, so
  $\dim Z(G, q) \le D|W| - D(|V(G)| - 1) = D(|W \setminus V(G)| + 1)$,
  and $(G, q)$ is infinitesimally rigid on $V(G)$
  (\cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}). Its rank is then
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}. As
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, it is at the target rank
  (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}), and its normals are in general
  position. So it is a generic full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem takes the framework $(G, q_0)$ with an endpoint
  selector that assigns each edge of $G$ its two ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), and it gives the generic
  full-rank realization the same selector.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Realizing a cycle; KT Lemma 5.4]
  \label{lem:cycle-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.cycle_realization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:rank-hypothesis, def:cycle-data, def:k-dof,
        lem:cycle-realization-rigid, lem:cycle-normals}
  Let $K$ be an infinite field, let $3 \le m \le d + 1$, and let $G$ be a
  cycle on $m$ vertices (\cref{def:cycle-data}) that is a minimal
  $0$-dof-graph. Then $G$ has a generic full-rank realization
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), of rank $D(m - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:cycle-realization-rigid,
        lem:cycle-normals, lem:generic-realization-of-rigid}
  Let
  $v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}$ and $f_0, \dots, f_{m-1}$ be the vertices and the
  edges of $G$, where $f_i$ joins $v_i$ and $v_{i+1}$, with indices
  modulo $m$ (\cref{def:cycle-data}). Let $e_0, \dots, e_d$ be the
  standard basis of $K^{d+1}$, and give the body $v_i$ the normal $e_i$,
  which is possible as $m \le d + 1$. Up to sign, the hinge at $f_i$ is
  $C(e_i, e_{i+1})$, again with indices modulo $m$
  (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). These $m$ extensors are linearly
  independent, by \cref{lem:cycle-normals} and its proof, and so are the
  hinges.

  So every infinitesimal motion is constant on
  $\{v_0, \dots, v_{m-1}\} = V(G)$ (\cref{lem:cycle-realization-rigid}).
  As $G$ is a $0$-dof-graph, $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, and the
  realization is at the target rank $D(m - 1)$
  (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). Any two distinct
  standard basis vectors are linearly independent, so the normals are in
  general position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). Hence the
  realization is a generic full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  Katoh and Tanigawa state the lemma for $3 \le m \le D$ (KT p.~670).
  The statement has the range of the pinned theorem, $m \le d + 1$, which
  is theirs when $d = 2$, where $D = 3$. For $d \ge 3$ we have
  $d + 1 < D$, and the cycles with $d + 1 < m \le D$ are not covered. No
  use needs them. Katoh and Tanigawa apply the lemma to the triangle in
  their proof of Lemma~6.7(i) (KT p.~677), and to cycles on at most $d$
  vertices in their proof of Lemma~6.13 (KT p.~692;
  \cref{lem:chain-cycle-dichotomy}). Case~III applies it to the same
  cycles (\cref{lem:case-III}).

  The formal framework also has bodies outside $V(G)$
  (\cref{fmlnote:generic-full-rank}), and general position concerns their
  normals too. The pinned theorem gives those bodies the zero normal.
  It then chooses new normals for all the bodies, in general position, at
  which the rank of $R(G,p)$ is still $D(m - 1)$. Such normals exist
  because $K$ is infinite: each of the two conditions holds off the zero
  set of a nonzero polynomial in the normals, for the rank a minor of
  the rows that does not vanish at the given normals. This is the
  argument of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, later in this
  chapter (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative}), for one
  realization, and the step is \cref{lem:generic-realization-of-rigid}.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa's Case~I contracts a proper rigid subgraph $H$ of $G$
to one vertex (KT \S6.2, p.~673). In terms of infinitesimal motions, the
corresponding operation pins all the bodies of $H$ at once, as
$\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ does for one body in KT Lemma~5.1
(\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}).

\begin{definition}[The block pin of a set of bodies]
  \label{def:pinned-motions-on}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.pinnedMotionsOn,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.pinnedMotionsOn_singleton,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.pinnedMotionsOn_eq_iInf}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, lem:rank-delete-vertex}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, and let $s$ be a set of bodies.
  The \emph{block pin} of $s$ is the subspace
  \[
    Z_s(G,p) \;=\; \{S \in Z(G,p) : S(v) = 0 \text{ for every } v \in s\}
  \]
  of the infinitesimal motions that vanish on $s$.
\end{definition}

For a single body, $Z_{\{v\}}(G,p)$ is the subspace
$\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ of \cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}. For a
nonempty set $s$, $Z_s(G,p)$ is the intersection of the subspaces
$\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ with $v \in s$.

\begin{lemma}[Deleting edges enlarges the block pin]
  \label{lem:pinned-motions-on-mono}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.pinnedMotionsOn_le_withGraph_of_le,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_pinnedMotionsOn_le_of_graph_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pinned-motions-on, def:framework-with-graph, lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, let $s$ be a set of bodies, and
  let $(G',p)$ be the restriction of $(G,p)$ to a subgraph $G'$ of $G$
  (\cref{def:framework-with-graph}). Then
  $Z_s(G,p) \subseteq Z_s(G',p)$. In particular,
  $\dim Z_s(G,p) \le \dim Z_s(G',p)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}
  The conditions $S(v) = 0$ for $v \in s$ do not involve the graph, and
  $Z(G,p) \subseteq Z(G',p)$ (\cref{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}).
\end{proof}

For one body, $\dim \operatorname{pinned}(v) + D = \dim Z(G,p)$
(\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}). For a larger set of bodies, only an
inequality holds.

\begin{lemma}[The block pin and the trivial motions]
  \label{lem:pinned-motions-on-rank-bound}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.screwDim_add_finrank_pinnedMotionsOn_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pinned-motions-on, lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, and let $s$ be a nonempty set of
  bodies. Then
  \[
    D + \dim Z_s(G,p) \;\le\; \dim Z(G,p).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex}
  Let $v \in s$. Then $Z_s(G,p) \subseteq \operatorname{pinned}(v)$, and
  $\operatorname{pinned}(v)$ meets the space of trivial motions only in
  $0$ (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}). The trivial motions form a
  $D$-dimensional subspace of $Z(G,p)$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}). So $Z(G,p)$ contains the
  direct sum of this subspace and $Z_s(G,p)$.
\end{proof}

A block pin also detects infinitesimal rigidity.

\begin{lemma}[Rigidity and the block pin]
  \label{lem:pinned-motions-on-rigid-iff}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.pinnedMotionsOn_eq_bot_of_isInfinitesimallyRigid,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_pinnedMotionsOn_eq_zero_of_isInfinitesimallyRigid,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigid_of_block_of_pinnedMotionsOn_eq_bot}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:pinned-motions-on}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, and let $s$ be a nonempty set of
  bodies. If $(G,p)$ is infinitesimally rigid, then $Z_s(G,p) = 0$.
  Conversely, if every infinitesimal motion of $(G,p)$ is constant on $s$
  and $Z_s(G,p) = 0$, then $(G,p)$ is infinitesimally rigid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $(G,p)$ be infinitesimally rigid, and let $S \in Z_s(G,p)$. Then
  $S$ is trivial, that is, constant (\cref{def:trivial-motions-rigid}),
  and it vanishes at a body of $s$. So $S = 0$.

  Conversely, let $S$ be an infinitesimal motion, let $v \in s$, and let
  $T$ be the trivial motion with the value $S(v)$ at every body. Then
  $S - T$ is an infinitesimal motion, and it vanishes on $s$ because $S$
  is constant on $s$. So $S - T \in Z_s(G,p) = 0$, and $S = T$ is
  trivial.
\end{proof}

We now follow Katoh and Tanigawa's sketch of Case~I at the start of KT
\S6.2 (p.~673). Let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph, let $H$ be a proper
rigid subgraph of $G$, and let $G/E(H)$ be its contraction to a vertex
$r$ of $H$ (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}). Then $H$ is a minimal
$0$-dof-graph (KT Lemma~3.3, \cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}), and
$G/E(H)$ is a minimal $k$-dof-graph with fewer vertices than $G$ (KT
Lemma~3.5, \cref{lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}). So the induction
hypothesis, KT eq.~(6.1), gives a realization $(H, p_1)$ of rank
$D(|V(H)| - 1)$ and a realization $(G/E(H), p_2)$ of rank
$D(|V(G)| - |V(H)|) - k$. Katoh and Tanigawa replace the body $r$ of
$(G/E(H), p_2)$ by the rigid framework $(H, p_1)$. They show that the
rank of the resulting realization of $G$ is the sum of the two ranks,
which is $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$, the target rank of $G$. We prove this rank
count, KT eq.~(6.3), and then KT Claim~6.4, which Katoh and Tanigawa use
when $G$ and $G/E(H)$ are simple.

Katoh and Tanigawa read the rank count off the shape (6.3) of
$R(G,p)$: with the columns of $V(H)$ first, the rows of $H$ have zeros
in all the other columns~\cite[pp.~673--674]{katohTanigawa2011}. The
next lemma is the bound that this shape gives, for any set $W$ of
bodies, where $\pi_W$ deletes the columns of $W$
(\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix-blocks}).

\begin{lemma}[The block-triangular rank bound; KT eq.~(6.3)]
  \label{lem:block-rank-contract}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_induce_add_map_extProj_le}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a panel-hinge framework, let $W$ be a set of bodies, and
  let $H = G[W]$. Let $R_W(G \setminus E(H), p)$ be the matrix of the rows
  of the edges of $G$ not in $H$, with the columns of $W$ deleted. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(H, p) + \operatorname{rank} R_W(G \setminus E(H), p)
    \;\le\; \operatorname{rank} R(G, p).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $\mathcal R$, $\mathcal R_H$ and $\mathcal R'$ be the row spaces of
  $R(G,p)$, $R(H,p)$ and $R(G \setminus E(H), p)$. The rows of $H$ and of
  $G \setminus E(H)$ are rows of $G$, so $\mathcal R_H$ and $\mathcal R'$
  lie in $\mathcal R$. A row of an edge of $H$ depends only on the screws
  at the bodies of $W$, so $\pi_W$ maps it to $0$. Hence
  $\mathcal R_H \subseteq \mathcal R \cap \ker \pi_W$. The rank of
  $R_W(G \setminus E(H), p)$ is $\dim \pi_W(\mathcal R')$, which is at
  most $\dim \pi_W(\mathcal R)$. The bound follows, since
  $\dim(\mathcal R \cap \ker \pi_W) + \dim \pi_W(\mathcal R)
  = \dim \mathcal R$ by rank-nullity for $\pi_W$ on $\mathcal R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Case~I: the rank adds over the contraction; KT eq.~(6.3)]
  \label{lem:case-I}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_iff_pinnedMotionsOn_le,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.toBodyHinge_rankHypothesis_iff_finrank_pinnedMotionsOn,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_iff_finrank_pinnedMotionsOn}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, def:pinned-motions-on,
        lem:pinned-motions-on-rank-bound, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph, let $H$ be a proper rigid subgraph
  of $G$, and let $G/E(H)$ be its contraction to a vertex $r$ of $H$.
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, and let $(G/E(H), p_2)$ be a
  realization of $G/E(H)$ at the target rank
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). Then $(G,p)$ is at the target rank if and
  only if
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;=\; D\bigl(|V(H)| - 1\bigr)
      + \operatorname{rank} R\bigl(G/E(H), p_2\bigr).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:pinned-motions-on-rank-bound,
    lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}
  By KT Lemma~3.5 (\cref{lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}), $G/E(H)$ is
  a minimal $k$-dof-graph. It has $|V(G)| - |V(H)| + 1$ vertices
  (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}), so
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G/E(H), p_2) = D(|V(G)| - |V(H)|) - k$. The
  right side of the identity is therefore $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$, the
  target rank of $G$. This proves the lemma.

  Katoh and Tanigawa attain the right side in their Lemmas~6.2 and~6.3
  (pp.~673--675). There $(H, p_1)$ is a realization of $H$ of rank
  $D(|V(H)| - 1)$, and $p$ has the hinges of $p_1$ at the edges of $H$
  (KT eqs.~(6.2) and~(6.6)). Put the columns of the bodies of $H$ first.
  The hinge constraint at an edge of $H$ involves only the screws at
  bodies of $H$, so $R(G,p)$ has the block form of KT eq.~(6.3):
  \[
    R(G,p) \;=\;
    \begin{pmatrix}
      R(H, p_1) & 0 \\
      \ast & R_{V(H)}\bigl(G \setminus E(H), p\bigr)
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The first block row consists of the rows of the edges of $H$, and the
  second of the rows of the other edges. Its right block
  $R_{V(H)}(G \setminus E(H), p)$ consists of these rows with the
  columns of $V(H)$ deleted. Suppose that some rows of the first block
  row are linearly independent, and that some rows of the second are
  linearly independent after the columns of $V(H)$ are deleted. Then all
  of them together are linearly independent. Indeed, in a vanishing
  linear combination only the rows of the second block row have nonzero
  entries in the columns of $V(G) \setminus V(H)$, so their coefficients
  vanish, and then so do the others. Hence
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;\ge\; D\bigl(|V(H)| - 1\bigr)
      + \operatorname{rank} R_{V(H)}\bigl(G \setminus E(H), p\bigr)
  \]
  (\cref{lem:block-rank-contract}).

  When the rows of $H$ have rank $D(|V(H)| - 1)$, as here, this bound is
  an equality. A screw assignment that vanishes on $V(H)$ satisfies the
  hinge constraints at the edges of $H$. It satisfies those at the other
  edges exactly when its values at the other bodies lie in the null space
  of $R_{V(H)}(G \setminus E(H), p)$. So the block pin $Z_{V(H)}(G,p)$
  (\cref{def:pinned-motions-on}) has dimension
  $D(|V(G)| - |V(H)|) - \operatorname{rank} R_{V(H)}(G \setminus E(H), p)$.
  Since $\operatorname{rank} R(G,p) = D|V(G)| - \dim Z(G,p)$, the bound
  $D + \dim Z_{V(H)}(G,p) \le \dim Z(G,p)$
  (\cref{lem:pinned-motions-on-rank-bound}) is the reverse inequality.

  It remains to compare the second block with $R(G/E(H), p_2)$. The
  bodies of $G/E(H)$ meet $V(H)$ only in $r$, so deleting the columns of
  $V(H)$ from $R(G/E(H), p_2)$ deletes those of $r$. By KT Lemma~5.1
  (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}), this does not change the rank. Katoh
  and Tanigawa note this at eq.~(6.5), and
  \cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter} states it in this form. In
  KT Lemma~6.2, $p$ has the hinges of $p_2$ at the edges not in $H$. The
  row of such an edge in $R(G,p)$ then differs from its row in $R(G/E(H), p_2)$ only in the
  columns of $V(H)$ (KT eq.~(6.4)). So the second block has the rank of
  $R(G/E(H), p_2)$, and the identity holds.

  In KT Lemma~6.3, $G$ and $G/E(H)$ are simple, and $p$ changes the
  hinges at the edges between $V(H)$ and the other bodies (KT
  eq.~(6.6)). With KT Lemma~5.1, KT Claim~6.4 shows that the second
  block still has at least the rank of $R(G/E(H), p_2)$ (KT eq.~(6.9);
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}, and \cref{lem:claim-6-4} at
  one point, below). The proof of the claim uses that $p_1$
  and $p_2$ are generic, so that the rank is the largest over all choices
  of the panels.
  The general form of this argument is the assertion of
  \cref{lem:genericity-device} on the largest rank, in
  \cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-genericity}: where every hinge
  is nondegenerate, the largest rank is attained on a dense Zariski-open
  set of normals. Hence the rank of
  $R(G,p)$ is at least the target rank. It is also at most the target
  rank, as the hinges are nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  No pinned theorem mentions the contraction. The second and third state
  the lemma with the block pin in place of $R(G/E(H), p_2)$, for
  panel-hinge and for body-hinge frameworks. Let $s$ be a nonempty set of
  bodies, and suppose that $\dim Z(G,p) \le D + \dim Z_s(G,p)$. Then
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + k'$, the form of the target rank in
  \cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}, if and only if
  $\dim Z_s(G,p) = k'$. For $s = V(H)$, the hypothesis holds when the rows
  of $H$ have rank $D(|V(H)| - 1)$, by the proof above, and
  $\dim Z_s(G,p) = k$ says that the second block has the rank of
  $R(G/E(H), p_2)$. The first pinned theorem assumes that every
  infinitesimal motion of $(G,p)$ is constant on $s$, and lets
  $s \subseteq t$. Then every infinitesimal motion is constant on $t$ if
  and only if $Z_s(G,p) \subseteq Z_t(G,p)$. It is the one used
  elsewhere, with $s$ a single body, in
  \cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}, later in this chapter.
\end{fmlnote}

For $k = 0$ the rank count has a form in terms of infinitesimal motions.
In it the contraction has the hinges of $p$: it is the framework
$(G/E(H), p)$ of \cref{def:framework-with-graph}. Katoh and Tanigawa's
framework of eq.~(6.7) is of this kind.

\begin{lemma}[Rigidity from $H$ and the contraction; KT eq.~(6.3)]
  \label{lem:case-I-splice-seed}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_of_splice}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework-with-graph, def:rigid-contraction}
  Let $(G,p)$ be a body-hinge framework, let $H$ be a proper rigid
  subgraph of $G$, and let $G/E(H)$ be its contraction to a vertex $r$ of
  $H$, with vertex set $s_c = (V(G) \setminus V(H)) \cup \{r\}$. Suppose
  that every infinitesimal motion of the restriction $(H,p)$ is constant
  on $V(H)$, and that every infinitesimal motion of $(G/E(H), p)$ is
  constant on $s_c$. Then every infinitesimal motion of $(G,p)$ is
  constant on $V(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}
  Let $S$ be an infinitesimal motion of $(G,p)$. As $H$ is a subgraph of
  $G$, $S$ is an infinitesimal motion of $(H,p)$
  (\cref{lem:motions-mono-of-graph-le}), so $S$ is constant on $V(H)$.
  Let $f$ map every vertex of $H$ to $r$ and fix the other bodies. Then
  $S(f(w)) = S(w)$ for every body $w$, as $r \in V(H)$. An edge $e$ of
  $G/E(H)$ is an edge of $G$ not in $H$. If its ends in $G$ are $u$ and
  $v$, then its ends in $G/E(H)$ are $f(u)$ and $f(v)$
  (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}), and
  $S(f(u)) - S(f(v)) = S(u) - S(v)$. So $S$ satisfies the hinge
  constraint of $(G/E(H), p)$ at $e$, because it satisfies that of
  $(G,p)$ (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). Hence $S$ is an infinitesimal
  motion of $(G/E(H), p)$, and it is constant on $s_c$. The sets $V(H)$
  and $s_c$ share the body $r$ and cover $V(G)$, so $S$ is constant on
  $V(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem joins the restrictions of $(G,p)$ to two subgraphs
  $G_1$ and $G_2$ of $G$. Let $s_1$ and $s_2$ be sets of bodies with a
  common body, and suppose that every infinitesimal motion of $(G_i, p)$
  is constant on $s_i$ for $i = 1, 2$. Then every infinitesimal motion of
  $(G,p)$ is constant on every set $t \subseteq s_1 \cup s_2$. Its proof
  is the first and the last step of the proof above. The contraction
  $G/E(H)$ is not a subgraph of $G$ when an edge not in $H$ has an end in
  $V(H) \setminus \{r\}$. So the lemma is not an instance of the pinned
  theorem, and no pinned theorem gives the step from $(G,p)$ to
  $(G/E(H), p)$. No main result uses the pinned theorem.
\end{fmlnote}

The lemma takes a different set of bodies for each of its two
frameworks, and the contraction needs this. Contracting $H$ identifies
all of $V(H)$ with $r$, so the bodies of $V(H) \setminus \{r\}$ are not
vertices of $G/E(H)$. Each edge of $G/E(H)$ is an edge of $G$ not in
$H$, and each of its ends in $V(H)$ is replaced by $r$, so both of its
ends lie in $s_c$. Hence no hinge constraint of $(G/E(H), p)$ involves a
body of $V(H) \setminus \{r\}$, and an infinitesimal motion of
$(G/E(H), p)$ may take any values there. So the lemma can ask that the
infinitesimal motions of $(G/E(H), p)$ be constant on $s_c$, but on no
larger set, and in particular not on $V(G)$. The bodies of
$V(H) \setminus \{r\}$ are held by the edges of $H$, which are the
edges of the restriction $(H, p)$.

The same two sets of bodies appear in the rank count of
\cref{lem:case-I}. The first block of KT eq.~(6.3) has the columns of
$V(H)$, and its rank is $D(|V(H)| - 1)$. The second has the columns of
$V(G) \setminus V(H) = s_c \setminus \{r\}$. In KT Lemma~6.2 its rank
is $\operatorname{rank} R(G/E(H), p_2) = D(|s_c| - 1) - k$, since
deleting the columns of the one body $r$ does not change the rank, and
in KT Lemma~6.3 it is at least this. The two sets share exactly the body
$r$, so
$|V(H)| + |s_c| = |V(G)| + 1$ and
\[
  D\bigl(|V(H)| - 1\bigr) + D\bigl(|s_c| - 1\bigr) - k
  \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - k,
\]
the target rank of $G$ (KT p.~675).

In KT Lemma~6.3 the graphs $G$ and $G/E(H)$ are simple, and the
realizations $(H, p_1)$ and $(G/E(H), p_2)$ are nonparallel. Katoh and
Tanigawa give an edge of $G$ from a body $u \notin V(H)$ to a body
$v \in V(H)$ the hinge in which the panel of $u$ in $p_2$ meets the panel
of $v$ in $p_1$ (KT eq.~(6.6)). On the edges not in $H$, these hinges
form a body-hinge framework $(G/E(H), p')$ on the contraction, in which
every body other than $r$ is a panel and $r$ need not be one (KT
eq.~(6.7), where $p'$ is written $p_{E \setminus E'}$). KT Claim~6.4
states that
$\operatorname{rank} R(G/E(H), p') \ge \operatorname{rank} R(G/E(H), p_2)$
(p.~675). Its proof has two steps. First, as Katoh and Tanigawa note
after Jackson and Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}, the entries of
$R(G/E(H), p')$ can be written in terms of the coefficients of the
equations of the panels of $p_1$ and $p_2$. Second, if every body of $H$
is given the panel of $r$ in $p_2$, then $p'$ becomes $p_2$. As the
coefficients of $p_1$ and $p_2$ are algebraically independent,
$R(G/E(H), p')$ has the largest rank over all these choices, which is at
least $\operatorname{rank} R(G/E(H), p_2)$. With KT Lemma~5.1 the claim
gives KT eq.~(6.9), the bound on the second block in the proof of
\cref{lem:case-I}. For panel normals, the first step is the next lemma.
The lemmas after it give the rows outside $H$ at every deficiency
(\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}), and \cref{lem:claim-6-4}
gives the rank at the placement of the second step when $G/E(H)$ is
simple and rigid.

\begin{lemma}[Rows polynomial in the normals; KT Claim~6.4]
  \label{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.annihRowPoly_eval,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.annihRowPoly_totalDegree_le,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.span_annihRow_eq_dualAnnihilator}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $e$ be an edge of $G$. There is a
  finite family of linear forms on screw assignments, depending on the
  normals $n = (n_v)$, whose coordinates in the standard basis are
  polynomials of degree two in the coordinates of the normals. For every
  panel-hinge framework $(G, n)$ whose hinge at $e$ is nondegenerate, the
  family at $n$ spans the same space as the rows of $R(G,n)$ at $e$
  (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $u$ and $v$ be the ends of $e$, and let $C = C(n_u, n_v)$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). For $x \in \bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$,
  write $x_t$ for its coordinate at the $t$-th standard basis vector. The
  family consists of the forms
  \[
    S \;\mapsto\; C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t,
    \qquad \text{where } x = S(u) - S(v),
  \]
  for all pairs of indices $t$ and $t'$. As a form in $x$, each of them
  vanishes at $x = C$. If $C \neq 0$, say $C_{t_0} \neq 0$, then the
  forms $x \mapsto C_{t_0} x_{t'} - C_{t'} x_{t_0}$ with $t' \neq t_0$ are
  $D - 1$ linearly independent forms that vanish at $C$. So they span the
  space of forms that vanish at $C$, which has dimension $D - 1$. Hence
  the family spans the same space as the rows of $R(G,n)$ at $e$
  (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}).

  The coordinates of these forms are linear in those of $C$. The
  coordinates of the join $n_u \vee n_v$ (\cref{def:join}) are the
  $2 \times 2$ minors $n_u(i)\, n_v(j) - n_u(j)\, n_v(i)$ with $i < j$,
  which are polynomials of degree two in the normals. The complement
  isomorphism $*$ maps each standard basis vector of
  $\bigwedge^2 K^{d+1}$ to a nonzero multiple of one of
  $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ (the proof of
  \cref{def:meet-complement-iso}). So the coordinates of
  $C = *(n_u \vee n_v)$ are polynomials of degree two in the normals, and
  so are those of the forms.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorems state the lemma for the forms in $x$. The first
  gives the coordinate of the form $x \mapsto C_t x_{t'} - C_{t'} x_t$ at
  each standard basis vector as the value at the normals of a polynomial
  in the normals of $u$ and $v$, and the second bounds the degree of that
  polynomial by two. The coordinates of the form in $S$ are these, up to
  sign, at the columns of $u$ and $v$. The third is the spanning: for
  $C \neq 0$, the forms in $x$ span the forms that vanish at $C$. The
  pinned theorem of \cref{lem:relative-device-count} takes a minor of
  these forms, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}.
\end{fmlnote}

The algebraic induction chooses its realizations off the zeros of
polynomials in the panel normals. The rows of $R(G,q)$ can be chosen
with entries that are polynomials in the normals $q$
(\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So if some $N$ rows of
$R(G,q_0)$ are linearly independent, one of their $N \times N$ minors is
a polynomial $Q$ in the normals with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$, and the rank of
$R(G,q)$ is at least $N$ wherever $Q(q) \neq 0$. This is, in
polynomial form, the lower semicontinuity of the rank by which Katoh
and Tanigawa prove KT Lemma~5.2 over $\R$ (KT p.~669; the passage
before \cref{lem:generic-realization-of-rigid}), and
\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} states it in
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative}. The next lemma is its
form for rows with the columns of a set of bodies deleted.

\begin{lemma}[Independent rows with columns deleted, off the zeros of a polynomial]
  \label{lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankPolynomial_of_rigidOn_linking_set_proj_eval}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $G$ be a graph, let $t$ be a set of bodies, and let $q_0$ be panel
  normals at which some $m$ rows of $R(G,q_0)$ are linearly independent
  after the columns of $t$ are deleted. Then there is a polynomial $Q$ in
  the panel normals with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$ such that at every $q$ with
  $Q(q) \neq 0$, some $m$ rows of $R(G,q)$ are linearly independent after
  the columns of $t$ are deleted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}
  Deleting the columns of $t$ replaces some entries of a row by zeros,
  independently of the normals, so the rows of $R(G,q)$ with these
  columns deleted again have entries that are polynomials in $q$
  (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). Take $m$ rows that are
  independent after the deletion at $q_0$. Some $m \times m$ minor of
  these $m$ rows, with the columns of $t$ deleted, is nonzero at $q_0$.
  Let $Q$ be this minor, as a polynomial in the normals. Wherever
  $Q(q) \neq 0$, the same $m$ rows are independent after the deletion.
  This is the argument of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}.
\end{proof}

The induction hypothesis, KT eq.~(6.1), supplies a realization of
$G/E(H)$ whose rank is $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}$
(\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) and whose normals are in general position:
any two of them are linearly independent
(\cref{def:panel-general-position}). Such a
realization also gives one with nondegenerate hinges, the form of the
hypothesis in \cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}
(\cref{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}). Case~I compares the rows of the
realization of $G/E(H)$ with those of $G \setminus E(H)$ edge by edge,
and the next lemma first orients the edges of $G/E(H)$ as those of
$G \setminus E(H)$.

\begin{lemma}[Reorienting a realization of the contraction]
  \label{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofNormals_relabel_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq,
    def:rank-hypothesis, def:panel-general-position}
  Let $H$ be a subgraph of $G$, let $r \in V(H)$, let $f$ map every body
  of $V(H)$ to $r$ and fix every other body, and let $G_1 = G/E(H)$.
  Orient the edges of $G \setminus E(H)$, and orient an edge of $G_1$
  from $f(u)$ to $f(v)$ if it is oriented from $u$ to $v$ in
  $G \setminus E(H)$. Suppose that $G_1$ has a panel-hinge realization
  whose normals are in general position and whose rank is
  $D(|V(G_1)|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G_1)$. Then there are panel
  normals in general position at which the panel-hinge framework on $G_1$,
  with this orientation, has the same rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}
  Take the normals of the given realization; general position is a
  condition on the normals alone. The given realization orients each edge
  of $G_1$ one way or the other. Reversing an edge changes the sign of the
  extensor of its hinge, which is built from the normals of its ends in
  order (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), and so does not change its
  hinge constraint. The two orientations therefore give the same
  infinitesimal motions, and so the same row space and rank
  (\cref{lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization records an orientation of each edge with a
  panel-hinge framework (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), and compares
  the rows of two frameworks edge by edge only for equal orientations.
  The realization that the induction hypothesis supplies orients
  $G/E(H)$ in its own way; the lemma gives it the orientation of
  $G \setminus E(H)$.
\end{fmlnote}

The next lemma combines the last two with
\cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}, as KT eq.~(6.9) combines KT
Lemma~5.1 and Claim~6.4.

\begin{lemma}[The rows outside a contracted subgraph; KT Claim~6.4]
  \label{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankPolynomial_of_IH_relabel_linking_set_proj}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank, lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter,
    def:rigid-contraction}
  Let $H$ be a proper rigid subgraph of $G$, let $r \in V(H)$, and let
  $G_1 = G/E(H)$. Suppose that $G_1$ is a minimal $k$-dof-graph
  (\cref{def:k-dof}) with a panel-hinge realization whose normals are in
  general position and whose rank is $D(|V(G_1)|-1) - k$. Then there is a nonzero polynomial $Q$ in the
  panel normals such that at every $q$ with $Q(q) \neq 0$, some
  $D(|V(G_1)|-1) - k$ rows of $R(G \setminus E(H), q)$ are linearly
  independent after the columns of $V(H)$ are deleted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank, lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter,
    lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval,
    lem:general-position-support-nonzero, lem:independent-rows-of-rank}
  Let $N = D(|V(G_1)|-1) - k$, and let $f$ map the bodies of $V(H)$ to
  $r$ and fix the others. Orient $G \setminus E(H)$, and $G_1$ as in
  \cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}. By that lemma there are
  normals $n$ in general position at which $R(G_1, n)$ has rank $N$. The
  bodies of $G_1$ meet $V(H)$ only in $r$, so deleting the columns of
  $V(H)$ keeps the rank $N$ (\cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}).
  A minimal $k$-dof-graph has no loops: every base of $M(\tilde G_1)$
  meets the copies of every edge (\cref{def:k-dof}), and the copies of a
  loop lie in no base
  (\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}loopless\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}isMinimalKDof}).
  So the hinges of $G_1$ at $n$ are nonzero
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}), and some $N$ rows of
  $R(G_1, n)$ are independent after the deletion.

  Let $q_0$ give each body $v$ the normal $n_{f(v)}$: the bodies of
  $V(H)$ get the normal of $r$, and the other bodies keep theirs. At
  $q_0$ an edge of $G \setminus E(H)$ from $u$ to $v$ has the hinge that
  the same edge of $G_1$, from $f(u)$ to $f(v)$, has at $n$. Its rows
  in the two matrices differ only in the columns of the bodies of $V(H)$.
  So after the columns of $V(H)$ are deleted, the rows of
  $R(G \setminus E(H), q_0)$ are those of $R(G_1, n)$, and $N$ of them
  are independent. By \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval} there is a
  polynomial $Q$ with $Q(q_0) \neq 0$, so that $Q \neq 0$, such that at
  every $q$ with $Q(q) \neq 0$ some $N$ rows of
  $R(G \setminus E(H), q)$ are independent after the deletion.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The rows in this lemma and in \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval} are
  those of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}: for each edge, a fixed
  family of linear forms that vanish on the extensor of its hinge, with
  entries polynomial in the normals. They span the row space of the edge
  when its hinge is nonzero, which is why the proof needs $G/E(H)$ to
  have no loops: a loop has a zero hinge.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa reach the placement $q_0$ by giving every body of
$V(H)$ the panel of the contracted body, and conclude from the algebraic
independence of the coefficients of their panels that the rows of
$G \setminus E(H)$ attain the rank of $G/E(H)$~\cite[Claim~6.4,
p.~675]{katohTanigawa2011}. The polynomial $Q$ takes the place of that
argument, and Case~I uses the lemma in \cref{lem:case-I-realization}.
The next lemma gives the rank at one placement, $q_0$ in its proof, when
$G/E(H)$ is simple and has deficiency zero. Its conclusion is that of
\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj} for $k = 0$ at one point, and
it assumes $G/E(H)$ simple where that lemma assumes it minimal.

\begin{lemma}[The rows outside $H$ at one point; KT Claim~6.4 and eq.~(6.9)]
  \label{lem:claim-6-4}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rigidContract_exterior_rank_transport_htransport}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidity-matrix, def:rigid-contraction, lem:rank-delete-vertex}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $H$ be a proper rigid subgraph of $G$, and
  let $G/E(H)$ be its contraction to a vertex $r$ of $H$, with vertex set
  $s_c = (V(G) \setminus V(H)) \cup \{r\}$. Suppose that $G/E(H)$ is a
  simple $0$-dof-graph (\cref{def:k-dof}) with a generic full-rank
  realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). Then there are normals $q_0$
  at which some $D(|s_c| - 1)$ rows of $R(G \setminus E(H), q_0)$ are
  linearly independent after the columns of $V(H)$ are deleted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-delete-vertex, lem:general-position-support-nonzero,
    lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
    lem:independent-rows-of-rank, lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count,
    lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}
  Let $N = D(|s_c| - 1)$, and let $f$ map every body of $V(H)$ to $r$ and
  fix the other bodies. As $G/E(H)$ is a $0$-dof-graph, its target rank
  is $N$. Its generic full-rank realization has normals $n$ in general
  position at which $R(G/E(H), n)$ has rank $N$, so it is infinitesimally
  rigid on $s_c$ (\cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}). The bodies
  of $G/E(H)$ meet $V(H)$ only in $r$. So deleting the columns of $V(H)$
  from $R(G/E(H), n)$ deletes those of the one body $r$, and by KT
  Lemma~5.1 (\cref{lem:rank-delete-vertex}) the rank stays $N$
  (\cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}).

  Now give each body $v$ the normal $q_0(v) = n_{f(v)}$, so that the
  bodies of $V(H)$ get the normal of $r$. This is the choice in Katoh and
  Tanigawa's proof of Claim~6.4 that turns $p'$ into $p_2$. Let $e$ be an
  edge of $G \setminus E(H)$ with ends $u$ and $v$. At $q_0$ its hinge is
  $C(n_{f(u)}, n_{f(v)})$, the hinge of $e$ in $G/E(H)$ at $n$, as the
  ends of $e$ in $G/E(H)$ are $f(u)$ and $f(v)$. Its two rows differ only
  in the columns of the bodies of $V(H)$. So after the columns of $V(H)$
  are deleted, the rows of
  $R(G \setminus E(H), q_0)$ are those of $R(G/E(H), n)$, and they have
  rank $N$.

  Finally, $G/E(H)$ is simple, so it has no loops. As $n$ is in general
  position, the hinges of $G/E(H)$ at $n$, and so those of
  $G \setminus E(H)$ at $q_0$, are nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). So the forms of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals} at $q_0$ span the rows of every
  edge of $G \setminus E(H)$, and some $N$ of them are linearly
  independent after the deletion, as in
  \cref{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The rows of the statement are the forms of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}, as in
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}. Of $H$ the pinned theorem
  assumes only that $r \in V(H) \subseteq V(G)$, and of $G$ nothing. It
  assumes that the endpoint selector gives each edge of
  $G \setminus E(H)$ its two ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). Its statement gives some
  normals $q_0$; its proof takes them of the form above, giving every
  body of $V(H)$ the normal of $r$. Its proof first
  orients each edge of $G/E(H)$ as the same edge of $G \setminus E(H)$, as in
  \cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}, so that the rows of
  the two graphs can be compared edge by edge. The polynomial $Q$, for
  every deficiency, is \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}, whose
  proof takes the same steps and then those of
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-proj-eval}. The formal proof of Case~I uses
  that lemma, and not this one.
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa need three cases to obtain a nonparallel realization
when $G$ is simple~\cite[pp.~673--676]{katohTanigawa2011}. The reason
is that the contraction $G/E(H)$ need not be simple when $G$ is. The
other graphs that Case~I builds from $G$, such as $G \setminus E(H)$,
$H$, and the graph $G_v$ obtained by removing a vertex $v$
(\cref{def:graph-operations}), are subgraphs of $G$, and a subgraph of a
simple graph is simple. The contraction instead identifies every vertex
of $H$ with $r$ (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}), and this can create a
loop or two parallel edges. An edge of $G$ that is not in $H$ but has
both ends in $V(H)$ becomes a loop at $r$. Two edges not in $H$ become
parallel when their ends are identified in pairs. For instance, two
edges that join a vertex $w \notin V(H)$ to two vertices of $H$ both
join $w$ and $r$ in $G/E(H)$. The simplicity of $G$ excludes neither
configuration. So the simplicity of $G/E(H)$ is a hypothesis of KT
Lemma~6.3, and it does not follow from that of $G$.

This gives the three cases. If $G$ is not simple, then two parallel
edges of $G$ form a proper rigid subgraph, and KT Lemma~6.2 contracts it
(\cref{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple}). The induction asks for a
nonparallel realization only of a simple graph, so this case needs no
general position. If $G$ is simple and some proper rigid subgraph $H$
has a simple contraction, then the induction hypothesis gives generic
full-rank realizations of $H$ and $G/E(H)$, and KT Lemma~6.3 combines
them (\cref{lem:case-I-realization}). If $G$ is simple and no proper
rigid subgraph has a simple contraction, then KT Lemma~6.5 removes a
vertex $v$ of degree two instead (\cref{lem:case-I-realization-h65}).
The graph $G_v$ is a subgraph of $G$, so it is simple, and the induction
hypothesis gives a generic full-rank realization of it. By KT
Claim~6.6 (\cref{lem:claim-6-6}), this last case occurs only for
$k = 0$. We prove KT Lemma~6.3 first, then KT Lemma~6.2, KT Claim~6.6
and KT Lemma~6.5, and finally the case split.

\begin{lemma}[Case~I with a simple contraction; KT Lemma~6.3]
  \label{lem:case-I-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_I_realization_all_k_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, lem:case-I, lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof,
        lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj,
        lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals, lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count,
        def:genuine-hinge-realization}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a simple
  minimal $k$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$. Let $H$ be a proper rigid
  subgraph of $G$ whose contraction $G/E(H)$ to a vertex $r$ of $H$ is
  simple, and suppose that $H$ and $G/E(H)$ have generic full-rank
  realizations (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). Then $G$ has a generic
  full-rank realization, of rank $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals, lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj,
        lem:case-I, lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof,
        lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count, lem:subgraph-minimality,
        lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound,
        lem:moment-curve-general-position}
  By KT Lemma~3.3 (\cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}), $H$ is a minimal
  $0$-dof-graph. By KT Lemma~3.5 (\cref{lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}),
  $G/E(H)$ is a minimal $k$-dof-graph, with the vertex set
  $s_c = (V(G) \setminus V(H)) \cup \{r\}$. So the given realizations
  $(H, p_1)$ and $(G/E(H), p_2)$ have the ranks $D(|V(H)| - 1)$ and
  $D(|s_c| - 1) - k$. In Katoh and Tanigawa's proof the induction
  hypothesis gives them, with algebraically independent coefficients,
  and KT eq.~(6.6) combines them into a realization of $G$. Its rank is
  the target rank by KT eq.~(6.3) and Claim~6.4, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-I}.

  We choose instead one set of normals $q_0$ for all the bodies, off the
  zero sets of three nonzero polynomials in the normals. First, some
  $D(|V(H)| - 1)$ rows of $R(H, p_1)$ are linearly independent. The rows
  can be chosen with entries that are polynomials in the normals
  (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So one of the maximal minors
  of these rows is a polynomial $Q_1$ that does not vanish at the normals
  of $p_1$, and wherever $Q_1(q) \neq 0$, the same rows of $R(H, q)$ are
  linearly independent. This is the argument of
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, later in this chapter
  (\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-relative}).

  Second, there is a nonzero polynomial $Q_2$ such that wherever
  $Q_2(q) \neq 0$, some $D(|s_c| - 1) - k$ rows of
  $R(G \setminus E(H), q)$ are linearly independent after the columns of
  $V(H)$ are deleted (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}, applied
  to $(G/E(H), p_2)$). This is KT Claim~6.4 with KT eq.~(6.9), in
  polynomial form.

  Third, write $q(a)_i$ for the $i$-th coordinate of the normal $q(a)$,
  from $i = 0$. Let $P$ be the product, over the ordered pairs of
  distinct bodies $a$ and $b$, of the minors
  $q(a)_0\,q(b)_1 - q(a)_1\,q(b)_0$. Wherever $P(q) \neq 0$, any two
  normals are linearly independent, so the normals are in general
  position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). The polynomial $P$ is not
  zero: at normals on the moment curve
  (\cref{lem:moment-curve-general-position}), each factor is a difference
  $t_b - t_a \neq 0$. As $K$ is infinite, the nonzero polynomial
  $Q_1 Q_2 P$ has a non-root $q_0$.

  Let $(G, q_0)$ be the panel-hinge framework on $G$ with the normals
  $q_0$, in which every hinge is the meet of the panels of the ends of
  its edge, as in KT eq.~(6.6). As $Q_1(q_0) \neq 0$, some
  $D(|V(H)| - 1)$ rows of the edges of $H$ are linearly independent. As
  $Q_2(q_0) \neq 0$, some $D(|s_c| - 1) - k$ rows of the other edges are
  linearly independent after the columns of $V(H)$ are deleted. By the
  block form of KT eq.~(6.3), all these rows together are linearly
  independent (the proof of \cref{lem:case-I}). The sets $V(H)$ and $s_c$
  share only the body $r$ and cover $V(G)$, so there are
  \[
    D\bigl(|V(H)| - 1\bigr) + D\bigl(|s_c| - 1\bigr) - k
    \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - k
  \]
  of them. As $P(q_0) \neq 0$, the normals $q_0$ are in general position.
  The graph $G$ has no loops, so every hinge of $(G, q_0)$ is
  nondegenerate (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). Hence the
  rank of $R(G, q_0)$ is at most $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), and $(G, q_0)$ is a
  generic full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes the induction hypothesis KT eq.~(6.1) for
  every graph with fewer vertices than $G$ (\cref{thm:theorem-55}), and
  uses it only for $H$ and $G/E(H)$. The realization of $H$ that the
  induction hypothesis gives orients the edges of $H$ in its own way. The
  pinned theorem first gives that realization the orientation of $G$,
  which changes no infinitesimal motion, as for $G/E(H)$ in
  \cref{lem:rank-transport-relabel-of-le-finrank}. It obtains the
  infinitesimal rigidity of that realization on $V(H)$ from its rank by
  the relative count of \cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}, later
  in this chapter, as the formal framework has bodies outside $V(H)$.
  Given $Q_2$, the choice of $q_0$ and the count of the rows are one
  step of its proof
  (\texttt{hasGenericFullRankRealization\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}couple\_\allowbreak{}blockTriangular\_\allowbreak{}ofNormals\_\allowbreak{}set\_\allowbreak{}kdof}).
\end{fmlnote}

\medskip\noindent\emph{Rank and general position.} The normals $q_0$
satisfy conditions of two kinds: the rank conditions $Q_1(q_0) \neq 0$
and $Q_2(q_0) \neq 0$, and general position. Katoh and Tanigawa obtain
both from one hypothesis~\cite[\S5.1, p.~668]{katohTanigawa2011}. They
call two panels nonparallel if they meet in a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine
subspace. The panel of a body $v$ is defined by a vector
$c(v) \in \R^d$, and two panels are nonparallel when their vectors are
linearly independent. A nonparallel realization is generic if the
coordinates of all the vectors $c(v)$ are algebraically independent
over $\Q$. In the proof of KT Lemma~6.3 (pp.~674--675), $p_1$ and
$p_2$ are taken so that the coefficients of the panels of $p_1$ at the
bodies of $H$ and of $p_2$ at the other bodies are algebraically
independent over $\Q$. This choice makes any two of these panels
nonparallel, so the realization of KT eq.~(6.6) is nonparallel. In
Claim~6.4 the same choice makes the rank of $R(G/E(H), p')$ the largest
over all the realizations of the form of KT eq.~(6.7). Katoh and
Tanigawa note that their definition of a generic realization gives a
smaller class of frameworks than the usual one, by the largest rank of
the rigidity matrix, and that they use it to make their proof simpler
(KT footnote~4, p.~668).

Over an infinite field that is algebraic over its prime field, such as
the algebraic closure of $\Q$ or of a finite field, no coordinate is
transcendental over the prime field, so no realization is generic in
this sense. We therefore state the two kinds of condition separately,
each as the non-vanishing of a polynomial in the normals, and use only
that a nonzero polynomial over an infinite field has a non-root. A rank
condition is the non-vanishing of a minor of the rows that does not
vanish at a given realization
(\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). Stated for open sets, this
is the lower semicontinuity of the rank where every hinge is
nondegenerate (\cref{lem:genericity-device}, in
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-genericity}). General
position is a condition on pairs of normals that Katoh and Tanigawa's
nonparallel panels satisfy (the remark after
\cref{def:panel-general-position}), and it holds wherever $P$ does not
vanish. A product of nonzero polynomials is again a nonzero polynomial.
At a non-root of the product all the conditions hold at once, and this
takes the place of Katoh and Tanigawa's one hypothesis. The proof of KT
Lemma~6.5 below uses the same product with a factor of a third kind.

\begin{lemma}[Case~I for a graph that is not simple; KT Lemma~6.2]
  \label{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.case_I_realization_nonsimple_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, lem:case-I, lem:two-vertex-zero-dof,
        lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}
  Let $d \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a minimal $k$-dof-graph with
  $|V(G)| \ge 3$ that is not simple. Suppose that for every $k'$, every
  minimal $k'$-dof-graph with at least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$
  vertices has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization, and a generic
  full-rank realization if it is simple. Then $G$ has a
  nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}), of rank $D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel, lem:theorem-55-base,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound, lem:rigidityRows-splice-rank-add,
        lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter,
        lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract,
        lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}
  A minimal $k$-dof-graph has no loops, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-IH-relabel-proj}. As $G$ is not simple, it
  has two edges $e \neq f$ that join the same vertices $a \neq b$. Let
  $H'$ be the subgraph with the vertices $a$ and $b$ and the edges $e$
  and $f$. It is a $0$-dof-graph (\cref{lem:two-vertex-zero-dof}; KT
  p.~673), and a proper rigid subgraph of $G$ as $|V(G)| \ge 3$. Let
  $G' = G/E(H')$ be its contraction to $a$. By KT Lemma~3.5
  (\cref{lem:rigidContract-isMinimalKDof}), $G'$ is a minimal
  $k$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| - 1$ vertices. So, by the hypothesis, $G'$
  has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization $(G', p_2)$, of rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 2) - k$.

  As Katoh and Tanigawa do, we give $a$ and $b$ the panel of the body
  $a$ in $(G', p_2)$, and every other body its panel in $(G', p_2)$. We
  choose two linearly independent extensors $C_e$ and $C_f$ in the panel
  of $a$ (\cref{lem:extensor-pair-in-panel}, in the form for $K^{d+1}$
  given after it). Let $p$ give $e$ and $f$ the hinges $C_e$ and $C_f$,
  and every other edge its hinge in $(G', p_2)$ (KT eq.~(6.2)). Every
  hinge of $p$ is nonzero. It lies in the panels of the ends of its edge:
  the ends of an edge in $G'$ are its ends in $G$ with $b$ replaced by
  $a$ (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}), and $a$ and $b$ have the same
  panel.

  We compute the rank as in the proof of \cref{lem:case-I} for KT
  Lemma~6.2. By KT Lemma~5.3 (\cref{lem:theorem-55-base}), the restriction of
  $(G,p)$ to $H'$ is infinitesimally rigid, so the rows of $e$ and $f$
  have rank $D$. The other edges have their hinges in $(G', p_2)$. After
  the columns of $a$ and $b$ are deleted, their rows are those of
  $R(G', p_2)$ with the columns of $a$ deleted (KT eq.~(6.4)). By KT
  Lemma~5.1, these have the rank of $R(G', p_2)$ (KT eq.~(6.5);
  \cref{lem:extProj-preserves-rank-of-inter}). By the block form of KT
  eq.~(6.3),
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,p) \;\ge\; D + D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr) - k
    \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr) - k
  \]
  (\cref{lem:rigidityRows-splice-rank-add}). As every hinge is nonzero,
  the rank is at most this (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem proves more than the statement, which keeps the
  induction hypothesis in the form that Case~I has. It assumes of the
  graphs with fewer vertices only that they have nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realizations, as the proof above uses no other. It is the form
  that the formal proof of KT Theorem~5.5 uses
  (\cref{lem:case-I-dispatch}, \cref{lem:case-I-all-k}).
\end{fmlnote}

The third case rests on KT Claim~6.6 (p.~676), which Katoh and Tanigawa
state under the hypotheses of KT Lemma~6.5, for every $k$.

\begin{lemma}[A vertex of degree two; KT Claim~6.6]
  \label{lem:claim-6-6}
  \lean{Graph.deficiency_eq_zero_of_simple_rigid_no_simpleContraction,
        Graph.exists_degree_two_removeVertex_of_no_simple_contraction}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:rigid-subgraph, def:rigid-contraction,
        def:graph-operations}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a simple minimal $k$-dof-graph with
  $|V(G)| \ge 3$ that has a proper rigid subgraph, and in which no proper
  rigid subgraph $H$ has a simple contraction $G/E(H)$
  (\cref{def:rigid-contraction}). Then $k = 0$, and $G$ has a vertex $v$
  of degree two, joined to two distinct vertices, such that $G_v$
  (\cref{def:graph-operations}) is a simple minimal $0$-dof-graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:D-deficiency, def:edge-multiply, lem:removal-deficiency,
        lem:subgraph-minimality, thm:def-eq-corank}
  Let $G'$ be a proper rigid subgraph of $G$ whose vertex set is maximal
  under inclusion. The subgraph of $G$ induced on $V(G')$ contains $G'$ and
  has the same vertices, so its deficiency is at most that of $G'$,
  which is zero (\cref{def:D-deficiency}). So we may take $G'$ induced.
  Then an edge of $G$ not in $G'$ has at most one end in $V(G')$, and
  $G/E(G')$ has no loop. By the hypothesis, $G/E(G')$ is not simple, so
  it has two edges $e \neq f$ with the same ends. As $G$ is simple, $e$
  and $f$ are not parallel in $G$, so one of these ends is the vertex $r$
  to which $G'$ is contracted. So each of $e$ and $f$ has exactly one end
  in $V(G')$, and they join a common vertex $v \notin V(G')$ to two
  vertices $a \neq b$ of $G'$. Katoh and Tanigawa read off $v$, $e$ and
  $f$ from the non-simplicity of $G/E(G')$ directly (KT p.~676). This
  needs $G'$ induced: otherwise an edge of $G$ with both ends in $V(G')$
  could give a loop of $G/E(G')$ instead.

  Let $G''$ be obtained from $G'$ by adding $v$ and the edges $e$ and
  $f$. Removing $v$ from $G''$ gives $G'$, so
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G') = 0$,
  by KT Lemma~4.4 (\cref{lem:removal-deficiency}). So $G''$ is a rigid
  subgraph of $G$ with more vertices than $G'$. By the choice of $G'$ it
  is not proper, so $V(G'') = V(G)$. As $G$ contains $G''$ and has the
  same vertices,
  $k = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G'') = 0$
  (\cref{def:D-deficiency}), so $k = 0$. Let $e'$ be an edge of $G$ not in
  $G''$. Then $G - e'$ contains $G''$ and has the same vertices, so its
  deficiency is zero, as is that of $G$. This contradicts the minimality
  of $G$ (the remark after \cref{def:k-dof}), so $G = G''$. Hence $v$
  has degree two in $G$, with the neighbours $a \neq b$, and $G_v = G'$
  is a minimal $0$-dof-graph (KT Lemma~3.3,
  \cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}). It is simple, as a subgraph of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem gives $k = 0$, and the second, for $k = 0$,
  the vertex $v$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Case~I with no simple contraction; KT Lemma~6.5]
  \label{lem:case-I-realization-h65}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_I_realization_h65_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, def:genuine-hinge-realization}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a simple
  minimal $0$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$ that has a proper rigid
  subgraph, and in which no proper rigid subgraph $H$ has a simple
  contraction $G/E(H)$. Suppose that every minimal $0$-dof-graph with at
  least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices has a nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realization, and a generic full-rank realization if it is
  simple. Then $G$ has a generic
  full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:claim-6-6, lem:case-II-placement-block-independent,
    def:hinge-row-block,
    lem:general-position-support-nonzero,
    lem:generic-realization-of-rigid,
    lem:panel-support-extensor-independence, lem:rank-parallel-full,
    lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank,
    lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound,
    lem:span-rigidityRows-eq-of-motions-eq,
    lem:splitoff-edge-substitution, lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}
  By KT Claim~6.6 (\cref{lem:claim-6-6}), $G$ has a vertex $v$ of degree
  two, joined to two distinct vertices $a$ and $b$, such that $G_v$ is a
  simple minimal $0$-dof-graph. It has
  $|V(G)| - 1 \ge 2$ vertices, so it has a generic full-rank realization,
  of rank $D(|V(G)| - 2)$. Katoh and Tanigawa keep this realization, and
  give $v$ a panel $\Pi^\circ$ that is nonparallel to the other panels
  and does not contain $\Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b)$. Then the hinges
  $\Pi^\circ \cap \Pi(a)$ and $\Pi^\circ \cap \Pi(b)$ of $va$ and $vb$
  are distinct (KT p.~676).

  We choose all the normals at once, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-I-realization}, at a non-root $q$ of the product of
  three nonzero polynomials. The first is a polynomial $Q$ that does not
  vanish at the normals of this realization, such that $R(G_v, q)$ has
  rank at least $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ wherever $Q(q) \neq 0$
  (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). The polynomial $Q$ is the
  minor by which the proof of \cref{lem:genericity-device} gives the
  lower semicontinuity of the rank.
  The second is the polynomial $P$ of the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-I-realization}, so that the normals $q$ are in general
  position.

  The third is a $3 \times 3$ minor $\Delta$ of the matrix with the rows
  $q(v)$, $q(a)$ and $q(b)$, which is not the zero polynomial as
  $d + 1 \ge 3$. As $\Delta(q) \neq 0$, the normals $q(v)$, $q(a)$ and
  $q(b)$ are linearly independent. Since $q(a)$ and $q(b)$ are linearly
  independent, this says that the panel of $v$ does not contain the
  intersection of the panels of $a$ and $b$, as Katoh and Tanigawa ask
  of $\Pi^\circ$.

  Let $(G, q)$ be the panel-hinge framework on $G$ with the normals $q$.
  The edges of $G_v$ have the same hinges in $(G, q)$ and in $(G_v, q)$,
  so their rows are those of $R(G_v, q)$, and these vanish in the columns
  of $v$. The hinges of $va$ and $vb$ are $C(q(v), q(a))$ and
  $C(q(v), q(b))$. They are linearly independent, because the joins
  $q(v) \vee q(a)$ and $q(v) \vee q(b)$ are
  (\cref{lem:panel-support-extensor-independence}). Indeed, if $q(v)$,
  $q(a)$ and $q(b)$ are extended to a basis of $K^{d+1}$, the two joins
  belong to the corresponding basis of $\bigwedge^2 K^{d+1}$ (the remark
  after \cref{def:extensor}). So, as in the proof of KT Lemma~5.3
  (\cref{lem:rank-parallel-full}), the rows of $va$ and $vb$ have rank
  $D$ in the columns of $v$. This is the block form of KT eq.~(6.10).

  Take $D$ of these rows that are linearly independent in the columns of
  $v$, and $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ linearly independent rows of $G_v$. In a
  vanishing linear combination of them, only the first have nonzero
  entries in the columns of $v$, so their coefficients vanish, and then
  so do the others. Hence
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \;\ge\; D + D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr)
    \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 1\bigr),
  \]
  and the rank is at most this (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}).
  As the normals $q$ are in general position, $(G, q)$ is a generic
  full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The three cases of Case~I; KT Lemmas 6.2, 6.3 and 6.5]
  \label{lem:case-I-dispatch}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.case_I_dispatch_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, def:rank-hypothesis,
        lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple, lem:case-I-realization,
        lem:case-I-realization-h65}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a minimal
  $0$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$ that has a proper rigid subgraph.
  Suppose that for every $k'$, every minimal $k'$-dof-graph with at
  least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices has a nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realization, and a generic full-rank realization if it is
  simple. Then $G$ has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}), and if $G$ is simple, a
  generic full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple, lem:case-I-realization,
        lem:case-I-realization-h65, lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}
  The hypothesis is the induction hypothesis KT eq.~(6.1) for the graphs
  with fewer vertices than $G$. We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's three
  cases (KT p.~673). If $G$ is not simple, then KT Lemma~6.2 gives a
  nondegenerate-hinge panel
  realization (\cref{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple}). Let $G$ be
  simple. Suppose that some proper rigid subgraph $H$ has a simple
  contraction $G/E(H)$. Then $H$ and $G/E(H)$ are simple minimal
  $0$-dof-graphs with fewer vertices than $G$ (KT Lemmas~3.3 and~3.5).
  The induction hypothesis gives generic full-rank realizations of both,
  and KT Lemma~6.3 gives one of $G$ (\cref{lem:case-I-realization}).
  Otherwise, KT Lemma~6.5 gives one (\cref{lem:case-I-realization-h65}).
  In both cases, $G$ also has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Case~I; KT \S6.2]
  \label{lem:case-I-all-k}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.case_I_hcontract_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a minimal
  $k$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$ that has a proper rigid subgraph.
  Suppose that for every $k'$, every minimal $k'$-dof-graph with at
  least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices has a nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realization, and a generic full-rank realization if it is
  simple. Then $G$ has a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization
  (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}), and if $G$ is simple, a
  generic full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-I-dispatch, lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple,
        lem:case-I-realization, lem:claim-6-6,
        lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}
  For $k = 0$ this is \cref{lem:case-I-dispatch}. Let $k > 0$. If $G$ is
  not simple, then KT Lemma~6.2 gives a nondegenerate-hinge panel
  realization (\cref{lem:case-I-realization-nonsimple}). Let $G$ be
  simple. Suppose that some proper rigid subgraph $H$ has a simple
  contraction $G/E(H)$. Then $H$ is a simple minimal $0$-dof-graph and
  $G/E(H)$ a simple minimal $k$-dof-graph, both with fewer vertices than
  $G$ (KT Lemmas~3.3 and~3.5). The hypothesis gives generic full-rank
  realizations of both, and KT Lemma~6.3 gives one of $G$
  (\cref{lem:case-I-realization}), which is also a nondegenerate-hinge
  panel realization (\cref{lem:generic-yields-genuine-hinge}). Otherwise
  no proper rigid subgraph of $G$ has a simple contraction, and KT
  Claim~6.6 gives $k = 0$ (\cref{lem:claim-6-6}), a contradiction.
\end{proof}
