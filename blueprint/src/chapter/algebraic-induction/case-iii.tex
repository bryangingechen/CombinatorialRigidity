\subsection{Case III: no proper rigid subgraph and \texorpdfstring{$k = 0$}{k = 0}}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-caseIII}

This subsection proves Case~III of KT Theorem~5.5, in which the minimal
$0$-dof-graph $G$ has at least three vertices and no proper rigid
subgraph (KT \S6.4). As in Case~II, Katoh and Tanigawa split off a
vertex $v$ of degree two, whose edges join it to vertices $a$ and $b$.
The graph $G_v^{ab}$ is a minimal $0$-dof-graph with fewer vertices
(KT Lemma~4.8), and the induction hypothesis gives a realization
$(G_v^{ab}, q)$ of it. Putting $v$
back as in Case~II gives a framework $(G, p_1)$ of rank at least
$D(|V(G)| - 1) - 1$, one less than the target rank (KT eq.~(6.12),
p.~680; the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}). Katoh and
Tanigawa find the missing row in three steps. By KT Claim~6.11, one of
the $D - 1$ rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at the new edge $ab$ is redundant.
Row and column operations then bring $R(G, p_1)$ to a block form whose
rank is $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ if a $D \times D$ block is nonsingular (KT
eqs.~(6.24)--(6.29)). This block depends on the hinge of $va$, and KT
Claim~6.12 shows that one of several such frameworks has a nonsingular
block.

The first two subsubsections prove KT Claim~6.11 and give the row and
column operations. The third proves the case $d = 3$, KT Lemma~6.10,
with its three frameworks and KT Claim~6.12. The fourth proves KT
Lemma~6.13, for every $d$ and including the graphs with three vertices.
This is the form that the formal proof of KT Theorem~5.5 uses
(\cref{lem:case-III}).

\subsubsection{KT Claim~6.11: a redundant row at the new edge}

In Katoh and Tanigawa's proof, the induction hypothesis gives a generic
realization $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ of $G_v^{ab}$ of rank $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ (KT
eq.~(6.18), p.~682). They call a row of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ redundant if
removing it does not lower the rank. KT Claim~6.11 says that one of the
$D - 1$ rows at the new edge $ab$ is
redundant~\cite[p.~684]{katohTanigawa2011}. Its proof finds the
redundancy first in the matroid $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ and then in
the rows, in three steps. First, $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ has a base
that does not contain every copy of $ab$
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-base}). Second, it follows that the
graph $G_v = G - v$, which is $G_v^{ab}$ without the edge $ab$, is a
minimal $k'$-dof-graph with $k' \le D - 2$
(\cref{lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof}). So the induction hypothesis
gives $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \ge D(|V(G)| - 2) - (D - 2)$ (KT
eq.~(6.22), \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}). Third, the rows of
$R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ are those of $R(G_v, q)$ and the rows at $ab$. The rows
at $ab$ therefore raise the rank by at most $D - 2$, and one of them is
redundant (KT eq.~(6.23), \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}).

The first step is the forward half of KT Lemma~4.3(ii), which
\cref{lem:splitoff-kdof-criterion} states with its converse. We state it
on its own, as Katoh and Tanigawa use it here.

\begin{lemma}[A base of $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ without some copy of $ab$; KT Lemma~4.3(ii)]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-base}
  \lean{Graph.splitOff_exists_base_inter_fiber_lt}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:matroid-MG, thm:def-eq-corank, lem:forest-surgery-count, lem:splitoff-deficiency}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a $k$-dof-graph with a vertex $v$ of
  degree two, whose edges $e_a$ and $e_b$ join $v$ to distinct vertices
  $a, b \neq v$. Let $\widetilde{ab}$ be the set of the $D - 1$ copies
  in $\widetilde{G_v^{ab}}$ of the new edge $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$. If
  $G_v^{ab}$ is a $k$-dof-graph, then $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ has a
  base $B'$ with
  \[
    \bigl|\widetilde{ab} \cap B'\bigr| \;<\; D - 1.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We recall Katoh and Tanigawa's
  proof~\cite[p.~661]{katohTanigawa2011}, which
  \cref{lem:splitoff-kdof-criterion} also gives. Let $B$ be a base of
  $M(\tilde G)$. By \cref{lem:balanced-forest-packing}, $B$ is the union
  of $D$ pairwise disjoint forests of $\tilde G$, each of which has an
  edge incident with $v$, as the proof of KT Lemma~4.1 assumes
  (\cref{rem:kt-lemma-41}). The surgery of KT Lemma~4.1 on these forests
  (\cref{lem:forest-surgery-count}) gives an independent set $I'$ of
  $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ with $|I'| = |B| - D$ and
  $|\widetilde{ab} \cap I'| < D - 1$.

  The vertices of $G_v^{ab}$ are the $|V(G)| - 1$ vertices of $G$ other
  than $v$. Both graphs are $k$-dof-graphs, so KT eq.~(2.4)
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) gives $|B| = D(|V(G)| - 1) - k$ and
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})
    \;=\; D\,(|V(G)| - 2) - k \;=\; |B| - D \;=\; |I'|.
  \]
  So $I'$ is a base, and we take $B' = I'$. A copy of $ab$ outside $B'$
  can be removed from $\widetilde{G_v^{ab}}$ without lowering the rank of
  its matroid. Katoh and Tanigawa call such a copy a redundant edge
  (p.~684).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is also the first pinned theorem of
  \cref{lem:splitoff-kdof-criterion}. It assumes that the label of the
  new edge $ab$ lies outside $E(G)$ (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}).
\end{fmlnote}

In the second step, $G$ is a minimal $0$-dof-graph, as in Case~III.

\begin{lemma}[The graph $G - v$ is a minimal $k'$-dof-graph with $k' \le D - 2$; from the proof of KT Claim~6.11]
  \label{lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof}
  \lean{Graph.splitOff_removeVertex_minimalKDof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof, def:graph-operations, lem:subgraph-minimality, thm:def-eq-corank,
        lem:splitoff-deficiency, lem:case-III-claim-6-11-base}
  Let $D \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a minimal $0$-dof-graph with a vertex $v$
  of degree two, whose edges $e_a$ and $e_b$ join $v$ to distinct
  vertices $a, b \neq v$. Let $G_v = G - v$ and
  $k' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G_v)$. Then $G_v$ is a minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph, and
  \[
    0 \;\le\; k' \;\le\; D - 2.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~684]{katohTanigawa2011}. The
  graph $G_v$ is a subgraph of $G$, so it is a minimal $k'$-dof-graph by
  KT Lemma~3.3 (\cref{lem:subgraph-minimality}). It has the vertex $a$,
  so the partition of $V(G_v)$ into one part shows that $k' \ge 0$
  (\cref{def:D-deficiency}). For the same reason
  $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}}) \ge 0$. By KT Lemma~4.3(i)
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-deficiency}),
  $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}}) \le \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$,
  so $G_v^{ab}$ is a $0$-dof-graph.

  By \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-base}, at $k = 0$,
  $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ has a base $B'$ with
  $h = |\widetilde{ab} \cap B'| < D - 1$. As $G_v^{ab}$ has the
  $|V(G)| - 1$ vertices of $G$ other than $v$, KT eq.~(2.4)
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}) gives $|B'| = D(|V(G)| - 2)$. The graph
  $G_v$ is $G_v^{ab}$ without the edge $ab$
  (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}). So $M(\tilde G_v)$ is the
  restriction of $M(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}})$ to $E(\tilde G_v)$
  (\cref{lem:matroid-restrict-subgraph}), in which
  $B' \setminus \widetilde{ab}$ is an independent set of $|B'| - h$
  elements. As $G_v$ and $G_v^{ab}$ have the same vertices, KT
  eq.~(2.4) gives
  \[
    k' \;=\; D\,(|V(G)| - 2) - \operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G_v)
    \;\le\; |B'| - \bigl(|B'| - h\bigr) \;=\; h \;\le\; D - 2.
  \]
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem also assumes that some edge label lies outside
  $E(G)$, the label of the new edge $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$, which the proof
  uses (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}).
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa prove KT eq.~(6.22) at the normals $q$ of KT
eq.~(6.18), from the genericity of $q$ (the remark below). As
explained after \cref{lem:case-I-realization}, we replace genericity by
the non-vanishing of polynomials in the normals. So we state KT
eq.~(6.22) as a bound off the zeros of a polynomial.

\begin{lemma}[The rank of $G - v$ off the zeros of a polynomial; KT eq.~(6.22)]
  \label{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_nested_rankPolynomial_lower_all_k}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rank-hypothesis, lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $G$ be a minimal $0$-dof-graph with a vertex $v$ of
  degree two, whose edges join $v$ to distinct vertices $a, b \neq v$,
  and let $G_v = G - v$. Then there is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the
  normals such that
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \;\ge\; D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) - (D - 2)
  \]
  for all normals $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We assume, as in Case~III, that $G$ is simple and that the induction
  hypothesis KT eq.~(6.1) holds for the graphs with fewer vertices than
  $G$ (the note after the proof). By
  \cref{lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof}, $G_v$ is a minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph with $k' = \operatorname{def}(\tilde G_v) \le D - 2$.
  It is simple, as a subgraph of $G$, and it has at least one and fewer
  than $|V(G)|$ vertices. So the induction hypothesis gives a generic
  full-rank realization $(G_v, q_0)$ (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), with
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q_0) \;=\; D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'.
  \]
  Its normals are in general position and $G_v$ has no loops, so its
  hinges are nondegenerate (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}).
  By \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, there is a polynomial $P$
  in the normals with $P(q_0) \neq 0$ such that
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \ge D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'$ wherever
  $P(q) \neq 0$. In particular $P \neq 0$. As $k' \le D - 2$, this is
  the bound of the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ is simple, and the induction
  hypothesis for every $k'$ and every minimal $k'$-dof-graph with at
  least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices. The statement omits both.
  The pinned theorem also assumes that some edge label lies outside
  $E(G)$ (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). It fixes an endpoint selector
  that assigns each edge of $G_v^{ab}$ its ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}) before it gives $P$.
\end{fmlnote}

The lemma needs $G$ to be simple. Let $d = 2$, so that $D = 3$, and let
$G$ have the vertices $v, a, b, c$, the edges $va$, $vb$ and $cb$, and
two edges $ac$. Comparing the partitions of $V(G)$
(\cref{def:D-deficiency}) shows that $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
Removing an edge $ac$ gives the value $1$ to the partition into
singletons. Removing $va$ or $vb$ gives it to the partition
$\{\{v\}, \{a, b, c\}\}$, and removing $cb$ to
$\{\{b\}, \{a, c, v\}\}$. So $G$ is a minimal $0$-dof-graph, and $v$ has
degree two. In $(G_v, q)$ the two edges $ac$ have the same hinge, and so
the same rows. Let $Q$ be the product of a $2 \times 2$ minor of the
normals at $a$ and $c$ and one of the normals at $c$ and $b$. Where
$Q(q) \neq 0$, the hinges of $(G_v, q)$ are nondegenerate, and
\[
  \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \;\le\; 2(D - 1) \;=\; 4
  \;<\; 5 \;=\; D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) - (D - 2).
\]
Over an infinite field, $PQ$ has a non-root for every nonzero
polynomial $P$, so no polynomial $P$ as in the lemma exists. The two
edges $ac$ form a proper rigid subgraph of $G$, so this graph falls under
Case~I.

\begin{remark}[KT eq.~(6.22) and KT footnote~6]
  We recall Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of KT
  eq.~(6.22)~\cite[pp.~684--685]{katohTanigawa2011}. The realization
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ of KT eq.~(6.18) is generic: the coordinates of the
  vectors $c(u)$ that define its panels are algebraically independent
  over $\Q$ (KT \S5.1, p.~668; the passage after
  \cref{lem:case-I-realization}). By KT
  eq.~(6.1), the simple minimal $k'$-dof-graph $G_v$ has a nonparallel
  realization of rank $D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'$. A generic nonparallel
  realization of $G_v$ has the largest rank of all nonparallel
  realizations of $G_v$ (KT \S5.1). No realization with nondegenerate
  hinges has a rank larger than $D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). So every
  generic nonparallel realization of $G_v$ has rank
  $D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'$ (KT footnote~6, p.~684). The realization
  $(G_v, q)$ has the bodies and the panels of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, so it is a
  generic nonparallel realization of $G_v$. Hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) = D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k'$.

  In our proof the normals of KT eq.~(6.18) are not given first. Case~III
  chooses them off the zeros of a product of nonzero polynomials, fixed
  before the choice (the proof of \cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator},
  later in this subsection). One factor is the polynomial $P$ of
  \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}. Another is the polynomial that
  \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} gives for the realization of
  $G_v^{ab}$ from the induction hypothesis, and a third puts the normals
  in general position. Off the zeros of the second,
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) \ge D(|V(G_v)| - 1)$. Off those of
  the third, the hinges are nondegenerate, and as
  $\operatorname{def}(\widetilde{G_v^{ab}}) = 0$, the rank is at most
  $D(|V(G_v)| - 1)$ (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). So KT
  eqs.~(6.18) and~(6.22) hold at the same normals, as in Katoh and
  Tanigawa's proof. They hold by the choice of the normals, not by their
  genericity.
\end{remark}

The third step is a count in linear algebra, which we state for one
hinge of any body-hinge framework.

\begin{lemma}[A redundant row at a hinge; from the proof of KT Claim~6.11]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-redundant-row}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_redundant_panelRow_of_edge_of_finrank_lt}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof, lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}
  Let $(G, p)$ be a body-hinge framework, let $e$ be an edge of $G$ with
  distinct ends whose hinge is nondegenerate, and let $R_e$ be the span
  of the rows of $R(G, p)$ at $e$. Let $W$ be a space of linear forms on
  the screw assignments such that
  \[
    \dim (W + R_e) \;<\; \dim W + (D - 1).
  \]
  Then $R_e$ has a basis $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$ with
  $r_i \in W + \operatorname{span}\{r_j : j \neq i\}$ for some $i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By \cref{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}, $R_e$ contains $D - 1$
  linearly independent forms $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$. The space $R_e$ has
  dimension $D - 1$, as in the proof of that lemma, so they form a basis
  of $R_e$. Suppose that no $r_i$ lies in
  $W + \operatorname{span}\{r_j : j \neq i\}$. If
  $\sum_j c_j r_j \in W$ with $c_i \neq 0$, then dividing by $c_i$
  puts $r_i$ in $W + \operatorname{span}\{r_j : j \neq i\}$. So
  $\sum_j c_j r_j \in W$ only if every $c_j$ is $0$, and the images of
  the $r_j$ in the quotient by $W$ are linearly independent. Then
  $\dim (W + R_e) = \dim W + (D - 1)$, contrary to the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  As for \cref{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}, the pinned theorem holds
  for every body-hinge framework, and it reads the ends of $e$ from the
  endpoint selector (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

We apply this count at the edge $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$, where $W$ is the row
space of $R(G_v, q)$.

\begin{lemma}[KT Claim~6.11: a redundant row at $ab$; KT eq.~(6.23)]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_redundant_panelRow_ab_of_finrank_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-redundant-row, lem:case-III-gap3-minimalKDof}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $G$ be a multigraph with a vertex $v$ of degree
  two, whose edges join $v$ to distinct vertices $a, b \neq v$, and let
  $G_v = G - v$. Let $q$ be normals at which the hinge of the new edge
  $ab$ of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ is nondegenerate. Suppose that, for some
  $k' \le D - 2$,
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) = D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1)
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) = D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) - k',
  \]
  as in KT eqs.~(6.18) and~(6.22). Then one of the $D - 1$ rows of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$ lies in the span of the rows of $R(G_v, q)$
  and the other rows at $ab$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~685]{katohTanigawa2011}. The
  edges of $G_v^{ab}$ are those of $G_v$, with the same ends, and the
  new edge $ab$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}). The two
  frameworks have the same normals, so an edge of $G_v$ has the same
  hinge, and the same rows, in both. Let $W$ be the row space of
  $R(G_v, q)$, and let $R_{ab}$ be the span of the rows of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$. Then the row space of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ is
  $W + R_{ab}$, and the two rank equations give
  \[
    \dim (W + R_{ab}) \;=\; D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) \;=\; \dim W + k'.
  \]
  As $k' \le D - 2 < D - 1$, the rows at $ab$ raise $\dim W$ by fewer
  than $D - 1$.

  The $D - 1$ rows $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$ of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$
  form a basis of $R_{ab}$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). The proof of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11-redundant-row} applies to every basis of
  $R_{ab}$. So some $r_i$ lies in
  $W + \operatorname{span}\{r_j : j \neq i\}$.
  The rows of $R(G_v, q)$ and the $r_j$ with $j \neq i$ then span
  $W + R_{ab}$. So removing the row $r_i$ does not lower the rank of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$, which is KT eq.~(6.23).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem gives a basis of the span of the rows at $ab$, one
  member of which lies in the span of the rows of $R(G_v, q)$ and the
  other members. This is the lemma for one choice of the rows at $ab$
  (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). The pinned theorem holds for any two
  multigraphs whose edges, with their ends, agree except for one edge
  $ab$ of the first, and with any number in place of $|V(G_v)|$. Their
  frameworks share one endpoint selector, which assigns $ab$ its ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). The formal proof of
  Case~III applies it with
  $k' = D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q)$, for which the
  second rank equation holds by definition, and
  \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower} gives $k' \le D - 2$.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsubsection{The row operations of KT eqs.\ (6.24)--(6.29)}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-candidate-completion}

Let $G$, $v$, $a$, $b$ and $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ be as in KT Claim~6.11
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}), and write $q(e)$ for the hinge of an
edge $e$ in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$. Katoh and Tanigawa put $v$ back as in
Case~II. Their framework $(G, p_1)$ of KT eq.~(6.12) keeps the hinge
$q(e)$ of each edge $e$ of $G_v$, gives $vb$ the hinge $q(ab)$, and gives
$va$ a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace $L$ of the panel $\Pi(a)$.
For every such $L$, the rank of $R(G, p_1)$ is at least
$D(|V(G)| - 1) - 1$, one less than the target rank (KT p.~680; the
proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}). Katoh and Tanigawa find the
missing row by row and column
operations~\cite[pp.~685--686]{katohTanigawa2011}. They give the argument for
$d = 3$, where $D = 6$, and repeat it for every $d$ in KT
eqs.~(6.50)--(6.52) (p.~693). We follow the first, with $D$ in place
of $6$.

The redundant row of KT Claim~6.11 gives a linear relation among the
rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ in which a row $(ab)i^*$ at $ab$ has the
coefficient $1$ (KT eqs.~(6.24) and~(6.25)). The rows of $R(G, p_1)$ at
$vb$ correspond to those of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$, and the edges of
$G_v$ have the same rows in both matrices. Katoh and Tanigawa add to the
row $(vb)i^*$ of $R(G, p_1)$ the other terms of the relation, with the
rows at $vb$ in place of those at $ab$ (KT eqs.~(6.26) and~(6.27)).
After the columns of $v$ are added to those of $a$ (KT eqs.~(6.14)
and~(6.15)), the new row has nonzero entries only in the columns of $v$
(KT eq.~(6.28)), as do the rows of $va$. The matrix then has the block
form of KT eq.~(6.29). Its rank is $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ if its top-left
$D \times D$ block, formed by the new row and the rows of $va$, is
nonsingular. Whether it is depends on $L$. Katoh and Tanigawa show that
one of three such frameworks has a nonsingular block (KT Claim~6.12,
\cref{lem:case-III-claim612}, in the next subsubsection).

We write the rows of a rigidity matrix as linear forms on the screw
assignments, as in the paragraphs after \cref{def:rigidity-matrix}. The
entries of a form in the columns of a body $u$ are its restriction to
the screw assignments that vanish off $u$.

\begin{lemma}[Forms that vanish where $S(v) = 0$; the form of KT eq.\ (6.28)]
  \label{lem:case-III-vanish-off-column}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.dualMap_eq_comp_single_proj_of_vanish_off}
  \leanok
  Let $v$ be a body, and let $f$ be a linear form on the screw
  assignments such that $f(S) = 0$ for every screw assignment $S$ with
  $S(v) = 0$. For a screw $y$, let $S_y$ be the screw assignment with
  the value $y$ at $v$ and $0$ elsewhere. Then $f(S) = f(S_{S(v)})$ for
  every screw assignment $S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The screw assignment $S - S_{S(v)}$ has the value $0$ at $v$, so
  $f(S - S_{S(v)}) = 0$. By linearity, $f(S) = f(S_{S(v)})$.
\end{proof}

In terms of forms, adding the columns of $v$ to those of $a$ is a change
of variables.

\begin{lemma}[The column operation of KT eqs.\ (6.14) and~(6.15)]
  \label{lem:case-III-columnop}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.columnOp,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_comp_columnOp_apply,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_comp_columnOp_vanish_off}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-vanish-off-column}
  Let $v \neq a$ be bodies, and let $\Phi$ be the map on screw
  assignments with $\Phi(S)(v) = S(v) + S(a)$ and $\Phi(S)(u) = S(u)$
  for every body $u \neq v$. Then $\Phi$ is a linear automorphism. For
  every linear form $x$ on $K^D$, the form $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$
  composed with $\Phi$ is the form $S \mapsto x(S(v))$, which vanishes
  at every $S$ with $S(v) = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Each value of $\Phi(S)$ is a linear function of $S$, so $\Phi$ is
  linear. Let $\Psi$ be the map with $\Psi(S)(v) = S(v) - S(a)$ and
  $\Psi(S)(u) = S(u)$ for $u \neq v$. As $a \neq v$, both maps keep the
  value at $a$. Hence
  $\Psi(\Phi(S))(v) = (S(v) + S(a)) - S(a) = S(v)$ and
  $\Phi(\Psi(S))(v) = (S(v) - S(a)) + S(a) = S(v)$, and both composites
  keep the values at the other bodies. So $\Psi$ is the inverse of
  $\Phi$. As $\Phi(S)(a) = S(a)$, we have
  \[
    x\bigl(\Phi(S)(v) - \Phi(S)(a)\bigr)
    \;=\; x\bigl(S(v) + S(a) - S(a)\bigr) \;=\; x\bigl(S(v)\bigr),
  \]
  which is $0$ when $S(v) = 0$. So the composite depends only on $S(v)$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-vanish-off-column}).

  Let us check that composing with $\Phi$ is Katoh and Tanigawa's column
  operation. Let $f$ be a linear form on the screw assignments. On the
  screw assignments that vanish off $v$, $\Phi$ is the identity, so
  $f \circ \Phi$ has the entries of $f$ in the columns of $v$. If $S$
  vanishes off $a$, then $\Phi(S)$ has the value $S(a)$ at both $v$
  and $a$. So the entries of $f \circ \Phi$ in the columns of $a$ are
  the sums of those of $f$ in the columns of $a$ and in the columns of
  $v$. The entries in the other columns do not change. Replacing each
  row $f$ of $R(G, p_1)$ by $f \circ \Phi$ therefore adds the columns of
  $v$ to those of $a$ (KT eqs.~(6.14) and~(6.15)). As $\Phi$ is
  invertible, $f \mapsto f \circ \Phi$ is an injective linear map, so it
  does not change the rank. The rows of $va$ are forms
  $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$, so they become forms $S \mapsto x(S(v))$,
  with zero entries outside the columns of $v$, as in KT eq.~(6.15).
\end{proof}

The framework $(G, p_1)$ keeps the hinges of the edges of $G_v$, so

\begin{lemma}[The rows at an edge whose ends keep their normals; KT eq.\ (6.13)]
  \label{lem:case-III-seam}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.ofNormals_panelRow_eq_of_ends_seed_eq}
  \leanok

\end{lemma}

We now turn to the relation that KT Claim~6.11 gives. The rows of
$R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$ depend on a choice of linear forms on $K^D$
(\cref{def:hinge-row-block}), so we state the relation for one such
choice.

\begin{lemma}[The linear relation of KT eqs.\ (6.24) and~(6.25)]
  \label{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_redundant_panelRow_ab_decomposition}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, let $W$ be the
  row space of $R(G_v, q)$. Then there are linearly independent linear
  forms $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$ on $K^D$ that vanish on $C(q(a), q(b))$, an
  index $i^*$ and scalars $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{D-1}$ with
  $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$, such that the form
  $S \mapsto \sum_j \lambda_j r_j(S(a) - S(b))$ lies in $W$. That is,
  writing $R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j)$ for the row
  $S \mapsto r_j(S(a) - S(b))$ at $ab$ and $R(G_v^{ab}, q; e_j)$ for the
  rows of an edge $e$ of $G_v$, there are scalars $\lambda_{ej}$ with
  \[
    \sum_{1 \le j \le D-1} \lambda_j\, R\bigl(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j\bigr)
    \;+\; \sum_{e \in E(G_v)} \sum_{j}
      \lambda_{ej}\, R\bigl(G_v^{ab}, q; e_j\bigr) \;=\; 0 .
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~685]{katohTanigawa2011}. Let
  $R_{ab}$ be the span of the rows at $ab$. As $a \neq b$, the map
  $x \mapsto (S \mapsto x(S(a) - S(b)))$ is injective. It takes the
  linear forms on $K^D$ that vanish on $C(q(a), q(b))$ onto $R_{ab}$
  (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). So every basis of $R_{ab}$ consists of
  the rows at $ab$ for one choice of $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$. By
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, for one such choice a row
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)i^*)$ lies in the span of $W$ and the other rows at
  $ab$. Write it as $g - \sum_{j \neq i^*} \lambda_j R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j)$
  with $g \in W$, and let $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$. Then
  $\sum_j \lambda_j R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j) = g$ lies in $W$.

  The edges of $G_v$ have the same rows in $R(G_v, q)$ as in
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ (\cref{lem:splitoff-edge-substitution}). Writing $g$
  as a combination $-\sum_{e, j} \lambda_{ej} R(G_v^{ab}, q; e_j)$ of
  these rows gives the relation, which is KT eq.~(6.24) with
  $\lambda_{(ab)j} = \lambda_j$, and $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$ is KT
  eq.~(6.25).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem gives a basis of $R_{ab}$, and it states the
  relation as $R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)i^*) = g + h$, with $g \in W$ and $h$
  in the span of the other members of the basis. Then
  $g = \sum_j \lambda_j R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j)$, where $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$
  and the other $\lambda_j$ are the coefficients of $-h$. As for
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, the pinned theorem holds for any two
  multigraphs whose edges, with their ends, agree except for one edge
  $ab$ of the first.
\end{fmlnote}

The rank in KT eq.~(6.29) comes from the following independence. It

\begin{lemma}[Two blocks of independent forms; KT eq.\ (6.29)]
  \label{lem:case-III-conditional-block}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sum_pinned_block_augment}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-II-placement-block-independent, lem:case-III-vanish-off-column,
        lem:case-III-columnop}
  Let $v$ be a body, and let $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{D-1}$, $\omega$
  and $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_l$ be linear forms on the screw assignments.
  Suppose that the restrictions of $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{D-1}$ and
  $\omega$ to the screw assignments that vanish off $v$ are linearly
  independent, and that $\psi_1, \dots, \psi_l$ are linearly independent
  and vanish on these screw assignments. Then the forms
  $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{D-1}, \omega, \psi_1, \dots, \psi_l$ are
  linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-II-placement-block-independent}
  We argue as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-II-placement-block-independent}. Suppose that
  $\sum_i c_i \varphi_i + c\,\omega + \sum_j c'_j \psi_j = 0$. Every
  $\psi_j$ vanishes on the screw assignments that vanish off $v$, so
  $\sum_i c_i \varphi_i + c\,\omega$ vanishes on them. By the first
  hypothesis, $c$ and every $c_i$ are $0$. Then
  $\sum_j c'_j \psi_j = 0$, and every $c'_j$ is $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem allows any finite number of forms in place of
  $\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{D-1}$.
\end{fmlnote}

The next two lemmas compute the new row of KT eq.~(6.27) at the

$v$ and have $q_0(v) = q(a) + t\,q(b)$, as in the proof of
\cref{lem:case-II-realization}, where $t = 1$. Only the hinge of $vb$
enters, and it is $q(ab)$, as in $(G, p_1)$.

\begin{lemma}[The row operation at one row; KT eqs.\ (6.26) and~(6.27)]
  \label{lem:case-III-transport-collapse}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.panelRow_vb_sub_panelRow_ab_eq_hingeRow_va,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_sub_hingeRow_eq,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.comp_columnOp_eq_comp_single_proj}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-seam, lem:case-III-columnop, lem:case-III-vanish-off-column}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph with a vertex $v$ of degree two, whose edges
  join $v$ to distinct vertices $a, b \neq v$, and orient $vb$ from $v$
  to $b$ and $ab$ from $a$ to $b$. Let $q$ be normals, $t \in K$, and
  $q_0$ normals with $q_0(v) = q(a) + t\,q(b)$ and $q_0(b) = q(b)$. Then
  each row of $R(G, q_0)$ at $vb$, minus the row of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at
  $ab$ with the same index (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}), is a
  form $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$ for a linear form $x$ on $K^D$ that
  vanishes on $C(q(a), q(b))$. Moreover, for every linear form $x$ on
  $K^D$, the form $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$ composed with the map
  $\Phi$ of \cref{lem:case-III-columnop} depends only on $S(v)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As $C$ is bilinear and $C(n, n) = 0$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}),
  \[
    C\bigl(q_0(v), q_0(b)\bigr) \;=\; C\bigl(q(a) + t\,q(b),\, q(b)\bigr)
    \;=\; C\bigl(q(a), q(b)\bigr).
  \]
  So $vb$ has in $(G, q_0)$ the hinge that $ab$ has in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$.
  The two rows with the same index are therefore the forms
  $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(b))$ and $S \mapsto x(S(a) - S(b))$ for one form
  $x$, which vanishes on $C(q(a), q(b))$
  (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). As
  $(S(v) - S(b)) - (S(a) - S(b)) = S(v) - S(a)$, their difference is
  $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$. By \cref{lem:case-III-columnop}, the
  composite in the last claim is $S \mapsto x(S(v))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem is the first claim, for edge labels that the
  endpoint selector (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}) orients
  so, in any two multigraphs. The second is the identity
  $x(S(u) - S(w)) - x(S(u') - S(w)) = x(S(u) - S(u'))$, and the third is
  the last claim.
\end{fmlnote}

The previous lemma gives KT eq.~(6.27) term by term. As the rows at $ab$
are the forms $S \mapsto r_j(S(a) - S(b))$, the whole relation of KT
eq.~(6.24) can be treated at once, with the single form
$r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$.

\begin{lemma}[The new row of KT eq.\ (6.27)]
  \label{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_candidate_row_eq612}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition, lem:case-III-transport-collapse}
  Let $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$, where the forms $r_j$ and the scalars
  $\lambda_j$ are those of \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition},
  under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}. Let $t \in K$,
  and let $q_0$ be normals with $q_0(v) = q(a) + t\,q(b)$ and
  $q_0(b) = q(b)$. Then the combination of the rows at $ab$ with the
  coefficients $\lambda_j$ is the form $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$, the
  form $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(b))$ is a row of $R(G, q_0)$ at $vb$, and
  \[
    r\bigl(S(v) - S(b)\bigr) - r\bigl(S(a) - S(b)\bigr)
    \;=\; r\bigl(S(v) - S(a)\bigr).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-transport-collapse}
  The first claim holds as $R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ab)j)$ is the form
  $S \mapsto r_j(S(a) - S(b))$. Each $r_j$ vanishes on $C(q(a), q(b))$,
  and so does $r$. Moreover $r \neq 0$, as the $r_j$ are linearly
  independent and $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$. As in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-III-transport-collapse}, the hinge of $vb$ in
  $(G, q_0)$ is $C(q(a), q(b))$. So $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(b))$ is a row
  of $R(G, q_0)$ at $vb$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). The identity holds
  as $(S(v) - S(b)) - (S(a) - S(b)) = S(v) - S(a)$.

  This is Katoh and Tanigawa's row operation, applied to the whole
  relation at once~\cite[pp.~685--686]{katohTanigawa2011}. In $(G, p_1)$,
  as in $(G, q_0)$, the hinge of $vb$ is $q(ab)$. So the rows
  $R(G, p_1; (vb)j)$ of $R(G, p_1)$ at $vb$ are the forms
  $S \mapsto r_j(S(v) - S(b))$, and they correspond to the rows $(ab)j$
  of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ (KT eq.~(6.16)). The edges of $G_v$ have the same
  rows in both matrices (KT eq.~(6.13)). Katoh and Tanigawa add to the
  row $(vb)i^*$ the combination of the rows $(vb)j$ with $j \neq i^*$ and
  of the rows of the edges of $G_v$, with the coefficients of KT
  eq.~(6.24) (KT eq.~(6.26)). As $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$, the result is the
  row of KT eq.~(6.27):
  \begin{align*}
    &\sum_j \lambda_j\, R\bigl(G, p_1; (vb)j\bigr)
      + \sum_{e \in E(G_v)} \sum_j \lambda_{ej}\, R\bigl(G, p_1; e_j\bigr) \\
    &\qquad= r\bigl(S(v) - S(b)\bigr) - r\bigl(S(a) - S(b)\bigr)
      = r\bigl(S(v) - S(a)\bigr),
  \end{align*}
  as forms in $S$, by KT eq.~(6.24). We call this row $w$. It reads
  $S(a)$ as well as $S(v)$: on the screw assignments that vanish off
  $a$ it is the form $S \mapsto -r(S(a))$, which is not zero. Its entries
  outside the columns of $v$ vanish only after the column operation,
  which turns it into the form $S \mapsto r(S(v))$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-columnop}). This is the row $(r, 0)$ of KT
  eq.~(6.28).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem holds for every form $g$ in the span of the rows at
  $ab$ in place of their combination with the coefficients $\lambda_j$.
  It finds the form $r$ with $g(S) = r(S(a) - S(b))$ as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}. It holds for any two
  multigraphs, with $vb$ and $ab$ oriented by the endpoint selector
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The rank of KT eq.\ (6.29); from the proof of KT Lemma~6.10]
  \label{lem:case-III-candidate-row}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sum_augment_candidateRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim-6-11, lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition,
        lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction,
        lem:case-III-vanish-off-column, lem:case-III-columnop, lem:case-III-seam,
        lem:case-III-transport-collapse,
        lem:case-III-conditional-block, lem:case-III-claim612,
        lem:case-II-realization-placement}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, let the forms
  $r_j$, the index $i^*$ and the scalars $\lambda_j$ be as in
  \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}, and let
  $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$. Let $L$ be a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine
  subspace of the panel $\Pi(a)$ of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and let $(G, p_1)$
  be the body-hinge framework with the hinge $L$ at $va$, the hinge
  $q(ab)$ of $ab$ at $vb$, and at each edge $e$ of $G_v$ the hinge $q(e)$
  of $e$ in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ (KT eq.~(6.12)). If $r$ does not vanish on
  $C(L)$, that is, if the top-left $D \times D$ block of KT eq.~(6.29) is
  nonsingular, then $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_1) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction, lem:case-III-columnop,
        lem:case-III-vanish-off-column, lem:case-III-transport-collapse,
        lem:case-III-conditional-block, lem:case-III-claim612,
        lem:case-II-realization-placement}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~685--686]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  Let $W$ be the row space of $R(G_v, q)$. The edges of $G_v$ have the
  same hinges in $(G, p_1)$ as in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, so $W$ lies in the row
  space of $R(G, p_1)$. The hinge of $vb$ is $q(ab)$, so the form
  $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(b))$ is a row at $vb$, as in
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}. By KT eq.~(6.24), the
  form $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$ lies in $W$. So the row $w$ of KT
  eq.~(6.27), the form $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(a))$, lies in the row space
  of $R(G, p_1)$.

  Let $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ be linearly independent linear forms on
  $K^D$ that vanish on $C(L)$, so that the rows of $va$ are the forms
  $S \mapsto r'_i(S(v) - S(a))$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). After the
  column operation (\cref{lem:case-III-columnop}), the rows of $va$ and
  $w$ are the forms $S \mapsto r'_i(S(v))$ and $S \mapsto r(S(v))$. They
  have entries only in the columns of $v$. There they form the top-left
  $D \times D$ block of KT eq.~(6.29), with the rows $r'_1, \dots,
  r'_{D-1}$ and $r$. This block is nonsingular exactly when $r$ is not a
  combination of the $r'_i$, that is, when $r$ does not vanish on $C(L)$
  (KT p.~690). The other rows of KT eq.~(6.29) are the rows $(vb)j$ with
  $j \neq i^*$ and the rows of the edges of $G_v$. Outside the columns of
  $v$ they have the entries of $R(G_v^{ab} \setminus (ab)i^*, q)$, the
  matrix $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ without the row $(ab)i^*$. By KT eq.~(6.24)
  that row lies in the span of the others, so KT eq.~(6.23) holds:
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R\bigl(G_v^{ab} \setminus (ab)i^*, q\bigr)
    \;=\; \operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) \;=\; D\bigl(|V(G)| - 2\bigr).
  \]

  Now suppose that $r$ does not vanish on $C(L)$. Then the $r'_i$ and $r$
  span the linear forms on $K^D$. So for every linear form $x$ on $K^D$,
  the form $S \mapsto x(S(v) - S(a))$ is a combination of the rows of
  $va$ and of $w$, and it lies in the row space of $R(G, p_1)$. Hence so
  does each row $(ab)j$ of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$:
  \[
    r_j\bigl(S(a) - S(b)\bigr)
    \;=\; r_j\bigl(S(v) - S(b)\bigr) - r_j\bigl(S(v) - S(a)\bigr).
  \]
  In KT eq.~(6.29) this is the row operation, with the rows of the
  top-left block, that clears the entries below it. So every row of
  $R(G_v^{ab} \setminus (ab)i^*, q)$ lies in the row space of
  $R(G, p_1)$, and we choose $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ linearly independent ones.
  They vanish on the screw assignments that vanish off $v$, as no edge of
  $G_v^{ab}$ has the end $v$. On these screw assignments the rows of $va$
  and $w$ are the forms $S \mapsto r'_i(S(v))$ and $S \mapsto r(S(v))$,
  which are linearly independent. By \cref{lem:case-III-conditional-block},
  the $(D - 1) + 1 + D(|V(G)| - 2) = D(|V(G)| - 1)$ forms are linearly
  independent. They lie in the row space of $R(G, p_1)$, so its rank is
  at least $D(|V(G)| - 1)$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityRows-pinned-placement-rank-add}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is the independence in the last paragraph, for any
  finite families of forms in place of the rows of $va$ and of
  $R(G_v^{ab} \setminus (ab)i^*, q)$, and any form $r$. It takes the
  restrictions to the screw assignments that vanish off $v$ after
  composition with $\Phi$, which fixes these screw assignments. It does
  not mention $G$, $p_1$ or the rank. The formal proof of Case~III
  proves the rank bound for $(G, p_1)$, with $L$ the meet of $\Pi(a)$ and
  a second panel, in Katoh and Tanigawa's form: after the column
  operation, $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ rows at $vb$ and at the edges of $G_v$ are
  linearly independent outside the columns of $v$. No node pins that
  theorem. Of the theorems pinned in this subsubsection, the
  proofs of the main results use only those of \cref{lem:case-III-columnop}
  and \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}, and the identity of
  \cref{lem:case-III-transport-collapse}. At the normals $q_0$ of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}, if $q_0(a) = q(a)$ and
  $t \neq 0$, the hinge of $va$ is $-t\,C(q(a), q(b))$. This is the case
  $L = q(ab)$, where $r$ vanishes on $C(L)$ and the block is singular.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsubsection{The case \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3}: three frameworks and KT Claim~6.12 (KT \S6.4.1)}
\label{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-claim612}

Katoh and Tanigawa prove Case~III first for $d = 3$, where $D = 6$
(KT Lemma~6.10), and then for every $d$ (KT
Lemma~6.13)~\cite[\S6.4]{katohTanigawa2011}. The formal proof of KT
Theorem~5.5 uses the general case at every $d$, including $d = 3$
(\cref{lem:case-III}). We give the case $d = 3$ in full as well, as it
has the same idea with fewer parts.

Let $G$ be a $2$-edge-connected minimal $0$-dof-graph with no proper
rigid subgraph, as in KT Lemma~6.10. If $|V(G)| = 3$, then $G$ is a
triangle, which KT Lemma~6.7(i) realizes by KT Lemma~5.4 (p.~677). So
let $|V(G)| \ge 4$, as Katoh and Tanigawa do (p.~682). At $d = 3$, KT
Lemma~4.6 then gives two adjacent vertices $v$ and $a$ of degree two,
with $N_G(v) = \{a, b\}$ and $N_G(a) = \{v, c\}$ (KT p.~680). The
vertices $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$ are distinct, as otherwise $v$, $a$ and
$b$ would span a triangle, a proper rigid subgraph, as in the proof of
KT Lemma~6.7(ii). By KT Lemma~4.8, the graph $G_v^{ab}$ and the graph
$G_a^{vc}$ obtained by splitting off at $a$ along $vc$ are both minimal
$0$-dof-graphs.

The argument then has fewer parts than for general $d$. The case of a
short cycle in KT Lemma~4.6, which KT Lemma~6.13 treats with KT
Lemma~5.4, does not arise. There are three frameworks, one for each of
the panels of $a$, $b$ and $c$, in place of $d$ frameworks along a
chain of length $d$. The third is built on $G_a^{vc}$, through one
isomorphism $G_a^{vc} \cong G_v^{ab}$ that sends $v$ to $a$ (KT
p.~686, \cref{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}). For general $d$ there are
$d - 2$ such isomorphisms, and the rows are matched along the chain
(KT eqs.~(6.54) and~(6.62)). The rank of each of the three frameworks
is read off its block form (KT eqs.~(6.29), (6.30) and~(6.41)). The
last step is the span of the six joins of four points in
$\bigwedge^2 K^4$, in place of the $D$ joins of $d - 1$ of $d + 1$
points in $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ (KT eq.~(6.67), p.~698).

Let $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ and the linear form $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$ on
$K^D$ be as in \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}, and let
$\Pi(u)$ be the panel of a vertex $u$ in $(G_v^{ab}, q)$. The first
framework is $(G, p_1)$ of KT eq.~(6.12), with a line
$L \subset \Pi(a)$ at $va$. By \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row},
$\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_1) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$ if the top-left
block $M_1$ of KT eq.~(6.29) is nonsingular. The second framework,
$(G, p_2)$, exchanges the roles of $a$ and $b$, with a line
$L' \subset \Pi(b)$ at $vb$ (KT eqs.~(6.19) and~(6.30)). For $d = 2$,
one of $M_1$ and $M_2$ is nonsingular for some choice of $L$ and $L'$,
but for $d \ge 3$ this may fail (KT p.~686). The third framework,
$(G, p_3)$, has a line $L'' \subset \Pi(c)$ at $ac$ (KT eqs.~(6.31)
to~(6.41)). KT Claim~6.12 says that for some choice of $L$, $L'$ and
$L''$, at least one of the three blocks $M_1$, $M_2$, $M_3$ is
nonsingular (KT p.~690).

Katoh and Tanigawa prove the claim by
contradiction~\cite[pp.~690--691]{katohTanigawa2011}. The form $r$ is
not zero (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-r-nonzero}). The block $M_1$ is
singular if and only if $r$ vanishes on $C(L)$
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}), and likewise $M_2$ for
$L'$. The last row of $M_3$ is $-r$ (KT eq.~(6.44)), so $M_3$ is
singular if and only if $r$ vanishes on $C(L'')$. If no choice makes a
block nonsingular, then $r$ vanishes on the extensors of all lines in
$\Pi(a)$, $\Pi(b)$ and $\Pi(c)$, the set of KT eq.~(6.45). Four points
with suitable incidences with the three panels show that these
extensors span $K^D$, by KT Lemma~2.1. So $r = 0$, a contradiction.
The rest of this subsubsection proves the span and chooses the points.
It then gives the three frameworks and Claim~6.12
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612}). The case $d = 3$ of Case~III follows
(\cref{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}), and then the general
case.

The proof of KT Claim~6.12 ends with KT Lemma~2.1
(\cref{lem:extensor-independence}), which holds for any linearly
independent vectors (the remark after it).

\begin{lemma}[The joins of $d + 1$ linearly independent vectors span $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$; from KT Lemma~2.1]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.span_omitTwoExtensor_eq_top}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:extensor-independence, def:screw-space}
  Let $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_{d+1} \in K^{d+1}$ be linearly
  independent. Then the $D$ joins $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$
  ($1 \le i < j \le d + 1$) span $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For $i < j$, let $E_{ij}$ be the join of the $\bar p_l$ with
  $l \neq i, j$, in increasing order of $l$, as in
  \cref{lem:extensor-independence}. By the remark after that lemma, the
  $D$ extensors $E_{ij}$ form a basis of $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$. At
  $d = 3$, $E_{ij} = \bar p_k \vee \bar p_l$, where $k < l$ and
  $\{k, l\} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\} \setminus \{i, j\}$. So the six joins are
  the six $E_{ij}$, and they span $\bigwedge^2 K^4$.

  For $d \neq 3$ the join $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$ lies in
  $\bigwedge^2 K^{d+1}$, not in $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$. The argument
  then proves the claim with the $E_{ij}$ in place of the joins, the
  form that the general case uses
  (\cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem states the lemma for every $d$, with $E_{ij}$ in
  place of $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A linear form that vanishes on a spanning set is zero; from the proof of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-orthseq-vanish}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eq_zero_of_annihilates_span_top}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}
  Let $S$ be a subset of $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ that spans it, and
  let $r$ be a linear form on $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ that vanishes on
  $S$. Then $r = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The linear forms $r$ and $0$ agree on $S$, hence on its span, which
  is $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa take four points of $K^3$ with
\begin{align*}
  p_1 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c), &
  p_2 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \setminus \Pi(c), \\
  p_3 &\in \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c) \setminus \Pi(a), &
  p_4 &\in \Pi(c) \cap \Pi(a) \setminus \Pi(b),
\end{align*}
which they can choose affinely independent, as $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ is
generic and nonparallel (KT p.~691). The points $p_i$ are not the
frameworks $(G, p_i)$; we keep Katoh and Tanigawa's notation. Any two
of the points lie on a common panel. So the line through them lies in
$\Pi(a) \cup \Pi(b) \cup \Pi(c)$, and its extensor
$\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$ (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}) is in
the set of KT eq.~(6.45). By
\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}, the six joins span $K^D$.

For $d$ generic panels in $K^d$, Katoh and Tanigawa use that any $j$
of them meet in an affine subspace of dimension $d - j$ (KT p.~698).
The point $p_1$ is the case $j = d = 3$. A point $x$ lies on the panel
with normal $n$ if and only if $\bar x \cdot n = 0$ (the remarks
after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). In homogeneous coordinates,
fewer than $d + 1$ panels always have a common nonzero vector.

\begin{lemma}[A common nonzero vector of at most $d$ panels; from the proof of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-panel-point-exists}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_ne_zero_dotProduct_eq_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework}
  Let $m < d + 1$, and let $n_1, \dots, n_m \in K^{d+1}$. Then there is
  a nonzero $x \in K^{d+1}$ with $x \cdot n_i = 0$ for
  $1 \le i \le m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The map $K^{d+1} \to K^m$ that sends $x$ to
  $(x \cdot n_1, \dots, x \cdot n_m)$ is linear, and $m < d + 1$. By
  rank--nullity its kernel has dimension at least $d + 1 - m > 0$, so
  it contains a nonzero vector.
\end{proof}

The next four lemmas concern the affine choice of the points. The
formal proof does not use them: it chooses vectors of $K^4$ in place
of the points
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals}). If the
determinant of four points that depend on some variables is a nonzero
polynomial in them, the points are affinely independent for some
values of the variables
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-producer}). The
determinant is a polynomial in the variables when the coordinates of
the points are (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-detpoly}). The coordinate planes of $K^3$
give an example of four affinely independent points with the
incidences above
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-witness}). So three
planes with such points exist
(\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep}).

\begin{lemma}[Affine independence off the zeros of a polynomial; for the points of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-producer}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_affineIndependent_of_det_polynomial_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:affine-indep-iff}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $\sigma$ be a set of variables. For each
  $t \in K^\sigma$, let $p_1(t), \dots, p_4(t)$ be points of $K^3$.
  Suppose that a nonzero polynomial $P \in K[\sigma]$ satisfies
  $P(t) = \det\bigl(\bar p_1(t), \dots, \bar p_4(t)\bigr)$, the
  determinant of the matrix with these rows, for every $t$. Then
  $p_1(t), \dots, p_4(t)$ are affinely independent for some $t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As $K$ is infinite and $P \neq 0$, there is a $t \in K^\sigma$ with
  $P(t) \neq 0$. At this $t$ the vectors
  $\bar p_1(t), \dots, \bar p_4(t)$ have a nonzero determinant, so the
  points are affinely independent (\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The determinant of points with polynomial coordinates; for the points of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-detpoly}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_detPolynomial_of_pointPolynomial}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:affine-indep-iff}
  Let $\sigma$ be a set of variables, and let $P_{ij} \in K[\sigma]$ for
  $1 \le i \le 4$ and $1 \le j \le 3$. For $t \in K^\sigma$, let
  $p_i(t) = \bigl(P_{i1}(t), P_{i2}(t), P_{i3}(t)\bigr) \in K^3$. Then
  some $P \in K[\sigma]$ satisfies
  $P(t) = \det\bigl(\bar p_1(t), \dots, \bar p_4(t)\bigr)$ for every
  $t \in K^\sigma$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $P$ be the determinant of the $4 \times 4$ matrix of polynomials
  with the rows $(P_{i1}, P_{i2}, P_{i3}, 1)$. Evaluation at $t$ is a
  ring homomorphism $K[\sigma] \to K$, so it commutes with the
  determinant. It takes the row
  $(P_{i1}, P_{i2}, P_{i3}, 1)$ to $(p_i(t), 1) = \bar p_i(t)$
  (\cref{def:homogeneous-coords}). So $P(t)$ is the determinant of
  $\bar p_1(t), \dots, \bar p_4(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorems of this lemma and of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-producer} hold for
  $d + 1$ points of $K^d$, for every $d$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[An example of the points of KT Claim~6.12, at the coordinate planes]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-witness}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_affineIndependent_panel_incidence}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:affine-indep-iff}
  There are linearly independent $n_a, n_b, n_c \in K^4$ and affinely
  independent points $p_1, \dots, p_4 \in K^3$ such that
  \begin{align*}
    p_1 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c), &
    p_2 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \setminus \Pi(c), \\
    p_3 &\in \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c) \setminus \Pi(a), &
    p_4 &\in \Pi(c) \cap \Pi(a) \setminus \Pi(b),
  \end{align*}
  where $\Pi(u)$ is the plane $\{x \in K^3 : \bar x \cdot n_u = 0\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $n_a, n_b, n_c$ be the first three standard basis vectors of
  $K^4$, so that $\Pi(a)$, $\Pi(b)$ and $\Pi(c)$ are the coordinate
  planes $x_1 = 0$, $x_2 = 0$ and $x_3 = 0$ of $K^3$. Let $p_1 = 0$,
  $p_2 = e_3$, $p_3 = e_1$ and $p_4 = e_2$, where $e_1, e_2, e_3$ is
  the standard basis of $K^3$. The point $p_1$ lies on the three
  planes. The point $e_3$ lies on $\Pi(a)$ and $\Pi(b)$ but not on
  $\Pi(c)$, and $e_1$ and $e_2$ have the incidences required of $p_3$
  and $p_4$ in the same way. Subtracting $\bar p_1 = (0, 0, 0, 1)$ from
  the other rows of the matrix with the rows
  $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_4$ leaves a permutation matrix, so its
  determinant is $\pm 1$. Hence the points are affinely independent
  (\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three planes with the points of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_affineIndependent_panel_incidence}

  \begin{align*}
    p_1 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c), &
    p_2 &\in \Pi(a) \cap \Pi(b) \setminus \Pi(c), \\
    p_3 &\in \Pi(b) \cap \Pi(c) \setminus \Pi(a), &
    p_4 &\in \Pi(c) \cap \Pi(a) \setminus \Pi(b),
  \end{align*}
  where $\Pi(u)$ is the plane $\{x \in K^3 : \bar x \cdot n_u = 0\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The normals and the points of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-witness} have these

\end{proof}

The formal proof of the case $d = 3$ chooses the normals $q$ of
$(G_v^{ab}, q)$ off the zeros of a product of polynomials, as in the
passage after \cref{lem:case-I-realization}. One factor makes $q(a)$,
$q(b)$ and $q(c)$ linearly independent
(\cref{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}, below). The proof needs
the four points for these normals. It takes them in homogeneous
coordinates: vectors $\bar p_i \in K^4$, whose last coordinate need
not be $1$, with $\bar p_i \cdot q(u) = 0$ exactly for the panels
$\Pi(u)$ that contain $p_i$. The span of

vectors are linearly independent. A line in the panel $\Pi(u)$ is

$\bar p_i$ and $\bar p_j$ are orthogonal to both normals, this
extensor is a multiple of $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$

for any three linearly independent normals.

\begin{lemma}[The points of KT Claim~6.12 in homogeneous coordinates, for given normals]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_homogeneousIncidence_of_normals}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim612-panel-point-exists, lem:affine-indep-iff}
  Let $n_a, n_b, n_c \in K^4$ be linearly independent. Then there are
  linearly independent $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_4 \in K^4$ with
  $\bar p_1 \cdot n_a = \bar p_1 \cdot n_b = \bar p_1 \cdot n_c = 0$ and
  \begin{align*}
    \bar p_2 \cdot n_a &= \bar p_2 \cdot n_b = 0 \neq \bar p_2 \cdot n_c, \\
    \bar p_3 \cdot n_b &= \bar p_3 \cdot n_c = 0 \neq \bar p_3 \cdot n_a, \\
    \bar p_4 \cdot n_c &= \bar p_4 \cdot n_a = 0 \neq \bar p_4 \cdot n_b.
  \end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The linear map $K^4 \to K^3$ that sends $x$ to
  $(x \cdot n_a, x \cdot n_b, x \cdot n_c)$ has the linearly
  independent rows $n_a$, $n_b$, $n_c$, so it has rank $3$ and is onto.
  Let $\bar p_2$, $\bar p_3$ and $\bar p_4$ be vectors that it sends to
  $(0, 0, 1)$, $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$. Let $\bar p_1$ be a nonzero
  vector orthogonal to $n_a$, $n_b$ and $n_c$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-panel-point-exists}, with $m = 3$). These
  vectors have the incidences of the claim.

  Suppose that $\sum_i g_i \bar p_i = 0$. The dot product with $n_a$
  gives $g_3 = 0$, that with $n_b$ gives $g_4 = 0$, and that with $n_c$
  gives $g_2 = 0$. Then $g_1 \bar p_1 = 0$, and $g_1 = 0$ as
  $\bar p_1 \neq 0$. So the four vectors are linearly independent.
\end{proof}

  independent normals in $K^{d+1}$, which has no node. The formal proof
  of the general case uses that form
  (\cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator}).
\end{fmlnote}

By the proof of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}, the block $M_1$ of
KT eq.~(6.42) has the rows $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ and $r$, where the
$r'_i$ are $D - 1$ linearly independent linear forms on $K^D$ that
vanish on $C(L)$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}). A nondegenerate hinge of
a panel-hinge framework gives such forms
(\cref{lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}). Katoh and Tanigawa test the
singularity of the blocks by the extensors of the lines (KT p.~690).

\begin{lemma}[When the blocks of KT eq.\ (6.42) are nonsingular]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sumElim_candidateRow_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.mem_hingeRowBlock_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-row-block, lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, let $r$ be
  the linear form of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}.
  Let $L$ be a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace of the panel of a
  vertex of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and let $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ be linearly
  independent linear forms on $K^D$ that vanish on $C(L)$. Then
  $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}, r$ are linearly independent if and only if
  $r(C(L)) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The extensor $C(L)$ is not zero (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}).
  So the linear forms on $K^D$ that vanish on $C(L)$ form a space of
  dimension $D - 1$ (\cref{def:hinge-row-block}), and
  $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ is a basis of it. A form vanishes on
  $\operatorname{span} C(L)$ if and only if it vanishes at $C(L)$. So
  $r(C(L)) = 0$ if and only if $r$ is a linear combination of the
  $r'_i$, that is, if and only if the $D$ forms are linearly
  dependent.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorems hold for every linear form $r$ and every edge of
  a body-hinge framework, with any linearly independent forms that span
  the forms vanishing on its extensor. The first is the lemma. The
  second is the step that a form vanishes on the span of the extensor
  if and only if it vanishes at the extensor.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The form $r$ is not zero; from the proof of KT Claim~6.12]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-r-nonzero}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.candidateRow_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition, lem:case-II-placement-new-rows}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, let the forms
  $r_j$ and the scalars $\lambda_j$ be as in
  \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}. Then the linear form
  $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$ on $K^D$ is not zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[p.~690]{katohTanigawa2011}. The
  forms $r_1, \dots, r_{D-1}$ are linearly independent, and
  $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$ (KT eq.~(6.25),
  \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}). A linear combination of
  linearly independent vectors with a nonzero coefficient is not zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem holds for any linearly independent family and any
  scalars with $\lambda_{i^*} = 1$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The second framework; KT eqs.\ (6.19) and~(6.30)]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sum_p2_candidateRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-II-realization-placement, lem:case-III-columnop,
        lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, let $r$ be the
  linear form of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}. Let $L'$
  be a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace of the panel $\Pi(b)$ of
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and let $(G, p_2)$ be the body-hinge framework with
  the hinge $q(ab)$ at $va$, the hinge $L'$ at $vb$, and the hinge $q(e)$
  at each edge $e$ of $G_v$ (KT eq.~(6.19)). If $r$ does not vanish on
  $C(L')$, that is, if the top-left $D \times D$ block $M_2$ of KT
  eq.~(6.30) is nonsingular, then
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_2) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We argue as in the proof of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}, with
  the roles of $a$ and $b$ exchanged (KT pp.~682 and~686). Let $W$ be the
  row space of $R(G_v, q)$, which lies in the row space of $R(G, p_2)$.
  The hinge of $va$ is $q(ab)$, on which $r$ vanishes, so the form
  $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(a))$ is a row at $va$. By KT eq.~(6.24), the
  form $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$ lies in $W$. So their sum, the form
  $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(b))$, lies in the row space of $R(G, p_2)$.

  Let $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ be linearly independent forms that vanish
  on $C(L')$, so that the rows of $vb$ are the forms
  $S \mapsto r'_i(S(v) - S(b))$. Katoh and Tanigawa add the columns of
  $v$ to those of $b$ (KT p.~682). By \cref{lem:case-III-columnop}, with
  $b$ in place of $a$, this turns the rows of $vb$ and the form
  $S \mapsto r(S(v) - S(b))$ into the forms $S \mapsto r'_i(S(v))$ and
  $S \mapsto r(S(v))$. These make up the block $M_2$, with the rows
  $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ and $r$, which is nonsingular exactly when
  $r(C(L')) \neq 0$ (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}). The
  count in the proof of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}, with $b$ in
  place of $a$, then gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is the independence of the last step of the count,
  for any finite families of forms in place of the rows of $vb$ and of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$, any form in place of $r$, and any hinge in place of
  $L'$. It combines the pinned theorems of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}, with $b$ in place of $a$, and of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}. It does not mention $G$,
  $p_2$ or the rank.
\end{fmlnote}

The next three lemmas give Katoh and Tanigawa's realization of
$G_a^{vc}$ with the panels of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, in which the panel of $a$
becomes that of $v$ (KT pp.~686--687). The third framework is built on
it. The formal proof of the case $d = 3$ does not use these lemmas.

\begin{lemma}[The isomorphism from $G_a^{vc}$ to $G_v^{ab}$; KT p.~686]
  \label{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}
  \lean{Graph.splitOff_isLink_relabel}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-operations}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and let $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$ be distinct
  vertices of $G$ such that the edges of $G$ at $v$ are $va$ and $vb$,
  and those at $a$ are $va$ and $ac$. Let
  $\rho \colon V(G) \setminus \{a\} \to V(G) \setminus \{v\}$ be the
  bijection with $\rho(v) = a$ and $\rho(u) = u$ for $u \neq v$. Then
  $\rho$ is an isomorphism from $G_a^{vc}$ to $G_v^{ab}$ that maps the
  edge $vb$ to the new edge $ab$, the new edge $vc$ to the edge $ac$, and
  every other edge to itself.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The edges of $G_a^{vc}$ are $vb$, the new edge $vc$, and the edges of
  $G$ that meet neither $v$ nor $a$. Those of $G_v^{ab}$ are $ac$, the
  new edge $ab$, and the same edges of $G$ (\cref{def:graph-operations}).
  The bijection $\rho$ fixes the ends of an edge that meets neither $v$
  nor $a$. It maps the ends $v, b$ of $vb$ to $a, b$, and the ends $v, c$
  of $vc$ to $a, c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  In the formalization the edges of a multigraph are labels
  (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}). The pinned theorem takes labels
  $e_0 \neq e_1$ outside $E(G)$ for the new edges $ab$ of $G_v^{ab}$ and
  $vc$ of $G_a^{vc}$. It replaces $\rho$ by the transposition of $a$ and
  $v$ on all bodies, and the map on edges by the permutation of the
  labels that exchanges the label of $vb$ with $e_0$ and that of $ac$
  with $e_1$. It states that an edge joins $x$ and $y$ in $G_a^{vc}$ if
  and only if its image joins the images of $x$ and $y$ in $G_v^{ab}$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[A realization of $G_a^{vc}$; KT eq.\ (6.31)]
  \label{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.ofNormals_relabel,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.hasGenericFullRankRealization_of_splitOff_relabel}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}
  Let $G$, $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$ be as in
  \cref{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}, and let $q$ be normals. Let
  $q_\rho$ be the normals with $q_\rho(v) = q(a)$, $q_\rho(a) = q(v)$, and
  $q_\rho(u) = q(u)$ for every other body $u$. Suppose that the normals
  $q$ are in general position and that every infinitesimal motion of
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ is constant on $V(G_v^{ab})$. Then the normals $q_\rho$
  are in general position, and every infinitesimal motion of
  $(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$ is constant on $V(G_a^{vc})$. In particular, if
  $G_v^{ab}$ and $G_a^{vc}$ are $0$-dof-graphs and $G_v^{ab}$ has a
  generic full-rank realization, then so has $G_a^{vc}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}
  The normals $q_\rho$ are those of $q$, permuted, so they are in general
  position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). By
  \cref{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}, an edge of $G_a^{vc}$ with ends $u$
  and $w$ corresponds to an edge of $G_v^{ab}$ with ends $\rho(u)$ and
  $\rho(w)$. The two edges have the same hinge
  $C(q(\rho(u)), q(\rho(w)))$, which is KT eq.~(6.31).

  For a screw assignment $S$, let $S_\rho$ agree with $S$ except that
  $S_\rho(a) = S(v)$ and $S_\rho(v) = S(a)$. Then
  $S_\rho(\rho(u)) - S_\rho(\rho(w)) = S(u) - S(w)$ for all vertices
  $u, w$ of $G_a^{vc}$. So if $S$ is an infinitesimal motion of
  $(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$, then $S_\rho$ is one of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$. Then
  $S_\rho$ is constant on $V(G_v^{ab})$, which is the image of
  $V(G_a^{vc})$ under $\rho$, and so $S$ is constant on $V(G_a^{vc})$.
  The last claim follows, as a realization of a $0$-dof-graph is at the
  target rank exactly when its infinitesimal motions are constant on the
  vertices (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem is the first claim. It replaces $\rho$ by the
  transposition of $a$ and $v$, so that $q_\rho$ is $q$ composed with it.
  It also transports the endpoint selector of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}) along this transposition and
  the permutation of the labels of \cref{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}. It
  assumes that the selector assigns each edge of $G_v^{ab}$ its ends, and
  concludes the same for the transported selector and $G_a^{vc}$. The
  second pinned theorem is the last claim.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The rows of the realization of $G_a^{vc}$; KT eq.\ (6.31)]
  \label{lem:splitOff-rigidityRows-relabel}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.rigidityRows_ofNormals_relabel}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel, lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel}
  Let $G$, $v$, $a$, $b$, $c$, $q$ and $q_\rho$ be as in
  \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel}. For a screw assignment $S$, let
  $S_\rho$ agree with $S$ except that $S_\rho(a) = S(v)$ and
  $S_\rho(v) = S(a)$. Then the rows of $R(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$ are the
  forms $S \mapsto f(S_\rho)$ for the rows $f$ of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$, where
  the rows at an edge with ends $u$ and $w$ are all the forms
  $S \mapsto x(S(u) - S(w))$ for the linear forms $x$ on $K^D$ that
  vanish on its hinge. In particular, the two matrices have the same
  rank.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}
  Let $uw$ be an edge of $G_a^{vc}$. As in the proof of
  \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel}, the edge $\rho(u)\rho(w)$ of
  $G_v^{ab}$ has the same hinge, and every
  edge of $G_v^{ab}$ arises in this way. For a form $x$ that vanishes on
  this hinge, the row $f \colon S \mapsto x(S(\rho(u)) - S(\rho(w)))$ of
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ satisfies $f(S_\rho) = x(S(u) - S(w))$. This proves
  the first claim. The map $S \mapsto S_\rho$ is a linear automorphism,
  so $f \mapsto (S \mapsto f(S_\rho))$ is injective, and the two row
  spaces have the same dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is the first claim, for the transported endpoint
  selector of \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel} and for every
  endpoint selector of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The last row of $M_3$; KT eq.\ (6.44)]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.candidateRow_ac_eq_neg,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_comp_single_tail,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_comp_single_off,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.acolumn_mem_hingeRowBlock_of_span_rigidityRows,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeRow_acolumn_mem_span_rigidityRows}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-acolumn-zero, lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, suppose that
  the edges of $G$ at $a$ are $va$ and $ac$, where $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$
  are distinct. Let the forms $r_j$ and the scalars $\lambda_j$ and
  $\lambda_{ej}$ be as in \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition},
  and let $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$. Write the rows
  $R(G_v^{ab}, q; (ac)j)$ at $ac$ as the forms
  $S \mapsto s_j(S(a) - S(c))$. Then
  \[
    r \;=\; -\sum_{1 \le j \le D-1} \lambda_{(ac)j}\, s_j .
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~690--691]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  By KT eq.~(6.43) (\cref{lem:case-III-acolumn-zero}), the combination
  of the rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ in KT eq.~(6.24) vanishes on the screw
  assignments that vanish off $a$. Let $S$ be such a screw assignment,
  with $S(a) = y$. A row $S \mapsto x(S(u) - S(w))$ at an edge with ends
  $u, w \neq a$ vanishes at $S$. As the edges of $G$ at $a$ are $va$ and
  $ac$, the edges of $G_v^{ab}$ at $a$ are $ab$ and $ac$
  (\cref{def:graph-operations}). Their rows take the values $r_j(y)$ and
  $s_j(y)$ at $S$. So
  \[
    0 \;=\; \sum_j \lambda_j\, r_j(y) + \sum_j \lambda_{(ac)j}\, s_j(y)
    \;=\; r(y) + \sum_j \lambda_{(ac)j}\, s_j(y)
  \]
  for every $y \in K^D$, which is KT eq.~(6.44).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pinned theorem is the computation of the proof, for any forms
  at $ab$ and at $ac$ and any further form that vanishes on the screw
  assignments that vanish off $a$. The second and third give the
  restriction of a form $S \mapsto x(S(u) - S(w))$ to these screw
  assignments: it is $x$ if $u = a \neq w$, and $0$ if
  $a \notin \{u, w\}$. The fourth is the form of the lemma that the
  formal proof of the case $d = 3$ uses. Let $ac$ be the only edge at $a$
  of a body-hinge framework, and let $f$ be a form in the span of its
  rows. Then the restriction of $f$ to the screw assignments that vanish
  off $a$, a linear form $x$ on $K^D$, vanishes on the hinge of $ac$. The
  fifth adds that $S \mapsto x(S(a) - S(c))$ lies in the span of the
  rows. For the framework $(G_v, q)$ and the form
  $f \colon S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$, the fourth says that $r$ vanishes
  on $C(q(a), q(c))$, as KT eq.~(6.44) shows.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The third framework; KT eqs.\ (6.31)--(6.41)]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612-p3-placement}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.linearIndependent_sum_p3_candidateRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-II-realization-placement, lem:case-III-columnop,
        lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp, lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}, suppose that
  the edges of $G$ at $a$ are $va$ and $ac$, where $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$
  are distinct. Let $r$ be the linear form of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}. Let $L''$ be a
  $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace of the panel $\Pi(c)$ of
  $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and let $(G, p_3)$ be the body-hinge framework with
  the hinge $q(ac)$ at $va$, the hinge $q(ab)$ at $vb$, the hinge $L''$
  at $ac$, and the hinge $q(e)$ at every other edge $e$ (KT
  eq.~(6.33)). If $r$ does not vanish on $C(L'')$, that is, if the
  top-left $D \times D$ block $M_3$ of KT eq.~(6.41) is nonsingular, then
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_3) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~686--689]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  Let $(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$ be the panel-hinge framework of
  \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel}. In it the panel of $v$ is
  $\Pi(a)$, the hinge of $vb$ is $q(ab)$, the hinge of the new edge $vc$
  is $q(ac)$, and the other edges keep their hinges (KT eq.~(6.31)).
  Katoh and Tanigawa obtain $(G, p_3)$ from $(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$ as
  $(G, p_1)$ from $(G_v^{ab}, q)$, with $a$, $c$ and $v$ in the roles of
  $v$, $a$ and $b$. So $va$ gets the hinge $q(ac)$ of $vc$, and $ac$ gets
  $L''$ (KT eqs.~(6.32) and~(6.33)).

  Write the rows of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at $ab$ and at $ac$ as the forms
  $S \mapsto r_j(S(a) - S(b))$ and $S \mapsto s_j(S(a) - S(c))$, as in
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}. The rows of $R(G, p_3)$ at $vb$ and
  at $va$ are then the forms $S \mapsto r_j(S(v) - S(b))$ and
  $S \mapsto s_j(S(v) - S(a))$. The edges of $G$ other than $va$, $vb$
  and $ac$ have the same rows in both matrices (KT eq.~(6.34)). Katoh and
  Tanigawa add to the row $(vb)i^*$ the other terms of KT eq.~(6.24),
  with the rows at $vb$ and $va$ in place of those at $ab$ and $ac$ (KT
  eq.~(6.40)). Call the result $w_3$. For a screw assignment $S$, let
  $S'$ agree with $S$ except that $S'(a) = S(v)$. The edges of $G_v$
  other than $ac$ do not meet $a$, so KT eq.~(6.24) at $S'$ reads
  \[
    \sum_j \lambda_j\, r_j\bigl(S(v) - S(b)\bigr)
    + \sum_j \lambda_{(ac)j}\, s_j\bigl(S(v) - S(c)\bigr)
    + \sum_{e \neq ac} \sum_j \lambda_{ej}\, R\bigl(G_v^{ab}, q; e_j\bigr)(S)
    \;=\; 0 ,
  \]
  where $e$ runs over the edges of $G_v$. Subtracting this from $w_3(S)$
  leaves
  \[
    \sum_j \lambda_{(ac)j}\, s_j\bigl(S(c) - S(a)\bigr)
    \;=\; r\bigl(S(a) - S(c)\bigr),
  \]
  by KT eq.~(6.44) (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}). So $w_3$ is the
  form $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(c))$.

  Let $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ be linearly independent forms that vanish
  on $C(L'')$, so that the rows of $ac$ are the forms
  $S \mapsto r'_i(S(a) - S(c))$. Katoh and Tanigawa add the columns of
  $a$ to those of $c$ (KT eqs.~(6.35) and~(6.36)). By
  \cref{lem:case-III-columnop}, with $a$ and $c$ in place of $v$ and $a$,
  this turns the rows of $ac$ and $w_3$ into the forms
  $S \mapsto r'_i(S(a))$ and $S \mapsto r(S(a))$. These make up the
  top-left block $M_3$ of KT eq.~(6.41), with the rows
  $r'_1, \dots, r'_{D-1}$ and $r$, up to sign. Katoh and Tanigawa write
  its last row as $\sum_j \lambda_{(ac)j} s_j$, which is $-r$. The block
  is nonsingular exactly when $r(C(L'')) \neq 0$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp}).

  Now suppose that $r(C(L'')) \neq 0$. Then the $r'_i$ and $r$ span the
  linear forms on $K^D$. So for every linear form $x$ on $K^D$, the form
  $S \mapsto x(S(a) - S(c))$ lies in the row space of $R(G, p_3)$. Hence
  so does every row of $R(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$. Its rows at $vb$ and at the
  edges of $G$ that meet neither $v$ nor $a$ are rows of $R(G, p_3)$. Its
  rows at $vc$ are the forms
  \[
    s_j\bigl(S(v) - S(c)\bigr) \;=\; s_j\bigl(S(v) - S(a)\bigr)
      + s_j\bigl(S(a) - S(c)\bigr),
  \]
  sums of a row of $va$ and a form of this kind. By
  \cref{lem:splitOff-rigidityRows-relabel}, $R(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$ has the
  rank $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ of $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$, and we choose
  $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ linearly independent rows of it. They vanish on the screw
  assignments that vanish off $a$, as $a$ is not a vertex of $G_a^{vc}$.
  On these screw assignments the rows of $ac$ and $w_3$ are the forms
  $S \mapsto r'_i(S(a))$ and $S \mapsto r(S(a))$, which are linearly
  independent. By \cref{lem:case-III-conditional-block}, the
  $(D - 1) + 1 + D(|V(G)| - 2) = D(|V(G)| - 1)$ forms are linearly
  independent. They lie in the row space of $R(G, p_3)$, which gives the
  bound. Katoh and Tanigawa read the same bound off KT eq.~(6.41), as $D$
  plus the rank of the block below $M_3$, which KT eq.~(6.23) gives.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem is the independence of the last step, for any
  finite families of forms in place of the rows of $ac$ and of
  $R(G_a^{vc}, q_\rho)$, any form in place of $r$, and any hinge in place
  of $L''$. It is the pinned theorem of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement} with $a$ and $c$ in place of
  $v$ and $b$, and it does not mention $G$, $p_3$ or the rank.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[KT Claim~6.12: $r$ does not vanish on all six joins]
  \label{lem:case-III-claim612}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.case_III_claim612_gen}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals, lem:case-III-claim612-r-nonzero}
  Let $d = 3$. Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11},
  let $r$ be the linear form of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}, and let
  $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_4 \in K^4$ be linearly independent. Then
  $r(\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j) \neq 0$ for some $1 \le i < j \le 4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span, lem:case-III-claim612-orthseq-vanish}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~690--691]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  The form $r$ is not zero (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-r-nonzero}). For
  $d = 3$, the screw space $K^D$ is $\bigwedge^2 K^4$, and the six joins
  $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$ span it
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}, from KT Lemma~2.1). If $r$
  vanished on all of them, then $r$ would be zero
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-orthseq-vanish}).

  This gives KT Claim~6.12: for some choice of the lines
  $L \subset \Pi(a)$, $L' \subset \Pi(b)$ and $L'' \subset \Pi(c)$, one
  of $M_1$, $M_2$ and $M_3$ is nonsingular. Indeed, let $c$ be as at the
  start of this subsubsection, with $q(a)$, $q(b)$ and $q(c)$ linearly
  independent. Let $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_4$ be the vectors of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals} for these
  normals. Any two of them are orthogonal to a common normal $q(u)$, with
  $u \in \{a, b, c\}$. Let $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$ be a join on which
  $r$ does not vanish,
  and let $n'$ be a vector orthogonal to $\bar p_i$ and $\bar p_j$ and
  linearly independent of $q(u)$. Then $\bar p_i \vee \bar p_j$ is a
  multiple of $C(q(u), n')$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-line-in-panel-union}), so $r$ does not
  vanish on $C(q(u), n')$. This is a nonzero multiple of the extensor of
  the line in which $\Pi(u)$ meets the plane with normal $n'$ (the
  remarks after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). So the block $M_1$,
  $M_2$ or $M_3$ for this line is nonsingular, by
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-block-iff-perp} and, for $M_3$, KT
  eq.~(6.44).

  The lines are chosen after $r$, and the proof needs this freedom. The
  extensors of three fixed lines span at most three of the six dimensions
  of $\bigwedge^2 K^4$, so a nonzero $r$ can vanish on all three.
\end{proof}

  (\cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator}). The step from a join to a

  \texttt{BodyHingeFramework.\allowbreak{}exists\_\allowbreak{}complementIso\_\allowbreak{}ne\_\allowbreak{}zero\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}homogeneousIncidence},

We now prove the case $d = 3$ of Case~III. The lemma assumes a generic
full-rank realization of $G_v^{ab}$, as the induction hypothesis gives.
It also assumes the bound of KT eq.~(6.22), as
\cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower} gives.

\begin{lemma}[The case $d = 3$ of Case~III; KT Lemma~6.10]
  \label{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_III_candidate_dispatch}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rank-hypothesis, lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}
  Let $d = 3$, so that $D = 6$, and let $K$ be an infinite field. Let $G$
  be a simple $0$-dof-graph, and let $v$, $a$, $b$ and $c$ be distinct
  vertices of $G$ such that the edges of $G$ at $v$ are $va$ and $vb$,
  and those at $a$ are $va$ and $ac$. Suppose that $G_v^{ab}$ is a
  $0$-dof-graph with a generic full-rank realization
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), and that there is a
  nonzero polynomial $P$ in the normals with
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \;\ge\; D\,(|V(G_v)| - 1) - (D - 2)
  \]
  wherever $P(q) \neq 0$, as in \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}.
  Then $G$ has a generic full-rank realization, of rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim-6-11, lem:case-III-candidate-row, lem:case-III-claim612,
        lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement, lem:case-III-claim612-p3-placement,
        lem:case-II-realization-placement, lem:genericity-device}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of KT Lemma~6.10 (pp.~682--691).
  We choose the normals off the zeros of a product of polynomials fixed
  before the choice, as explained after \cref{lem:case-I-realization}.
  The first factor comes from the generic full-rank realization of
  $G_v^{ab}$ (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). The second is
  $P$, the third puts the normals in general position, and the fourth
  makes $q(a)$, $q(b)$ and $q(c)$ linearly independent. As $K$ is
  infinite, the product has a non-root $q$. The first factor gives
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v^{ab}, q) \ge D(|V(G)| - 2)$. This is also
  an upper bound (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), so
  equality holds, which is KT eq.~(6.18). The second factor gives KT
  eq.~(6.22), $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q) \ge D(|V(G_v)| - 1) - (D - 2)$.

  So the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11} hold, where
  $k' \le D - 2$ is the difference between $D(|V(G_v)| - 1)$ and
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_v, q)$. Let $r$ be the linear form of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}. It is not zero, it vanishes on $C(q(a), q(b))$, and the form
  $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$ lies in the row space of $R(G_v, q)$ (KT
  eqs.~(6.24) and~(6.25), \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}).
  Let $\bar p_1, \dots, \bar p_4$ be the vectors of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals}. By the
  proof of KT Claim~6.12 (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612}), there are
  $u \in \{a, b, c\}$ and a vector $n'$, linearly independent of $q(u)$,
  such that $r$ does not vanish on $C(q(u), n')$. This is a nonzero
  multiple of the extensor of the line $L$ in which $\Pi(u)$ meets the
  plane with normal $n'$.

  If $u = a$, then $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_1) \ge D(|V(G)| - 1)$ for
  this $L$ (\cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}). If $u = b$, the same
  holds for $(G, p_2)$ with $L' = L$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement}). If $u = c$, it holds for
  $(G, p_3)$ with $L'' = L$ (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p3-placement}).

  Let $u = a$. Katoh and Tanigawa rotate the panel of $v$ slightly (KT
  Lemma~5.2, \cref{lem:rank-rotation-semicont}), and we rotate it about
  $L$. For $t \in K$, let $F_t$ be the body-hinge framework on $G$ whose
  hinges have the extensors $C(q(a), n')$ at $va$,
  $C(q(a) + t\,n', q(b))$ at $vb$, and those of $(G_v^{ab}, q)$ at the
  edges of $G_v$. Then $F_0$ has the rank of $(G, p_1)$. Let $q_t$ agree
  with $q$ except that $q_t(v) = q(a) + t\,n'$, whose panel contains
  $L$. For $t \neq 0$, the extensor of the hinge of $va$ in $(G, q_t)$ is
  $C(q(a) + t\,n', q(a)) = -t\,C(q(a), n')$, so $(G, q_t)$ has the rank
  of $F_t$. The rows of $F_t$ can be chosen polynomial in $t$, as in the
  proof of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. So the rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ at $t = 0$ persists for all but finitely many $t$, as
  in the proof of \cref{lem:rank-rotation-semicont}. For all but at most
  one $t$, the normals $q(a) + t\,n'$ and $q(b)$ are linearly
  independent. As $K$ is infinite, some $t \neq 0$ gives a panel-hinge
  framework $(G, q_t)$ with nondegenerate hinges and rank at least
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$. For $u = b$ we rotate the panel of $v$ from $\Pi(b)$
  about $L$. For $u = c$ the panel of $v$ is $\Pi(a)$, and we rotate the
  panel of $a$ from $\Pi(c)$ about $L$.

  Finally, as in the proof of \cref{lem:case-II-realization}, there are
  normals in general position at which the rank is at least
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). The rank
  there is at most $D(|V(G)| - 1)$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). So $G$ has a generic
  full-rank realization of this rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal proof treats the three cases by one theorem: the rank bound

  followed by the rotation. For $u = b$ it applies this theorem with $a$
  and $b$ exchanged. For $u = c$ it applies it with $a$, $c$ and $v$ in
  the roles of $v$, $a$ and $b$, to the normals $q_\rho$ of
  \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel} and the form $-r$, with $G - a$
  in place of $G_v$. There it uses the fourth pinned theorem of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}. So it does not use
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement} and
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p3-placement}. The pinned theorem also
  assumes that the label of the new edge $ab$ lies outside $E(G)$
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). It asks for $P$ for every endpoint
  selector that assigns each edge of $G_v^{ab}$ its ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

\subsubsection{The case of general \texorpdfstring{$d$}{d}: KT Lemma~6.13 (KT \S6.4.2)}

Katoh and Tanigawa prove Case~III for every $d$ as KT
Lemma~6.13~\cite[pp.~692--698]{katohTanigawa2011}. Let $G$ be a
minimal $0$-dof-graph with at least three vertices and no proper rigid
subgraph, so that $G$ is simple (\cref{lem:simple-minimal-noRigid}).
If $|V(G)| = 3$, then $G$ is a triangle, and Katoh and Tanigawa
realize it by KT Lemma~5.4, as the cycle on three vertices (KT
Lemma~6.7(i), p.~677). Their proof for $|V(G)| \ge 4$ splits off a
vertex $v$ of degree two, with neighbours $a$ and $b$, and uses a
generic realization of $G_v^{ab}$, whose panels are in general position
(KT pp.~682 and~691). The induction hypothesis gives one when
$G_v^{ab}$ is simple, which holds for $|V(G)| \ge 4$ (KT Lemma~6.7(ii),
\cref{lem:splitoff-simple}). For the triangle, $G_v^{ab}$ has the
two vertices $a$ and $b$ and two edges that join them. It is not
simple, and the induction hypothesis gives it only a
nondegenerate-hinge panel realization, whose two panels may coincide,
as in KT Lemma~5.3.

If $|V(G)| \ge 4$, then by KT Lemma~4.6 either $G$ is a cycle on at
most $d$ vertices, which KT Lemma~5.4 realizes, or $G$ has a chain
$v_0 v_1 \cdots v_d$ of length $d$ (\cref{lem:chain-data-extract}).
For $d \le 3$ the first alternative does not arise, as $G$ has at
least four vertices. For the second, Katoh and Tanigawa note that
``the proof strategy is exactly the same as $d = 3$'' (p.~692). They
split off once, at $v_1$ along $v_0 v_2$. The induction hypothesis
gives a generic full-rank realization of $G_1 = G_{v_1}^{v_0 v_2}$,
and KT Claim~6.11 gives the form $r$ of KT eq.~(6.52) at its new edge
$v_0 v_2$, as for $d = 3$. There are $d$ frameworks, one for each of
the panels of $v_0, v_2, v_3, \dots, v_d$. The first two put $v_1$
back, as for $d = 3$. For $2 \le i \le d - 1$, the $i$-th puts $v_i$
back into the graph $G_i = G_{v_i}^{v_{i-1} v_{i+1}}$. An isomorphism
from $G_i$ to $G_1$ renames the bodies, so the realization of $G_1$
serves for every $G_i$, and the induction hypothesis is used once (KT
eqs.~(6.54) and~(6.55)). For the $i$-th framework, $r$ is read from
the linear relation of KT eq.~(6.52) at the edge $v_i v_{i+1}$ of
$G_1$, not at $v_0 v_2$. It appears there up to sign, because
$v_2, \dots, v_{d-1}$ have degree two (KT eq.~(6.66)). Finally, the
span of KT eq.~(6.67), for $d + 1$ points in $K^{d+1}$, shows that one
of the $d$ frameworks has a nonsingular block, as for $d = 3$.

We first identify the triangle. Like Katoh and Tanigawa, we then
realize it as the cycle on three vertices, by
\cref{lem:cycle-realization} with $m = 3$ (in the proof of
\cref{lem:case-III}).

\begin{lemma}[A minimal $0$-dof-graph on three vertices is a triangle; from the proof of KT Lemma~6.7(i)]
  \label{lem:triangle-third-edge}
  \lean{Graph.exists_isLink_of_isMinimalKDof_card_three}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:k-dof}
  Let $d \ge 2$, and let $G$ be a simple minimal $0$-dof-graph with
  three vertices. Let $e_a \neq e_b$ be edges of $G$ that join a vertex
  $v$ to vertices $a$ and $b$. Then $a \neq b$, $V(G) = \{v, a, b\}$,
  and an edge of $G$ joins $a$ and $b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:def-eq-corank}
  As $G$ is simple, it has no loops, so $a \neq v$ and $b \neq v$. Two
  distinct edges of $G$ do not join the same two vertices, so
  $a \neq b$. The three distinct vertices $v$, $a$ and $b$ are then all
  the vertices of $G$.

  As $G$ is a $0$-dof-graph,
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) = D(|V(G)| - 1) = 2D$ (KT
  eq.~(2.4), \cref{thm:def-eq-corank} and \cref{lem:rigid-full-rank}).
  The ground set of $M(\tilde G)$ has $D - 1$ copies of each edge of $G$
  (\cref{def:edge-multiply}), so $(D - 1)\,|E(G)| \ge 2D > 2(D - 1)$.
  Hence $G$ has an edge $f$ other than $e_a$ and $e_b$. Its ends are two
  distinct vertices among $v$, $a$ and $b$. As $G$ is simple, $f$ does
  not join $v$ and $a$, which $e_a$ joins, nor $v$ and $b$. So $f$ joins
  $a$ and $b$.
\end{proof}

The triangle can also be realized with three chosen normals, as in the
proof of \cref{lem:cycle-realization} for $m = 3$. The proof of
\cref{lem:case-III} does not use this realization.

\begin{lemma}[Realizing a triangle; KT Lemma~6.7(i)]
  \label{lem:triangle-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.hasGenericFullRankRealization_of_triangle}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:triangle-third-edge, lem:theorem-55-triangle, lem:triangle-normals}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a simple
  minimal $0$-dof-graph with three vertices. Let $e_a \neq e_b$ be edges
  of $G$ that join a vertex $v$ to vertices $a$ and $b$. Then $G$ has a
  generic full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), of rank
  $2D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:triangle-third-edge, lem:theorem-55-triangle, lem:triangle-normals,
        lem:genericity-device}
  By \cref{lem:triangle-third-edge}, $V(G) = \{v, a, b\}$, and an edge
  $f$ joins $a$ and $b$. Let $n_0$, $n_1$ and $n_2$ be the normals of
  \cref{lem:triangle-normals}, and give $v$, $a$ and $b$ the normals
  $n_0$, $n_1$ and $n_2$. Up to sign, the hinges of $e_a$, $f$ and
  $e_b$ are $C(n_0, n_1)$, $C(n_1, n_2)$ and $C(n_2, n_0)$
  (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), and these are linearly
  independent. By \cref{lem:theorem-55-triangle}, every infinitesimal
  motion is constant on $\{v, a, b\} = V(G)$. As
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, the realization is at the target
  rank $2D$ (\cref{rem:rank-hypothesis-relative}). The joins
  $n_0 \vee n_1$, $n_1 \vee n_2$ and $n_2 \vee n_0$ are nonzero, so any
  two of the three normals are linearly independent, and the normals
  are in general position (\cref{def:panel-general-position}). Hence
  the realization is a generic full-rank realization of $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  As in the note after \cref{lem:cycle-realization}, the formal
  framework also has bodies outside $V(G)$. The pinned theorem gives
  them the normal $0$, and then chooses normals in general position for
  all the bodies, at which the rank is still $2D$.
\end{fmlnote}

Write $u_0 = v_0$ and $u_i = v_{i+1}$ for $1 \le i \le d - 1$, so that
the panels $\Pi(u_0), \dots, \Pi(u_{d-1})$ are Katoh and Tanigawa's
$\Pi_0, \dots, \Pi_{d-1}$ (KT p.~698). The next lemma realizes $G_1$,
gives the form $r$, and chooses one of these panels. The lemma after it
builds the framework for this panel.

\begin{lemma}[The form $r$ and the choice of a panel; KT eqs.~(6.65)--(6.67)]
  \label{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.chainData_fire_discriminator}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:chain-data, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a simple
  minimal $0$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$. Let $v_0 v_1 \cdots v_d$ be
  a chain of length $d$ in $G$ (\cref{def:chain-data}), let
  $G_1 = G_{v_1}^{v_0 v_2}$, and let $u_0 = v_0$ and $u_i = v_{i+1}$ for
  $1 \le i \le d - 1$. Suppose that $G_1$ is a $0$-dof-graph with a
  generic full-rank realization, and that for every $k'$, every minimal
  $k'$-dof-graph with at least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices has
  a nondegenerate-hinge panel realization, and a generic full-rank
  realization if it is simple. Then there are normals $q$, a nonzero
  linear form $r$ on $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ with
  $r(C(q(v_0), q(v_2))) = 0$, an index $0 \le i \le d - 1$ and a vector
  $n' \in K^{d+1}$, linearly independent of $q(u_i)$, such that
  $r(C(q(u_i), n')) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-claim-6-11, lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower,
        lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span,
        lem:case-III-claim612-orthseq-vanish, lem:case-III-claim612,
        lem:meet-two-hyperplanes}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~692--698]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  As $d \ge 2$, the vertex $v_1$ is interior, so its only edges join it
  to $v_0$ and to $v_2$ (\cref{def:chain-data}). The vertices
  $u_0, \dots, u_{d-1}$ are distinct.

  We choose the normals $q$ off the zeros of a product of four nonzero
  polynomials, fixed before the choice, as explained after
  \cref{lem:case-I-realization} and in the remark on KT footnote~6 after
  \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower}. The first factor is the
  polynomial that \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank} gives for the
  generic full-rank realization of $G_1$. The second is the polynomial
  $P$ of \cref{lem:case-III-nested-rank-lower} for $G$ and its vertex
  $v_1$. The third puts the normals in general position. The fourth is
  a $d \times d$ minor of the matrix with the rows
  $q(u_0), \dots, q(u_{d-1})$, which is a nonzero polynomial as the
  $u_l$ are distinct. As $K$ is infinite, the product has a non-root $q$.

  The first factor gives
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G_1, q) \ge D(|V(G)| - 2)$. This is also an
  upper bound (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), so equality
  holds, which is KT eq.~(6.46). The second factor gives KT eq.~(6.22)
  for $G - v_1$, and the third makes the hinge of the new edge
  $v_0 v_2$ nondegenerate. So the hypotheses of KT Claim~6.11
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim-6-11}) hold, with $v_1$, $v_0$ and $v_2$ in
  the roles of $v$, $a$ and $b$. Let $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$ be the
  linear form of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row-construction}, which
  is Katoh and Tanigawa's $r$ (KT p.~698). It is not zero, it vanishes
  on $C(q(v_0), q(v_2))$, and the form $S \mapsto r(S(v_0) - S(v_2))$
  lies in the row space of $R(G - v_1, q)$ (KT eqs.~(6.51)
  and~(6.52), \cref{lem:case-III-redundant-decomposition}).

  Katoh and Tanigawa take a point $p_d$ on all the panels
  $\Pi(u_0), \dots, \Pi(u_{d-1})$, and for $0 \le l \le d - 1$ a point
  $p_l$ on all of them but $\Pi(u_l)$ (KT p.~698). As for $d = 3$, we
  take them in homogeneous coordinates. The normals
  $q(u_0), \dots, q(u_{d-1})$ are linearly independent, by the fourth
  factor. So there are linearly independent
  $\bar p_0, \dots, \bar p_d \in K^{d+1}$ such that $\bar p_d$ is
  orthogonal to every $q(u_{l'})$, and, for $0 \le l \le d - 1$,
  $\bar p_l$ is orthogonal to $q(u_{l'})$ exactly when $l' \neq l$. The
  proof of \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-points-affineIndep-real-normals}
  gives them, with $d$ normals in place of three (the note after it).

  For $0 \le l < m \le d$, let $E_{lm}$ be the join of the $d - 1$
  vectors $\bar p_{l'}$ with $l' \neq l, m$. These $D$ extensors span
  $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$, by KT Lemma~2.1 (the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-extensor-span}; KT eq.~(6.67)). As
  $r \neq 0$, it does not vanish on some $E_{lm}$
  (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-orthseq-vanish}). This is KT Claim~6.12
  in the form for every $d$ (the note after
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612}).

  Let $i = l$. As $l < m \le d$, the index $i$ is at most $d - 1$, and
  the $d - 1$ vectors $\bar p_{l'}$ with $l' \neq l, m$ are orthogonal to
  $q(u_i)$. They are linearly independent, so their span has an
  orthogonal complement of dimension $2$, which contains $q(u_i)$. Let
  $n'$ be a vector of this complement that is linearly independent of
  $q(u_i)$. Then the $d - 1$ vectors are orthogonal to $q(u_i)$ and to
  $n'$, so $E_{lm}$ is a nonzero multiple of $C(q(u_i), n')$ (the
  remarks after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}, and
  \cref{lem:meet-two-hyperplanes}, of which
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-line-in-panel-union} is the case $d = 3$).
  Hence $r(C(q(u_i), n')) \neq 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem gives more than the statement, and
  \cref{lem:case-III-chain-dispatch} uses it. The normals $q$ are in
  general position. The form $S \mapsto r(S(v_0) - S(v_2))$ lies in the
  row space of $R(G - v_1, q)$, and it is a combination of the rows of
  the edges of $G - v_1$, grouped by edge. The form $r$ is
  $\sum_j \lambda_j r_j$ for forms $r_j$ that give the rows of
  $v_0 v_2$. The pinned theorem also gives $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ linearly
  independent rows of $R(G_1, q)$, and an endpoint selector that assigns
  each edge of $G_1$ its ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Case~III for a chain of length $d$; from the proof of KT Lemma~6.13]
  \label{lem:case-III-chain-dispatch}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.chainData_dispatch}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator}, $G$
  has a generic full-rank realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), of
  rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator, lem:case-II-realization-placement,
        lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel, lem:splitOff-rigidityRows-relabel,
        lem:genericity-device}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~692--698]{katohTanigawa2011}.
  Let $q$, $r = \sum_j \lambda_j r_j$, $i$ and $n'$ be as in
  \cref{lem:case-III-chain-discriminator} and its proof, with the linear
  relation of KT eq.~(6.52) among the rows of $R(G_1, q)$. The extensor
  $C(q(u_i), n')$ is a nonzero multiple of the extensor of the
  $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace $L_i$ in which $\Pi(u_i)$ meets
  the hyperplane with normal $n'$ (the remarks after
  \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). So $r(C(L_i)) \neq 0$. Katoh and
  Tanigawa's $i$-th framework $(G, p_i)$ has the hinge $L_i$, and its
  rank is at least $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ when $r$ does not vanish on $C(L_i)$,
  that is, when its block $M_i$ is nonsingular (KT p.~697). We show this
  first for $i \le 1$ and then for $i \ge 2$.

  Let $i = 0$ or $i = 1$. Katoh and Tanigawa's $(G, p_0)$ is the
  framework of KT eq.~(6.12) with $v_1$, $v_0$ and $v_2$ in the roles of
  $v$, $a$ and $b$, and with $L = L_0$. Their $(G, p_1)$ is the
  framework of KT eq.~(6.19) for the same roles, with $L' = L_1$ (KT
  eqs.~(6.47) and~(6.48)). By \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row} and
  \cref{lem:case-III-claim612-p2-placement}, their ranks are at least
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ when $r$ does not vanish on $C(L_0)$, respectively
  $C(L_1)$ (KT eqs.~(6.50) and~(6.53)).

  Now let $2 \le i \le d - 1$, so that $d \ge 3$. Katoh and Tanigawa
  split off at $v_i$ instead, along $v_{i-1} v_{i+1}$. Their isomorphism
  $\rho_i$ from $G_i = G_{v_i}^{v_{i-1} v_{i+1}}$ to $G_1$ maps $v_l$ to
  $v_{l+1}$ for $1 \le l \le i - 1$ and fixes the other vertices (KT
  eq.~(6.54)). It maps the new edge $v_{i-1} v_{i+1}$ of $G_i$ to the
  edge $v_i v_{i+1}$ of $G_1$. So it maps $G - v_i$, which is $G_i$
  without its new edge, onto $G_1$ without $v_i v_{i+1}$. Let
  $q_i = q \circ \rho_i$. Then $(G_i, q_i)$ is $(G_1, q)$ with its
  bodies renamed, ``exactly the same framework'' (KT eq.~(6.55)), and
  $R(G_i, q_i)$ has the rank $D(|V(G)| - 2)$ of $R(G_1, q)$.

  For an edge $e$ of $G_1$, let $r_{e1}, \dots, r_{e(D-1)}$ be the
  linear forms on $K^D$ that give its rows, and let
  $\psi_e = \sum_j \lambda_{ej} r_{ej}$, with the coefficients of KT
  eq.~(6.52). Then $\psi_e$ vanishes on the hinge $q(e)$, and
  $\psi_{v_0 v_2} = r$. The relation says that the forms
  $S \mapsto \psi_e(S(x) - S(y))$, for the edges $e$ of $G_1$ with ends
  $x$ and $y$, sum to zero. On the screw assignments that vanish off a
  vertex $u$, it says that the forms $\psi_e$ of the edges at $u$ sum to
  zero, with signs given by the orientations. For $2 \le l \le d - 1$,
  the vertex $v_l$ has two edges in $G_1$: the edge $v_{l-1} v_l$, or
  the new edge $v_0 v_2$ when $l = 2$, and the edge $v_l v_{l+1}$. So
  $\psi_{v_l v_{l+1}}$ is $\pm$ the form of the other edge, and by
  induction on $l$,
  \[
    \psi_{v_l v_{l+1}} \;=\; \pm r \qquad (2 \le l \le d - 1),
  \]
  which is KT eq.~(6.66). For $d = 3$ and $l = 2$ this is KT
  eq.~(6.44) (\cref{lem:case-III-claim612-eq644}).

  This has two consequences at $l = i$. First, $r$ vanishes on the
  hinge $q(v_i v_{i+1})$ of $(G_1, q)$, which is the hinge of the new
  edge $v_{i-1} v_{i+1}$ of $(G_i, q_i)$. Second, the term of the
  relation at $v_i v_{i+1}$ is the form
  $S \mapsto \pm r(S(v_i) - S(v_{i+1}))$, so this form lies in the span
  of the rows of $R(G_1, q)$ at the other edges. Renamed by $\rho_i$, the
  form $S \mapsto r(S(v_{i-1}) - S(v_{i+1}))$ lies in the row space of
  $R(G - v_i, q_i)$. Katoh and Tanigawa make this step with the row
  correspondence of KT eq.~(6.62) and the row operations of KT
  eq.~(6.63). The new row of the $i$-th framework thus comes from the
  coefficients of the relation at the edge $v_i v_{i+1}$ of $G_1$, an
  edge that depends on $i$.

  Katoh and Tanigawa's $(G, p_i)$ is the framework of KT eq.~(6.12)
  built from $(G_i, q_i)$, with $v_i$, $v_{i+1}$ and $v_{i-1}$ in the
  roles of $v$, $a$ and $b$, and with $L = L_i$, which lies in the panel
  $\Pi(v_{i+1})$ of $v_{i+1}$ in $(G_i, q_i)$ (KT eqs.~(6.57)
  and~(6.58)). The proof of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row} applies to
  it with $-r$ in place of $r$. There we used only that $r$ vanishes on
  the hinge of the new edge $ab$, that $S \mapsto r(S(a) - S(b))$ lies in
  the row space of $R(G_v, q)$, and that $R(G_v^{ab}, q)$ has rank
  $D(|V(G)| - 2)$. Here these hold by the two consequences and by the
  rank of $R(G_i, q_i)$. So $\operatorname{rank} R(G, p_i) \ge
  D(|V(G)| - 1)$ when $r$ does not vanish on $C(L_i)$ (KT eq.~(6.64)
  and p.~697).

  In each case the vertex put back, $v_1$ or $v_i$, has the panel
  $\Pi(u_i)$ in $(G, p_i)$, which contains $L_i$ (KT pp.~692 and~694).
  As in the proof of \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}, we
  rotate this panel about $L_i$ (KT Lemma~5.2, \cref{lem:rank-rotation-semicont}).
  This gives a panel-hinge framework on $G$ with nondegenerate hinges
  and rank at least $D(|V(G)| - 1)$. Finally, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:case-II-realization}, there are normals in general position
  at which the rank is at least $D(|V(G)| - 1)$
  (\cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}). The rank there is at most
  $D(|V(G)| - 1)$ (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}). So $G$
  has a generic full-rank realization of this rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  As for \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}, the formal proof
  treats every framework by one theorem: the rank bound for the
  framework of KT eq.~(6.12) (the note after
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-row}), followed by the rotation. For
  $i = 1$ it applies this theorem with $v_0$ and $v_2$ exchanged. For
  $i \ge 2$ it applies it with $v_i$, $v_{i+1}$ and $v_{i-1}$ in the
  roles of $v$, $a$ and $b$, to the normals $q_i$ and the form $-r$, with
  $G - v_i$ in place of $G_v$. So it does not use
  \cref{lem:case-II-realization-placement}. For $d = 3$ and $i = 2$, the
  isomorphism $\rho_2$ is that of \cref{lem:splitOff-isLink-relabel}, and
  the renaming of the normals and the rows is that of
  \cref{lem:splitOff-ofNormals-relabel} and
  \cref{lem:splitOff-rigidityRows-relabel}. The formal proof does not
  use these lemmas either. It renames by a permutation of the bodies
  that extends $\rho_i$ and sends $v_i$ to $v_1$, and no node pins the
  steps that use it.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Case~III: no proper rigid subgraph and $k = 0$; KT Lemma~6.13]
  \label{lem:case-III}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_III_realization_all_k,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.case_III_realization}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rank-hypothesis,
        lem:simple-minimal-noRigid, lem:reduction-step}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G$ be a simple
  minimal $0$-dof-graph with $|V(G)| \ge 3$ and no proper rigid
  subgraph. Suppose that for every $k'$, every minimal $k'$-dof-graph
  with at least one and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices has a
  nondegenerate-hinge panel realization, and a generic full-rank
  realization if it is simple. Then $G$ has a generic full-rank
  realization (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), of rank $D(|V(G)| - 1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:triangle-third-edge, lem:chain-data-extract,
        lem:cycle-realization, def:cycle-data,
        lem:simple-minimal-noRigid, lem:reduction-step,
        lem:case-III-chain-dispatch, lem:low-degree-vertex}
  We follow Katoh and Tanigawa's proof of KT
  Lemma~6.13~\cite[p.~692]{katohTanigawa2011}, with the graphs on three
  vertices treated first, as in KT Lemma~6.7(i) (p.~677).

  Let $|V(G)| = 3$. We need a single vertex $v$ of degree two, and it
  exists for every $d \ge 2$. Indeed, some vertex $v$ has degree at most
  two (\cref{lem:low-degree-vertex}). As a $0$-dof-graph, $G$ is
  $2$-edge-connected (KT Lemma~3.1, \cref{lem:two-edge-conn}), so $v$
  has degree two. Let $e_a$ and $e_b$ be the edges of $v$, which join it
  to vertices $a$ and $b$. By
  \cref{lem:triangle-third-edge}, $V(G) = \{v, a, b\}$, and an edge $f$
  joins $a$ and $b$. As $G$ is simple, $e_a$, $e_b$ and $f$ are its only
  edges, so $G$ is a cycle on three vertices (\cref{def:cycle-data}). As
  $3 \le d + 1$, \cref{lem:cycle-realization} gives a generic full-rank
  realization of $G$.

  Let $|V(G)| \ge 4$. By \cref{lem:chain-data-extract}, $G$ is a cycle
  on at most $d$ vertices, or $G$ has a chain $v_0 v_1 \cdots v_d$ of
  length $d$ such that $G_1 = G_{v_1}^{v_0 v_2}$ is a simple minimal
  $0$-dof-graph with at least two and fewer than $|V(G)|$ vertices. In
  the first case, which arises only for $d \ge 4$,
  \cref{lem:cycle-realization} gives the realization, as
  $|V(G)| \le d$. In the second case, the induction hypothesis gives
  $G_1$ a generic full-rank realization, and
  \cref{lem:case-III-chain-dispatch} gives one of $G$. For $d = 3$ the
  chain is $b\,v\,a\,c$ in the notation of
  \cref{lem:case-III-candidate-dispatch-d3}, whose three frameworks are
  the $d$ frameworks of this case.
\end{proof}

  the new edge $v_0 v_2$ (the note after \cref{def:chain-data}). The
