\section{The generic bar-joint rigidity matroid}
\label{sec:bar-joint-3d}

Jord\'an~\cite{jordan2016} (\S1.3.1, p.~39) defines the
$d$-dimensional rigidity matroid $\mathcal{R}_d(G)$ of a graph $G$ as
the rigidity matroid of a generic framework of $G$, one whose
coordinates satisfy no algebraic dependency (p.~36), and writes
$r_d(G)$ for its rank. At $d = 3$, $r_3$ is the rank function $r$ of
KT Corollary~5.7: for a graph $G$ of minimum degree at least two,
\[
  r(G^2) \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
\]
where the square $G^2$ joins every two vertices at distance one or two
in $G$ (\cref{def:square-graph}), and $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is
the deficiency of \cref{def:D-deficiency} at $D = 6$. Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008} proved the inequality $\le$ (their
Theorem~4.1), and showed that equality is equivalent to the molecular
conjecture (their Theorem~4.3). We prove the formula as
\cref{cor:molecule-rank-formula}. In this chapter we construct the
matroid and its rank function, in every dimension $d$.

In the plane we needed no notion of genericity
(\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-linear-framing}). There we found one
framework at which every $(2,3)$-sparse edge set is row-independent,
using Pollaczek-Geiringer's and Laman's characterization of these edge
sets as the ones that are row-independent at some framework. No
combinatorial characterization of this kind is known in dimension three
or more (Jord\'an, p.~39; KT~\S7), and none is needed here. We work
with placements that are generic for row independence: every edge set
that is row-independent at some placement is row-independent at them.
Such placements exist in every dimension, by the argument of the planar case
(\cref{sec:bar-joint-3d-generic-placement}). We define the matroid
$\mathcal{R}_d(V)$ on the edges of the complete graph $K_V$ as the
linear rigidity matroid at one of them. Its
independent sets are the edge sets that are row-independent at some
placement, so it does not depend on the placement, and in the plane it
is the planar rigidity matroid. Finally, for a graph $H$ on $V$, the
rank of $E(H)$ in $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$ is the rank of the rigidity map
of $H$ at any generic placement (\cref{sec:bar-joint-3d-matroid}).

Throughout, $V$ is a finite set and $d \in \N$. As in
\cref{sec:frameworks}, a placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$ is a framework
on $V$ in $\R^{d}$, and $R(G, p)$ is the rigidity map. The rigidity
rows $r_e$ and row-independent edge sets are defined in
\cref{sec:laman-theorem}.

\subsection{Generic placements}
\label{sec:bar-joint-3d-generic-placement}

Jackson and Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008} (\S2) call a framework
generic when its rigidity matrix and all of its submatrices have the
largest rank among all placements of the graph. The entries of the
rigidity matrix are polynomials with integer coefficients in the
coordinates (Jord\'an, p.~39), so a placement whose coordinates are
algebraically independent over $\Q$ is generic in this sense. We ask
this of the rigidity matrix of $K_V$ and its submatrices formed by sets
of rows: every set of edges of $K_V$ that is row-independent at some
placement must be row-independent at the generic one. This is all that
the construction of the matroid uses, and placements with this
property exist by an argument that needs no algebraic independence.

\begin{definition}[Generic placement for row independence]
  \label{def:generic-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGenericPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $p$ be a placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$. We say that $p$ is
  \emph{generic for row independence} if every set
  $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ that is row-independent at some placement of $V$
  in $\R^{d}$ is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}, at $G = K_V$).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Existence of generic placements]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.exists_isGenericPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite and $d \in \N$. Then there is a placement of $V$ in
  $\R^{d}$ that is generic for row independence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of sets $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ that are
  row-independent at some placement. It is finite, since $E(K_V)$ is,
  and a placement at which every member of $\mathcal{F}$ is
  row-independent is generic. We find one as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:exists-uniform-rowIndependent-placement}. There each
  $(2,3)$-sparse edge set is row-independent at some framework by
  Pollaczek-Geiringer's and Laman's characterization
  (\cref{thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse}); here each member of
  $\mathcal{F}$ is row-independent at some placement by definition.

  We show, by induction on $|\mathcal{F}'|$, that for every
  $\mathcal{F}' \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ there is a placement at which
  every member of $\mathcal{F}'$ is row-independent. For
  $\mathcal{F}' = \emptyset$ any placement will do. Otherwise let
  $I_0 \in \mathcal{F}'$, let $p_0$ be a placement at which every
  member of $\mathcal{F}' \setminus \{I_0\}$ is row-independent, and
  let $q$ be a placement at which $I_0$ is row-independent. For
  $t \in \R$, let $p^{t} = p_0 + t\,(q - p_0)$. The rigidity row of an
  edge $uv$ at a placement $p'$ is
  $x \mapsto \langle p'_u - p'_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which depends linearly on $p'$.

  Let $I$ be a member of $\mathcal{F}' \setminus \{I_0\}$, or $I_0$,
  and let $t_0$ be $0$ or $1$ accordingly. A set of edges is
  row-independent at a placement exactly when its rigidity rows there
  are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow}).
  Fix a basis of $((\R^{d})^{V})^{*}$. The coordinate vectors of the
  rows at $p^{t}$ of the edges in $I$ are the rows of a matrix
  $A + t\,B$, where $A$ and $B$ do not depend on $t$. These rows are
  linearly independent at $t_0$, so some $|I| \times |I|$ minor of
  $A + t_0 B$ is nonzero. The same minor of $A + t\,B$ is a polynomial
  in $t$ that is nonzero at $t_0$, so it has finitely many roots. At
  every other $t$, the rows at $p^{t}$ of the edges in $I$ are linearly
  independent. Choose $t$ outside the roots of these finitely many
  polynomials, one for each member of $\mathcal{F}'$. Then every member
  of $\mathcal{F}'$ is row-independent at $p^{t}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The matroid and its rank function}
\label{sec:bar-joint-3d-matroid}

In the plane we defined $\mathcal{R}(V)$ by counting, and showed that
the linear rigidity matroid at one framework equals it
(\cref{thm:linearRigidityMatroid-eq-rigidityMatroid}). In general
dimension there is no count to start from, and we take the linear
rigidity matroid at a generic placement as the definition.

\begin{definition}[The generic rigidity matroid $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$]
  \label{def:genericRigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.genericRigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:linearRigidityMatroid, lem:exists-generic-placement}
  Fix a placement $p$ of $V$ in $\R^{d}$ that is generic for row
  independence (\cref{lem:exists-generic-placement}). The
  \emph{generic rigidity matroid} $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$ is the linear
  rigidity matroid $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ at $p$
  (\cref{def:linearRigidityMatroid}), a matroid on $E(K_V)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Independence in $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$]
  \label{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.genericRigidityMatroid_indep_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRigidityMatroid, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $I \subseteq E(K_V)$. Then $I$ is independent in
  $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$ if and only if $I$ is row-independent at some
  placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff, def:generic-placement}
  Let $p$ be the placement in the definition of $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$. A
  set is independent in $\mathcal{R}_d(V) = \mathcal{L}_p(V)$ if and
  only if it is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}). If $I$ is
  row-independent at $p$, then it is row-independent at some placement,
  namely $p$. Conversely, if $I$ is row-independent at some
  placement, then it is row-independent at $p$, since $p$ is generic
  (\cref{def:generic-placement}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Placement independence]
  \label{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-eq-genericRigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.linearRigidityMatroid_eq_genericRigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRigidityMatroid, def:generic-placement}
  Let $p$ be a placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$ that is generic for row
  independence. Then $\mathcal{L}_p(V) = \mathcal{R}_d(V)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff,
    lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}
  Both matroids have ground set $E(K_V)$, so it suffices to show that
  they have the same independent sets. A set $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ is
  independent in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ if and only if it is
  row-independent at $p$ (\cref{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}).
  Since $p$ is generic, this holds if and only if $I$ is
  row-independent at some placement, that is, if and only if $I$ is
  independent in $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$
  (\cref{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dimension two: the planar rigidity matroid]
  \label{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-two-eq-rigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.genericRigidityMatroid_two_eq_rigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRigidityMatroid, def:rigidityMatroid}
  The generic rigidity matroid $\mathcal{R}_2(V)$ is the planar
  rigidity matroid $\mathcal{R}(V)$, the $(2,3)$-count matroid on $V$
  (\cref{def:rigidityMatroid}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff,
    thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}
  Both matroids have ground set $E(K_V)$. A set $I \subseteq E(K_V)$
  is independent in $\mathcal{R}_2(V)$ if and only if it is
  row-independent at some placement in $\R^{2}$
  (\cref{lem:genericRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}). The same holds in
  $\mathcal{R}(V)$ (\cref{thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}),
  so the two matroids are equal.
\end{proof}

Jord\'an's $r_d(G)$ is the rank of the rigidity matroid of a generic
framework of $G$ (p.~39). The rows of $R(G, p)$ are the rows of
$R(K_V, p)$ indexed by $E(G)$, so this matroid is the restriction of
the rigidity matroid of $K_V$ to $E(G)$. We therefore define the rank of
a graph on $V$ through $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$.

\begin{definition}[Generic rank]
  \label{def:genericRank}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.genericRank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRigidityMatroid}
  Let $H$ be a graph on $V$. The \emph{generic rank} $r_d(H)$ of $H$ in
  dimension $d$ is the rank of $E(H)$ in $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Row-space form of the generic rank]
  \label{lem:genericRank-eq-finrank-span}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.genericRank_eq_finrank_span}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genericRank, def:generic-placement, def:rigidityRow}
  Let $p$ be a placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$ that is generic for row
  independence, and let $H$ be a graph on $V$. Then
  \[
    r_d(H) \;=\; \dim \operatorname{span}
      \{\, r_e \mid e \in E(H) \,\},
  \]
  where $r_e$ is the rigidity row of $K_V$ at $p$ indexed by $e$
  (\cref{def:rigidityRow}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-eq-genericRigidityMatroid,
    lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff,
    lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}
  The matroid $\mathcal{R}_d(V)$ is $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$
  (\cref{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-eq-genericRigidityMatroid}). It is
  represented by the extended rigidity rows $\tilde r_e$ at $p$
  (\cref{def:linearRigidityMatroid}), and $\tilde r_e = r_e$ for every
  edge $e$ of $K_V$ (\cref{def:linearRigidityRow}). In a represented
  matroid, the vectors of a set span a space whose dimension is the
  rank of the set (\cref{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}). For the
  set $E(H)$, this is the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The rank of the rigidity map at a generic placement]
  \label{lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.finrank_range_rigidityMap_eq_genericRank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement, def:genericRank, def:rigidityMap}
  Let $p$ be a placement of $V$ in $\R^{d}$ that is generic for row
  independence, and let $H$ be a graph on $V$. Then
  $\operatorname{rank} R(H, p) = r_d(H)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:genericRank-eq-finrank-span, lem:span-range-rigidityRow}
  The map $R(H, p)$ has the same rank as its transpose, whose image is
  spanned by the rigidity rows of $H$ at $p$
  (\cref{lem:span-range-rigidityRow}). For an edge $uv$ of $H$, the
  rigidity row of $H$ at $uv$ is
  $x \mapsto \langle p_u - p_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which is also the row of $K_V$ at
  $uv$. So the rank of $R(H, p)$ is the dimension of the span in
  \cref{lem:genericRank-eq-finrank-span}, which is $r_d(H)$.
\end{proof}
