\section{Matroid union and Edmonds' partition theorem}
\label{sec:matroid-union}

This chapter defines the union of finitely many matroids on a common
finite set, describes its independent sets, and computes its rank. The
rank formula is the matroid-union theorem of
Nash-Williams~\cite{nashWilliams1966} and Edmonds. For $k$ copies of
one matroid $M$, its independence criterion is Edmonds'
matroid-partition theorem~\cite[Theorem~1]{edmonds1965}
(\cref{thm:matroid-partition}): a set can be partitioned into $k$
independent sets of $M$ if and only if every subset $Y$ of it has at
most $k\, r_M(Y)$ elements. We follow Oxley's
presentation~\cite[\S\S11.1--11.3]{oxley2011}.

In \cref{sec:matroid-union-submodular} we show that a non-decreasing
integer-valued submodular function $f$ on the subsets of a finite set
$E$ induces a matroid $M_f$ on $E$, and we compute the rank function of
$M_f$ when $f(\emptyset) = 0$. In \cref{sec:matroid-union-union} we
define the union $\bigcup_j M_j$ of matroids $M_1, \dots, M_k$ on $E$
as the matroid induced from their direct sum by a bipartite graph. Its
independent sets are the sets $I_1 \cup \dots \cup I_k$ with each $I_j$
independent in $M_j$. By Rado's theorem on independent transversals,
the union is the matroid $M_f$ of the function
$f(Y) = \sum_j r_{M_j}(Y)$, and the rank formula for $M_f$ gives its
rank.

In \cref{sec:body-bar} we apply these results to $k$ copies of the
cycle matroid of a multigraph. There the independence criterion becomes
$(k, k)$-sparsity, and it gives the tree-packing theorem of Tutte and
Nash-Williams~\cite{tutte1961,nashWilliams1961}.

The formal proofs of this chapter come from Peter Nelson's matroid
package for Lean,
\href{https://github.com/apnelson1/Matroid}{\texttt{apnelson1/Matroid}},
which, like this project, is licensed under Apache-2.0. In the package
they rest on a matroid constructor that it has since replaced. We port
them onto its current constructor, keeping his authorship, and add the
forms of the rank formula proved for \cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank}.
The header of each ported file lists the changes.

\subsection{Matroids from submodular functions}
\label{sec:matroid-union-submodular}

Let $E$ be a finite set. The rank function $r$ of a matroid on $E$ is
non-decreasing and submodular, and the circuits of the matroid are the
minimal non-empty sets $C$ with $r(C) < |C|$. By a result of Edmonds
and Rota, every non-decreasing integer-valued submodular function $f$
defines a matroid $M_f$ in the same way, and when $f(\emptyset) = 0$
the rank function of $M_f$ is given by a minimization
formula~\cite[\S11.1]{oxley2011}. We follow Oxley's proofs. For $M_f$,
the circuit elimination axiom follows from the submodularity of
$f$~\cite[Proposition~11.1.1]{oxley2011}. Edmonds~\cite{edmonds1970}
studies these functions and the polyhedra they define.

\begin{definition}[Submodular function]
  \label{def:submodular}
  \lean{Matroid.Submodular}
  \leanok
  Let $E$ be a finite set. A function $f$ from the subsets of $E$ to
  the integers is \emph{submodular} if
  $f(X \cap Y) + f(X \cup Y) \le f(X) + f(Y)$ for all
  $X, Y \subseteq E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Polymatroid rank function]
  \label{def:polymatroidFn}
  \lean{Matroid.PolymatroidFn}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:submodular}
  A \emph{polymatroid rank function} on a finite set $E$ is a
  non-decreasing submodular function $f$ on the subsets of $E$ with
  $f(\emptyset) = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Matroid of a submodular function]
  \label{def:ofSubmodular}
  \lean{Matroid.ofSubmodular, Matroid.circuit_ofSubmodular_iff,
    Matroid.indep_ofSubmodular_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:submodular}
  Let $f$ be a non-decreasing submodular function on the subsets of a
  finite set $E$. The matroid \emph{induced} by $f$ is the matroid
  $M_f$ on $E$ whose circuits are the minimal non-empty sets
  $C \subseteq E$ with $f(C) < |C|$. Equivalently, a set
  $I \subseteq E$ is independent in $M_f$ if and only if
  $|I'| \le f(I')$ for every non-empty $I' \subseteq I$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Matroid of a polymatroid rank function]
  \label{def:ofPolymatroidFn}
  \lean{Matroid.ofPolymatroidFn, Matroid.indep_ofPolymatroidFn_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:ofSubmodular, def:polymatroidFn}
  Let $f$ be a polymatroid rank function on a finite set $E$. The
  matroid $M_f$ is the matroid on $E$ induced by $f$
  (\cref{def:ofSubmodular}). Its independent sets are the sets
  $I \subseteq E$ with $|I'| \le f(I')$ for every non-empty
  $I' \subseteq I$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Polymatroid rank formula]
  \label{lem:polymatroid-rank}
  \lean{Matroid.polymatroid_rank_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:ofPolymatroidFn}
  Let $f$ be a polymatroid rank function on a finite set $E$, and let
  $X \subseteq E$. Then the rank of $X$ in $M_f$ is
  \[
    r_{M_f}(X) \;=\; \min_{Y \subseteq X}\bigl(f(Y) + |X \setminus Y|\bigr).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:ofSubmodular}
  Let $B$ be a basis of $X$ in $M_f$, so that $r_{M_f}(X) = |B|$. Let
  $Y \subseteq X$. The set $B \cap Y$ is independent, so
  $|B \cap Y| \le f(B \cap Y)$ (\cref{def:ofSubmodular}; if
  $B \cap Y$ is empty, because $f(\emptyset) = 0$). As $f$ is
  non-decreasing, $f(B \cap Y) \le f(Y)$. Also
  $|B \setminus Y| \le |X \setminus Y|$. Hence
  $|B| \le f(Y) + |X \setminus Y|$.

  For the reverse inequality we follow Oxley's
  proof~\cite[Proposition~11.1.7]{oxley2011}. If $X$ is independent,
  then $Y = \emptyset$ attains $|B|$. Otherwise, for each
  $e \in X \setminus B$ the set $B \cup \{e\}$ is dependent, so it
  contains a circuit $C_e$ of $M_f$, and $e \in C_e$. The set
  $I_e = C_e \setminus \{e\}$ is an independent subset of $B$, and
  $f(C_e) \le |C_e| - 1 = |I_e|$ (\cref{def:ofSubmodular}). Let $Y$ be
  the union of the sets $C_e$.

  We show that $f(Y) \le |Y \cap B|$, adding the sets $C_e$ one at a
  time. A single $C_e$ satisfies it, as $C_e \cap B = I_e$. Let $A$ be a
  union of some of them with $f(A) \le |A \cap B|$, and let $C_a$ be
  another. Each $C_e$ lies in $B \cup \{e\}$, so $a \notin A$. Hence
  $A \cap C_a$ lies in the independent set $I_a$, and
  $f(A \cap C_a) \ge |A \cap I_a|$. By submodularity,
  \[
    f(A \cup C_a) \;\le\; f(A) + f(C_a) - f(A \cap C_a)
    \;\le\; |A \cap B| + |I_a| - |A \cap I_a|
    \;=\; |(A \cup C_a) \cap B|.
  \]
  Finally, $Y$ contains $X \setminus B$, so
  $X \setminus Y = B \setminus Y$, and
  $f(Y) + |X \setminus Y| \le |Y \cap B| + |B \setminus Y| = |B|$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Matroid union}
\label{sec:matroid-union-union}

Let $M_1, \dots, M_k$ be matroids on a finite set $E$. Their direct
sum $M_1 \oplus \dots \oplus M_k$ is a matroid on
$\{1, \dots, k\} \times E$, in which $M_j$ lives on the copy
$\{j\} \times E$ of $E$.

A bipartite graph \emph{matches} a set $I$ of vertices on one side onto
a set $I'$ on the other if some bijection from $I$ to $I'$ sends each
element of $I$ to a neighbour. Let $N$ be a matroid on one side of a
bipartite graph, and let $S$ be the other side. Perfect showed that the
subsets of $S$ that the graph matches onto independent sets of $N$ are
the independent sets of a matroid on $S$, the matroid \emph{induced}
from $N$ by the graph~\cite[Theorem~11.2.12]{oxley2011}. The union of
the $M_j$ is the matroid on $E$ induced from their direct sum by the
graph that joins each element of $E$ to its copies. We show that its
independent sets are the unions of independent sets of the $M_j$. Then
we prove Rado's theorem, and from it the rank of the union.

\begin{definition}[Matroid union]
  \label{def:matroid-union}
  \lean{Matroid.Union, Matroid.union}
  \leanok
  Let $M_1, \dots, M_k$ be matroids on a finite set $E$, and let
  $\Delta$ be the bipartite graph that joins each $e \in E$ to its
  copies $(1, e), \dots, (k, e)$ in the ground set of the direct sum
  $M_1 \oplus \dots \oplus M_k$. The \emph{union} $\bigcup_j M_j$ is
  the matroid on $E$ induced from $M_1 \oplus \dots \oplus M_k$ by
  $\Delta$: a set $I \subseteq E$ is independent in $\bigcup_j M_j$ if
  and only if $\Delta$ matches $I$ onto an independent set of
  $M_1 \oplus \dots \oplus M_k$. For two matroids we write
  $M_1 \cup M_2$.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  Nelson's package proves that a bipartite graph induces a matroid by
  checking the augmentation axiom. In the formalization the $M_j$ need
  not have the same ground set: each is a matroid on a subset of $E$,
  and the union is a matroid on all of $E$. An element of $E$ outside
  the ground set of $M_j$ has no independent copy in the direct sum, so
  in the union it behaves as a loop of $M_j$. This is how Oxley defines
  the union of matroids on different ground
  sets~\cite[\S11.3]{oxley2011}.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Independent sets of a matroid union]
  \label{lem:union-indep-iff}
  \lean{Matroid.union_indep_iff, Matroid.union_indep_iff'}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-union}
  Let $M_1, \dots, M_k$ be matroids on a finite set $E$. A set
  $I \subseteq E$ is independent in $\bigcup_j M_j$ if and only if
  $I = I_1 \cup \dots \cup I_k$ for some sets $I_j$ independent in
  $M_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $I$ be independent in $\bigcup_j M_j$, so that $\Delta$ matches
  $I$ onto an independent set of the direct sum. The matching sends each
  $e \in I$ to a copy $(j(e), e)$. Its image is independent, so each
  set $I_j = \{e \in I : j(e) = j\}$ is independent in $M_j$, and the
  union of these sets is $I$.

  Conversely, let $I = I_1 \cup \dots \cup I_k$ with each $I_j$
  independent in $M_j$. For each $e \in I$ choose one index $j(e)$ with
  $e \in I_{j(e)}$. Each set $\{e \in I : j(e) = j\}$ is a subset of
  $I_j$, so $e \mapsto (j(e), e)$ matches $I$ onto an independent set of
  the direct sum.
\end{proof}

The rank of the union rests on Rado's theorem, which Rado proved in 1942
(see~\cite[Theorem~11.2.2]{oxley2011}). A \emph{transversal} of a
family $(A_i : i \in J)$ of sets is a family $(e_i : i \in J)$ of
distinct elements with $e_i \in A_i$ for every $i$.

\begin{lemma}[Rado's theorem]
  \label{lem:rado}
  \lean{Matroid.rado}
  \leanok
  Let $M$ be a matroid on a set $S$, and let $(A_i : i \in J)$ be a
  finite family of finite subsets of $S$. Then the family has a
  transversal $(e_i : i \in J)$ such that $\{e_i : i \in J\}$ is
  independent in $M$ if and only if
  $|K| \le r_M\bigl(\bigcup_{i \in K} A_i\bigr)$ for every
  $K \subseteq J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:submodular}
  If such a transversal exists, then for $K \subseteq J$ the set
  $\{e_i : i \in K\}$ is an independent subset of
  $\bigcup_{i \in K} A_i$ with $|K|$ elements, so the rank of
  $\bigcup_{i \in K} A_i$ is at least $|K|$.

  For the converse we follow Oxley, who derives Rado's theorem from a
  theorem of Welsh~\cite[Theorem~11.2.3]{oxley2011}
  (\texttt{Matroid.\allowbreak{}generalized\_\allowbreak{}halls\_\allowbreak{}marriage}). Let $f$ be a
  non-negative, integer-valued, non-decreasing submodular function on
  the subsets of $S$, and let the sets $A_i$ be non-empty. Suppose that
  $f\bigl(\bigcup_{i \in K} A_i\bigr) \ge |K|$ for every
  $K \subseteq J$. Then Welsh's theorem gives elements $e_i \in A_i$,
  not necessarily distinct, with $f(\{e_i : i \in K\}) \ge |K|$ for
  every $K \subseteq J$.

  We apply it to $f = r_M$. The sets $A_i$ are non-empty, since the
  hypothesis at $K = \{i\}$ gives $r_M(A_i) \ge 1$. For $i \ne i'$, the
  case $K = \{i, i'\}$ gives $r_M(\{e_i, e_{i'}\}) \ge 2$, so
  $e_i \ne e_{i'}$. The case $K = J$ then shows that the $|J|$ elements
  $e_i$ form an independent set.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The rank of a matroid union]
  \label{thm:matroid-partition-rank}
  \lean{Matroid.Union_rank_eq, Matroid.Union_rk_eq, Matroid.Union_indep_iff_count, Matroid.Union_pow_rk_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-union, lem:polymatroid-rank}
  Let $M_1, \dots, M_k$ be matroids on a finite set $E$, with rank
  functions $r_1, \dots, r_k$, and let $X \subseteq E$. Then the rank
  of $X$ in $\bigcup_j M_j$ is
  \[
    \min_{Y \subseteq X} \Bigl(|X \setminus Y| + \sum_{j} r_j(Y)\Bigr).
  \]
  In particular, $X$ is independent in $\bigcup_j M_j$ if and only if
  $|Y| \le \sum_j r_j(Y)$ for every $Y \subseteq X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:polymatroid-rank, lem:rado}
  Let $\Delta$ be as in \cref{def:matroid-union}. The neighbours in
  $\Delta$ of a set $Y \subseteq E$ form the set
  $\{1, \dots, k\} \times Y$, whose rank in the direct sum is
  $f(Y) = \sum_j r_j(Y)$. As a sum of matroid rank
  functions, $f$ is a polymatroid rank function
  (\cref{def:polymatroidFn}).

  We show that $\bigcup_j M_j$ is the matroid $M_f$
  (\cref{def:ofPolymatroidFn}; \texttt{Matroid.\allowbreak{}polymatroid\_\allowbreak{}of\_\allowbreak{}adjMap}).
  Let $I \subseteq E$, and apply Rado's theorem (\cref{lem:rado}) to the
  direct sum and the family of the sets $\{1, \dots, k\} \times \{e\}$
  for $e \in I$. A transversal of this family that is independent in
  the direct sum is a matching of $I$ onto an independent set. The
  condition of Rado's theorem says that $|Y| \le f(Y)$ for every
  $Y \subseteq I$. So $I$ is independent in $\bigcup_j M_j$ if and only
  if it is independent in $M_f$. The polymatroid rank formula
  (\cref{lem:polymatroid-rank}) now gives the rank of $X$. This is
  Oxley's proof~\cite[Lemma~11.2.13 and Theorem~11.3.1]{oxley2011}.

  The set $X$ is independent exactly when its rank is $|X|$. For
  $Y \subseteq X$ we have $|X \setminus Y| = |X| - |Y|$, so the rank
  formula gives the independence criterion.
\end{proof}

By \cref{lem:union-indep-iff}, a set is independent in the union of
$k$ copies of a matroid $M$ exactly when it is the union of $k$
independent sets of $M$, which can be taken disjoint. So for $k$ copies
of one matroid the independence criterion is Edmonds' matroid-partition
theorem~\cite[Theorem~1]{edmonds1965}.

\begin{theorem}[Edmonds' matroid-partition theorem]
  \label{thm:matroid-partition}
  \lean{Matroid.Union_pow_indep_iff_count}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:matroid-union}
  Let $M$ be a matroid on a finite set $E$, and let $k$ be a natural
  number. A set $X \subseteq E$ is independent in the union of $k$
  copies of $M$ if and only if $|Y| \le k\, r_M(Y)$ for every
  $Y \subseteq X$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:matroid-partition-rank}
  The set $X$ is independent exactly when its rank is $|X|$. By
  \cref{thm:matroid-partition-rank} with $M_1 = \dots = M_k = M$, this
  rank is the minimum of $|X \setminus Y| + k\, r_M(Y)$ over the sets
  $Y \subseteq X$. For such $Y$ we have
  $|X \setminus Y| = |X| - |Y|$, so the minimum is $|X|$ if and only if
  $|Y| \le k\, r_M(Y)$ for every $Y \subseteq X$.
\end{proof}
