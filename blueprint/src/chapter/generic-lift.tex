\section{The generic form of the realization theorems}
\label{sec:generic-lift}

Several theorems of the preceding chapters assert that a graph has
some realization with a given property, or give the rank of a matroid.
Tay's theorem (\cref{thm:tay-witness}) and the theorem of Tay and
Whiteley (\cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}) characterize the multigraphs that
have an independent, or an independent and infinitesimally rigid,
body-bar or body-hinge framework. In the formalization the body-hinge
frameworks of the second theorem are bundles of bars, which need not
come from hinges (the note after \cref{def:body-hinge-framework}). KT
Theorem~5.6 (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6}) gives a panel-hinge realization
of a simple graph, with nonparallel panels, at the target rank of
\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}. The molecule rank formula
(\cref{cor:molecule-rank-formula}) gives the rank of a square $G^2$ in
the generic rigidity matroid $\mathcal{R}_3(V)$. This chapter proves
generic forms of these results: they hold at every generic
realization, and almost all realizations are generic. We follow the
coordinate argument of Jackson and Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009},
not Whiteley's argument on irreducible
varieties~\cite[\S3.1]{whiteley1988}, which the body-bar chapter
recalls (\cref{sec:body-bar}).

In each class of frameworks a realization is given by parameters: the
coordinates of the points of a placement, of the normals of the
panels, of the endpoints of the bars, or of points that span the
hinges. A fixed family of linear forms, with coefficients that are
polynomials in the parameters, spans the rows of the rigidity matrix.
For placements and bars the forms are the rows. For a hinge they span
its rows when it is nondegenerate. A realization is generic for row
independence if every subfamily that is linearly independent at some
realization is linearly independent at it (\cref{def:generic-placement}
is the bar-joint instance). So a rank that the rigidity matrix of a
graph attains at one realization, with nondegenerate hinges where there
are hinges, is attained or exceeded at every generic realization. A
bound that holds at every realization then gives the rank there. The
realizations that are not generic lie in the zero set of a nonzero
polynomial in the parameters (\cref{lem:generic-placement-abundance}
for placements). So over an infinite field generic realizations exist,
and over $\R$ they form a dense open set, which is the precise content
of ``almost all realizations are generic''. Jackson and Jord\'an
instead note that realizations whose coordinates are algebraically
independent over $\Q$ are generic~\cite[\S5]{jacksonJordan2009}.

Jackson and Jord\'an call a realization generic if its rigidity matrix
and all its edge-induced submatrices have the largest rank over all
realizations of the graph~\cite[\S\S5--7]{jacksonJordan2009}. Where
each edge has one row, as for placements
(\cref{sec:bar-joint-3d-generic-placement}) and for bars, the two
notions agree; for bars this needs the dimension $d$ to be positive and
the multigraph to have no loops. A hinge has $D - 1$ rows, where
$D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and a degenerate hinge welds its two bodies
together. For $d \ge 2$ and a graph without loops, a generic assignment
of normals or of hinge points has nondegenerate hinges. It attains the
largest ranks over the assignments whose hinges are nondegenerate, as
Jackson and Jord\'an's are. For hinge points these assignments are
their realizations, so a generic hinge-point assignment is generic in
their sense. The panel, body-bar and body-hinge sections each compare
the two notions.

\Cref{sec:generic-lift-abundance} shows that the placements that are
not generic lie in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial.
\Cref{sec:generic-lift-molecule} proves the molecule rank formula at
every generic placement. It deduces that every generic placement is
infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$ when $5 \cdot G$ contains six
edge-disjoint spanning trees, as Jackson and Jord\'an noted the
molecular conjecture would imply. \Cref{sec:generic-lift-panel} proves
the rank formula of KT Proposition~2.3, Jackson and Jord\'an's
Theorem~6.1, at every generic assignment of normals to a simple graph.
Its rigidity corollary contains their theorem on generic
panel-and-hinge realizations (their Theorem~7.2).
\Cref{sec:generic-lift-bodybar} proves Tay's theorem at every generic
choice of the endpoints of the bars. \Cref{sec:generic-lift-bodyhinge}
proves the rank formula at every generic choice of points spanning the
hinges of a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, and with it the theorem of Tay and Whiteley
for frameworks with hinges.

\subsection{Abundance of generic placements}
\label{sec:generic-lift-abundance}

Generic placements exist in every dimension, by an argument that moves
along one line at a time (\cref{lem:exists-generic-placement}). Taken
as polynomials in all the coordinates of a placement, the minors of
that argument show more: the placements that are not generic lie in
the zero set of one nonzero polynomial.

\begin{lemma}[Abundance of generic placements]
  \label{lem:generic-placement-abundance}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.exists_isGenericPlacement_abundance}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite and $d \in \N$. Then there is a nonzero real
  polynomial $P$ in the $d\,|V|$ coordinates of a placement of $V$ in
  $\R^{d}$ such that every placement $p$ with $P(p) \neq 0$ is generic
  for row independence. In particular, the placements that are not
  generic lie in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the family of sets $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ that are
  row-independent at some placement. It is finite, since $E(K_V)$ is.
  Let $I \in \mathcal{F}$, and let $q$ be a placement at which $I$ is
  row-independent. The rigidity row of an edge $uv$ at a placement $p$
  is $x \mapsto \langle p_u - p_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which depends linearly on $p$. Fix a
  basis of $((\R^{d})^{V})^{*}$. The coordinate vectors of the rows at
  $p$ of the edges in $I$ are the rows of a matrix $A(p)$ whose entries
  are linear forms in the coordinates of $p$. These rows are linearly
  independent at $q$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow}), so
  some $|I| \times |I|$ minor of $A(q)$ is nonzero. The same minor of
  $A(p)$ is a polynomial $P_I$ in the coordinates of $p$. It is nonzero,
  as $P_I(q) \neq 0$, and $I$ is row-independent at every placement $p$
  with $P_I(p) \neq 0$.

  Let $P$ be the product of the polynomials $P_I$ for
  $I \in \mathcal{F}$. It is nonzero, as a finite product of nonzero
  polynomials. If $P(p) \neq 0$, then $P_I(p) \neq 0$ for every
  $I \in \mathcal{F}$. So every member of $\mathcal{F}$ is
  row-independent at $p$, and $p$ is generic
  (\cref{def:generic-placement}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Every generic realization of a square}
\label{sec:generic-lift-molecule}

The molecule rank formula (\cref{cor:molecule-rank-formula}) gives the
rank of the square $G^2$ in the generic rigidity matroid
$\mathcal{R}_3(V)$. We show that it gives the rank of the rigidity map
of $G^2$ at every placement that is generic for row independence, and
we deduce when every such placement is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$.

\begin{theorem}[The molecule rank formula at every generic placement]
  \label{thm:molecule-generic-rank}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_generic_rank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement, def:square-graph, def:D-deficiency,
    def:rigidityMap}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph of minimum degree at least two on a
  finite, nonempty vertex set $V$, and let $p$ be a placement of $V$ in
  $\R^3$ that is generic for row independence. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G^2, p)
      \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  where $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the deficiency of
  \cref{def:D-deficiency} at $D = 6$, with $\tilde G = 5 \cdot G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:molecule-rank-formula, lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}
  As $p$ is generic, $\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, p) = r(G^2)$, the rank
  of $G^2$ in $\mathcal{R}_3(V)$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-rank-at-generic}). By the molecule rank
  formula (\cref{cor:molecule-rank-formula}),
  $r(G^2) = 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every generic realization of a rigid square]
  \label{cor:molecule-generic-rigid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_generic_rigid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement, def:square-graph, def:D-deficiency,
    def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph of minimum degree at least two on a
  finite, nonempty vertex set $V$, and suppose that
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, where $\tilde G = 5 \cdot G$.
  Then every placement $p$ of $V$ in $\R^3$ that is generic for row
  independence is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:molecule-generic-rank}
  By \cref{thm:molecule-generic-rank},
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, p) = 3|V| - 6$. The space of placements
  has dimension $3|V|$ (\cref{lem:framework-finrank}), so by
  rank--nullity $\dim \ker R(G^2, p) = 6$. This is the bound of
  \cref{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid} at $d = 3$.
\end{proof}

Jackson and Jord\'an noted that the molecular conjecture (their
Conjecture~1.1), together with Tay and Whiteley's theorem on
body-and-hinge frameworks (their Corollary~6.3;
\cref{sec:body-hinge-tay}), would imply that every generic bar-joint
realization of $G^2$ in $\R^3$ is infinitesimally rigid whenever
$5 \cdot G$ contains six edge-disjoint spanning
trees~\cite[p.~586]{jacksonJordan2009}. Katoh and Tanigawa proved the
molecular conjecture~\cite{katohTanigawa2011}, and
\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture} is its form for simple graphs. We
derive the consequence from \cref{cor:molecule-generic-rigid}, and so
from the molecule rank formula.

\begin{corollary}[Generic rigidity of squares from six spanning
  trees; Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{cor:molecule-generic-square-packing}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.molecule_generic_square_packing}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-placement, def:square-graph,
    def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}
  Let $G$ be a simple graph on a finite, nonempty vertex set $V$, and
  suppose that $5 \cdot G$ contains six edge-disjoint spanning trees.
  Then every placement of $V$ in $\R^3$ that is generic for row
  independence is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:molecule-generic-rigid, thm:def-eq-corank, def:matroid-MG,
    def:cut-edges-2ec}
  If $|V| = 1$, then $G^2$ has no edge, and every placement $p$ is
  infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$, as
  $\dim \ker R(G^2, p) \le 3 \le 6$ (\cref{lem:framework-finrank}). Let
  $|V| \ge 2$, and let $\tilde G = 5 \cdot G$.

  We first show that $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. The argument
  is that of the proof of KT Proposition~2.2
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}). Each of the six
  spanning trees has $|V| - 1$ edges and is a forest of $\tilde G$.
  They are edge-disjoint, so their union $F$ has $6(|V| - 1)$ edges,
  and $F$ is independent in the union of six copies of the cycle
  matroid of $\tilde G$ (\cref{lem:union-indep-iff}). This union is
  $M(\tilde G)$ (\cref{def:matroid-MG}). As
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \ge 0$, KT eq.~(2.4) gives
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) \le 6(|V| - 1)$
  (\cref{thm:def-eq-corank}). So $F$ is a base of $M(\tilde G)$, and KT
  eq.~(2.4) gives $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.

  Next we show that $G$ has minimum degree at least two. Regard $G$ as
  the multigraph of \cref{lem:molecule-graph-carrier}. As its
  deficiency is $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, it is a
  $0$-dof-graph (\cref{def:k-dof}), with at least two vertices. So each
  of its vertices has degree at least two
  (\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-connected}). It is
  simple, and its edges join exactly the adjacent pairs of $G$, so each
  vertex has the same degree in $G$. Now
  \cref{cor:molecule-generic-rigid} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Every generic panel-and-hinge realization}
\label{sec:generic-lift-panel}

As in \cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction-panel}, $d$ is the
dimension, $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and $K$ is a field. A
panel-and-hinge realization of a multigraph $G = (V, E)$ is a
panel-hinge framework (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). It gives
each body $v$ a normal $q_v \in K^{d+1}$, and the hinge of an edge
with ends $u$ and $v$ has the supporting extensor $C(q_u, q_v)$. So
the realization parameters are the $(d+1)|V|$ coordinates of the
normals. Jackson and Jord\'an describe the panel of $v$ as the
hyperplane $\{x \in \R^d : x \cdot p_v = 1\}$ for a vector
$p_v \in \R^d$~\cite[\S7]{jacksonJordan2009}. Its normal is
$(p_v, -1)$ (the remarks after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}).

Our main result is Jackson and Jord\'an's rank formula, which Katoh
and Tanigawa state for body-hinge frameworks as their Proposition~2.3
(\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}), at every generic assignment of
normals to a simple graph
(\cref{thm:panel-generic-rank}). Here $\tilde G = (D-1) \cdot G$, and
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is its deficiency
(\cref{def:D-deficiency}). For a multigraph the formula can fail. At a
generic assignment the two-vertex double edge has rank $D - 1$, while
the formula gives $D$ (\cref{fmlnote:molecular-conjecture-multigraph}).

Genericity is the transfer condition of \cref{def:generic-placement},
applied to the forms of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. These
forms span the rows of the rigidity matrix at each edge. Jackson and
Jord\'an derive their Theorem~7.2 from the realization of their
Theorem~6.5, whose hinges all lie in one hyperplane. They perturb it
so that each hinge is the intersection of two hyperplanes, one for each
end of its edge (their Lemma~7.1). These hyperplanes are the panels of
a panel-and-hinge realization. We start instead from KT Theorem~5.6
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6}). It gives a panel-hinge realization at the
target rank $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
(\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) whose panels are nonparallel, so that its
hinges are nondegenerate.

\begin{definition}[Generic normal assignment]
  \label{def:generic-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.normalRow,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.IsGenericNormals,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.isGenericNormals_iff_of_graph_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph. A \emph{normal assignment} $q$ of
  $G$ gives each body $v$ a vector $q_v \in K^{d+1}$, and $(G, q)$ is
  the panel-hinge framework with these normals. The
  \emph{annihilator-row family} of $q$ is the family of linear forms
  \[
    S \;\mapsto\; C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t,
    \qquad \text{where } x = S(u) - S(v) \text{ and }
    C = C(q_u, q_v),
  \]
  on screw assignments $S$, indexed by an edge $e$ of $G$, with ends
  $u$ and $v$, and a pair $(t, t')$ of indices of the standard basis of
  $\bigwedge^{d-1}K^{d+1}$. Here $y_t$ is the coordinate of $y$ at the
  $t$-th basis vector. The assignment $q$ is \emph{generic for row
  independence}, and $(G, q)$ has \emph{generic normals}, if every
  subfamily of the annihilator-row family that is linearly independent
  at some normal assignment is linearly independent at $q$.
\end{definition}

These are the forms of \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals} at the
edges of $G$. A form does not depend on the order of $u$ and $v$,
since exchanging them changes the signs of both $x$ and $C$
(\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). When the hinge of $e$ is
nondegenerate, the forms at $e$ span the same space as the rows of
$R(G, q)$ at $e$ (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}).

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:generic-normals-selector}
  The formalization indexes the family by all the edge labels, and
  reads the ends of each label from an endpoint selector
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}), not from $G$. Genericity
  is a property of a panel-hinge framework: it asks about the
  subfamilies indexed by edges of $G$, with the framework's own
  selector, which gives each edge of $G$ its two ends. As a form does
  not depend on the order of the ends, two frameworks with the same
  graph and the same normals have generic normals together, whatever
  their selectors. The pinned lemma on abundance fixes one selector
  that gives each edge of $G$ its two ends, and the one on existence
  assumes a body, for the endpoint choice of $G$
  (\cref{fmlnote:graph-operations}). The first pin is the family, the
  second genericity, and the third that genericity reads only the graph
  and the normals.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Abundance of generic normal assignments]
  \label{lem:generic-normals-abundance}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_isGenericNormals_abundance}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-normals}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V$ and $E$ finite. Then there
  is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the $(d+1)|V|$ coordinates of a normal
  assignment such that every normal assignment $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$
  is generic for row independence. In particular, the normal
  assignments that are not generic lie in the zero set of a nonzero
  polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals, def:plucker-coords}
  Fix the standard basis of the space of screw assignments. In its
  dual basis, each coordinate of a member of the
  annihilator-row family at an edge with ends $u$ and $v$ is $0$ or
  $\pm$ a coordinate of $C(q_u, q_v) = *(q_u \vee q_v)$, where
  $q_u \vee q_v$ is the join (\cref{def:join}). Its coordinates are
  the $2 \times 2$ minors $q_u(i)\, q_v(j) - q_u(j)\, q_v(i)$
  (\cref{lem:plucker-expansion}). The complement
  isomorphism $*$ sends each standard basis vector to $\pm$ a standard
  basis vector (\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}). So each coordinate of
  each member of the family is a polynomial of degree at most two in
  the coordinates of $q$, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}.

  The argument of \cref{lem:generic-placement-abundance} now applies.
  The family has finitely many indices, as $E$ is finite. Let $s$ be a
  set of indices whose members are linearly independent at some normal
  assignment $q^s$. Some $|s| \times |s|$ minor of the coordinate
  matrix of these members is nonzero at $q^s$. As a polynomial $P_s$ in
  the coordinates of $q$, it is nonzero, and the members indexed by $s$
  are linearly independent at every $q$ with $P_s(q) \neq 0$. The
  product $P$ of the polynomials $P_s$ is nonzero, and every $q$ with
  $P(q) \neq 0$ is generic.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Existence of generic normal assignments]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-normals}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_isGenericNormals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-normals}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V$
  and $E$ finite. Then some normal assignment of $G$ is generic for row
  independence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:generic-normals-abundance}
  The polynomial $P$ of \cref{lem:generic-normals-abundance} is
  nonzero. Over an infinite field a nonzero polynomial has a non-root,
  and every non-root of $P$ is generic.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic normal assignments have nondegenerate hinges]
  \label{lem:generic-normals-nondegenerate}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ne_zero_of_isGenericNormals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-normals, def:panel-hinge-framework,
    def:panel-support-extensor}
  Let $d \ge 2$, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph without loops.
  Let $q$ be a normal assignment of $G$ that is generic for row
  independence. Then every hinge of $(G, q)$ is nondegenerate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:moment-curve-general-position, def:panel-support-extensor}
  Let $e$ be an edge of $G$, with ends $u \neq v$. Let $q^0$ be the
  normal assignment that gives $u$ the point $(1, 0, \dots, 0)$ of the
  moment curve and every other body the point $(1, 1, \dots, 1)$
  (\cref{lem:moment-curve-general-position}). These two vectors are
  linearly independent, so $C^0 = C(q^0_u, q^0_v)$ is not zero (the
  remark after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). Let $t$ be an index
  with $C^0_t \neq 0$. As $d \ge 2$, the screw space has dimension
  $D \ge 3$, so there is an index $t' \neq t$. As $u \neq v$, there is
  a screw assignment $S$ for which $S(u) - S(v)$ is the $t'$-th basis
  vector. At $S$ the member of the annihilator-row family of $q^0$ at
  $(e, t, t')$ takes the value $C^0_t \neq 0$.

  So this member alone is a linearly independent family at $q^0$. As
  $q$ is generic, the member of the family of $q$ at $(e, t, t')$ is
  not zero. If $C(q_u, q_v)$ were zero, every member of the family of
  $q$ at $e$ would be zero. So the hinge of $e$ in $(G, q)$ is
  nondegenerate.
\end{proof}

Jackson and Jord\'an call a panel-and-hinge realization generic if its
rigidity matrix, and the submatrix of the rows of every set of edges,
have the largest rank over all realizations of the graph. Their
realizations have nondegenerate
hinges~\cite[\S7]{jacksonJordan2009}. Let $d \ge 2$, let $G$ have no
loops, and let $q$ be generic, so that the hinges of $(G, q)$ are
nondegenerate. Then for every set $F$ of edges, the rows of
$R(G, q)$ at the edges of $F$ have the largest rank over all normal
assignments at which the hinges of these edges are nondegenerate.
Indeed, at such an assignment $q'$ the members of
the annihilator-row family at $F$ span the rows at $F$
(\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So as many of these members
as the rank are linearly independent at $q'$, and hence at $q$, where
they lie in the span of the rows at $F$. The condition on the hinges
is needed. A degenerate hinge welds its two bodies together (the
remarks after \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}), so its edge imposes
$D$ independent linear conditions, where a nondegenerate hinge imposes
$D - 1$.

\begin{lemma}[Independent annihilator rows of a realization]
  \label{lem:panel-witness-transplant}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_independent_normalRow_of_le_finrank}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:rigidity-matrix,
    def:panel-support-extensor, def:generic-normals}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V$ and $E$ finite, and let
  $(G, q)$ be a panel-hinge framework whose hinges are all
  nondegenerate. Then for every $N \le \operatorname{rank} R(G, q)$,
  some $N$ members of the annihilator-row family of $q$ are linearly
  independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}
  As the hinges are nondegenerate, the members of the family at each
  edge span the same space as the rows of $R(G, q)$ at that edge
  (\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So the family spans the row
  space of $R(G, q)$, which has dimension
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \ge N$. Hence some $N$ of its members
  are linearly independent (\cref{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned lemma takes the family with any selector that gives each
  edge of $G$ its two ends (\cref{fmlnote:generic-normals-selector}),
  and extracts the rows at that selector by the pinned theorem of
  \cref{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}, all at edges of $G$. Their
  hinges are the realization's, read with its own selector, which also
  gives each edge its two ends, in either order
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). Where the two orders
  differ, the hinge of the realization is $-C(q_u, q_v)$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), and the rows it extracts differ
  from the members of the family by a sign. Signs do not affect linear
  independence. The pinned lemma asks nondegenerate hinges at the edges
  of $G$ only.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[The rank formula at every generic normal assignment]
  \label{thm:panel-generic-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_of_isGenericNormals}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-normals, def:panel-hinge-framework,
    def:rank-hypothesis, def:D-deficiency}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a simple graph with $V$ finite and nonempty. Let $q$ be a normal
  assignment of $G$ that is generic for row independence. Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,q)
      \;=\; D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6, lem:panel-witness-transplant,
    lem:generic-normals-nondegenerate, lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  As $G$ has no loops, the hinges of $(G, q)$ are nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:generic-normals-nondegenerate}). So
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \le D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).

  For the reverse inequality, KT Theorem~5.6 (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6})
  gives a panel-hinge realization $(G, q^0)$ at the target rank
  $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, whose hinges are
  nondegenerate, as its panels are nonparallel. By
  \cref{lem:panel-witness-transplant}, some
  $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ members of the
  annihilator-row family of $q^0$ are linearly independent. As $q$ is
  generic, the members of the family of $q$ with the same indices are
  linearly independent. Each of them lies in the row space of
  $R(G, q)$. Indeed, a member at an edge with ends $u$ and $v$ is a
  linear form in $S(u) - S(v)$ that vanishes at $C(q_u, q_v)$, and so a
  linear combination of the rows of $R(G, q)$ at that edge (the remark
  after \cref{def:hinge-row-block}). Hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every generic panel-and-hinge realization of a
  rigid graph; Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{cor:panel-generic-rigid}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_of_isGenericNormals_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-normals, def:panel-hinge-framework,
    def:D-deficiency}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a simple graph with $V$ finite and nonempty. Then $(G, q)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid at every normal assignment $q$ of $G$ that is
  generic for row independence if and only if
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:panel-generic-rank, lem:exists-generic-normals,
    lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count,
    lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}
  Suppose first that $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, and let $q$ be
  generic. By \cref{thm:panel-generic-rank},
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1)$, so
  $\dim Z(G,q) = D|V| - D(|V|-1) = D$. Hence every infinitesimal
  motion of $(G, q)$ is trivial
  (\cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}, with $W = V$).

  Conversely, suppose that $(G, q)$ is infinitesimally rigid at every
  generic $q$. As $K$ is infinite, some normal assignment $q$ is
  generic (\cref{lem:exists-generic-normals}). Then $(G, q)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid, so its motions are constant on $V$, and
  $\dim Z(G,q) = D$ (\cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract},
  with $W = V$). As the screw assignments form a space of dimension
  $D|V|$, $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1)$, and
  \cref{thm:panel-generic-rank} gives
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization writes $d = k + 1$ here, as in the note after
  \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}; the pinned theorems of
  \cref{thm:panel-generic-rank} and \cref{cor:panel-generic-rigid}
  take $d$ and the frameworks with $k = d - 1$. They range over the
  panel-hinge frameworks on $G$ with generic normals, whatever their
  selectors (\cref{fmlnote:generic-normals-selector}). They assume
  that every body is a vertex of $G$, and nothing of the edge labels
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}): they apply to every simple graph on
  the bodies, a single edge or a tree among them.
\end{fmlnote}

Jackson and Jord\'an's Theorem~7.2 states that if $(d-1) \cdot G$
contains $d$ edge-disjoint spanning trees, then every generic
panel-and-hinge realization of the graph $G$ is infinitesimally
rigid~\cite[\S7]{jacksonJordan2009}. Their condition implies
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. Indeed, $\tilde G = (D-1) \cdot G$
has $D - d = \binom{d}{2}$ further copies of each edge, and these hold
$\binom{d}{2}$ edge-disjoint copies of a spanning tree of $G$. So
$\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees, as in the proof
of their Theorem~6.5, and $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ by KT
Proposition~2.2 (\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}). Hence
\cref{cor:panel-generic-rigid} contains their theorem for simple
graphs, under the weaker hypothesis $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$,
which it shows to be necessary.

\subsection{Every generic body-and-bar realization}
\label{sec:generic-lift-bodybar}

As in \cref{sec:body-bar}, $d$ is the dimension, $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$,
and the vertices and edges of a multigraph are its bodies and bars.
Jackson and Jord\'an realize each bar $e$ as a line segment of $\R^d$,
with distinct endpoints $p_e$ and
$p'_e$~\cite[\S\S2.1 and 3]{jacksonJordan2009}. A bar $e$ that joins
the bodies $u$ and $v$ imposes one linear condition on their screws
$S(u), S(v) \in \R^D$: the difference $S(u) - S(v)$ is orthogonal to
the vector of $2 \times 2$ minors of the matrix with rows $(p_e, 1)$
and $(p'_e, 1)$. So their realization is a body-bar framework
(\cref{def:body-bar-framework}) with this vector on each bar, while
Tay's frameworks allow any vector of $\R^D$. In this section the
realization parameters are the $2d$ coordinates of the endpoints of
each bar. So ``almost all realizations'' refers to the choice of the
endpoints, as in Jackson and Jord\'an's paper.

The coordinates of this vector are polynomials in the coordinates of
the endpoints. So, as for placements
(\cref{sec:generic-lift-abundance}), generic choices of endpoints
exist, and the choices that are not generic lie in the zero set of a
nonzero polynomial. The generic form of Tay's theorem then needs a
witness. For a finite multigraph $G$ and a set $E'$ of bars such that
$G \restriction E'$ is $(D, D)$-sparse, we need endpoints at which the
rows of the bars in $E'$ are linearly independent. This is Jackson and
Jord\'an's Lemma~5.1. We derive it from the framework of
\cref{prop:tay-witness-exists} by a change of coordinates of $\R^D$,
following Whiteley. Genericity then gives Tay's theorem at every
generic choice of endpoints (\cref{cor:bodybar-generic-tay}).

We put the coordinate $1$ of the rows first, and call the vector of
minors the two-extensor of the segment.

\begin{definition}[The two-extensor of a segment]
  \label{def:two-extensor}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.twoExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework}
  For $p \in \R^d$ let $h(p) = (1, p) \in \R^{d+1}$, with coordinates
  indexed by $0, 1, \dots, d$. Index the $D$ coordinates of $\R^D$ by
  the pairs $(i, j)$ with $0 \le i < j \le d$, through a fixed
  bijection. The \emph{two-extensor} of an ordered pair of points
  $p, p' \in \R^d$ is the vector $T(p, p') \in \R^D$ with
  \[
    T(p, p')_{(i,j)} \;=\; h(p)_i\, h(p')_j - h(p)_j\, h(p')_i,
    \qquad 0 \le i < j \le d.
  \]
\end{definition}

We call the coordinates $(0, j)$ of $T(p, p')$ its \emph{direction
coordinates} and the others its \emph{moment coordinates}. As
$h(p)_0 = 1$, they are
\[
  T(p, p')_{(0,j)} \;=\; p'_j - p_j
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  T(p, p')_{(i,j)} \;=\; p_i\, p'_j - p_j\, p'_i \quad (i \ge 1),
\]
polynomials of degree one and two in the $2d$ coordinates of $p$ and
$p'$. The vector $T(p, p')$ is the coordinate vector of the join
$h(p) \vee h(p')$ (\cref{def:join}), whence its name. Its coordinates
are taken in the basis of $\bigwedge^2 \R^{d+1}$ formed by the products
of two standard basis vectors in increasing order, as in
\cref{lem:plucker-expansion}. Jackson and Jord\'an's vector of
minors puts the coordinate $1$ last, as \cref{def:homogeneous-coords}
does. It has the same coordinates as $T(p, p')$ up to their order,
with the direction coordinates negated. Such a change of the
coordinates of $\R^D$ does not change which sets of rows are linearly
independent (\cref{lem:extensor-map-rows} below).

Not every vector of $\R^D$ is the two-extensor of a segment. If the
direction coordinates of $T(p, p')$ vanish, then $p = p'$, and the
moment coordinates vanish too. So the standard basis vector of $\R^D$
at a moment coordinate $(i, j)$ is the two-extensor of no pair of
points. It is the coordinate vector of the product of the $i$-th and
$j$-th standard basis vectors of $\R^{d+1}$, the line at infinity
through the points at infinity of the $i$-th and $j$-th coordinate
axes. Hence the framework of \cref{prop:tay-witness-exists}, which
puts standard basis vectors on the bars, does not in general come
from a choice of endpoints.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization calls the dimension $n$, as in the note after
  \cref{def:body-bar-framework}. For the bijection of the pairs
  $(i, j)$ with the coordinates of $\R^D$, it takes the one that the
  equality of their numbers provides
  (\texttt{pairIdxEquiv}).
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{definition}[Endpoint realization; generic endpoint
  assignment]
  \label{def:generic-endpoints}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.ofEndpoints, Graph.BodyBarFramework.IsGenericEndpoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:two-extensor, def:body-bar-framework,
    def:rigidity-map-body-bar}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, and fix an orientation of its bars
  (\cref{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}). An \emph{endpoint assignment}
  $q$ of $G$ gives each bar $e$ an ordered pair $(p_e, p'_e)$ of
  points of $\R^d$. Its \emph{endpoint realization} is the body-bar
  framework $(G, b)$ in $\R^d$ with $b_e = T(p_e, p'_e)$ for every bar
  $e$. The assignment $q$ is \emph{generic for row independence} if
  every set of bars whose rows are linearly independent at the
  endpoint realization of some endpoint assignment has linearly
  independent rows at the endpoint realization of $q$.
\end{definition}

Reversing a bar changes only the sign of its row
(\cref{sec:body-bar-framework}), so genericity does not depend on the
orientation.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization gives a pair of endpoints to every edge label, not
  only to the bars of $G$: an endpoint assignment is a real function of
  a label, one of its two ends and a coordinate. The pairs at labels
  that are not bars of $G$ do not enter the rows. The bodies and the
  labels are drawn from two fixed sets, which the pinned lemmas below
  assume finite. So the polynomial of
  \cref{lem:generic-endpoints-abundance} has $2d$ variables for each
  label, not only for each bar. The first pin is the endpoint
  realization, and the second is genericity.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The two-extensors are polynomial in the endpoints]
  \label{lem:two-extensor-polynomial}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.twoExtensorPoly_eval}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:two-extensor}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph, let $e$ be a bar of $G$, and let
  $0 \le i < j \le d$. Then there is a polynomial $P$ in the
  coordinates of the endpoints of the bars of $G$ such that
  $P(q) = T(p_e, p'_e)_{(i,j)}$ for every endpoint assignment $q$ of
  $G$, where $(p_e, p'_e)$ is the pair of points that $q$ gives $e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By \cref{def:two-extensor},
  $T(p_e, p'_e)_{(i,j)} = h(p_e)_i\, h(p'_e)_j - h(p_e)_j\, h(p'_e)_i$.
  Each coordinate of $h(p_e) = (1, p_e)$ and of $h(p'_e)$ is $1$ or a
  coordinate of $q$. So this is a polynomial of degree at most two in
  the coordinates of $q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Abundance of generic endpoint assignments]
  \label{lem:generic-endpoints-abundance}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.exists_isGenericEndpoints_abundance}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-endpoints}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph. Then there is a nonzero polynomial
  $P$ in the $2d\,|E(G)|$ endpoint coordinates such that every
  endpoint assignment $q$ of $G$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is generic for row
  independence. In particular, the endpoint assignments that are not
  generic lie in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:two-extensor, lem:two-extensor-polynomial}
  The row of a bar $e$ has the coefficient $-(b_e)_c\,(a_e)_v$ at a
  body $v$ and a coordinate $c$ of $\R^D$, where $a_e$ is the signed
  incidence vector of $e$ (the remarks after
  \cref{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}). So each coefficient is $0$ or
  $\pm (b_e)_c$. At the endpoint realization of $q$, it is $0$ or
  $\pm$ a coordinate of $T(p_e, p'_e)$, a polynomial in the coordinates
  of $q$ (\cref{lem:two-extensor-polynomial}).

  The argument of \cref{lem:generic-placement-abundance} now applies.
  There are finitely many sets of bars, as $E(G)$ is finite. Let $F$
  be a set of bars whose rows are linearly independent at some
  endpoint assignment $q^F$. Some $|F| \times |F|$ minor of the
  coefficient matrix of these rows is nonzero at $q^F$. As a
  polynomial $P_F$ in the coordinates of $q$, it is nonzero, and the
  rows of $F$ are linearly independent at every $q$ with
  $P_F(q) \neq 0$. The product $P$ of the polynomials $P_F$ is
  nonzero, and every $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$ is generic.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Existence of generic endpoint assignments]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-endpoints}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.exists_isGenericEndpoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-endpoints}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph. Then some endpoint assignment of $G$
  is generic for row independence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:generic-endpoints-abundance}
  The polynomial $P$ of \cref{lem:generic-endpoints-abundance} is
  nonzero. As $\R$ is infinite, $P$ has a non-root, and every non-root
  of $P$ is generic.
\end{proof}

Each bar has one row. So if $q$ is generic for row independence, then
for every set $F$ of bars the rows of $F$ have at $q$ the largest rank
over all endpoint assignments. Indeed, a subset of $F$ whose rows are
linearly independent at some assignment has linearly independent rows
at $q$. Conversely, an assignment with this property is generic.
Jackson and Jord\'an call a body-and-bar realization generic if its
rigidity matrix and the submatrices formed by its rows at sets of bars
have the largest rank over all realizations of the
multigraph~\cite[\S5]{jacksonJordan2009}. Their bars have distinct
endpoints. Let $d \ge 1$, and let $G$ be finite and without loops.
Then the row of each bar is nonzero at some endpoint assignment, so a
generic assignment gives every bar distinct endpoints. As a generic
assignment exists (\cref{lem:exists-generic-endpoints}), the largest
ranks over their realizations are those over all endpoint assignments.
So the two notions of genericity agree.

Let $c_0 = 0$, and let $c_1, \dots, c_d$ be the standard basis vectors
of $\R^d$, the vertices of a simplex. Jackson and Jord\'an put the
bars of each of $D$ disjoint forests $F_{ij}$, $0 \le i < j \le d$, on
the edge from $c_i$ to $c_j$. They prove that the rows of these bars
are linearly independent by an elimination on the coordinates of the
two-extensors of the edges. Whiteley pointed out that their lemma
follows from the framework in his proof of
Theorem~8~\cite{whiteley1988}, which is that of
\cref{prop:tay-witness-exists}~\cite[Remark after
Lemma~5.1]{jacksonJordan2009}. This framework places the bars on the
edges of a projective simplex with one vertex at the origin and the
others at infinity on the coordinate axes. A projective
transformation, which preserves independence, takes this simplex to
the one with vertices $c_0, \dots, c_d$. We use a linear form of the
argument. The two-extensors of the edges of the simplex form a basis
of $\R^D$ (\cref{lem:coordinate-extensor-basis}). An invertible linear
map of $\R^D$, applied to the vectors on all the bars, preserves
linear independence of rows (\cref{lem:extensor-map-rows}).

\begin{lemma}[The coordinate-segment two-extensors form a basis]
  \label{lem:coordinate-extensor-basis}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.linearIndependent_twoExtensor_coordPoint}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:two-extensor}
  Let $c_0 = 0 \in \R^d$, and for $1 \le i \le d$ let $c_i$ be the
  $i$-th standard basis vector of $\R^d$. Then the $D$ two-extensors
  $T(c_i, c_j)$, $0 \le i < j \le d$, are linearly independent, and so
  form a basis of $\R^D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $u_{ij}$ be the standard basis vector of $\R^D$ at $(i, j)$. By
  \cref{def:two-extensor}, $T(c_0, c_k) = u_{0k}$ for
  $1 \le k \le d$, and
  \[
    T(c_h, c_k) \;=\; u_{hk} + u_{0k} - u_{0h},
    \qquad 1 \le h < k \le d.
  \]
  Jackson and Jord\'an list these entries in the proof of their
  Lemma~5.1, with the signs of the direction coordinates reversed. So
  every $u_{0k}$ is one of the two-extensors, and
  $u_{hk} = T(c_h, c_k) - u_{0k} + u_{0h}$ is a linear combination of
  them. Hence the $D$ two-extensors span $\R^D$, and $D$ vectors that
  span $\R^D$ form a basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Row independence under an invertible map of $\R^D$]
  \label{lem:extensor-map-rows}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.linearIndependent_rigidityRow_mapPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-bar-framework, def:rigidity-map-body-bar}
  Let $(G, b)$ be a body-bar framework in $\R^d$, and let $M$ be an
  invertible linear map of $\R^D$. Let $(G, Mb)$ be the framework with
  the vector $M b_e$ on each bar $e$. If the rows of a set of bars are
  linearly independent in $(G, b)$, then they are linearly independent
  in $(G, Mb)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $M^{\top}$ be the transpose of $M$. In $(G, Mb)$ the row of a
  bar $e$ takes a motion $m$ to
  \[
    \langle M b_e,\, m(u_e) - m(v_e) \rangle
    \;=\; \langle b_e,\, M^{\top} m(u_e) - M^{\top} m(v_e) \rangle.
  \]
  So it is the row of $e$ in $(G, b)$, composed with the map $\Phi$ of
  motions that applies $M^{\top}$ to the vector of every body. As
  $M^{\top}$ is invertible, so is $\Phi$. Hence composition with
  $\Phi$ is an injective linear map of the space of linear functionals
  on motions, and it preserves linear independence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent rows at the coordinate segments;
  Jackson--Jord\'an Lemma~5.1]
  \label{lem:endpoint-witness}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.exists_endpoints_linearIndependent_rigidityRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-endpoints, def:graph-sparse}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph, and let $E' \subseteq E(G)$ be a set
  of bars such that $G \restriction E'$ is $(D, D)$-sparse. Then there
  is an endpoint assignment of $G$ at whose endpoint realization the
  rows of the bars in $E'$ are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:k-frame-forest-packing-of-sparse,
    lem:coordinate-extensor-basis, lem:extensor-map-rows,
    lem:k-frame-specialize-li}
  If $E'$ is empty there is nothing to prove, so let $E'$ be
  non-empty. By \cref{lem:k-frame-forest-packing-of-sparse}, $E'$ is
  the union of $D$ pairwise disjoint forests. Index them by the pairs,
  as $F_{ij}$ for $0 \le i < j \le d$. Give each bar $e$ a
  pair $\iota(e)$: the pair $(i, j)$ with $e \in F_{ij}$ if
  $e \in E'$, and any pair otherwise.

  Let $b_e$ be the standard basis vector of $\R^D$ at $\iota(e)$, as
  in \cref{prop:tay-witness-exists}. In $(G, b)$ the rows of the bars
  in $E'$ are linearly independent. Indeed, up to sign the row of $e$
  is $a_e^{(\iota(e))}$ (\cref{sec:body-bar-framework}). The vectors
  $a_e^{(ij)}$ with $e \in F_{ij}$ are linearly independent over $\R$,
  as the sets $F_{ij}$ are forests, by the argument of
  \cref{lem:k-frame-specialize-li}.

  Let $M$ be the linear map of $\R^D$ that sends the standard basis
  vector at $(i, j)$ to $T(c_i, c_j)$. It is invertible, by
  \cref{lem:coordinate-extensor-basis}. Let $q$ give each bar $e$ with
  $\iota(e) = (i, j)$ the endpoints $c_i$ and $c_j$. Then
  $M b_e = T(c_i, c_j)$ for every bar $e$, so $(G, Mb)$ is the
  endpoint realization of $q$. Its rows at the bars of $E'$ are
  linearly independent, by \cref{lem:extensor-map-rows}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Independence at a generic endpoint assignment]
  \label{thm:bodybar-generic-independence}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.linearIndependent_rigidityRow_ofEndpoints_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-endpoints, def:graph-sparse}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph, and let $q$ be an endpoint
  assignment of $G$ that is generic for row independence. Let
  $E' \subseteq E(G)$. Then the rows of the bars in $E'$ at the
  endpoint realization of $q$ are linearly independent if and only if
  $G \restriction E'$ is $(D, D)$-sparse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:endpoint-witness, lem:k-frame-pi-finrank,
    lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}
  If $G \restriction E'$ is $(D, D)$-sparse, then the rows of $E'$ are
  linearly independent at some endpoint assignment
  (\cref{lem:endpoint-witness}), and so at $q$, as $q$ is generic.

  By the argument of \cref{lem:tay-isSparse-of-independent}, the
  converse holds at every endpoint assignment. Suppose that the rows of
  $E'$ are linearly independent, and let $E''$ be a non-empty subset
  of $E'$. Let $A_{E''}$ be the span of the vectors $a_e$ with
  $e \in E''$. Up to sign, the row of a bar $e \in E''$ is
  $\sum_c (b_e)_c\, a_e^{(c)}$, which lies in $A_{E''}^D$. This space
  has dimension $D\, r(E'')$ (\cref{lem:k-frame-pi-finrank} and
  \cref{lem:k-frame-incidence-finrank-rk}), and it contains the
  $|E''|$ linearly independent rows of $E''$. As
  $r(E'') \le |V(E'')| - 1$, we get $|E''| \le D|V(E'')| - D$. So
  $G \restriction E'$ is $(D, D)$-sparse.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Tay's theorem at every generic endpoint
  assignment; Tay, Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{cor:bodybar-generic-tay}
  \lean{Graph.BodyBarFramework.isIndependent_ofEndpoints_iff,
    Graph.BodyBarFramework.isIndependent_and_isInfinitesimallyRigid_ofEndpoints_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-endpoints, def:independent-body-bar,
    def:infinitesimally-rigid-body-bar, def:graph-sparse}
  Let $G$ be a finite multigraph, and let $q$ be an endpoint
  assignment of $G$ that is generic for row independence. Then the
  endpoint realization of $q$ is independent if and only if $G$ is
  $(D, D)$-sparse. It is isostatic, that is, independent and
  infinitesimally rigid, if and only if $G$ is $(D, D)$-tight.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:bodybar-generic-independence}
  Take $E' = E(G)$ in \cref{thm:bodybar-generic-independence}. As
  $G \restriction E(G) = G$, the rows of all the bars are linearly
  independent, that is, the realization is independent
  (\cref{def:independent-body-bar}), if and only if $G$ is
  $(D, D)$-sparse. If the realization is independent, the rank of its
  rigidity map is $|E(G)|$. Then it is infinitesimally rigid if and
  only if $|E(G)| = D|V(G)| - D$
  (\cref{def:infinitesimally-rigid-body-bar}). So it is isostatic if
  and only if $G$ is $(D, D)$-sparse with $|E(G)| = D|V(G)| - D$, that
  is, $(D, D)$-tight (\cref{def:graph-sparse}).
\end{proof}

So Tay's theorem (\cref{thm:tay-witness}) holds at every generic
endpoint assignment. So does rigidity from spanning trees. Let $G$ be
a finite multigraph that has at least one body and contains $D$
edge-disjoint spanning trees, and let $F$ be their union. Then $F$ has
$D(|V(G)| - 1)$ bars. As $F$ is a union of $D$ forests,
$G \restriction F$ is $(D, D)$-sparse (\cref{lem:union-indep-iff} and
\cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}). By
\cref{thm:bodybar-generic-independence}, the rows of $F$ are linearly
independent at every generic endpoint assignment. So the rank of the
rigidity map there is at least $D|V(G)| - D$. It is at most
$D|V(G)| - D$ (the remarks after \cref{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}), so
every generic endpoint realization of $G$ is infinitesimally rigid.

By \cref{thm:bodybar-generic-independence} and
\cref{thm:unionPow-cycle-indep-iff-sparse}, a set of bars has linearly
independent rows at a generic endpoint assignment if and only if it is
independent in the union of $D$ copies of the cycle matroid of $G$.
Tay proved this for his generic frameworks, in which the vectors on
the bars are independent indeterminates~\cite[Corollary~5.4]{tay1984}.
So the rank of the rigidity map at a generic endpoint assignment is
the rank of this union. For bars that are segments, Jackson and
Jord\'an's Theorem~5.2, which they credit to Tay, gives this rank as
\[
  D\,(|V(G)| - 1) - \max_P \bigl( D\,(|P| - 1) - e_G(P) \bigr),
\]
where $P$ ranges over the partitions of $V(G)$, and $e_G(P)$ is the
number of bars that join different parts of
$P$~\cite[\S5]{jacksonJordan2009}. That the rank of the union has
this value is their Theorem~4.1(a), which they credit to Tutte and
Nash-Williams.

\subsection{Every generic body-and-hinge realization}
\label{sec:generic-lift-bodyhinge}

As in \cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix}, $d$ is the dimension,
$D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and $K$ is a field. Jackson and Jord\'an realize
a multigraph $G = (V, E)$ as a body-and-hinge framework by giving each
edge a hinge, a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace of
$\R^d$~\cite[\S3.3]{jacksonJordan2009}. This is a body-hinge framework
in the sense of \cref{def:hinge-constraint}. We give each hinge by
$d - 1$ points of $K^d$ that span it (\cref{def:generic-hinge-points}).
So the realization parameters are the $d(d-1)\,|E|$ coordinates of
these points, and ``almost all realizations'' refers to the choice of
the points. The coordinates of the supporting extensor of a hinge are
polynomials in the coordinates of its points
(\cref{lem:hinge-rows-polynomial-in-points}). So, as for normals and
endpoints, the choices of hinge points that are not generic lie in the
zero set of a nonzero polynomial
(\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-abundance}), and over an infinite field
generic choices exist.

Our main result is Jackson and Jord\'an's rank formula, KT
Proposition~2.3 (\cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}), at every generic
hinge-point assignment of a multigraph, with parallel edges and
loops allowed (\cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank}). Jackson and Jord\'an
prove the formula for every multigraph (their Theorem~6.1). Our proof
starts from the realization that KT Theorem~5.6 gives for every
multigraph (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}). At deficiency zero the formula gives the
theorem of Tay and Whiteley at every generic hinge-point assignment
(\cref{cor:bodyhinge-generic-tree-packing}).

For one hinge-point assignment at the target rank
$D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ we start, as for normals,
from KT Theorem~5.6 for multigraphs
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}). It gives a hinge-coplanar panel
realization at the target rank, whose hinges are nonzero. The hinge of
an edge with ends $u$ and $v$ lies in the panels of $u$ and $v$, so it
is an extensor of $d - 1$ vectors of the hyperplane $n_u^\perp$ of
$K^{d+1}$, where $n_u$ is the normal of $u$
(\cref{lem:extensor-in-panel-decomposable}). If these vectors lie in
the hyperplane of the vectors with last coordinate $0$, the hinge lies
at infinity. Then no points of $K^d$ span it, as their homogeneous
coordinate vectors have last coordinate $1$
(\cref{def:homogeneous-coords}). Such a hinge is the body-and-hinge
counterpart of the line at infinity of \cref{sec:generic-lift-bodybar}.
The construction of the realization does not keep its hinges off the
hyperplane at infinity, so we change coordinates. One invertible
linear map of $K^{d+1}$ moves every hinge off the hyperplane at
infinity (\cref{lem:simultaneous-affine-position}). Jackson and
Jord\'an make a similar choice of coordinates in the proof of their
Lemma~7.1, where they choose the origin off finitely many hyperplanes.
The map induces an invertible linear map of the screw space, which does
not change the rank of the rigidity matrix (\cref{lem:screw-map-rows}).
Each moved hinge is spanned by points of $K^d$
(\cref{lem:extensor-affine-representation}), and these points form a
hinge-point assignment at the target rank
(\cref{lem:hinge-point-witness}). Jackson and Jord\'an construct a
realization at the largest rank directly, with each hinge spanned by
$d - 1$ of the vertices $0, e_1, \dots, e_d$ of a simplex (their
Theorem~6.4). We use KT Theorem~5.6 instead, which the earlier chapters
prove.

\begin{definition}[Generic hinge-point assignment]
  \label{def:generic-hinge-points}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingePointRow,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingePointRow_eq_panelRow,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.IsGenericHingePoints,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isGenericHingePoints_iff_of_recordsLinks,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isGenericHingePoints_congr}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:rigidity-matrix,
    def:affine-subspace-extensor}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph. A \emph{hinge-point assignment} $q$
  of $G$ gives each edge $e$ a family $q_e$ of $d - 1$ points
  $q_{e,1}, \dots, q_{e,d-1}$ of $K^d$. Its framework $(G, q)$ is the
  body-hinge framework whose hinge at $e$ has the supporting extensor
  $C(q_e) = C(q_{e,1}, \dots, q_{e,d-1})$
  (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}). The \emph{annihilator-row
  family} of $q$ is the family of linear forms
  \[
    S \;\mapsto\; C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t,
    \qquad \text{where } x = S(u) - S(v) \text{ and } C = C(q_e),
  \]
  on screw assignments $S$, indexed by an edge $e$ of $G$, with ends
  $u$ and $v$ taken in a fixed order, and a pair $(t, t')$ of indices
  of the standard basis of $\bigwedge^{d-1}K^{d+1}$. Here $y_t$ is the
  coordinate of $y$ at the $t$-th basis vector. The assignment $q$ is
  \emph{generic for row independence} if every subfamily of the
  annihilator-row family that is linearly independent at some
  hinge-point assignment is linearly independent at $q$.
\end{definition}

The hinge of $e$ in $(G, q)$ is the affine span of the points of
$q_e$. It is nondegenerate if $C(q_e) \neq 0$ (the remarks after
\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). This holds exactly when the points
of $q_e$ are affinely independent (the remark after
\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}). The forms are those of
\cref{def:generic-normals}, with $C(q_e)$ in place of the panel support
extensor. Exchanging the ends of $e$ changes the signs of the forms at
$e$, so genericity does not depend on the order of the ends. As a form
at $e$ reads only the points of $q_e$, genericity depends only on the
points that $q$ gives the edges of $G$. When the
hinge of $e$ is nondegenerate, the forms at $e$ span the same space as
the rows of $R(G, q)$ at $e$, by the argument in the proof of
\cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}. Genericity transfers linear
independence only from hinge-point assignments. So a witness with a
hinge at infinity has to be moved first, as the opening of this
subsection explains.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization writes $d = k + 1$, as in the note after
  \cref{def:hinge-constraint}. As in
  \cref{fmlnote:generic-normals-selector}, it indexes the family by all
  the edge labels, and reads the ends of each label from an endpoint
  selector, not from $G$. A hinge-point assignment gives points to
  every label, not only to the edges of $G$. A body-hinge framework has
  no selector of its own, so genericity over $G$ asks about the
  subfamilies indexed by edges of $G$ at every selector that gives each
  edge of $G$ its two ends. The first pin is the family, and the third
  is genericity. The second identifies the family with the annihilator
  rows of the framework $(G, q)$, read with the same selector, for
  every multigraph $G$; it holds by definition. The fourth shows that
  genericity is the condition at any one such selector, as exchanging
  the ends of an edge changes signs only, and the fifth that it reads
  only the points at the edges of $G$. The pinned lemmas on abundance
  and existence take $G$ and finite sets of bodies and labels. Their
  polynomial has $d(d-1)$ variables for each label, and is a product
  over all the selectors, finitely many. The pinned lemma on
  nondegeneracy concludes at the edges of $G$ with two distinct ends,
  not at every label.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[The hinge extensors are polynomial in the hinge points]
  \label{lem:hinge-rows-polynomial-in-points}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.hingeExtensorPoly_eval}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:affine-subspace-extensor, def:homogeneous-coords,
    def:screw-space}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $E$ finite, let $e$ be an edge
  of $G$, and let $t$ be an index of the standard basis of
  $\bigwedge^{d-1}K^{d+1}$. Then there is a polynomial in the
  $d(d-1)|E|$ coordinates of a hinge-point assignment of $G$ whose
  value at every hinge-point assignment $q$ is the coordinate
  $C(q_e)_t$ of $C(q_e)$ at the $t$-th basis vector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:extensor, def:plucker-coords, lem:plucker-expansion}
  Let $A(q_e)$ be the $(d-1) \times (d+1)$ matrix whose $i$-th row is
  the homogeneous coordinate vector $\bar q_{e,i} = (q_{e,i}, 1)$
  (\cref{def:homogeneous-coords}). As $C(q_e)$ is the join
  $\bar q_{e,1} \vee \cdots \vee \bar q_{e,d-1}$, its coordinate at
  $t$ is the minor of $A(q_e)$ on the columns of $t$
  (\cref{lem:plucker-expansion}). Each entry of $A(q_e)$ is a coordinate
  of $q$ or the constant $1$. So the same minor of the matrix of
  indeterminates is a polynomial, and its value at $q$ is the minor of
  $A(q_e)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pins do not use the Pl\"ucker coordinates or
  \cref{lem:plucker-expansion}. The pin computes the coordinate
  at $t$ directly, as the pairing of the join with the dual basis vector
  at $t$, which a library lemma evaluates as the minor. Its polynomial is
  the minor of the matrix of indeterminates
  (\texttt{hingeExtensorPoly}), with $d(d-1)$ variables for each edge
  label.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Abundance of generic hinge-point assignments]
  \label{lem:generic-hinge-points-abundance}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_isGenericHingePoints_abundance}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points, lem:affine-indep-iff}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V$ and $E$ finite. Then there
  is a nonzero polynomial $P$ in the $d(d-1)|E|$ coordinates of a
  hinge-point assignment such that every hinge-point assignment $q$
  with $P(q) \neq 0$ is generic for row independence and gives each
  edge $d - 1$ affinely independent points. In particular, the
  hinge-point assignments that are not generic, or that give some edge
  affinely dependent points, lie in the zero set of a nonzero
  polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:hinge-rows-polynomial-in-points,
    def:affine-subspace-extensor}
  Fix the standard basis of the space of screw assignments. In its
  dual basis, each coordinate of a member of the annihilator-row
  family at an edge $e$ is $0$ or $\pm$ a coordinate of $C(q_e)$. So
  it is a polynomial in the coordinates of $q$
  (\cref{lem:hinge-rows-polynomial-in-points}).

  The argument of \cref{lem:generic-placement-abundance} now applies.
  The family has finitely many indices, as $E$ is finite. Let $s$ be a
  set of indices whose members are linearly independent at some
  hinge-point assignment $q^s$. Some $|s| \times |s|$ minor of the
  coordinate matrix of these members is nonzero at $q^s$. As a
  polynomial $P_s$ in the coordinates of $q$, it is nonzero, and the
  members indexed by $s$ are linearly independent at every $q$ with
  $P_s(q) \neq 0$.

  For the second property, let $q^0$ give every edge the first $d - 1$
  standard basis vectors of $K^d$. They are linearly independent, hence
  affinely independent. So $C(q^0_e) \neq 0$ for every edge $e$ (the
  remark after \cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}). Let $t$ be an
  index with $C(q^0_e)_t \neq 0$, the same for every $e$. Let $R_e$ be
  the polynomial of \cref{lem:hinge-rows-polynomial-in-points} whose
  value at $q$ is $C(q_e)_t$. It is nonzero, as $R_e(q^0) \neq 0$. If
  $R_e(q) \neq 0$, then $C(q_e) \neq 0$, so the points of $q_e$ are
  affinely independent.

  The product $P$ of the polynomials $P_s$ and $R_e$ is nonzero, as a
  finite product of nonzero polynomials. Every $q$ with $P(q) \neq 0$
  has both properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Existence of generic hinge-point assignments]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_isGenericHingePoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points}
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph with $V$
  and $E$ finite. Then some hinge-point assignment of $G$ is generic
  for row independence and gives each edge $d - 1$ affinely
  independent points.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:generic-hinge-points-abundance}
  The polynomial $P$ of \cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-abundance} is
  nonzero. Over an infinite field a nonzero polynomial has a non-root,
  and every non-root of $P$ is such an assignment.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic hinge-point assignments have nondegenerate
  hinges]
  \label{lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ofHinge_ne_zero_of_isGenericHingePoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points, def:hinge-constraint}
  Let $d \ge 2$, and let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph, with parallel
  edges and loops allowed. Let $q$ be a hinge-point assignment of $G$
  that is generic for row independence. Then the hinge of $(G, q)$ at
  every edge with two distinct ends is nondegenerate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:affine-indep-iff, def:affine-subspace-extensor}
  Let $e$ be an edge of $G$, with ends $u \neq v$. Let $q^0$ give every
  edge the first $d - 1$ standard basis vectors of $K^d$. They are
  linearly independent, hence affinely independent, so
  $C^0 = C(q^0_e)$ is not zero (the remark after
  \cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}). Let $t$ be an index with
  $C^0_t \neq 0$. As $d \ge 2$, the screw space has dimension
  $D \ge 3$, so there is an index $t' \neq t$. As $u \neq v$, there is
  a screw assignment $S$ for which $S(u) - S(v)$ is the $t'$-th basis
  vector. At $S$ the member of the annihilator-row family of $q^0$ at
  $(e, t, t')$ takes the value $C^0_t \neq 0$.

  So this member alone is a linearly independent family at $q^0$. As
  $q$ is generic, the member of the family of $q$ at $(e, t, t')$ is
  not zero. If $C(q_e)$ were zero, every member of the family of $q$
  at $e$ would be zero. So the hinge of $e$ in $(G, q)$ is
  nondegenerate.
\end{proof}

Jackson and Jord\'an call a realization of $G$ generic if its rigidity
matrix, and the submatrix of the rows of every set of edges, have the
largest rank over all realizations of $G$~\cite[\S6]{jacksonJordan2009}.
This is the genericity of \cref{def:dof-generic}. Each hinge of a
realization is a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace, the affine span
of $d - 1$ affinely independent points. A different choice of the
points does not change the hinge constraint (the remark after
\cref{def:hinge-constraint}). So the realizations of $G$ are, up to
this choice, the frameworks $(G, q)$ whose hinges are nondegenerate,
and \cref{def:dof-generic} asks this of the hinges at the links
between distinct bodies.

\begin{lemma}[Generic for row independence implies generic]
  \label{lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isMaxRankHingePoints_of_isGenericHingePoints,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_isMaxRankHingePoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points, def:dof-generic,
    lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate,
    lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $G$ be a multigraph with $V$ and $E$ finite, loops
  allowed, and let $q$ be a hinge-point assignment of $G$ that is
  generic for row independence. Then $(G, q)$ is generic in the sense of
  \cref{def:dof-generic}. If $K$ is infinite, some hinge-point assignment
  of $G$ is generic in this sense.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate,
    lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}
  By \cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate}, every hinge of
  $(G, q)$ at a link between distinct bodies is nondegenerate. Let $F$
  be a set of edges, and let $(G, q')$ be a realization. Replace the
  points of each degenerate hinge of $q'$, which lies at a loop, by a
  fixed affinely independent family. This changes no row, as the rows
  of a loop are zero, and gives every hinge a nondegenerate one. The
  members of the annihilator-row family of the new assignment at $F$
  span the rows of its rigidity matrix at $F$. So some of these
  members, as many as the rank of these rows, are linearly independent.
  As $q$ is generic, the members of the family of $q$ with the same
  indices are linearly independent too. They lie in the span of the
  rows of $R(G, q)$ at $F$ (the remark after
  \cref{def:hinge-row-block}), whose dimension is therefore at least
  that rank. Over all hinge-point assignments with a degenerate hinge
  allowed, the rows at $F$ can have a larger rank, as a degenerate
  hinge welds its two bodies together (the remarks after
  \cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}); this is why the realizations are
  required to be nondegenerate. Over an infinite field, a hinge-point
  assignment generic for row independence exists
  (\cref{lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}), and it is generic in this
  sense.
\end{proof}

We now construct a hinge-point assignment at the target rank. For
$d = 3$, part~(1) of \cref{thm:projective-invariance} shows that a
linear automorphism of the screw space, applied to every hinge, does
not change the rank of the rigidity matrix. The next lemma shows this
in every dimension, by comparing the rows.

\begin{lemma}[Row rank under a change of screw coordinates]
  \label{lem:screw-map-rows}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_mapExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:rigidity-matrix,
    thm:projective-invariance}
  Let $(G, p)$ be a body-hinge framework in $K^d$, and let $\Lambda$ be
  a linear automorphism of the screw space $\bigwedge^{d-1}K^{d+1}$.
  Let $(G, \Lambda p)$ be the body-hinge framework on $G$ whose
  supporting extensor at each edge $e$ is $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$. Then
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, \Lambda p) = \operatorname{rank} R(G, p)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-row-block}
  Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u$ and $v$. The rows of $R(G, p)$ at
  $e$ span the linear forms $S \mapsto r(S(u) - S(v))$ on screw
  assignments, where $r$ ranges over the linear forms on the screw
  space that vanish on $C(p(e))$ (the remark after
  \cref{def:hinge-row-block}). A form $r$ vanishes on $C(p(e))$ if and
  only if $r \circ \Lambda^{-1}$ vanishes on $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$. So
  the rows of $R(G, \Lambda p)$ at $e$ span the forms
  $S \mapsto r(\Lambda^{-1}(S(u) - S(v)))$. These are the forms at $e$
  for $(G, p)$, composed with the map $\Phi \colon S \mapsto
  \Lambda^{-1} \circ S$ of screw assignments.

  The map $\Phi$ is linear and invertible. So composition with $\Phi$
  is an invertible linear map of the space of linear forms on screw
  assignments. It maps the row space of $R(G, p)$ onto that of
  $R(G, \Lambda p)$, and the two have the same dimension. In
  \cref{lem:extensor-map-rows} the corresponding map sends the row of
  each bar to the row of the same bar. Here it sends the rows at each
  edge onto the rows at that edge, but not each form
  $C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t$ of the annihilator-row family
  (\cref{def:generic-hinge-points}) to such a form. These forms are
  indexed by pairs of coordinates of the screw space, and $\Lambda$
  does not preserve the coordinates.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Points that span an extensor off the hyperplane at
  infinity]
  \label{lem:extensor-affine-representation}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_affineSubspaceExtensor_eq_smul_extensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:affine-subspace-extensor, def:extensor,
    def:homogeneous-coords}
  Let $u_1, \dots, u_{d-1} \in K^{d+1}$, and suppose that some $u_i$
  has nonzero last coordinate. Then there are points
  $p_1, \dots, p_{d-1}$ of $K^d$ and a nonzero scalar $c$ with
  \[
    C(p_1, \dots, p_{d-1}) \;=\; c\,(u_1 \vee \cdots \vee u_{d-1}).
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $\lambda \neq 0$ be the last coordinate of $u_i$, and replace
  $u_i$ by $\lambda^{-1} u_i$. As the join is linear in each vector,
  this multiplies it by $\lambda^{-1}$ (the remarks after
  \cref{def:extensor}). Now add to each other $u_j$ the vector
  $(1 - \mu_j)\, u_i$, where $\mu_j$ is the last coordinate of $u_j$.
  Adding a multiple of one vector to another does not change the join,
  and $u_j$ gets the last coordinate $1$. So every vector now has last
  coordinate $1$, and is the homogeneous coordinate vector $\bar p_j$
  of a point $p_j$ of $K^d$ (\cref{def:homogeneous-coords}). Hence
  $C(p_1, \dots, p_{d-1}) = \bar p_1 \vee \cdots \vee \bar p_{d-1}$ is
  $\lambda^{-1} (u_1 \vee \cdots \vee u_{d-1})$
  (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moving finitely many vectors off the hyperplane at
  infinity]
  \label{lem:simultaneous-affine-position}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_linearEquiv_forall_last_ne_zero}
  \leanok
  Let $K$ be infinite, and let $w_1, \dots, w_m$ be nonzero vectors of
  $K^{d+1}$. Then there is an invertible linear map $g$ of $K^{d+1}$
  such that every $g(w_i)$ has nonzero last coordinate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For each $i$ the linear form $x \mapsto w_i \cdot x$ is nonzero, as
  $w_i \neq 0$. So the product of these forms is a nonzero polynomial
  in the coordinates of $x$. As $K$ is infinite, it has a non-root $n$,
  and $w_i \cdot n \neq 0$ for every $i$. Let $\varphi$ be the linear
  form $x \mapsto n \cdot x$, and let $\psi$ be the last coordinate.
  Both are nonzero, so their kernels have dimension $d$. Extending a
  linear isomorphism of $\ker \varphi$ onto $\ker \psi$, we obtain an
  invertible linear map $g$ of $K^{d+1}$ with
  $g(\ker \varphi) = \ker \psi$. If $g(w_i)$ had last coordinate $0$,
  then $g(w_i) \in \ker \psi$, so $w_i \in \ker \varphi$, which is
  false.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A nonzero extensor in a panel is decomposable]
  \label{lem:extensor-in-panel-decomposable}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_extensor_eq_of_extensorInPanel}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:genuine-hinge-realization, def:extensor}
  Let $d \ge 2$ and let $C \neq 0$ be an extensor that lies in the
  panel with normal $n$ (\cref{def:genuine-hinge-realization}). Then
  $C = x_1 \vee \cdots \vee x_{d-1}$ for some vectors
  $x_1, \dots, x_{d-1}$ of $K^{d+1}$, with $x_1 \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  By definition, $C = x_1 \vee \cdots \vee x_{d-1}$ for some vectors of
  $n^\perp$. As $C \neq 0$, the vectors are linearly independent (the
  remark after \cref{def:extensor}), so $x_1 \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent annihilator rows at some hinge-point
  assignment]
  \label{lem:hinge-point-witness}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.exists_hingePoints_independent_hingePointRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points, def:D-deficiency,
    thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph, lem:extensor-in-panel-decomposable}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Then there is a
  hinge-point assignment $q$ of $G$ at which some
  $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ members of the
  annihilator-row family of $q$ are linearly independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph, lem:extensor-in-panel-decomposable,
    lem:simultaneous-affine-position,
    lem:extensor-affine-representation, lem:screw-map-rows,
    lem:independent-rows-of-rank, def:induced-screw-map}
  Let $N = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. By KT
  Theorem~5.6 for multigraphs (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-multigraph}),
  $G$ has a hinge-coplanar panel realization $(G, p)$ at the target
  rank $N$. Its hinge $C_e$ at every edge $e$ is nonzero and lies in a
  panel, so $C_e = w_{e,1} \vee \cdots \vee w_{e,d-1}$ for some
  vectors $w_{e,i}$ of $K^{d+1}$ with $w_{e,1} \neq 0$
  (\cref{lem:extensor-in-panel-decomposable}).

  By \cref{lem:simultaneous-affine-position}, there is an invertible
  linear map $g$ of $K^{d+1}$ such that $g(w_{e,1})$ has nonzero last
  coordinate for every edge $e$. Let $\Lambda = \Lambda_g$ be the screw
  map of $g$ (\cref{def:induced-screw-map}), a linear automorphism of
  the screw space. The hinge of $(G, \Lambda p)$ at $e$ is
  $g w_{e,1} \vee \cdots \vee g w_{e,d-1}$. The rigidity matrix of
  $(G, \Lambda p)$ has rank $N$ (\cref{lem:screw-map-rows}), and its
  hinges are nondegenerate, as $\Lambda$ is injective. By
  \cref{lem:extensor-affine-representation}, there are points
  $q_{e,1}, \dots, q_{e,d-1}$ of $K^d$ and a scalar $c_e \neq 0$ with
  $C(q_e) = c_e\, \Lambda\, C_e$. Let $q$ be the hinge-point
  assignment that gives each edge $e$ the points $q_e$.

  At each edge $e$ of $(G, \Lambda p)$, consider the forms
  $S \mapsto C_t\, x_{t'} - C_{t'}\, x_t$ of
  \cref{def:generic-hinge-points}, with $C = \Lambda\, C_e$.
  As the hinge is nondegenerate, they span the same space as the rows
  of $R(G, \Lambda p)$ at $e$ (the argument in the proof of
  \cref{lem:rows-polynomial-in-normals}). So these forms, at all the
  edges, span the row space of $R(G, \Lambda p)$, of dimension $N$, and
  some $N$ of them are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:independent-rows-of-rank}). The member of the
  annihilator-row family of $q$ at an index $(e, t, t')$ is $c_e$ times the form at $(e, t, t')$, as
  $C(q_e) = c_e\, \Lambda\, C_e$. Multiplying the members of a
  family by nonzero scalars does not affect their linear independence.
  So the members of the family of $q$ at these $N$ indices are linearly
  independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The rank formula at every generic hinge-point
  assignment]
  \label{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofHinge_of_isGenericHingePoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:generic-hinge-points, def:hinge-constraint,
    def:D-deficiency}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Let $q$ be a hinge-point assignment of $G$ that is generic for row independence.
  Then
  \[
    \operatorname{rank} R(G,q)
      \;=\; D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:hinge-point-witness,
    lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate,
    lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  The hinges of $(G, q)$ at the edges with distinct ends are
  nondegenerate (\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate}), and
  \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound} asks nothing of a loop. So
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \le D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).

  For the reverse inequality, let $q^0$ be a hinge-point assignment at
  which some $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ members of the
  annihilator-row family are linearly independent
  (\cref{lem:hinge-point-witness}). As $q$ is generic, the members of
  the family of $q$ with the same indices are linearly independent.
  Each of them lies in the row space of $R(G, q)$. Indeed, a member at
  an edge $e$ with ends $u$ and $v$ is a linear form in $S(u) - S(v)$
  that vanishes at $C(q_e)$, and so a linear combination of the rows of
  $R(G, q)$ at $e$ (the remark after \cref{def:hinge-row-block}). Hence
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) \ge D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[KT Proposition 2.3]
  \label{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.tfae_finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofHinge_deficiency_of_isMaxRankHingePoints,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.tfae_finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofHinge_deficiency}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:dof-generic, def:generic-hinge-points, def:D-deficiency,
    def:matroid-MG, thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank, thm:def-eq-corank,
    cor:dof-generic-realization, lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Let $q$ be a hinge-point assignment of $G$ that is
  generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic}, and let $c$ be an
  integer. Then the following are equivalent, as Katoh and Tanigawa
  state~\cite[p.~655]{katohTanigawa2011}, crediting Jackson and
  Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2009}:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1) - c$;
    \item[(ii)] $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = c$;
    \item[(iii)] $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) = D(|V|-1) - c$.
  \end{enumerate}
  The same holds at every $q$ that is generic for row independence
  (\cref{def:generic-hinge-points}), the form the rest of the
  development uses.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:dof-generic-realization, thm:def-eq-corank,
    thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank, lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}
  By \cref{cor:dof-generic-realization},
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
  which is (i)~$\Leftrightarrow$~(ii). By \cref{thm:def-eq-corank},
  $\operatorname{rank} M(\tilde G) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$,
  which is (ii)~$\Leftrightarrow$~(iii). A $q$ that is generic for row
  independence is generic in this sense
  (\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}), and
  \cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank} gives the formula there directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A generic realization has $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ degrees of freedom]
  \label{cor:dof-generic-realization}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_span_rigidityRows_ofHinge_of_isMaxRankHingePoints,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.dof_ofHinge_of_isMaxRankHingePoints}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:dof-generic, def:D-deficiency, thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank,
    lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank, lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Let $q$ be a hinge-point assignment of $G$ that is
  generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic}. Then
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
  So $(G,q)$ has $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ degrees of freedom.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank, lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank,
    lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Some $q_0$ is generic for row independence
  (\cref{lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}), so by
  \cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank},
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q_0) = D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
  The hinges of $q_0$ at the links between distinct bodies are
  nondegenerate, so $q_0$ is a realization
  (\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-nondegenerate}). As $q$ is generic in
  the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic}, the rank of $R(G,q)$ is at least
  that of $R(G,q_0)$. It is at most
  $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ by
  \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}, as the hinges of $q$ at
  the links between distinct bodies are nondegenerate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every generic body-and-hinge realization of a
  rigid graph; Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{cor:bodyhinge-generic-rigid}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_ofHinge_isMaxRankHingePoints_iff,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_ofHinge_isGenericHingePoints_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:dof-generic, def:generic-hinge-points, def:hinge-constraint,
    def:D-deficiency}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Then $(G, q)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid at every hinge-point assignment $q$ of $G$ that
  is generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic} if and only if
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. The same holds with the
  assignments that are generic for row independence
  (\cref{def:generic-hinge-points}) in place of these.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:dof-generic-realization, lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank,
    thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank, lem:exists-generic-hinge-points,
    lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count,
    lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}
  Suppose first that $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, and let $q$ be
  generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic}. By
  \cref{cor:dof-generic-realization},
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1)$, so
  $\dim Z(G,q) = D|V| - D(|V|-1) = D$. Hence every infinitesimal
  motion of $(G, q)$ is trivial
  (\cref{lem:isInfRigidOn-of-relative-count}, with $W = V$).

  Conversely, suppose that $(G, q)$ is infinitesimally rigid at every
  such $q$. As $K$ is infinite, some hinge-point assignment $q$ is
  generic in this sense (\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}).
  Then $(G, q)$ is infinitesimally rigid, so its motions are constant
  on $V$, and $\dim Z(G,q) = D$
  (\cref{lem:case-II-placement-old-rows-extract}, with $W = V$). As the
  screw assignments form a space of dimension $D|V|$,
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G,q) = D(|V|-1)$, and
  \cref{cor:dof-generic-realization} gives
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$.

  At the assignments that are generic for row independence the
  argument is the same, with \cref{thm:bodyhinge-generic-rank} in place
  of \cref{cor:dof-generic-realization}, and with an assignment that is
  generic for row independence, which exists as $K$ is infinite
  (\cref{lem:exists-generic-hinge-points}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Generic body-and-hinge rigidity from spanning
  trees; Tay, Whiteley, Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{cor:bodyhinge-generic-tree-packing}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_ofHinge_isMaxRankHingePoints_iff_spanningTrees,
    CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigidOn_ofHinge_isGenericHingePoints_iff_spanningTrees}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:dof-generic, def:generic-hinge-points, def:matroid-MG}
  Let $d \ge 2$, let $K$ be an infinite field, and let $G = (V, E)$ be
  a multigraph, with parallel edges and loops allowed, with $V$ finite
  and nonempty. Then $(G, q)$ is
  infinitesimally rigid at every hinge-point assignment $q$ of $G$ that
  is generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic} if and only if
  $\tilde G = (D-1) \cdot G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees.
  The same holds with the assignments that are generic for row
  independence (\cref{def:generic-hinge-points}) in place of these.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{cor:bodyhinge-generic-rigid,
    lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}
  By \cref{cor:bodyhinge-generic-rigid}, the condition on the generic
  hinge-point assignments, in either sense, holds if and only if
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. As $D \ge 3$, KT
  Proposition~2.2 (\cref{lem:deficiency-zero-iff-tree-packing}) shows
  that this holds if and only if $\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint
  spanning trees.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization states \cref{prop:rigidity-matrix-prop11} and the
  two corollaries above twice: first at the assignments that are
  generic in the sense of \cref{def:dof-generic}, Katoh and Tanigawa's,
  then at those that are generic for row independence. The
  proposition's second form follows from its first
  (\cref{lem:generic-hinge-points-max-rank}). It writes $d = k + 1$, as
  in the note after
  \cref{def:hinge-constraint}. The pinned results of this subsection
  from \cref{lem:hinge-point-witness} on take the dimension in
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ as a separate parameter, as in the
  note after \cref{def:panel-support-extensor}. They assume that every
  body is a vertex of $G$, and state rigidity on $V(G)$, which is then
  the set of all bodies. They assume nothing of the edge labels
  (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}): they apply to every multigraph on the
  bodies, a single edge, a tree or a double edge among them. The pinned
  witness lemma takes a selector that gives
  each edge of $G$ its two ends. The rank formula and its corollaries
  take none, as genericity over $G$ asks about every such selector (the
  note after \cref{def:generic-hinge-points}).
\end{fmlnote}

Tay~\cite[\S7]{tay1989} and Whiteley~\cite[Theorem~10]{whiteley1988}
characterized, independently, the multigraphs $G$ that have an
infinitesimally rigid body-and-hinge realization in $\R^d$: those for
which $(D-1) \cdot G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning trees.
Jackson and Jord\'an state this as their Corollary~6.3, a consequence
of their Theorem~6.1~\cite[\S6]{jacksonJordan2009}. The body-hinge
chapter proves the characterization in its existence form, for
frameworks given by bundles of bars (\cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}).
\Cref{cor:bodyhinge-generic-tree-packing} is its generic form for
frameworks whose hinges are spanned by points, for multigraphs.
