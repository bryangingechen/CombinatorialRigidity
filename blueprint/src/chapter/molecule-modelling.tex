\section{Projective duality and the molecule modelling equivalence}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling}

The molecule application of the conjecture rests on a chain of three
equivalent rigidity models for a graph $G$ of minimum degree at least
two, all in $\R^3$:
\[
  \text{bar-joint framework of } G^2
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \text{molecular (hinge-concurrent body-hinge) framework of } G
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \text{panel-hinge framework of } G .
\]
The first equivalence is the modelling fact that a molecule --- atoms
joined by covalent bonds --- is a bar-joint framework on the square
graph exactly when it is a body-hinge framework whose hinges at each
atom meet at the atom's centre (Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1999}, in the
form used by Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008}). The second is
projective duality: in $\R^3$ the projective dual sends points to
planes and lines to lines, carrying a hinge-concurrent body-hinge
framework to a panel-hinge framework, and infinitesimal rigidity is
invariant under it (Crapo--Whiteley~\cite{crapoWhiteley1982}, \S3.6).

This chapter states both links at the level of \emph{motion-space
dimensions} (equivalently, rigidity-matrix ranks), not merely as
realizability equivalences. The stronger form is what the rank formula
needs: composed with Theorem~5.6 (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6}, at $d = 3$
\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-d3}) and the generic $3$-dimensional bar-joint
rigidity matroid of \cref{sec:bar-joint-3d}, the dimension-carrying
chain yields the molecule rank formula
$r(G^2) = 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
(KT Corollary~5.7, due to Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008}),
assembled in the final chapter with no further combinatorial input.
(With only the realizability equivalences, the rank formula would
instead require the deficiency induction and independent-cover bounds
of~\cite{jacksonJordan2008}, a substantial separate development.) All
the geometry here is specific to $\R^3$: it is where the projective
dual transposes points and planes and where the molecule model lives
(KT \S1.2 and the reconciliation on p.~671).

\subsection{The square graph and molecular frameworks}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-defs}

Katoh and Tanigawa describe two models of a molecule whose atoms are
joined by covalent bonds~\cite[\S1.2]{katohTanigawa2011}. Let $G$ be
the graph of the bonds. In the first model the molecule is a bar-joint
framework on the square of $G$, which joins two atoms when they are at
distance one or two in $G$ (\cref{def:square-graph}). In the second it
is a body-hinge framework on $G$, with the atoms as bodies and the
bonds as hinges, in which all the hinges at an atom pass through its
centre (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}). In the square, an atom and its
neighbours induce a complete subgraph (\cref{lem:square-cliques}),
which acts as a rigid body~\cite[\S2.1]{jacksonJordan2008}.

\begin{definition}[Square of a graph]
  \label{def:square-graph}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.square}
  \leanok
  The \emph{square} of a graph $G = (V,E)$ is the graph
  $G^2 = (V, E^2)$ with
  \[
    E^2 = E \cup \{\, uv \mid u \ne v,\ \exists w \in V \setminus \{u,v\}
      \text{ with } uw, wv \in E \,\},
  \]
  that is, $G$ together with an edge joining every two vertices at
  distance two in $G$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Closed neighbourhoods are cliques of the square]
  \label{lem:square-cliques}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.isClique_closedNeighborSet_square,
        SimpleGraph.exists_mem_closedNeighborSet_of_square_adj}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:square-graph}
  Let $G$ be a graph. For every vertex $v$ of $G$, the closed
  neighbourhood $N[v] = \{v\} \cup N(v)$ induces a complete subgraph of
  $G^2$. Moreover, every edge of $G^2$ has both ends in $N[w]$ for some
  vertex $w$ of $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $x \neq y$ be vertices of $N[v]$. If one of them is $v$, then
  $xy$ is an edge of $G$. Otherwise $v$ is a common neighbour of $x$ and
  $y$. In both cases $xy \in E^2$. Now let $uw$ be an edge of $G^2$. If
  it is an edge of $G$, then $u$ and $w$ lie in $N[u]$. Otherwise $u$
  and $w$ have a common neighbour $v$, and they lie in $N[v]$.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a body-hinge framework \emph{hinge-concurrent}
if all the hinges at each body pass through one point, the centre of the
body~\cite[p.~650]{katohTanigawa2011}. A molecule is
modelled by such a framework, since the bonds at an atom pass through
its centre. Following Whiteley~\cite[Definition~4]{whiteley1999}, we
give the framework by the centres of its bodies.

\begin{definition}[Molecular (hinge-concurrent) framework]
  \label{def:hinge-concurrent}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecularOfCentres}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph, and let $c \colon V \to \R^3$
  assign a \emph{centre} $c(v)$ to each body $v$. The \emph{molecular
  framework} on $(G, c)$ is the body-hinge framework on $G$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) whose supporting extensor at an edge
  $e$ with ends $u$ and $v$ is
  \[
    C\bigl(c(u), c(v)\bigr) \;=\; \bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)
  \]
  (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}), where
  $\bar c(v) = (c(v), 1)$.
\end{definition}

Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u$ and $v$. If $c(u) \neq c(v)$, the
extensor at $e$ is a nonzero extensor of the line through $c(u)$ and
$c(v)$ (the remarks after \cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}), and
this line is the hinge at $e$. So when adjacent bodies have distinct
centres, every hinge at a body $v$ passes through $c(v)$, and the
molecular framework is hinge-concurrent. Conversely, let a body-hinge
framework on $G$ be hinge-concurrent with centres $c$, where
$c(u) \neq c(v)$ at every edge $uv$. The hinge at $uv$ passes through
$c(u)$ and $c(v)$, so it is the line through them, and the framework is
the molecular framework on $(G, c)$. Jackson and Jord\'an call these
frameworks molecular frameworks~\cite[\S2.1]{jacksonJordan2008}, and
Katoh and Tanigawa use their name~\cite[p.~671]{katohTanigawa2011}.

If $c(u) = c(v)$, the extensor at $e$ is zero. The hinge at $e$ is then
degenerate, and it welds $u$ and $v$ together (the remarks after
\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). Whiteley calls a molecular structure
non-degenerate if distinct bodies have distinct
centres~\cite[Definition~4]{whiteley1999}. Where nondegenerate hinges
are needed below, the centres are assumed distinct or in general
position (\cref{def:general-position-placement}).

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal molecular framework records an ordered pair of bodies for
  every edge label, as a formal panel-hinge framework does
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). Its extensor at a label is
  that of the centres of the pair.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Projective invariance and duality}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-projective}

A projective transformation of space acts linearly on homogeneous
coordinates, and through them on the screw space. A collineation sends
points to points, and a correlation sends points to planes. Crapo and
Whiteley~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982} showed that both take the
infinitesimal motions of a body-hinge framework to those of the
transformed framework. So infinitesimal rigidity is a projective
invariant. We prove this for every linear automorphism of the screw
space (\cref{thm:projective-invariance}). We then apply it to a
polarity, which turns a molecular framework into a panel-hinge framework
(\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}).

\begin{theorem}[Projective invariance of infinitesimal rigidity;
  Crapo--Whiteley]
  \label{thm:projective-invariance}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigid_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.supportExtensor_mapExtensor_ne_zero,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_scaleExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:rank-hypothesis}
  Let $K$ be a field, let $(G, p)$ be a body-hinge framework in $K^3$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}), and let $\Lambda$ be a linear
  automorphism of the screw space $\bigwedge^2 K^4$. Let
  $(G, \Lambda p)$ be the body-hinge framework on $G$ whose supporting
  extensor at each edge $e$ is $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The map $S \mapsto \Lambda \circ S$ is an isomorphism from
      $Z(G, p)$ onto $Z(G, \Lambda p)$. In particular the two motion
      spaces have the same dimension, the two rigidity matrices have the
      same rank, and $(G, \Lambda p)$ is infinitesimally rigid if and
      only if $(G, p)$ is.
    \item The hinge at an edge is nondegenerate in $(G, \Lambda p)$ if
      and only if it is nondegenerate in $(G, p)$.
    \item The space $Z(G, p)$ does not change when the supporting
      extensor at each edge is multiplied by a nonzero scalar.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u$ and $v$. As $\Lambda$ is linear
  and injective, a screw $w$ lies in $\operatorname{span} C(p(e))$ if
  and only if $\Lambda w$ lies in
  $\operatorname{span} \Lambda C(p(e))$. Taking $w = S(u) - S(v)$
  shows that $S$ satisfies the hinge constraint of $(G, p)$ at $e$ if
  and only if $\Lambda \circ S$ satisfies that of $(G, \Lambda p)$. The
  map $S \mapsto \Lambda \circ S$ is a linear automorphism of the space
  of screw assignments, with inverse $S \mapsto \Lambda^{-1} \circ S$,
  so it maps $Z(G, p)$ onto $Z(G, \Lambda p)$. Moreover $\Lambda \circ S$
  is constant if and only if $S$ is, so the map takes the trivial
  motions of one framework onto those of the other
  (\cref{def:dof-generic}), and infinitesimal rigidity transfers. The
  ranks agree because the rank of the rigidity matrix is
  $D|V| - \dim Z$ (the remark after \cref{def:rigidity-matrix}).

  (2) A linear automorphism sends nonzero vectors to nonzero vectors.
  (3) A nonzero multiple of $C(p(e))$ spans the same line as $C(p(e))$,
  so no hinge constraint changes.

  This is the argument of Crapo and
  Whiteley~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}. A collineation ``is
  expressed by a non-singular linear transformation on the projective
  coordinates of the points''. The linear map that it induces on the
  space of screws ``will transform an instantaneous motion into an
  instantaneous motion'', and it has an inverse.
\end{proof}

For a general $\Lambda$, the screw $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$ need not be the
extensor of a line. The hinge constraint at $e$ is then
$S(u) - S(v) \in \operatorname{span} \Lambda\, C(p(e))$, as in the
formal definition (the note after \cref{def:hinge-constraint}).

Over $\R$, every projective transformation of real projective space
induces such a map $\Lambda$, and \cref{thm:projective-invariance} is
then Crapo and Whiteley's projective invariance of infinitesimal
rigidity~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}; see also
Whiteley~\cite[Theorem~6]{whiteley1999}. A collineation is given by a
nonsingular linear map $g$ of $\R^4$ on homogeneous coordinates. It
induces the automorphism $\Lambda$ with
$\Lambda(v_0 \vee v_1) = g v_0 \vee g v_1$, which sends the extensor of
the line through two points to that of the line through their images. A
correlation sends points to planes by a nonsingular linear map, and it
also induces a linear automorphism of the screw space that sends lines
to lines~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}. The polarity of the next
lemma is a correlation. The proof uses only that $\Lambda$ is linear
and invertible, so we state the theorem over every field. The pencil
chapters apply it over an arbitrary field (\cref{sec:pencil-duality}).
For the rows of the rigidity matrix, \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} proves
the invariance of the rank directly.

For $d = 3$ and $j = 2$, the complement isomorphism $*$
(\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}) is a linear automorphism of the screw
space $\bigwedge^2 K^4$, over every field $K$. It sends a nonzero
extensor of a two-dimensional subspace $U$ of $K^4$ to a nonzero
extensor of $U^\perp$ (the remarks after \cref{def:meet}). So it is the
map that the polarity $x \mapsto x^\perp$ of projective space induces on
lines: the line through two points goes to the line in which their
polar planes meet. We call $*$ the \emph{polarity}. By the definition
of the panel support extensor (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), the
polarity sends the join $v_0 \vee v_1$ of any two vectors
$v_0, v_1 \in K^4$ to $C(v_0, v_1)$.

The polar plane of a point $c \in \R^3$ is
$\{x \in \R^3 : x \cdot c + 1 = 0\}$, since
$(x, 1) \cdot (c, 1) = x \cdot c + 1$, and $c$ is the \emph{pole} of
this plane. Its normal is $\bar c = (c, 1)$, and it is the panel that
Katoh and Tanigawa attach to the vector
$c$~\cite[\S5.1]{katohTanigawa2011}. They observe that the polar of a
hinge-concurrent framework is a panel-hinge framework. Conversely, the
polar of a nonparallel panel-hinge framework, in which any two panels
meet in a line, is hinge-concurrent, with the poles of the panels as
centres. By projective invariance, the two frameworks are
infinitesimally rigid together~\cite[pp.~650 and~671]{katohTanigawa2011}.
Whiteley states this correspondence for molecular
structures~\cite[Corollary~12]{whiteley1999}. The next lemma proves it
in the form that we use.

\begin{lemma}[Projective dual of a panel-hinge framework]
  \label{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso_lineExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso_mk_extensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecularOfCentres_mapExtensor_screwComplementIso,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_homogenize,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.rankHypothesis_ofNormals_homogenize_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.isInfinitesimallyRigid_ofNormals_homogenize_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.supportExtensor_ofNormals_homogenize_ne_zero_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:panel-support-extensor,
        def:meet-complement-iso, def:hinge-concurrent,
        thm:projective-invariance}
  Let $*$ be the polarity of $\bigwedge^2 \R^4$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For all $v_0, v_1 \in \R^4$, $*(v_0 \vee v_1) = C(v_0, v_1)$.
      In particular, for points $a, b \in \R^3$, the polarity sends the
      extensor $C(a, b) = \bar a \vee \bar b$ to $C(\bar a, \bar b)$.
    \item Let $G$ be a multigraph on $V$, and let $c \colon V \to \R^3$.
      The panel-hinge framework on $G$ with normals $\bar c(v)$
      (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}) is obtained from the molecular
      framework on $(G, c)$ (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}) by applying
      $*$ to the supporting extensor at every edge. Hence the two
      frameworks have motion spaces of the same dimension and rigidity
      matrices of the same rank. One of them is infinitesimally rigid if
      and only if the other is, and the hinge at an edge is
      nondegenerate in one if and only if it is nondegenerate in the
      other.
    \item The conclusions of (2) also hold for the panel-hinge framework
      on $G$ with normals $\lambda_v\, \bar c(v)$, for any nonzero
      scalars $\lambda_v$. Its motion space is that of the panel-hinge
      framework with normals $\bar c(v)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Statement (1) is the definition of $C(v_0, v_1)$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}), and
  $C(a, b) = \bar a \vee \bar b$ (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}).
  For (2), both frameworks have the multigraph $G$. At an edge with ends
  $u$ and $v$, the molecular framework has the supporting extensor
  $\bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)$. By (1) its image under $*$ is
  $C(\bar c(u), \bar c(v))$, the supporting extensor of the panel-hinge
  framework at that edge. As $*$ is a linear automorphism of
  $\bigwedge^2 \R^4$ (\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}),
  \cref{thm:projective-invariance} gives the rest. For (3), the join is
  bilinear, so
  $C(\lambda_u \bar c(u), \lambda_v \bar c(v)) =
  \lambda_u \lambda_v\, C(\bar c(u), \bar c(v))$. The two panel-hinge
  frameworks differ by nonzero multiples of their supporting extensors,
  so they have the same motion space by
  \cref{thm:projective-invariance}(3).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  Statement (1) is formalized over every field, as is the polarity.
  Statement (3) has no formal counterpart: the proof of
  \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular} rescales the normals it uses, by
  the argument above.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[Panel-hinge $\Leftrightarrow$ molecular, rank-carrying]
  \label{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_molecular_rankHypothesis_generalPosition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-concurrent, def:general-position-placement,
        lem:theorem-56-general-position, lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}
  Let $G$ be a simple spanning graph on at least one body, with enough
  hinge labels. Then $G$ has an endpoint selector $\mathrm{ends}$
  genuinely recording every $G$-link and a molecular realization in
  $\R^3$ --- centres $c : V \to \R^3$ in general position up to order
  four --- whose motion space attains the target dimension
  \[
    \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  This is the panel-hinge realization of
  \cref{lem:theorem-56-general-position} carried to the molecular model:
  the two models realize the same rank on general-position data, the
  modelling equivalence KT state on p.~671 (for which
  Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008} is the citable published
  anchor). Taking $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$ gives an
  infinitesimally rigid molecular realization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:projective-invariance}
  Apply \cref{lem:theorem-56-general-position} to obtain a panel-hinge
  realization at $\dim Z = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ whose normals
  are in general position up to order four --- in particular each normal
  has a nonvanishing last coordinate. Dehomogenize each normal by its
  last coordinate to a pole $c(v) \in \R^3$; the homogenized pole
  $(c(v), 1)$ is that normal rescaled by a nonzero scalar, so the
  panel-hinge framework on the homogenized poles has the same hinge spans
  --- hence the same motion space --- as the realization just obtained
  (\cref{thm:projective-invariance}, the rescaling normalization). The
  projective duality \cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular} then carries
  that dimension to the molecular framework on the centres $c$. Finally,
  order-four linear independence of the normals is, through
  homogenization, order-four affine independence of the poles, so the
  centres are in general position. The endpoint selector is the
  realization's own $\mathrm{ends}$, unchanged by the rescaling and
  duality steps, so it inherits genuine-link recording from
  \cref{lem:theorem-56-general-position} verbatim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Screw velocity fields}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-velocity}

The square-graph dictionary (\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint})
compares the infinitesimal motions of the two models of a molecule. A
motion of the molecular framework assigns a screw to each body, and a
motion of the bar-joint framework assigns a velocity to each atom. A
screw is an infinitesimal rigid motion of space. It moves a point $x$
with velocity $\omega \times x + t$, where $\omega$ is its angular
velocity and $t$ is the velocity of the origin. This subsection defines
this velocity field and proves the facts about it that the dictionary
uses.

\begin{definition}[Velocity field of a screw]
  \label{def:screw-velocity}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwVel,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwOmega,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwTau}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint}
  For $v \in \R^4$, let $v' \in \R^3$ be its first three coordinates
  and $v_4$ its last. The \emph{rotation part} $\omega_S \in \R^3$ and
  the \emph{translation part} $t_S \in \R^3$ of a screw
  $S \in \bigwedge^2 \R^4$ are the linear functions of $S$ with
  \[
    \omega_{v \vee w} = v_4\, w' - w_4\, v', \qquad
    t_{v \vee w} = v' \times w' \qquad (v, w \in \R^4).
  \]
  The \emph{velocity field} of $S$ is
  \[
    \mathrm{vel}_S(x) = \omega_S \times x + t_S \qquad (x \in \R^3).
  \]
\end{definition}

The two formulas are bilinear and alternating in $v$ and $w$, so they
define linear maps $S \mapsto \omega_S$ and $S \mapsto t_S$ on
$\bigwedge^2 \R^4$. Write $S = \sum_{i < j} S_{ij}\, e_i \vee e_j$ in the
standard basis. Then $\omega_S = -(S_{14}, S_{24}, S_{34})$ and
$t_S = (S_{23}, -S_{13}, S_{12})$, so up to sign and order $\omega_S$
and $t_S$ are the six coordinates of $S$. In particular $S$ is
determined by $\omega_S$ and $t_S$. For two points $a, b \in \R^3$, the
extensor $\bar a \vee \bar b$ of the line through them has
$\omega = b - a$ and $t = a \times b$, and
\[
  \mathrm{vel}_{\bar a \vee \bar b}(x) \;=\; (b - a) \times x + a \times b
  \;=\; (b - a) \times (x - a).
\]
This is the velocity field of the rotation about the line through $a$
and $b$ with angular velocity $b - a$. For every screw $S$ and all
$x, y \in \R^3$,
\[
  \mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) \;=\; \omega_S \times (x - y).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Screws whose velocity field vanishes at two points]
  \label{lem:screw-velocity-line}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwVel_eq_zero_iff_mem_span,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.dotProduct_screwVel_sub}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-velocity, def:hinge-concurrent}
  Let $a \neq b$ be points of $\R^3$, and let $S$ be a screw. Then
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) = 0$ if and only if $S$ is a
  multiple of $\bar a \vee \bar b$. Moreover
  $\langle x - y,\, \mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) \rangle = 0$
  for all $x, y \in \R^3$, that is, every velocity field is an
  infinitesimal isometry.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The second statement holds because
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) = \omega_S \times (x - y)$ is
  orthogonal to $x - y$. The velocity field of $\bar a \vee \bar b$ is
  $(b - a) \times (x - a)$, which vanishes at $a$ and at $b$, and so do
  the velocity fields of its multiples. Conversely, suppose that
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) = 0$. Then
  $\omega_S \times (b - a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) - \mathrm{vel}_S(a) = 0$,
  and since $b - a \neq 0$, $\omega_S = \lambda\,(b - a)$ for some
  $\lambda \in \R$. From $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = 0$ we get
  $t_S = -\omega_S \times a = \lambda\, a \times b$. So $S$ and
  $\lambda\,(\bar a \vee \bar b)$ have the same rotation and translation
  parts, and they are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A screw is determined by point velocities]
  \label{lem:screw-determination}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eq_zero_of_screwVel_eq_zero,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.existsUnique_screwVel_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-velocity, lem:screw-velocity-line}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A screw whose velocity field vanishes at three non-collinear
      points of $\R^3$ is zero.
    \item Let $(p_i)_{i \in I}$ be a finite family of points of $\R^3$
      that contains three non-collinear points, such that any three
      non-collinear points of the family and any fourth member of it
      are affinely independent. Let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be vectors of
      $\R^3$ with $\langle p_i - p_j,\, x_i - x_j \rangle = 0$ for all
      $i, j \in I$. Then there is a unique screw $S$ with
      $\mathrm{vel}_S(p_i) = x_i$ for every $i \in I$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) Let $\mathrm{vel}_S$ vanish at non-collinear points
  $q_0, q_1, q_2$. Then
  $\omega_S \times (q_i - q_0) = \mathrm{vel}_S(q_i) - \mathrm{vel}_S(q_0)
  = 0$ for $i = 1, 2$, so $\omega_S$ is a multiple of both $q_1 - q_0$
  and $q_2 - q_0$. These vectors are linearly independent, so
  $\omega_S = 0$, and then $t_S = \mathrm{vel}_S(q_0) = 0$. Hence
  $S = 0$ (the remarks after \cref{def:screw-velocity}).

  (2) Uniqueness follows from (1): two such screws differ by a screw
  whose velocity field vanishes at three non-collinear points of the
  family.

  For existence, let $q_0, q_1, q_2$ be three non-collinear points of
  the family, with prescribed velocities $y_0, y_1, y_2$, and let
  $e_i = q_i - q_0$ and $d_i = y_i - y_0$ for $i = 1, 2$. The hypothesis
  on the pairs among $q_0, q_1, q_2$ reads $e_1 \cdot d_1 = e_2 \cdot d_2 = 0$ and
  $e_1 \cdot d_2 + e_2 \cdot d_1 = 0$. We first find $\omega \in \R^3$
  with $\omega \times e_1 = d_1$ and $\omega \times e_2 = d_2$. By the
  vector triple product,
  $\omega_0 = (e_1 \cdot e_1)^{-1}\, (e_1 \times d_1)$ satisfies
  $\omega_0 \times e_1 = d_1$. The vector $u = d_2 - \omega_0 \times e_2$
  is orthogonal to $e_2$, and the third of these equations makes it
  orthogonal to $e_1$ as well. So $u = \mu\, (e_1 \times e_2)$ for some $\mu \in \R$,
  and $\omega = \omega_0 + \mu\, e_1$ satisfies both equations. Let $S$
  be the screw with $\omega_S = \omega$ and
  $t_S = y_0 - \omega \times q_0$. Then $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_0) = y_0$, and
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_i) = y_0 + \omega \times e_i = y_i$ for $i = 1, 2$.

  Let $w$ be a further member of the family, with prescribed velocity
  $y_w$, and let $r = \mathrm{vel}_S(w) - y_w$. For each $i$, the vector
  $w - q_i$ is orthogonal to $\mathrm{vel}_S(w) - \mathrm{vel}_S(q_i)$
  (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}) and to $y_w - y_i$ (by hypothesis).
  As $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_i) = y_i$, it is orthogonal to $r$. The
  points $w, q_0, q_1, q_2$ are affinely independent, so the vectors
  $w - q_0$, $w - q_1$, $w - q_2$ span $\R^3$, and $r = 0$.

  The hypothesis of affine independence cannot be dropped: four coplanar
  points, three of them not collinear, admit the out-of-plane flex of a
  flat complete quadrilateral, which is not the restriction of a screw.
  Indeed, every assignment of velocities perpendicular to the plane
  satisfies $\langle p_i - p_j,\, x_i - x_j \rangle = 0$, and these
  assignments form a space of dimension four. But on the plane, the
  perpendicular component of a velocity field is an affine function,
  which is determined by its values at three non-collinear points.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal version of (2) assumes less. The family may be infinite,
  and affine independence is asked only of one non-collinear triple of
  the family together with each further member.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{General position}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-general-position}

\begin{definition}[General position up to order four]
  \label{def:general-position-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGeneralPositionPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework}
  A placement $p : V \to \R^3$ is \emph{in general position up to
  order four} if every subfamily of at most four points is affinely
  independent (so in particular $p$ is injective and no three points
  are collinear). Points on the moment curve
  $t \mapsto (t, t^2, t^3)$ at distinct parameters witness the
  condition, by Vandermonde determinants.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Generic placements in general position]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-general-position}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.exists_isGenericPlacement_isGeneralPositionPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:general-position-placement, def:generic-placement}
  For every finite $V$ there is a placement $p : V \to \R^3$ that is
  simultaneously generic for row independence
  (\cref{def:generic-placement}) and in general position up to order
  four.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Re-run the finite-family interpolation of
  \cref{lem:exists-generic-placement} with the finitely many
  order-$\le 4$ affine-independence conditions added to the avoided
  sets: along an affine path of placements each condition is the
  nonvanishing of a polynomial in the path parameter, not identically
  zero by the moment-curve witness, hence with finitely many roots.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Theorem~5.6 at $d = 3$, general-position form]
  \label{lem:theorem-56-general-position}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankHypothesis_isGeneralPosition4}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:general-position-placement}
  Let $G$ be a simple spanning graph on at least one body. Then $G$
  has a panel-hinge realization in $\R^3$ with
  $\dim Z(G,p) = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ whose normals have
  nonvanishing last coordinate and whose poles are in general position
  up to order four.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-d3, lem:genericity-device,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}
  Dispatch on the number of bodies. On a single body $G$ is edgeless
  (a loopless graph on one vertex), so $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$,
  the realization is trivially rigid, and a moment-curve normal is
  vacuously in general position up to order four.

  With at least two bodies, start from the genuine realization of
  \cref{thm:theorem-55-6-d3}. Its realized rank
  $\dim Z = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is, through the
  rigidity-row/motion complement identity, an exact count of
  $D(|V|-1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ independent rigidity rows;
  since the row coordinates are polynomials in the normal coordinates,
  the independence is witnessed by a single nonzero rational minor
  polynomial (the mechanism of \cref{lem:genericity-device}). Multiply it
  by the order-four general-position avoidance polynomial --- the product
  of the last-coordinate variables $\prod_a X_{a,3}$ with, over the
  injective vertex tuples of sizes $2, 3, 4$, the leading square minors of
  the normal matrix, each nonzero at the moment-curve normals by the
  Vandermonde determinant. Both factors are nonzero polynomials, so their
  product has a non-root over the infinite field $\R$ --- a simultaneous
  non-root of both. There the realized rank is at least the
  witnessed value, the reverse inequality is the deterministic bound
  $D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G) \le \dim Z$ for genuine-hinge
  frameworks (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}, whose genuine hinges
  come from the general position), and the normals satisfy every
  general-position condition. KT compress this strengthening into the word
  ``nonparallel'' (p.~671); the order-four form is what the dictionary
  below consumes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The square-graph dictionary}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-equivalences}

\begin{theorem}[Molecule $\Leftrightarrow$ bar-joint of the square;
  Whiteley, Jackson--Jord\'an]
  \label{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecular_finrank_motions_eq_square_ker}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-concurrent, def:square-graph, lem:square-cliques,
        def:framework, lem:screw-velocity-line,
        lem:screw-determination, def:general-position-placement}
  Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph of minimum degree at least two and
  $c : V \to \R^3$ a placement in general position up to order four.
  Then the infinitesimal-motion space of the molecular framework on
  $(G, c)$ (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}) and the kernel of the
  bar-joint rigidity matrix of $(G^2, c)$ are isomorphic as vector
  spaces:
  \[
    \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) \;=\; \dim \ker R(G^2, c),
    \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
    \operatorname{rank} R(G^2, c) = 3|V| - \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c).
  \]
  In particular $G$ has an infinitesimally rigid molecular realization
  iff $G^2$ has an infinitesimally rigid bar-joint realization
  (Whiteley's modelling equivalence, for which
  Jackson--Jord\'an~\cite{jacksonJordan2008} is the citable published
  anchor; KT resolve the conjecture that closes the argument).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The isomorphism sends a screw assignment $S$ to the velocity
  assignment $v \mapsto \mathrm{vel}_{S(v)}(c(v))$. It lands in
  $\ker R(G^2, c)$: an original edge splits as one screw's isometry
  plus a hinge term vanishing at the endpoint
  (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}); a distance-two edge $uw$ through
  $v$ is handled entirely by body $v$'s screw, since the hinges
  $c(u)c(v)$ and $c(w)c(v)$ let $S(v)$ reproduce the velocities at
  both neighbours. It is injective: a motion in the kernel has each
  screw's velocity field vanishing on the $\ge 3$ non-collinear points
  of its closed neighbourhood (minimum degree two plus general
  position), so each screw is zero (\cref{lem:screw-determination}).
  It is surjective: a bar-joint motion of $G^2$ restricts to a
  pairwise-constrained velocity assignment on each closed-neighbourhood
  clique (\cref{lem:square-cliques}), which determines a unique screw
  per body (\cref{lem:screw-determination}); adjacent bodies' screws
  differ by a screw vanishing at both endpoint centres, i.e.\ by a
  multiple of the hinge line (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}), so the
  family is a molecular motion mapping back to the given one. The rank
  identity is rank--nullity; the realizability corollary is the
  dimension statement at
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}} = D = 6$ together with the existence of
  general-position placements on each side.
\end{proof}
