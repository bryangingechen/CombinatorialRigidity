\section{Projective duality and the molecule modelling equivalence}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph of minimum degree at least two. KT
Corollary~5.7 is the rank formula
\[
  r(G^2) \;=\; 3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
\]
where $r$ is the rank function of the generic bar-joint rigidity matroid
in $\R^3$ (\cref{sec:bar-joint-3d}), $G^2$ is the square of $G$, and
$\operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ is the deficiency of \cref{def:D-deficiency}
at $D = 6$. Jackson and Jord\'an conjectured the formula and proved it
equivalent to the molecular conjecture~\cite[Conjecture~2.2 and
Theorem~4.3]{jacksonJordan2008}. Its proof relates three models, all in
$\R^3$, of a molecule whose atoms and bonds form the graph $G$:
\[
  \begin{array}{c} \text{bar-joint} \\ \text{framework on } G^2 \end{array}
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \begin{array}{c} \text{molecular} \\ \text{framework on } G \end{array}
  \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
  \begin{array}{c} \text{panel-hinge} \\ \text{framework on } G \end{array}
\]
A molecular framework is a body-hinge framework in which the hinges at
each body pass through one point, the centre of the body. The second
equivalence is projective duality. A polarity of projective three-space
sends points to planes and lines to lines, so it turns a molecular
framework into a panel-hinge framework, and infinitesimal rigidity is
invariant under it~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}. The first equivalence
is Whiteley's: a graph of minimum degree at least two has an
infinitesimally rigid molecular realization if and only if its square has
an infinitesimally rigid bar-joint realization. Jackson and
Jord\'an~\cite[\S2.1]{jacksonJordan2008} and Katoh and
Tanigawa~\cite[pp.~650 and~671]{katohTanigawa2011} cite it from
Whiteley's manuscript \emph{The equivalence of molecular rigidity models
as geometric and generic graphs} (2004).

Whiteley's equivalence, in the form that Jackson and Jord\'an and Katoh
and Tanigawa state, concerns rigid realizations. In this form it gives the
rank formula only when $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, where the
formula says that $G^2$ is rigid. Jackson and Jord\'an deduce the general
formula from this case~\cite[\S\S3--4]{jacksonJordan2008}. Their upper
bound on $r(G^2)$ is the value of an independent cover of $G^2$ built from
a partition of $V$ of largest deficiency (their Theorem~4.1). It uses
results on such covers, and on the maximal subgraphs of deficiency zero,
from two further papers of theirs. Their lower bound is an induction on
the deficiency, which attaches to $G$ a path of length five between two
of these maximal subgraphs (their Theorem~4.3).

We avoid this development. We prove both equivalences as equalities
between the dimensions of the spaces of infinitesimal motions, at a
single placement. KT Theorem~5.6
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6}) realizes $G$ as a panel-hinge framework of rank
$6(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$. The two equivalences turn this
realization into a bar-joint framework on $G^2$ of rank
$3|V| - 6 - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$, as Katoh and Tanigawa do for
the second (KT p.~671). The reverse inequality comes from the first
equivalence at a generic placement, together with the lower bound
$6 + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$ on the dimension of the motions of a
molecular framework with nondegenerate hinges
(\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}).
\Cref{sec:molecule-application} derives the formula in this way.

The chapter proceeds in five steps. \Cref{sec:molecule-modelling-defs}
defines the square of a graph and molecular frameworks.
\Cref{sec:molecule-modelling-projective} proves the projective invariance
of the motion spaces, and shows that the polar of a molecular framework
is a panel-hinge framework. \Cref{sec:molecule-modelling-velocity}
describes a screw by its field of velocities.
\Cref{sec:molecule-modelling-general-position} defines general position
up to order four, and gives a molecular realization at the rank of KT
Theorem~5.6 whose centres are in general position.
\Cref{sec:molecule-modelling-equivalences} proves the first equivalence
at such centres. Throughout, $d = 3$ and $D = 6$, the setting of Katoh
and Tanigawa's connection with molecules (KT \S1.2). We use the extensors
and the meet of \cref{sec:molecular} and \cref{sec:molecular-meet}, the
body-hinge and panel-hinge frameworks of
\cref{sec:molecular-rigidity-matrix} and
\cref{sec:molecular-algebraic-induction}, and the bar-joint frameworks of
\cref{sec:frameworks} and \cref{sec:bar-joint-3d}.

\subsection{The square graph and molecular frameworks}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-defs}

Katoh and Tanigawa describe two models of a molecule whose atoms are
joined by covalent bonds~\cite[\S1.2]{katohTanigawa2011}. Let $G$ be
the graph of the bonds. In the first model the molecule is a bar-joint
framework on the square of $G$, which joins two atoms when they are at
distance one or two in $G$ (\cref{def:square-graph}). In the second it
is a body-hinge framework on $G$, with the atoms as bodies and the
bonds as hinges, in which all the hinges at an atom pass through its
centre (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}). In the square, an atom and its
neighbours induce a complete subgraph (\cref{lem:square-cliques}),
which acts as a rigid body~\cite[\S2.1]{jacksonJordan2008}.

\begin{definition}[Square of a graph]
  \label{def:square-graph}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.square}
  \leanok
  The \emph{square} of a graph $G = (V,E)$ is the graph
  $G^2 = (V, E^2)$ with
  \[
    E^2 = E \cup \{\, uv \mid u \ne v,\ \exists w \in V \setminus \{u,v\}
      \text{ with } uw, wv \in E \,\},
  \]
  that is, $G$ together with an edge joining every two vertices at
  distance two in $G$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Closed neighbourhoods are cliques of the square]
  \label{lem:square-cliques}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.isClique_closedNeighborSet_square,
        SimpleGraph.exists_mem_closedNeighborSet_of_square_adj}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:square-graph}
  Let $G$ be a graph. For every vertex $v$ of $G$, the closed
  neighbourhood $N[v] = \{v\} \cup N(v)$ induces a complete subgraph of
  $G^2$. Moreover, every edge of $G^2$ has both ends in $N[w]$ for some
  vertex $w$ of $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $x \neq y$ be vertices of $N[v]$. If one of them is $v$, then
  $xy$ is an edge of $G$. Otherwise $v$ is a common neighbour of $x$ and
  $y$. In both cases $xy \in E^2$. Now let $uw$ be an edge of $G^2$. If
  it is an edge of $G$, then $u$ and $w$ lie in $N[u]$. Otherwise $u$
  and $w$ have a common neighbour $v$, and they lie in $N[v]$.
\end{proof}

Katoh and Tanigawa call a body-hinge framework \emph{hinge-concurrent}
if all the hinges at each body pass through one point, the centre of the
body~\cite[p.~650]{katohTanigawa2011}. A molecule is
modelled by such a framework, since the bonds at an atom pass through
its centre. Following Whiteley~\cite[Definition~4]{whiteley1999}, we
give the framework by the centres of its bodies.

\begin{definition}[Molecular (hinge-concurrent) framework]
  \label{def:hinge-concurrent}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecularOfCentres}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a multigraph, and let $c \colon V \to \R^3$
  assign a \emph{centre} $c(v)$ to each body $v$. The \emph{molecular
  framework} on $(G, c)$ is the body-hinge framework on $G$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}) whose supporting extensor at an edge
  $e$ with ends $u$ and $v$ is
  \[
    C\bigl(c(u), c(v)\bigr) \;=\; \bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)
  \]
  (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}), where
  $\bar c(v) = (c(v), 1)$.
\end{definition}

Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u$ and $v$. If $c(u) \neq c(v)$, the
extensor at $e$ is a nonzero extensor of the line through $c(u)$ and
$c(v)$ (the remarks after \cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}), and
this line is the hinge at $e$. So when adjacent bodies have distinct
centres, every hinge at a body $v$ passes through $c(v)$, and the
molecular framework is hinge-concurrent. Conversely, let a body-hinge
framework on $G$ be hinge-concurrent with centres $c$, where
$c(u) \neq c(v)$ at every edge $uv$. The hinge at $uv$ passes through
$c(u)$ and $c(v)$, so it is the line through them, and the framework is
the molecular framework on $(G, c)$. Jackson and Jord\'an call these
frameworks molecular frameworks~\cite[\S2.1]{jacksonJordan2008}, and
Katoh and Tanigawa use their name~\cite[p.~671]{katohTanigawa2011}.

If $c(u) = c(v)$, the extensor at $e$ is zero. The hinge at $e$ is then
degenerate, and it welds $u$ and $v$ together (the remarks after
\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}). Whiteley calls a molecular structure
non-degenerate if distinct bodies have distinct
centres~\cite[Definition~4]{whiteley1999}. Where nondegenerate hinges
are needed below, the centres are assumed distinct or in general
position (\cref{def:general-position-placement}).

\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal molecular framework records an ordered pair of bodies for
  every edge label, as a formal panel-hinge framework does
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). Its extensor at a label is
  that of the centres of the pair.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Projective invariance and duality}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-projective}

A projective transformation of space acts linearly on homogeneous
coordinates, and through them on the screw space. A collineation sends
points to points, and a correlation sends points to planes. Crapo and
Whiteley~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982} showed that both take the
infinitesimal motions of a body-hinge framework to those of the
transformed framework. So infinitesimal rigidity is a projective
invariant. We prove this for every linear automorphism of the screw
space (\cref{thm:projective-invariance}). We then apply it to a
polarity, which turns a molecular framework into a panel-hinge framework
(\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}).

In every dimension $d$, an invertible linear map of $K^{d+1}$ induces a
linear automorphism of the screw space $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$. For
$d = 3$, Crapo and Whiteley let a collineation act on screws in this
way~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}.

\begin{definition}[The screw map of a linear map]
  \label{def:induced-screw-map}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.screwEquivOfLinearEquiv,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.screwEquivOfLinearEquiv_mk_extensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-space, def:extensor}
  Let $K$ be a field, let $d \ge 1$, and let $g$ be an invertible linear
  map of $K^{d+1}$. The \emph{screw map} of $g$ is the linear
  automorphism $\Lambda_g$ of the screw space $\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$
  (\cref{def:screw-space}) with
  \[
    \Lambda_g(v_1 \vee \cdots \vee v_{d-1})
    \;=\; g v_1 \vee \cdots \vee g v_{d-1}
    \qquad (v_1, \dots, v_{d-1} \in K^{d+1}).
  \]
\end{definition}

The map $\Lambda_g$ is the $(d-1)$-th exterior power of $g$, and its
inverse is $\Lambda_{g^{-1}}$. The extensors span
$\bigwedge^{d-1} K^{d+1}$ (the remarks after \cref{def:extensor}), so the
formula determines $\Lambda_g$.

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pin is $\Lambda_g$, and the second is its formula.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{theorem}[Projective invariance of infinitesimal rigidity;
  Crapo--Whiteley]
  \label{thm:projective-invariance}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.rankHypothesis_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.isInfinitesimallyRigid_mapExtensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.supportExtensor_mapExtensor_ne_zero,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.BodyHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_scaleExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-constraint, def:trivial-motions-rigid}
  Let $K$ be a field, let $(G, p)$ be a body-hinge framework in $K^3$
  (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}), and let $\Lambda$ be a linear
  automorphism of the screw space $\bigwedge^2 K^4$. Let
  $(G, \Lambda p)$ be the body-hinge framework on $G$ whose supporting
  extensor at each edge $e$ is $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The map $S \mapsto \Lambda \circ S$ is an isomorphism from
      $Z(G, p)$ onto $Z(G, \Lambda p)$. In particular the two motion
      spaces have the same dimension, the two rigidity matrices have the
      same rank, and $(G, \Lambda p)$ is infinitesimally rigid if and
      only if $(G, p)$ is.
    \item The hinge at an edge is nondegenerate in $(G, \Lambda p)$ if
      and only if it is nondegenerate in $(G, p)$.
    \item The space $Z(G, p)$ does not change when the supporting
      extensor at each edge is multiplied by a nonzero scalar.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:rank-rotation-semicont}
  (1) Let $e$ be an edge with ends $u$ and $v$. As $\Lambda$ is linear
  and injective, a screw $w$ lies in $\operatorname{span} C(p(e))$ if
  and only if $\Lambda w$ lies in
  $\operatorname{span} \Lambda C(p(e))$. Taking $w = S(u) - S(v)$
  shows that $S$ satisfies the hinge constraint of $(G, p)$ at $e$ if
  and only if $\Lambda \circ S$ satisfies that of $(G, \Lambda p)$. The
  map $S \mapsto \Lambda \circ S$ is a linear automorphism of the space
  of screw assignments, with inverse $S \mapsto \Lambda^{-1} \circ S$,
  so it maps $Z(G, p)$ onto $Z(G, \Lambda p)$. Moreover $\Lambda \circ S$
  is constant if and only if $S$ is, so the map takes the trivial
  motions of one framework onto those of the other
  (\cref{def:trivial-motions-rigid}), and infinitesimal rigidity
  transfers. The ranks agree because the rank of the rigidity matrix
  is $D|V| - \dim Z$ (the remark after \cref{def:rigidity-matrix}).

  (2) A linear automorphism sends nonzero vectors to nonzero vectors.
  (3) A nonzero multiple of $C(p(e))$ spans the same line as $C(p(e))$,
  so no hinge constraint changes.

  This is the argument of Crapo and
  Whiteley~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}. A collineation ``is
  expressed by a non-singular linear transformation on the projective
  coordinates of the points''. The linear map that it induces on the
  space of screws ``will transform an instantaneous motion into an
  instantaneous motion'', and it has an inverse.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned results hold for body-hinge frameworks in $K^d$ for every
  $d$. The first pin is the framework $(G, \Lambda p)$. The next four
  prove (1): the isomorphism, the equality of the dimensions, that
  $(G, \Lambda p)$ satisfies the rank hypothesis
  (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}) at a target exactly when $(G, p)$ does,
  and rigidity. The last two prove (2) and (3).
\end{fmlnote}

For a general $\Lambda$, the screw $\Lambda\, C(p(e))$ need not be the
extensor of a line. The hinge constraint at $e$ is then
$S(u) - S(v) \in \operatorname{span} \Lambda\, C(p(e))$, as in the
formal definition (the note after \cref{def:hinge-constraint}).

Over $\R$, every projective transformation of real projective space
induces such a map $\Lambda$, and \cref{thm:projective-invariance} is
then Crapo and Whiteley's projective invariance of infinitesimal
rigidity~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}; see also
Whiteley~\cite[Theorem~6]{whiteley1999}. A collineation is given by a
nonsingular linear map $g$ of $\R^4$ on homogeneous coordinates. It
induces the screw map $\Lambda_g$ (\cref{def:induced-screw-map}), with
$\Lambda_g(v_0 \vee v_1) = g v_0 \vee g v_1$, which sends the extensor of
the line through two points to that of the line through their images. A
correlation sends points to planes by a nonsingular linear map, and it
also induces a linear automorphism of the screw space that sends lines
to lines~\cite[\S3.6]{crapoWhiteley1982}. The polarity of the next
lemma is a correlation. The proof uses only that $\Lambda$ is linear
and invertible, so we state the theorem over every field. The pencil
chapters apply it over an arbitrary field (\cref{sec:pencil-duality}).
For the rows of the rigidity matrix, \cref{lem:screw-map-rows} proves
the invariance of the rank directly.

For $d = 3$ and $j = 2$, the complement isomorphism $*$
(\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}) is a linear automorphism of the screw
space $\bigwedge^2 K^4$, over every field $K$. It sends a nonzero
extensor of a two-dimensional subspace $U$ of $K^4$ to a nonzero
extensor of $U^\perp$ (the remarks after \cref{def:meet}). So it is the
map that the polarity $x \mapsto x^\perp$ of projective space induces on
lines: the line through two points goes to the line in which their
polar planes meet. We call $*$ the \emph{polarity}. The next lemma
gives its value on joins.

\begin{lemma}[The polarity on joins]
  \label{lem:polarity-join}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso_mk_extensor,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwComplementIso_lineExtensor}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-support-extensor, def:affine-subspace-extensor}
  Let $K$ be a field, and let $*$ be the polarity of $\bigwedge^2 K^4$.
  For all $v_0, v_1 \in K^4$, $*(v_0 \vee v_1) = C(v_0, v_1)$. In
  particular, for points $a, b \in \R^3$, the polarity of
  $\bigwedge^2 \R^4$ sends the extensor $C(a, b) = \bar a \vee \bar b$ to
  $C(\bar a, \bar b)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The first statement holds by the definition of $C(v_0, v_1)$
  (\cref{def:panel-support-extensor}). The second is the first at
  $K = \R$, $v_0 = \bar a$ and $v_1 = \bar b$, as
  $C(a, b) = \bar a \vee \bar b$ (\cref{def:affine-subspace-extensor}).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pins are the polarity and the two statements, in this order.
\end{fmlnote}

The polar plane of a point $c \in \R^3$ is
$\{x \in \R^3 : x \cdot c + 1 = 0\}$, since
$(x, 1) \cdot (c, 1) = x \cdot c + 1$, and $c$ is the \emph{pole} of
this plane. Its normal is $\bar c = (c, 1)$, and it is the panel that
Katoh and Tanigawa attach to the vector
$c$~\cite[\S5.1]{katohTanigawa2011}. They observe that the polar of a
hinge-concurrent framework is a panel-hinge framework. Conversely, the
polar of a nonparallel panel-hinge framework, in which any two panels
meet in a line, is hinge-concurrent, with the poles of the panels as
centres. By projective invariance, the two frameworks are
infinitesimally rigid together~\cite[pp.~650 and~671]{katohTanigawa2011}.
Whiteley states this correspondence for molecular
structures~\cite[Corollary~12]{whiteley1999}. The next lemma proves it
in the form that we use.

\begin{lemma}[Projective dual of a panel-hinge framework]
  \label{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecularOfCentres_mapExtensor_screwComplementIso,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.finrank_infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_homogenize,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.rankHypothesis_ofNormals_homogenize_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.isInfinitesimallyRigid_ofNormals_homogenize_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.supportExtensor_ofNormals_homogenize_ne_zero_iff,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.supportExtensor_ofNormals_smul,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.infinitesimalMotions_ofNormals_smul}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:panel-hinge-framework, def:panel-support-extensor,
        def:meet-complement-iso, def:hinge-concurrent,
        thm:projective-invariance, lem:polarity-join}
  Let $*$ be the polarity of $\bigwedge^2 \R^4$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $G$ be a multigraph on $V$, and let $c \colon V \to \R^3$.
      The panel-hinge framework on $G$ with normals $\bar c(v)$
      (\cref{def:panel-hinge-framework}) is obtained from the molecular
      framework on $(G, c)$ (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}) by applying
      $*$ to the supporting extensor at every edge. Hence the two
      frameworks have motion spaces of the same dimension and rigidity
      matrices of the same rank. One of them is infinitesimally rigid if
      and only if the other is, and the hinge at an edge is
      nondegenerate in one if and only if it is nondegenerate in the
      other.
    \item The conclusions of (1) also hold for the panel-hinge framework
      on $G$ with normals $\lambda_v\, \bar c(v)$, for any nonzero
      scalars $\lambda_v$. Its motion space is that of the panel-hinge
      framework with normals $\bar c(v)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For (1), both frameworks have the multigraph $G$. At an edge with ends
  $u$ and $v$, the molecular framework has the supporting extensor
  $\bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)$. Its image under $*$ is
  $C(\bar c(u), \bar c(v))$ (\cref{lem:polarity-join}), the supporting
  extensor of the panel-hinge framework at that edge. As $*$ is a linear
  automorphism of
  $\bigwedge^2 \R^4$ (\cref{def:meet-complement-iso}),
  \cref{thm:projective-invariance} gives the rest. For (2), the join is
  bilinear, so
  $C(\lambda_u \bar c(u), \lambda_v \bar c(v)) =
  \lambda_u \lambda_v\, C(\bar c(u), \bar c(v))$. The two panel-hinge
  frameworks differ by nonzero multiples of their supporting extensors,
  so they have the same motion space by
  \cref{thm:projective-invariance}(3).
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The first pin is the identity of the two frameworks in (1), and the
  next four are its consequences: the dimensions of the motion spaces,
  the rank hypothesis (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}), rigidity, and the
  nondegeneracy of the hinges. The last two pins are the first two of
  \cref{lem:pencil-jj-rescale}, which hold for any normals over any
  field, and give (2) at the normals $\bar c(v)$. The first scales the
  hinge at an edge with ends $u$ and $v$ by
  $\lambda_u \lambda_v \neq 0$, so it is nondegenerate exactly when the
  hinge at the normals $\bar c(v)$ is. The second is the equality of the
  motion spaces, from which the other conclusions of (1) follow by the
  first five pins. The proof of \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular}
  rescales the normals it uses directly, by the argument above.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Screw velocity fields}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-velocity}

The square-graph dictionary (\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint})
compares the infinitesimal motions of the two models of a molecule. A
motion of the molecular framework assigns a screw to each body, and a
motion of the bar-joint framework assigns a velocity to each atom. A
screw is an infinitesimal rigid motion of space. It moves a point $x$
with velocity $\omega \times x + t$, where $\omega$ is its angular
velocity and $t$ is the velocity of the origin. This subsection defines
this velocity field and proves the facts about it that the dictionary
uses.

\begin{definition}[Velocity field of a screw]
  \label{def:screw-velocity}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwVel,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwOmega,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwTau}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-space}
  For $v \in \R^4$, let $v' \in \R^3$ be its first three coordinates
  and $v_4$ its last. The \emph{rotation part} $\omega_S \in \R^3$ and
  the \emph{translation part} $t_S \in \R^3$ of a screw
  $S \in \bigwedge^2 \R^4$ are the linear functions of $S$ with
  \[
    \omega_{v \vee w} = v_4\, w' - w_4\, v', \qquad
    t_{v \vee w} = v' \times w' \qquad (v, w \in \R^4).
  \]
  The \emph{velocity field} of $S$ is
  \[
    \mathrm{vel}_S(x) = \omega_S \times x + t_S \qquad (x \in \R^3).
  \]
\end{definition}

The two formulas are bilinear and alternating in $v$ and $w$, so they
define linear maps $S \mapsto \omega_S$ and $S \mapsto t_S$ on
$\bigwedge^2 \R^4$. Write $S = \sum_{i < j} S_{ij}\, e_i \vee e_j$ in the
standard basis. Then $\omega_S = -(S_{14}, S_{24}, S_{34})$ and
$t_S = (S_{23}, -S_{13}, S_{12})$, so up to sign and order $\omega_S$
and $t_S$ are the six coordinates of $S$. In particular $S$ is
determined by $\omega_S$ and $t_S$. For two points $a, b \in \R^3$, the
extensor $\bar a \vee \bar b$ of the line through them has
$\omega = b - a$ and $t = a \times b$, and
\[
  \mathrm{vel}_{\bar a \vee \bar b}(x) \;=\; (b - a) \times x + a \times b
  \;=\; (b - a) \times (x - a).
\]
This is the velocity field of the rotation about the line through $a$
and $b$ with angular velocity $b - a$. For every screw $S$ and all
$x, y \in \R^3$,
\[
  \mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) \;=\; \omega_S \times (x - y).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Screws whose velocity field vanishes at two points]
  \label{lem:screw-velocity-line}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.screwVel_eq_zero_iff_mem_span,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.dotProduct_screwVel_sub}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-velocity, def:extensor, def:homogeneous-coords}
  Let $a \neq b$ be points of $\R^3$, and let $S$ be a screw. Then
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) = 0$ if and only if $S$ is a
  multiple of $\bar a \vee \bar b$. Moreover
  $\langle x - y,\, \mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) \rangle = 0$
  for all $x, y \in \R^3$, that is, every velocity field is an
  infinitesimal isometry.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The second statement holds because
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(x) - \mathrm{vel}_S(y) = \omega_S \times (x - y)$ is
  orthogonal to $x - y$. The velocity field of $\bar a \vee \bar b$ is
  $(b - a) \times (x - a)$, which vanishes at $a$ and at $b$, and so do
  the velocity fields of its multiples. Conversely, suppose that
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) = 0$. Then
  $\omega_S \times (b - a) = \mathrm{vel}_S(b) - \mathrm{vel}_S(a) = 0$,
  and since $b - a \neq 0$, $\omega_S = \lambda\,(b - a)$ for some
  $\lambda \in \R$. From $\mathrm{vel}_S(a) = 0$ we get
  $t_S = -\omega_S \times a = \lambda\, a \times b$. So $S$ and
  $\lambda\,(\bar a \vee \bar b)$ have the same rotation and translation
  parts, and they are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A screw is determined by point velocities]
  \label{lem:screw-determination}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.eq_zero_of_screwVel_eq_zero,
        CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.existsUnique_screwVel_eq}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:screw-velocity, lem:screw-velocity-line}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item A screw whose velocity field vanishes at three non-collinear
      points of $\R^3$ is zero.
    \item Let $(p_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of points of $\R^3$, and let
      $i_0, i_1, i_2 \in I$ be such that $p_{i_0}, p_{i_1}, p_{i_2}$ are
      not collinear and, for every $j \in I$ other than $i_0$, $i_1$
      and $i_2$, the points $p_j, p_{i_0}, p_{i_1}, p_{i_2}$ are
      affinely independent. Let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be vectors of $\R^3$
      with $\langle p_i - p_j,\, x_i - x_j \rangle = 0$ for all
      $i, j \in I$. Then there is a unique screw $S$ with
      $\mathrm{vel}_S(p_i) = x_i$ for every $i \in I$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  (1) Let $\mathrm{vel}_S$ vanish at non-collinear points
  $q_0, q_1, q_2$. Then
  $\omega_S \times (q_i - q_0) = \mathrm{vel}_S(q_i) - \mathrm{vel}_S(q_0)
  = 0$ for $i = 1, 2$, so $\omega_S$ is a multiple of both $q_1 - q_0$
  and $q_2 - q_0$. These vectors are linearly independent, so
  $\omega_S = 0$, and then $t_S = \mathrm{vel}_S(q_0) = 0$. Hence
  $S = 0$ (the remarks after \cref{def:screw-velocity}).

  (2) Uniqueness follows from (1): two such screws differ by a screw
  whose velocity field vanishes at the non-collinear points
  $p_{i_0}, p_{i_1}, p_{i_2}$.

  For existence, let $q_k = p_{i_k}$, with prescribed velocity
  $y_k = x_{i_k}$, for $k = 0, 1, 2$, and let
  $e_i = q_i - q_0$ and $d_i = y_i - y_0$ for $i = 1, 2$. The hypothesis
  on the pairs among $q_0, q_1, q_2$ reads $e_1 \cdot d_1 = e_2 \cdot d_2 = 0$ and
  $e_1 \cdot d_2 + e_2 \cdot d_1 = 0$. We first find $\omega \in \R^3$
  with $\omega \times e_1 = d_1$ and $\omega \times e_2 = d_2$. By the
  vector triple product,
  $\omega_0 = (e_1 \cdot e_1)^{-1}\, (e_1 \times d_1)$ satisfies
  $\omega_0 \times e_1 = d_1$. The vector $u = d_2 - \omega_0 \times e_2$
  is orthogonal to $e_2$, and the third of these equations makes it
  orthogonal to $e_1$ as well. So $u = \mu\, (e_1 \times e_2)$ for some $\mu \in \R$,
  and $\omega = \omega_0 + \mu\, e_1$ satisfies both equations. Let $S$
  be the screw with $\omega_S = \omega$ and
  $t_S = y_0 - \omega \times q_0$. Then $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_0) = y_0$, and
  $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_i) = y_0 + \omega \times e_i = y_i$ for $i = 1, 2$.

  Let $j \in I$ be other than $i_0$, $i_1$ and $i_2$, let $w = p_j$, with
  prescribed velocity $y_w = x_j$, and let
  $r = \mathrm{vel}_S(w) - y_w$. For $k = 0, 1, 2$, the vector
  $w - q_k$ is orthogonal to $\mathrm{vel}_S(w) - \mathrm{vel}_S(q_k)$
  (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}) and to $y_w - y_k$ (by hypothesis).
  As $\mathrm{vel}_S(q_k) = y_k$, it is orthogonal to $r$. By hypothesis
  the points $w, q_0, q_1, q_2$ are affinely independent, so the vectors
  $w - q_0$, $w - q_1$, $w - q_2$ span $\R^3$, and $r = 0$.

  The hypothesis of affine independence cannot be dropped: four coplanar
  points, three of them not collinear, admit the out-of-plane flex of a
  flat complete quadrilateral, which is not the restriction of a screw.
  Indeed, every assignment of velocities perpendicular to the plane
  satisfies $\langle p_i - p_j,\, x_i - x_j \rangle = 0$, and these
  assignments form a space of dimension four. But on the plane, the
  perpendicular component of a velocity field is an affine function,
  which is determined by its values at three non-collinear points.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Molecular realizations in general position}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-general-position}

The square-graph dictionary (\cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint})
needs the centres of a molecular framework to be in general position up
to order four. This subsection defines the condition and constructs
placements that satisfy it: first together with genericity for row
independence, then as the centres of a molecular realization at the rank
of KT Theorem~5.6.

\begin{definition}[General position up to order four]
  \label{def:general-position-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsGeneralPositionPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:framework}
  A placement $p \colon V \to \R^3$ (\cref{def:framework}) is \emph{in
  general position up to order four} if, for every set $S \subseteq V$
  of at most four vertices, the points $p(v)$ with $v \in S$ are
  affinely independent.
\end{definition}

Such a placement is injective, and no three of its points are
collinear. For every finite $V$ there is one. Let $t \colon V \to \R$
be injective, and place each vertex $v$ at the point
$(t_v, t_v^2, t_v^3)$ of the moment curve. For four vertices with
parameters $t_1, \dots, t_4$, the matrix with rows
$(t_i, t_i^2, t_i^3, 1)$ has the Vandermonde determinant
$\pm\prod_{i < j} (t_j - t_i) \neq 0$, so the four points are affinely
independent (\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}). Fewer than four points of the
curve lie among four of its points at distinct parameters, and a
subfamily of an affinely independent family is affinely independent.

\begin{lemma}[Generic placements in general position]
  \label{lem:exists-generic-general-position}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.exists_isGenericPlacement_isGeneralPositionPlacement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:general-position-placement, def:generic-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite. Then there is a placement $p \colon V \to \R^3$ that
  is generic for row independence (\cref{def:generic-placement}) and in
  general position up to order four.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:exists-generic-placement}
  Let $p_0$ be a placement that is generic for row independence
  (\cref{lem:exists-generic-placement}), let $q$ be a placement in general
  position up to order four, and let $p^t = p_0 + t\,(q - p_0)$ for
  $t \in \R$.

  Let $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ be a set of edges that is row-independent at
  some placement. Then it is row-independent at $p_0$. Its rigidity rows
  at $p^t$ are affine in $t$, so it is row-independent at $p^t$ for all
  but finitely many $t$, as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:exists-generic-placement}. Let $S \subseteq V$ have at most four
  vertices, and fix $v_0 \in S$. The points $p^t(v)$ with $v \in S$ are
  affinely independent exactly when the vectors $p^t(v) - p^t(v_0)$ with
  $v \in S \setminus \{v_0\}$ are linearly independent. These vectors are
  affine in $t$ and linearly independent at $t = 1$, so in the same way
  they are linearly independent for all but finitely many $t$.

  There are finitely many sets $I$ and $S$. Choose $t$ outside the
  finitely many exceptional values of all of them. Then $p^t$ is in
  general position up to order four, and it is generic for row
  independence, since every edge set that is row-independent at some
  placement is row-independent at $p^t$.
\end{proof}

KT Theorem~5.6 realizes a simple graph as a panel-hinge framework at the
target rank (\cref{def:rank-hypothesis}). The realization can be taken
with nonparallel panels and nondegenerate hinges
(\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}). We need more. A normal $n \in \R^4$ with a nonzero last coordinate is a nonzero
multiple of $\bar c = (c, 1)$ for a unique point $c \in \R^3$, the pole
of its panel
(\cref{sec:molecule-modelling-projective}). We ask that the poles be in
general position up to order four. Up to a rescaling of its normals,
the realization is then the image under the polarity of a molecular
framework whose centres are the poles
(\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}).

\begin{lemma}[KT Theorem~5.6 at $d = 3$ with poles in general position]
  \label{lem:theorem-56-general-position}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.PanelHingeFramework.exists_rankHypothesis_isGeneralPosition4}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:general-position-placement, def:panel-nonparallel}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple graph with $V \neq \emptyset$. Then $G$ has
  a panel-hinge realization $(G, p)$ in $\R^3$ whose normals are
  $n_v = \lambda_v\, \bar c(v)$, for nonzero scalars $\lambda_v$ and
  points $c(v) \in \R^3$ in general position up to order four
  (\cref{def:general-position-placement}), whose panels are nonparallel
  (\cref{def:panel-nonparallel}), and which has
  \[
    \dim Z(G, p) \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  where $D = 6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:theorem-55-6-d3, lem:genericity-device,
        lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound, lem:moment-curve-general-position}
  If $|V| = 1$, then $G$ has no edge, as it is simple, and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. Every realization of $G$ then has
  $\dim Z(G, p) = D$, the dimension of the screw space, and any normal
  $\bar c(v)$ will do. Its panels are nonparallel, as it has no two
  distinct bodies.

  Let $|V| \ge 2$, and let $N = D(|V| - 1) - \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.
  KT Theorem~5.6 at $d = 3$ (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-d3}) holds with
  nondegenerate hinges (\cref{thm:theorem-55-6-genuine}). So $G$ has a
  panel-hinge realization with normals $q_0$ whose hinges are all
  nondegenerate and whose rigidity matrix has rank $N$. We move the
  normals into general position without lowering the rank. The rank is
  lower semicontinuous on the normals at which every hinge is
  nondegenerate (\cref{lem:genericity-device}). We use this in the form
  of \cref{lem:rank-polynomial-of-le-finrank}, which keeps the minor of
  its proof: a polynomial $Q_1$ in the coordinates of the normals, an
  $N \times N$ minor of a matrix of rows of the rigidity matrix, such that
  $Q_1(q_0) \neq 0$ and $\operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \ge N$ at every $q$
  with $Q_1(q) \neq 0$.

  A second polynomial $Q_2$ tests general position. Regard the normals as
  the rows of a $|V| \times 4$ matrix of variables. Let $Q_2$ be the
  product of the last coordinates of all the normals and of the minors
  on the first $j$ columns of the submatrices formed by $j$ distinct
  rows, for $j = 2, 3, 4$. At normals with $Q_2 \neq 0$, each normal has
  a nonzero last coordinate, so that $n_v = \lambda_v\, \bar c(v)$ with
  $\lambda_v \neq 0$. Any $j$ of the normals, for $2 \le j \le 4$, have a
  nonzero $j \times j$ minor, so they are linearly independent, and so are
  the vectors $\bar c(v)$, which differ from them by nonzero factors. Hence any four
  or fewer of the points $c(v)$ are affinely independent
  (\cref{lem:affine-indep-iff}). The polynomial $Q_2$ is not zero. At the
  normals $(1, t_v, t_v^2, t_v^3)$ of the moment curve, with distinct
  nonzero $t_v$, the last coordinates are $t_v^3 \neq 0$, and each of the
  minors is a Vandermonde determinant, which is nonzero.

  A third polynomial $Q_3$ tests nonparallel panels: the product, over
  the ordered pairs $(u, v)$ of distinct bodies, of the $2 \times 2$ minor
  on the first two columns of the rows $n_u$ and $n_v$. Where it is not
  zero, the first three coordinates of $n_u$ and of $n_v$ are linearly
  independent, so the panels are nonparallel
  (\cref{def:panel-nonparallel}). At the same moment-curve normals each
  factor is $t_v - t_u \neq 0$
  (\cref{lem:moment-curve-general-position}), so $Q_3$ is not zero.

  As $\R$ is infinite, the nonzero polynomial $Q_1 Q_2 Q_3$ has a non-root
  $q$, and at $q$ the poles are in general position up to order four and
  the panels are nonparallel. In
  particular any two of the normals are linearly independent, so every
  hinge of $(G, q)$ is nondegenerate
  (\cref{lem:general-position-support-nonzero}). The deficiency bound for
  nondegenerate hinges (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}) gives
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \le N$, and $Q_1(q) \neq 0$ gives
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G, q) \ge N$. So
  $\dim Z(G, q) = D|V| - N = D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$.

  Katoh and Tanigawa need only a nonparallel realization, one whose
  panels meet pairwise in lines, and they obtain it by a projective
  transformation (KT pp.~670--671). The square-graph dictionary needs the
  poles in general position up to order four. The argument above
  provides both.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ spans the set $W$ of bodies and
  that $6(|W| - 1) < |L|$ (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). It states the
  general position of the poles for the normals: each has a nonzero last
  coordinate, and any four or fewer of them are linearly independent. It
  also states that every hinge at an edge of $G$ is nondegenerate. Its
  endpoint selector assigns each edge of $G$ its two ends, as every
  panel-hinge framework's does (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}).
\end{fmlnote}

Katoh and Tanigawa deduce from KT Theorem~5.6 that every simple graph has
a molecular realization at the target rank (KT p.~671). Taking the poles
of \cref{lem:theorem-56-general-position} as centres, we obtain such a
realization with centres in general position up to order four.

\begin{theorem}[Molecular realizations at the target rank; KT p.~671]
  \label{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.exists_molecular_rankHypothesis_generalPosition}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-concurrent, def:general-position-placement,
        lem:theorem-56-general-position, lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}
  Let $G = (V, E)$ be a simple graph with $V \neq \emptyset$. Then there
  is a placement $c \colon V \to \R^3$ in general position up to order
  four (\cref{def:general-position-placement}) such that the space
  $Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$ of infinitesimal motions of the molecular
  framework on $(G, c)$ (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent}) has dimension
  \[
    \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G),
  \]
  where $D = 6$. In particular, if $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$,
  then this molecular framework is infinitesimally rigid.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:projective-invariance, lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}
  Let $(G, p)$ be the realization of
  \cref{lem:theorem-56-general-position}, with normals
  $n_v = \lambda_v\, \bar c(v)$, where each $\lambda_v \neq 0$ and the
  points $c(v)$ are in general position up to order four. Up to the
  factors $\lambda_v$, these are the normals $\bar c(v)$ of the image of
  the molecular framework on $(G, c)$ under the polarity
  (\cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}(1)). The factors multiply the
  supporting extensor at an edge with ends $u$ and $v$ by
  $\lambda_u \lambda_v \neq 0$, which does not change the motion space
  (\cref{thm:projective-invariance}(3)). So by
  \cref{lem:panel-hinge-dual-molecular}(2),
  \[
    \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) \;=\; \dim Z(G, p)
    \;=\; D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G).
  \]
  If $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, then
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) = D$. As $V \neq \emptyset$, the trivial
  motions form a $D$-dimensional subspace of $Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}), so every infinitesimal motion
  is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem assumes that $G$ spans the set $W$ of bodies and
  that $6(|W| - 1) < |L|$ (\cref{rem:fresh-edge-supply}). It also returns
  the endpoint selector of the molecular framework, and states that it
  assigns each edge of $G$ its two ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). This is the form in which
  \cref{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint} takes the framework. The
  rescaling in the proof is proved directly, from the equality of the
  spans of the supporting extensors, by the inclusion of motion spaces
  of \cref{lem:rank-rotation-semicont} in both directions.
  The pinned theorem does not state the case
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$, which the last paragraph of the
  proof derives. Its formal step is the last pin of
  \cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}: a framework on a nonempty set
  of bodies whose motion space has dimension $D$ is infinitesimally
  rigid.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{The square-graph dictionary}
\label{sec:molecule-modelling-equivalences}

We prove Whiteley's equivalence for the motion spaces at one placement
$c$ in general position up to order four. A screw assignment $S$ of the
molecular framework on $(G, c)$ gives each atom $v$ the velocity of its
own screw $S(v)$ at its centre $c(v)$. We show that this map is an
isomorphism from the motions of the molecular framework onto those of
the bar-joint framework on $G^2$ at $c$. Its inverse recovers the screw
of a body $v$ from the velocities at the centres of the closed
neighbourhood $N[v]$, which is a clique of $G^2$
(\cref{lem:square-cliques}). Neither Jackson and Jord\'an nor Katoh and
Tanigawa say which general position the equivalence needs. The proof
needs any four centres of a closed neighbourhood to be affinely
independent, because four coplanar points admit the out-of-plane flex of
a flat complete quadrilateral, which is not the field of velocities of a
screw (the end of the proof of \cref{lem:screw-determination}). It also
needs every atom to have at least two bonds. Whiteley notes that, except
in how atoms with only one bond or none are handled, the hinge model of
a molecule is equivalent to the bond-bending networks used in modelling
glasses~\cite[p.~27]{whiteley1999}.

\begin{theorem}[Molecular frameworks and bar-joint frameworks on the
  square; Whiteley]
  \label{thm:molecular-iff-square-bar-joint}
  \lean{CombinatorialRigidity.Molecular.molecular_finrank_motions_eq_square_ker}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:hinge-concurrent, def:square-graph, lem:square-cliques,
        def:framework, lem:screw-velocity-line,
        lem:screw-determination, def:general-position-placement}
  Let $G = (V,E)$ be a graph of minimum degree at least two, and let
  $c \colon V \to \R^3$ be a placement in general position up to order
  four. Then the space $Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$ of infinitesimal motions
  of the molecular framework on $(G, c)$ (\cref{def:hinge-concurrent})
  and the kernel of the rigidity map $R(G^2, c)$ of the square of $G$
  (\cref{def:square-graph}) are isomorphic. So
  \[
    \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c) \;=\; \dim \ker R(G^2, c),
  \]
  and $\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, c) = 3|V| - \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$.
  Moreover, $G$ has an infinitesimally rigid molecular realization with
  nondegenerate hinges, that is, with $c(u) \neq c(v)$ at every edge
  $uv$, if and only if $G^2$ has an infinitesimally rigid bar-joint
  realization in $\R^3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:framework-finrank, thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular,
    def:isInfinitesimallyRigid, lem:exists-generic-general-position}
  \emph{The map.} For a screw assignment $S$, let $\Phi(S)$ be the
  velocity assignment $v \mapsto \mathrm{vel}_{S(v)}(c(v))$
  (\cref{def:screw-velocity}). It is linear in $S$. Let
  $S \in Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$, and let $u$ be a neighbour of a vertex
  $v$. By the hinge constraint at $uv$, the screw $S(u) - S(v)$ is a
  multiple of $\bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)$, whose velocity field vanishes
  at $c(u)$ (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}). So
  $\Phi(S)(u) = \mathrm{vel}_{S(v)}(c(u))$: on the closed neighbourhood
  $N[v]$, the velocities $\Phi(S)$ are those of the single screw $S(v)$.

  \emph{Motions go to motions.} Every edge $xy$ of $G^2$ has both ends in
  $N[w]$ for some vertex $w$ (\cref{lem:square-cliques}). Hence
  $\Phi(S)(x) - \Phi(S)(y) =
  \mathrm{vel}_{S(w)}(c(x)) - \mathrm{vel}_{S(w)}(c(y))$, which is
  orthogonal to $c(x) - c(y)$ (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}). So
  $\Phi(S)$ lies in $\ker R(G^2, c)$ (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}).

  \emph{Injectivity.} Let $\Phi(S) = 0$, and let $v$ be a vertex. The
  velocity field of $S(v)$ vanishes at the centres of $N[v]$. There are at
  least three of them, as $v$ has at least two neighbours, and they are
  not collinear, as $c$ is in general position. So $S(v) = 0$
  (\cref{lem:screw-determination}(1)).

  \emph{Surjectivity.} Let $x \in \ker R(G^2, c)$, and let $v$ be a
  vertex. The closed neighbourhood $N[v]$ is a clique of $G^2$
  (\cref{lem:square-cliques}), so
  $\langle c(a) - c(b),\, x(a) - x(b) \rangle = 0$ for all
  $a, b \in N[v]$. The centres of $N[v]$ include three non-collinear
  points, and any four of them are affinely independent. By
  \cref{lem:screw-determination}(2) there is a unique screw $S(v)$ with
  $\mathrm{vel}_{S(v)}(c(a)) = x(a)$ for every $a \in N[v]$. In particular
  $\Phi(S)(v) = x(v)$. Let $uv$ be an edge of $G$. Both $u$ and $v$ lie in
  $N[u]$ and in $N[v]$, so the velocity fields of $S(u)$ and $S(v)$ agree
  at $c(u)$ and at $c(v)$. As $c(u) \neq c(v)$, the screw $S(u) - S(v)$
  is a multiple of $\bar c(u) \vee \bar c(v)$
  (\cref{lem:screw-velocity-line}), which is the hinge constraint at $uv$.
  So $S \in Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$, and $\Phi(S) = x$.

  Hence $\Phi$ restricts to an isomorphism from $Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$
  onto $\ker R(G^2, c)$. The map $R(G^2, c)$ is defined on a space of
  dimension $3|V|$ (\cref{lem:framework-finrank}), so its rank is
  $3|V| - \dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c)$.

  \emph{Realizations.} Both sides of the equivalence hold when
  $V = \emptyset$, so let $V \neq \emptyset$. Suppose first that the
  molecular framework on $(G, c_0)$ is infinitesimally rigid for some
  $c_0$ with $c_0(u) \neq c_0(v)$ at every edge $uv$. Its motions are the
  trivial ones, so $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c_0) = D$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}). Its hinges are nondegenerate,
  so $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c_0) \ge D + \operatorname{def}(\tilde G)$
  (\cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound}), and
  $\operatorname{def}(\tilde G) = 0$. By
  \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular} there is a placement $c'$ in
  general position up to order four with
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c') = D = 6$. By the first part,
  $\dim \ker R(G^2, c') = 6$, so $c'$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$
  (\cref{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}).

  Conversely, let $p$ be infinitesimally rigid for $G^2$, so that
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, p) \ge 3|V| - 6$. Let $c'$ be a placement
  that is generic for row independence and in general position up to
  order four (\cref{lem:exists-generic-general-position}). A set of edges
  of $G^2$ that is row-independent at $p$ is row-independent at $c'$
  (\cref{def:generic-placement}), so
  $\operatorname{rank} R(G^2, c') \ge 3|V| - 6$. By the first part,
  $\dim Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c') \le 6 = D$. The trivial motions form a
  $D$-dimensional subspace of $Z_{\mathrm{mol}}(G, c')$
  (\cref{lem:trivial-motions-rank-bound}), so the molecular framework on
  $(G, c')$ is infinitesimally rigid. Its hinges are nondegenerate, as
  $c'$ is injective.
\end{proof}

\begin{fmlnote}
  The pinned theorem states the equality of the dimensions. It builds the
  molecular framework on a multigraph on $V$ in which two distinct
  vertices are joined by an edge exactly when they are adjacent in $G$,
  with an endpoint selector that assigns each edge its two ends
  (\cref{fmlnote:panel-hinge-nondegenerate}). Edges with the same ends
  impose the same hinge constraint, and a loop imposes none, so the
  motions are those of the molecular framework on $(G, c)$. No
  declaration states the equivalence of realizations in the last sentence
  of the theorem. Its proof above combines the pinned theorem with those
  of \cref{thm:panel-hinge-iff-molecular},
  \cref{lem:relative-deficiency-rank-bound} and
  \cref{lem:exists-generic-general-position}.
\end{fmlnote}
