\section{Body-hinge Tay--Whiteley theorem}
\label{sec:body-hinge}

A body-hinge framework in $\R^d$ consists of rigid bodies joined by
hinges. A hinge is a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace shared by two
bodies: a pin joint in the plane, a line in $3$-space, a plane in
$4$-space~\cite[\S3.3]{whiteley1988}. The two bodies can rotate
relative to each other only about the hinge. This chapter proves the
theorem of Tay~\cite{tay1989} and Whiteley~\cite{whiteley1988} on these
frameworks. It characterizes the graphs of the independent body-hinge
frameworks, and those of the independent and infinitesimally rigid
ones. As with Tay's theorem in \cref{sec:body-bar}, we prove it in the
existence form.

As in \cref{sec:body-bar}, let $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$. The relative
screws of two bodies form a space of dimension $D$, and a hinge between
them leaves a space of dimension one, the rotations about the hinge. So
the hinge acts as $D - 1$ bars, all meeting the hinge, whose Pl\"ucker
vectors are linearly independent (Whiteley~\cite[proof of
Theorem~10]{whiteley1988}). Replacing each hinge by such a bundle of
bars turns a body-hinge framework on a multigraph $G$ into a body-bar
framework on the multigraph $(D-1)\cdot G$, in which each edge of $G$
is repeated $D - 1$ times. We apply Tay's theorem
(\cref{thm:tay-witness}) to $(D-1)\cdot G$. The result,
\cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}, is that $G$ has an independent body-hinge
framework if and only if the edges of $(D-1)\cdot G$ form a union of
$D$ disjoint forests, and an independent and infinitesimally rigid one
if and only if $(D-1)\cdot G$ is $(D, D)$-tight.

\subsection{Body-hinge frameworks and the hinge-to-bar reduction}
\label{sec:body-hinge-reduction}

We define the multigraph $(D-1)\cdot G$ and body-hinge frameworks, and
we apply Tay's theorem to $(D-1)\cdot G$.

\begin{definition}[Edge multiplication]
  \label{def:edge-multiply}
  \lean{Graph.edgeMultiply}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:graph-sparse}
  Let $G$ be a multigraph and let $m$ be a natural number. The
  multigraph $m \cdot G$ has the vertices of $G$, and for each edge $e$
  of $G$ it has $m$ edges $(e, 1), \dots, (e, m)$, each with the ends of
  $e$.
\end{definition}

So $|E(m \cdot G)| = m\, |E(G)|$
(\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}edgeMultiply\_\allowbreak{}edgeSet\_\allowbreak{}ncard}).
For $m \ge 1$, a set of edges of $G$ meets the same vertices as the set
of their copies in $m \cdot G$
(\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}spanningVerts\_\allowbreak{}edgeMultiply}).
Katoh and Tanigawa write $\tilde G$ for
$(D-1)\cdot G$~\cite[\S1]{katohTanigawa2011}, and so do the later
chapters.

\begin{definition}[Body-hinge framework]
  \label{def:body-hinge-framework}
  \lean{Graph.BodyHingeFramework, Graph.BodyHingeFramework.toBodyBar}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edge-multiply, def:body-bar-framework}
  Let $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and let $G$ be a multigraph. A
  \emph{body-hinge framework} in $\R^d$ on $G$ assigns to each edge $e$
  of $G$ a $(d-2)$-dimensional affine subspace of $\R^d$, the hinge of
  $e$, and to the $D - 1$ copies of $e$ in $(D-1)\cdot G$ a basis of the
  space spanned by the Pl\"ucker vectors of the lines that meet the
  hinge. Its rigidity map is that of the body-bar framework on
  $(D-1)\cdot G$ with these vectors (\cref{def:rigidity-map-body-bar}).
  It is independent, or infinitesimally rigid, if this body-bar
  framework is.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formalization records a body-hinge framework on $G$ by its
  vectors alone. It is a body-bar framework on $(D-1)\cdot G$, with no
  hinge and no condition on the $D - 1$ vectors of an edge. So
  \cref{lem:edge-multiply-sparse} and \cref{thm:body-hinge-tay} are
  proved for these bundles of bars, and the frameworks they provide
  need not come from hinges. The rigidity statement for frameworks with
  hinges (\cref{def:hinge-constraint}), at every generic choice of the
  hinges, is \cref{cor:bodyhinge-generic-tree-packing}.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{lemma}[Tay's theorem for the multiplied multigraph]
  \label{lem:edge-multiply-sparse}
  \lean{Graph.BodyHingeFramework.edgeMultiply_isSparse_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edge-multiply, def:graph-sparse}
  Let $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and let $G$ be a multigraph. Then there is
  an independent body-hinge framework in $\R^d$ on $G$ if and only if
  $(D-1)\cdot G$ is $(D, D)$-sparse. There is a body-hinge framework in
  $\R^d$ on $G$ that is independent and infinitesimally rigid if and
  only if $(D-1)\cdot G$ is $(D, D)$-tight.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-hinge-framework, thm:tay-witness}
  By \cref{def:body-hinge-framework}, a body-hinge framework on $G$ is
  independent, or infinitesimally rigid, exactly when the body-bar
  framework on $(D-1)\cdot G$ with the same vectors is. In the
  formalization every body-bar framework on $(D-1)\cdot G$ arises in
  this way, as the formalization note after
  \cref{def:body-hinge-framework} explains
  (\texttt{Graph.\allowbreak{}BodyHingeFramework.\allowbreak{}exists\_\allowbreak{}toBodyBar\_\allowbreak{}iff}).
  So the two statements are those of \cref{thm:tay-witness} for the
  multigraph $(D-1)\cdot G$.
\end{proof}

Since $|E((D-1)\cdot G)| = (D-1)|E(G)|$, the multigraph $(D-1)\cdot G$
is $(D, D)$-tight if and only if $(D-1)|E(G)| = D|V(G)| - D$ and
$(D-1)|E'| \le D|V(E')| - D$ for every non-empty set $E'$ of edges of
$G$. These inequalities suffice, because a set of edges of
$(D-1)\cdot G$ meets the same vertices as the set of all copies of the
edges of $G$ that it uses, and is no larger.

\subsection{The Tay--Whiteley theorem}
\label{sec:body-hinge-tay}

Tay~\cite[\S7]{tay1989} and Whiteley~\cite[Theorem~10]{whiteley1988}
characterized, independently, the graphs of the infinitesimally rigid
body-hinge frameworks. Whiteley's introduction also states the
characterization of the graphs of the independent ones.

\begin{theorem}[Body-hinge frameworks; Tay, Whiteley]
  \label{thm:body-hinge-tay}
  \lean{Graph.BodyHingeFramework.body_hinge_tay}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:body-hinge-framework, lem:edge-multiply-sparse,
    thm:tay-witness, thm:tutte-nash-williams, cor:k-spanning-trees}
  Let $d \ge 2$ and $D = \binom{d+1}{2}$, and let $G$ be a multigraph.
  Then there is an independent body-hinge framework in $\R^d$ on $G$ if
  and only if $E((D-1)\cdot G)$ is the union of $D$ pairwise disjoint
  forests. There is a body-hinge framework in $\R^d$ on $G$ that is
  independent and infinitesimally rigid if and only if $(D-1)\cdot G$
  is $(D, D)$-tight.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edge-multiply-sparse, thm:tay-witness, thm:tutte-nash-williams,
    cor:k-spanning-trees}
  By \cref{lem:edge-multiply-sparse}, there is an independent
  body-hinge framework on $G$ if and only if $(D-1)\cdot G$ is
  $(D, D)$-sparse. By \cref{thm:tutte-nash-williams}, this holds if and
  only if $E((D-1)\cdot G)$ is the union of $D$ pairwise disjoint
  forests. The second statement is that of
  \cref{lem:edge-multiply-sparse}.
\end{proof}

If $G$ is connected, then so is $(D-1)\cdot G$. By
\cref{cor:k-spanning-trees}, the second condition then makes
$E((D-1)\cdot G)$ the union of $D$ disjoint spanning trees. Katoh and
Tanigawa state the theorem for infinitesimal rigidity alone (KT
Proposition~1.1): $G$ has an infinitesimally rigid body-hinge framework
in $\R^d$ if and only if $\tilde G$ contains $D$ edge-disjoint spanning
trees. For frameworks with hinges in general position, this form is
\cref{cor:bodyhinge-generic-tree-packing}.

Katoh and Tanigawa call a body-hinge framework a panel-hinge framework
if, for each body, the hinges at that body lie in a common
hyperplane~\cite[\S1]{katohTanigawa2011}. Tay and
Whiteley~\cite{tayWhiteley1984} conjectured that a graph has an
infinitesimally rigid body-hinge framework in $\R^d$ if and only if it
has an infinitesimally rigid panel-hinge framework in $\R^d$ (KT
Conjecture~1.2). This is the molecular conjecture. It does not follow
from \cref{thm:body-hinge-tay}, which places no condition on the hinges
at a body. Katoh and Tanigawa proved it, and we prove it in
\cref{thm:molecular-conjecture}, following them.
