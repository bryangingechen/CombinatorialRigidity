\section{Henneberg moves}
\label{sec:henneberg}

This section studies two ways of adding a vertex to a graph, the
Henneberg moves. The Type~I move adds a vertex of degree two. The
Type~II move deletes an edge $ab$ and adds a vertex of degree three,
joined to $a$, $b$ and a third vertex. The Laman graphs are exactly
the graphs that can be built from $K_2$ by these moves, a
characterization due to Henneberg~\cite{henneberg1911} and
Laman~\cite{laman1970}. Jord\'an~\cite{jordan2016} proves it as
Theorem~2.2.8, for minimally rigid graphs, which are the Laman graphs
(\cref{sec:laman}).

Each half of this characterization follows by induction on the number
of vertices, and we prove the two inductive steps. After the
definitions and a count of the edges that each move adds, we show that
the moves preserve the Laman property. The other step is the reverse
decomposition (\cref{thm:isLaman-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}):
every Laman graph on at least three vertices has a vertex $v$ of
degree two or three such that deleting $v$, and in degree three
joining two of its neighbours by an edge, leaves a Laman graph. The
proof that every Laman graph is generically rigid in the plane
(\cref{thm:isLaman-isGenericallyRigid-two}) is an induction with this
step. The section ends with an example.

Throughout, $G$ is a graph on a vertex set $V$, which is finite where
a statement says so. We write $ab$ for the edge with endpoints $a$ and
$b$, and $v$ for the vertex that a move adds, so that the graph the
move produces has vertex set $V \cup \{v\}$.

\subsection{Definitions}

In Jord\'an's survey~\cite{jordan2016} (\S 1.2.1, p.~38) the two moves
are the $0$-extension and the $1$-extension in the plane, also called
Henneberg moves of type~I and type~II.

\begin{definition}[Type~I move]
  \label{def:typeI}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI}
  \leanok
  Let $a, b \in V$. The \emph{Type~I move at $a$ and $b$} adds to $G$
  a new vertex $v$ and the edges $va$ and $vb$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Type~II move]
  \label{def:typeII}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII}
  \leanok
  Let $a, b, c \in V$. The \emph{Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$} deletes
  the edge $ab$ from $G$ if it is present, and adds a new vertex $v$
  and the edges $va$, $vb$ and $vc$.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:henneberg-new-vertex}
  Both moves are formalized as graphs on $\mathrm{Option}\,V$, which
  has an element $\mathrm{some}\,u$ for each $u \in V$ and one further
  element $\mathrm{none}$. The new vertex $v$ is $\mathrm{none}$, and
  each vertex $u$ of $G$ becomes $\mathrm{some}\,u$. This is the
  standard way in mathlib to adjoin one element, and it makes $v$ new
  by construction, so no statement needs a hypothesis saying so. The
  formal statements about a move, here and in later sections, compare
  a graph on $V$ with a graph on $\mathrm{Option}\,V$ through the map
  $u \mapsto \mathrm{some}\,u$, and the text identifies each $u$ with
  its image.
\end{fmlnote}

The definitions allow any vertices $a$, $b$ and $c$, but we use the
moves only under Jord\'an's conditions (p.~44). For the Type~I move at
$a$ and $b$ we assume $a \ne b$, so that $v$ has degree two; if
$a = b$, the move adds the single edge $va$. For the Type~II move on
$ab$ and $c$ we assume that $ab$ is an edge of $G$ and that $c$ is
neither $a$ nor $b$, so that $v$ has degree three and the number of
edges grows by two.

\subsection{Edge sets and edge counts}

We describe the edge set of the graph that each move produces, and
count it. We use the counts to verify the equation $|E| + 3 = 2|V|$
of a Laman graph in the preservation theorems below and in the reverse
decomposition.

\begin{lemma}[Edge set of Type~I]
  \label{lem:typeI-edgeSet}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_edgeSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI}
  Let $a, b \in V$, let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move
  at $a$ and $b$, and let $v$ be its new vertex. Then
  \[
    E(G') \;=\; E(G) \cup \{va, vb\}.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  An edge of $G'$ either contains $v$ or joins two vertices of $G$. By
  \cref{def:typeI}, the edges of $G'$ at $v$ are $va$ and $vb$, and two
  vertices of $G$ are adjacent in $G'$ exactly when they are adjacent
  in $G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Edge count of Type~I]
  \label{lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_edgeSet_ncard}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:typeI-edgeSet}
  Let $V$ be finite, let $a \ne b$ be vertices of $G$, and let $G'$ be
  obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$. Then
  \[
    |E(G')| \;=\; |E(G)| + 2.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The two sets on the right of \cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet} are disjoint,
  since the edges $va$ and $vb$ contain the new vertex $v$ and the
  edges of $G$ do not. The edges $va$ and $vb$ are distinct because
  $a \ne b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Edge set of Type~II]
  \label{lem:typeII-edgeSet}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_edgeSet}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII}
  Let $a, b, c \in V$, let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~II
  move on $ab$ and $c$, and let $v$ be its new vertex. Then
  \[
    E(G') \;=\; \bigl(E(G) \setminus \{ab\}\bigr) \cup \{va, vb, vc\}.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As in the proof of \cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet}, an edge of $G'$ either
  contains $v$ or joins two vertices of $G$. By \cref{def:typeII}, the
  edges of $G'$ at $v$ are $va$, $vb$ and $vc$, and two vertices of $G$
  are adjacent in $G'$ exactly when they are adjacent in $G$ and are
  not $a$ and $b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Edge count of Type~II]
  \label{lem:typeII-edgeSet-ncard}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_edgeSet_ncard}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:typeII-edgeSet}
  Let $V$ be finite, let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be distinct vertices of $G$,
  and let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~II move on $ab$ and
  $c$. Then
  \[
    |E(G')| \;=\; \bigl|E(G) \setminus \{ab\}\bigr| + 3.
  \]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As in the proof of \cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}, the two sets on
  the right of \cref{lem:typeII-edgeSet} are disjoint. The edges $va$,
  $vb$ and $vc$ are distinct because $a$, $b$ and $c$ are.
\end{proof}

When $ab$ is an edge of $G$, as in the preservation theorems, the
right-hand side is $|E(G)| + 2$.

\subsection{Preservation of the Laman property}

We show that both moves preserve the Laman property, and that the
Type~I move also reflects it. Jord\'an~\cite{jordan2016} proves the
sparsity part of these facts as Lemmas 2.1.5 and 2.1.4(a), where the
two moves are called the $0$-extension and the $1$-extension.

\begin{theorem}[Type~I preserves the Laman property]
  \label{thm:typeI-isLaman}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_isLaman}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:isLaman, lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}
  Let $G$ be a Laman graph and let $a \ne b$ be vertices of $G$. Let
  $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$. Then
  $G'$ is Laman.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $v$ be the new vertex of $G'$. The graph $G'$ has one more vertex
  than $G$ and two more edges (\cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}), so
  $|E(G')| + 3 = 2|V(G')|$ follows from $|E(G)| + 3 = 2|V(G)|$.

  For $(2,3)$-sparsity (\cref{def:isSparse}) we show that every set $X$
  of at least two vertices of $G'$ spans at most $2|X| - 3$ edges. Let
  $X_0 = X \setminus \{v\}$. The edges of $G'$ inside $X$ are the edges
  of $G$ inside $X_0$, together with those of the new edges $va$ and
  $vb$ that lie inside $X$. If $v \notin X$, then $X = X_0$, and the
  bound holds because $G$ is sparse. Otherwise $|X| = |X_0| + 1$. If
  $|X_0| = 1$, then $X_0$ spans no edge and at most one new edge lies
  inside $X$, so $X$ spans at most $1 = 2|X| - 3$ edges. If
  $|X_0| \ge 2$, then
  \[
    |\edgesIn[G']{X}| \;\le\; |\edgesIn{X_0}| + 2
    \;\le\; (2|X_0| - 3) + 2 \;=\; 2|X| - 3.
  \]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type~II preserves the Laman property]
  \label{thm:typeII-isLaman}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_isLaman}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:isLaman, lem:typeII-edgeSet-ncard}
  Let $G$ be a Laman graph, let $ab$ be an edge of $G$, and let
  $c \ne a, b$ be a vertex of $G$. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the
  Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$. Then $G'$ is Laman.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $v$ be the new vertex of $G'$. The move deletes $ab$ and adds
  $va$, $vb$ and $vc$, so $G'$ has one more vertex than $G$ and two more
  edges (\cref{lem:typeII-edgeSet-ncard}). The count follows as in
  \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}.

  For sparsity, let $X$ and $X_0$ be as in the proof of
  \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}. The edges of $G'$ inside $X$ are the edges
  of $G$ inside $X_0$ other than $ab$, together with those of $va$,
  $vb$ and $vc$ that lie inside $X$. The cases $v \notin X$ and
  $|X_0| = 1$ go as before. Suppose that $v \in X$ and $|X_0| \ge 2$.
  If $a$ and $b$ both lie in $X_0$, then $ab$ is an edge of $G$ inside
  $X_0$ that $G'$ does not have, and at most three new edges lie inside
  $X$. Otherwise one of $va$ and $vb$ does not lie inside $X$, so at
  most two new edges do. In both cases
  $|\edgesIn[G']{X}| \le |\edgesIn{X_0}| + 2 \le 2|X| - 3$.
\end{proof}

The reverse decomposition (\cref{sec:henneberg-decomp}) needs the
converse of \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}, to show that removing a vertex of
degree two from a Laman graph leaves a Laman graph.

\begin{theorem}[Type~I reflects the Laman property]
  \label{thm:typeI-reverse-isLaman}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_reverse_isLaman}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:isLaman, lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}
  Let $G$ be a finite graph and let $a \ne b$ be vertices of $G$. Let
  $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$. If $G'$
  is Laman, then $G$ is Laman.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $v$ be the new vertex of $G'$. Both new edges contain $v$, so a
  set of vertices of $G$ spans the same edges in $G'$ as in $G$. Hence
  $G$ is $(2,3)$-sparse because $G'$ is. The count follows from
  \cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet-ncard}, as in \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type~I preserves and reflects the Laman property]
  \label{thm:typeI-isLaman-iff}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_isLaman_iff}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:typeI-isLaman, thm:typeI-reverse-isLaman}
  Let $G$ be a finite graph and let $a \ne b$ be vertices of $G$. Let
  $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$. Then
  $G'$ is Laman if and only if $G$ is Laman.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Combine \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman} and
  \cref{thm:typeI-reverse-isLaman}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reverse decomposition}
\label{sec:henneberg-decomp}

Let $v$ be a vertex of $G$. We write $G - v$ for the graph on
$V \setminus \{v\}$ obtained from $G$ by deleting $v$, and, for
vertices $a, b \ne v$, we write $G - v + ab$ for the graph obtained
from $G - v$ by adding the edge $ab$, as in
\cref{sec:isSparse-typeII-reverse-blocker}. Deleting $v$ reverses a
move. If $v$ has degree two, with neighbours $a$ and $b$, then the
Type~I move at $a$ and $b$ applied to $G - v$ gives a copy of $G$. If
$v$ has degree three, with neighbours $a$, $b$ and $c$, where $a$ and
$b$ are not adjacent, then the Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$ applied to
$G - v + ab$ gives a copy of $G$. The theorem below finds a vertex at
which the smaller graph is again Laman.

Most of the work is in the same statement for $(2,3)$-sparse graphs,
\cref{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}, in which $v$ may
also have degree one. A sparse graph with an edge has a vertex $v$ of
degree one, two or three, and Jord\'an's Lemma~2.1.4~\cite{jordan2016}
treats the last two cases. If $v$ has degree two, then $G - v$ is
sparse. If $v$ has degree three, then $v$ has a suitable splitting
(\cref{sec:isSparse-typeII-reverse-blocker}): two neighbours $a$ and
$b$ of $v$ that are not adjacent and make $G - v + ab$ sparse. We
prove the sparse statement in
\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-sparse-reverse}, where it is also the
inductive step in the proof that the edge set of every $(2,3)$-sparse
graph is independent in the rigidity matroid. Its proof uses only the
results of \cref{sec:edgesIn} and \cref{sec:sparsity}.

\begin{theorem}[Laman reverse decomposition]
  \label{thm:isLaman-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.IsLaman.exists_typeI_or_typeII_reverse}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isLaman, def:typeI, def:typeII,
    thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse,
    thm:typeI-isLaman-iff,
    lem:typeII-edgeSet-ncard,
    lem:isLaman-two-le-degree}
  Let $V$ be finite with $|V| \ge 3$, and let $G$ be a Laman graph on
  $V$. Then $G$ has a vertex $v$ for which one of the following holds.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The vertex $v$ has degree two, with neighbours $a$ and $b$,
      and $G - v$ is Laman.
    \item The vertex $v$ has degree three, with neighbours $a$, $b$ and
      $c$, where $a$ and $b$ are not adjacent, and $G - v + ab$ is
      Laman.
  \end{enumerate}
  Here $G - v$ is the graph on $V \setminus \{v\}$ obtained from $G$
  by deleting $v$, and $G - v + ab$ is obtained from $G - v$ by adding
  the edge $ab$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Since $G$ is Laman and $|V| \ge 3$, it is $(2,3)$-sparse and has
  $2|V| - 3 \ge 3$ edges. So
  \cref{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse} gives a vertex
  $v$ of degree one, two or three. Every vertex of $G$ has degree at
  least two (\cref{lem:isLaman-two-le-degree}), so $v$ has degree two
  or three.

  Suppose that $v$ has degree two, with neighbours $a$ and $b$. Then
  $G$ is isomorphic to the graph obtained from $G - v$ by the Type~I
  move at $a$ and $b$, so that graph is Laman (\cref{lem:isLaman-iso}).
  Hence $G - v$ is Laman by \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman-iff}.

  Suppose that $v$ has degree three. Then the sparse statement gives
  neighbours $a$, $b$ and $c$ of $v$, where $a$ and $b$ are not
  adjacent, such that $H = G - v + ab$ is $(2,3)$-sparse. The Type~II
  move on $ab$ and $c$ deletes from $H$ the edge $ab$ that $H$ adds to
  $G - v$, and it adds $v$ back. So $G$ is isomorphic to the graph $H'$
  obtained from $H$ by this move, and $H'$ is Laman
  (\cref{lem:isLaman-iso}). As $H'$ has $|V|$ vertices,
  $|E(H')| + 3 = 2|V|$. Since $ab$ is an edge of $H$,
  \cref{lem:typeII-edgeSet-ncard} gives $|E(H')| = |E(H)| + 2$. Hence
  $|E(H)| + 3 = 2(|V| - 1)$, and $H$ is Laman.
\end{proof}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The formal statement builds $G - v$ and $G - v + ab$ on a separate
  copy of $V \setminus \{v\}$, as in \cref{fmlnote:graph-minus-vertex}.
  The isomorphisms between $G$ and the moved graphs, in the proof above
  and in the induction of Laman's theorem
  (\cref{thm:isLaman-isGenericallyRigid-two}), are built from the
  neighbours of $v$. \Cref{fmlnote:reverse-vs-lift-form} explains why
  a reverse decomposition describes the smaller graph directly, and not
  as a graph to which a move is applied.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{An example: \texorpdfstring{$K_4$}{K4} minus an edge}

We build $K_4$ minus an edge from $K_2$ by two Type~I moves.

\begin{theorem}[$K_4$ minus an edge is Laman]
  \label{thm:top-fin-four-minus-edge-isLaman}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.top_fin_four_minus_edge_isLaman}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:typeI-isLaman, thm:top-fin-two-isLaman, lem:isLaman-iso}
  The graph on $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ obtained from the complete graph $K_4$
  by deleting the edge $23$ is Laman.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The complete graph on $\{0, 1\}$ is Laman
  (\cref{thm:top-fin-two-isLaman}). The Type~I move at $0$ and $1$
  adds a new vertex $2$ and the edges $20$ and $21$. The result is a
  triangle, which is Laman by \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}. A second
  Type~I move at $0$ and $1$ adds a new vertex $3$ and the edges $30$
  and $31$. The result is Laman by \cref{thm:typeI-isLaman}, and its
  edges are the pairs from $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ other than $23$.

  In the formal proof the two new vertices are not the numbers $2$ and
  $3$ (\cref{fmlnote:henneberg-new-vertex}). The graph built is
  therefore isomorphic to the graph of the statement, by the bijection
  that sends the first new vertex to $2$ and the second to $3$. The
  Laman property passes along this isomorphism
  (\cref{lem:isLaman-iso}).
\end{proof}
