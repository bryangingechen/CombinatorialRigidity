\section{Rigidity preservation under Henneberg moves (dimension 2)}
\label{sec:henneberg-rigidity}

We show that both Henneberg moves (\cref{sec:henneberg}) preserve
injective generic rigidity in the plane
(\cref{def:isGenericallyRigidInj}). This is the inductive step in the
proof that every Laman graph is generically rigid in the plane
(\cref{thm:isLaman-isGenericallyRigid-two}). That induction is on the
number of vertices and starts from $K_2$
(\cref{thm:top-fin-two-isGenericallyRigidInj}). By the reverse
decomposition (\cref{thm:isLaman-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}),
every Laman graph on at least three vertices is obtained, up to
isomorphism, from a smaller Laman graph by a move.

Jord\'an~\cite{jordan2016} calls the two moves the $0$-extension and
the $1$-extension. His Lemmas~1.2.2 and~1.2.3 (p.~38) state that in
$\R^{d}$, if the points involved are in general position, each of
these moves raises the rank of the rigidity matrix of a framework by
$d$. He cites Whiteley's Vertex $d$-Addition Lemma~11.1.1 and Edge
$d$-Split Theorem~11.1.7~\cite{whiteley1996}. In the plane our
theorems assume the same general-position conditions. We prove the
half of the conclusion that rigidity needs: the kernel of the rigidity
map does not grow. By rank--nullity, this means that the rank grows by
at least~$2$.

Let $p$ be an infinitesimally rigid framework of $G$, and let $G'$ be
obtained from $G$ by a move with new vertex $v$. We extend $p$ to
$G'$ by placing $v$ at a point $q$, and we restrict each
infinitesimal motion of $G'$ to the vertices of $G$. We show that the
restriction is an infinitesimal motion of $G$, and that it determines
the velocity of $v$. It does so when two new edges at $v$ have
linearly independent directions. Then the kernel of the rigidity map
of $G'$ has dimension at most that of $G$, so the extended framework
is again infinitesimally rigid.

For the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$, every edge of $G$ is an edge of
$G'$, and we place $v$ off the line through $p_a$ and $p_b$. The
Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$ deletes the edge $ab$, so the
restriction must also satisfy the constraint of $ab$. As in
Whiteley's proof of his Theorem~11.1.7, we place $v$ on the line
through $p_a$ and $p_b$, distinct from both. The new edges $va$ and
$vb$ then point along this line, and together they imply the
constraint of $ab$. The new edges $va$ and $vc$ determine the
velocity of $v$ if $p_a$, $p_b$ and $p_c$ are not collinear. For each
move we first prove that the extended framework is infinitesimally
rigid for a given point $q$, and then choose $q$.

Throughout, frameworks are in $\R^{2}$. A framework $p$ on a finite
set $V$ is infinitesimally rigid for a graph $G$ on $V$ when
$\dim \ker R(G, p) \le 3$ (\cref{def:isInfinitesimallyRigid}). An
infinitesimal motion $x$, an element of this kernel, gives each
vertex $u$ a velocity $x_u \in \R^{2}$. As in \cref{sec:henneberg},
$v$ is the new vertex of a move, and the graph that the move produces
has vertex set $V \cup \{v\}$. \Cref{fmlnote:henneberg-new-vertex}
describes how the new vertex is encoded formally.

\subsection{The Type~I move}

To choose a point $q$ off the line through $p_a$ and $p_b$, we need
$p_a \ne p_b$. An injective framework has $p_a \ne p_b$ when
$a \ne b$, and this is why we work with injective frameworks.

\begin{theorem}[Infinitesimal rigidity under the Type~I move]
  \label{thm:typeI-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_isInfinitesimallyRigid_extend}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:isInfinitesimallyRigid, lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $p$ be a framework on
  $V$ in $\R^{2}$ that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$, and let $G'$
  be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at vertices $a$ and $b$, with
  new vertex $v$. Let $q \in \R^{2}$ be a point such
  that $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly independent, that is,
  $q$, $p_a$ and $p_b$ are not collinear. Let $\hat p$ be the
  framework on $V \cup \{v\}$ that agrees with $p$ on $V$ and places
  $v$ at $q$. Then $\hat p$ is infinitesimally rigid
  for $G'$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $x$ be an infinitesimal motion of $G'$ at $\hat p$, and let
  $\rho(x)$ be its restriction to $V$. Every edge $uw$ of $G$ is an
  edge of $G'$. The entry of the rigidity map at $uw$ depends only on
  the points and the velocities of $u$ and $w$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}). So $\rho(x)$ is an infinitesimal
  motion of $G$ at $p$, and $\rho$ is a linear map from
  $\ker R(G', \hat p)$ to $\ker R(G, p)$.

  We show that $\rho$ is injective. Suppose that $\rho(x) = 0$, so
  that $x_u = 0$ for every $u \in V$. The entries of
  $R(G', \hat p)(x)$ at the new edges $va$ and $vb$ vanish:
  \[
    \langle q - p_a,\, x_v - x_a \rangle \;=\; 0
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \langle q - p_b,\, x_v - x_b \rangle \;=\; 0.
  \]
  Since $x_a = x_b = 0$, the velocity $x_v$ is orthogonal to
  $q - p_a$ and to $q - p_b$. These two vectors are linearly
  independent, so they span $\R^{2}$, and hence $x_v = 0$. So $x = 0$.

  Since $\rho$ is injective and $p$ is infinitesimally rigid for $G$,
  \[
    \dim \ker R(G', \hat p) \;\le\; \dim \ker R(G, p) \;\le\; 3.
  \]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type~I preserves injective generic rigidity in dimension 2]
  \label{thm:typeI-isGenericallyRigidInj-two}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_isGenericallyRigidInj_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:isGenericallyRigidInj,
    thm:typeI-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$ that is injectively
  generically rigid in dimension~$2$, and let $a \ne b$ be vertices of
  $G$. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and
  $b$. Then $G'$ is injectively generically rigid in dimension~$2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $p$ be an injective framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ that is
  infinitesimally rigid for $G$. Since $p$ is injective and
  $a \ne b$, the points $p_a$ and $p_b$ are distinct. The line through
  them and the finitely many points $p_u$, for $u \in V$, do not cover
  $\R^{2}$. So we can choose a point $q$ off this line that is none of
  the points $p_u$. Since $q$ is off the line through $p_a$ and
  $p_b$, the vectors $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly independent.

  Let $\hat p$ be the framework that extends $p$ by placing the new
  vertex at $q$. It is infinitesimally rigid for $G'$
  (\cref{thm:typeI-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}). It is injective,
  since $p$ is injective and $q$ is none of the points $p_u$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Type~II move}

Here the point $q$ lies on the line through $p_a$ and $p_b$. We choose
it first when $p_a$, $p_b$ and $p_c$ are not collinear. We then remove
this assumption by moving $p_c$ slightly, which keeps the framework
infinitesimally rigid (\cref{sec:rigidity-openness}).

\begin{theorem}[Infinitesimal rigidity under the Type~II move]
  \label{thm:typeII-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_isInfinitesimallyRigid_extend}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:isInfinitesimallyRigid, lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $p$ be a framework on
  $V$ in $\R^{2}$ that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$, and let
  $a, b, c \in V$. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~II move
  on $ab$ and $c$, with new vertex $v$. Let
  $q = p_a + \alpha (p_b - p_a)$ for a real number $\alpha \ne 0, 1$,
  suppose that $q - p_a$ and $q - p_c$ are linearly independent, and
  let $\hat p$ be the framework on $V \cup \{v\}$ that agrees with $p$
  on $V$ and places $v$ at $q$. Then $\hat p$ is infinitesimally rigid
  for $G'$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  As in the proof of \cref{thm:typeI-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend},
  let $\rho(x)$ be the restriction to $V$ of an infinitesimal motion
  $x$ of $G'$ at $\hat p$. We first show that $\rho(x)$ is an
  infinitesimal motion of $G$ at $p$. Every edge of $G$ other than
  $ab$ is an edge of $G'$, so $\rho(x)$ satisfies the constraint of
  each such edge, as before.

  If $ab$ is an edge of $G$, the move deletes it, and we recover its
  constraint from the new edges $va$ and $vb$. We have
  $q - p_a = \alpha (p_b - p_a)$ and
  $q - p_b = (\alpha - 1)(p_b - p_a)$. The entries of
  $R(G', \hat p)(x)$ at $va$ and $vb$ vanish
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}). Dividing them by $\alpha$ and by
  $\alpha - 1$, which are not zero, gives
  \[
    \langle p_b - p_a,\, x_v - x_a \rangle \;=\; 0
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \langle p_b - p_a,\, x_v - x_b \rangle \;=\; 0.
  \]
  Subtracting the first equation from the second gives
  $\langle p_b - p_a,\, x_a - x_b \rangle = 0$. This is the
  constraint $\langle p_a - p_b,\, x_a - x_b \rangle = 0$ of the edge
  $ab$, up to sign.

  It remains to show that $\rho$ is injective. If $\rho(x) = 0$, the
  entries at the new edges $va$ and $vc$ show that $x_v$ is
  orthogonal to $q - p_a$ and to $q - p_c$. These vectors are linearly
  independent, so $x_v = 0$ and $x = 0$. As in the proof of
  \cref{thm:typeI-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}, it follows that
  $\dim \ker R(G', \hat p) \le \dim \ker R(G, p) \le 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type~II preserves injective generic rigidity, non-collinear case]
  \label{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two-of-nonCollinear}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_isGenericallyRigidInj_two_of_nonCollinear}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:isGenericallyRigidInj,
    thm:typeII-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, and let $p$ be an injective
  framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ that is infinitesimally rigid for $G$.
  Let $a, b, c \in V$ be such that $p_b - p_a$ and $p_c - p_a$ are
  linearly independent, that is, $p_a$, $p_b$ and $p_c$ are not
  collinear. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~II move on $ab$
  and $c$. Then $G'$ is injectively generically rigid in
  dimension~$2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  For $\alpha \in \R$, let $q_\alpha = p_a + \alpha (p_b - p_a)$.
  Since $p_b - p_a \ne 0$, different values of $\alpha$ give different
  points $q_\alpha$. So all but finitely many $\alpha$ satisfy
  $\alpha \ne 0, 1$ and $q_\alpha \ne p_u$ for every $u \in V$. Choose such an $\alpha$, and
  let $q = q_\alpha$. The vectors $q - p_a = \alpha (p_b - p_a)$ and
  $q - p_c = \alpha (p_b - p_a) - (p_c - p_a)$ are linearly
  independent, since $p_b - p_a$ and $p_c - p_a$ are and
  $\alpha \ne 0$.

  Let $\hat p$ be the framework that extends $p$ by placing the new
  vertex at $q$. It is infinitesimally rigid for $G'$
  (\cref{thm:typeII-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}). It is injective,
  since $p$ is injective and $q$ is none of the points $p_u$.
\end{proof}

The placement of the new vertex $v$ on the line through $p_a$ and
$p_b$ needs $p_c$ off this line. If $p_c$ lies on the line, then all
three new edges at $v$ point along it. Moving $v$ perpendicular to the
line, and no other vertex, is then an infinitesimal motion of $G'$.
This motion is not trivial, since a trivial motion that vanishes at
two distinct points vanishes everywhere. Jord\'an also uses openness,
in his proof of Laman's theorem (Theorem~2.2.1, p.~45), to make the
three neighbours of the new vertex of a $1$-extension non-collinear.

\begin{theorem}[Type~II preserves injective generic rigidity in dimension 2]
  \label{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_isGenericallyRigidInj_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:isGenericallyRigidInj,
    thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two-of-nonCollinear,
    lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-eventually}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$ that is injectively
  generically rigid in dimension~$2$, and let $a$, $b$ and $c$ be
  distinct vertices of $G$. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the
  Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$. Then $G'$ is injectively generically
  rigid in dimension~$2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $p$ be an injective framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ that is
  infinitesimally rigid for $G$. If $p_b - p_a$ and $p_c - p_a$ are
  linearly independent, then
  \cref{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two-of-nonCollinear} applies
  to $p$.

  Otherwise, since $p_b \ne p_a$ (as $p$ is injective and $a \ne b$),
  we have
  $p_c - p_a = \delta (p_b - p_a)$ for some $\delta \in \R$. Choose a
  vector $w \in \R^{2}$ such that $w$ and $p_b - p_a$ are linearly
  independent. For $t \in \R$, let $p^t$ be the framework that agrees
  with $p$ except at $c$, where $p^t_c = p_c + t w$. The map
  $t \mapsto p^t$ is continuous, and $p^0 = p$. Infinitesimal rigidity
  is an open condition on the framework
  (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-eventually}). So is injectivity,
  since $V$ is finite. Hence $p^t$ is injective and infinitesimally
  rigid for $G$ for every $t$ close enough to $0$.

  Since $c$ is neither $a$ nor $b$, the points of $a$ and $b$ do not
  move, and
  \[
    p^t_b - p^t_a \;=\; p_b - p_a, \qquad
    p^t_c - p^t_a \;=\; \delta (p_b - p_a) + t w.
  \]
  For $t \ne 0$ these two vectors are linearly independent, because
  $w$ and $p_b - p_a$ are. Choose such a $t \ne 0$ close enough to
  $0$, and apply
  \cref{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two-of-nonCollinear} to
  $p^t$.
\end{proof}
