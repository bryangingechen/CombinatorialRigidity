\section{The planar rigidity matroid}
\label{sec:rigidity-matroid}

In \cref{sec:laman-theorem} we proved one half of the characterization
of the independent sets of the rigidity matroid in the plane by
Pollaczek-Geiringer~\cite{pollaczekGeiringer1927} and
Laman~\cite{laman1970}: an edge set that is row-independent
at a framework in the plane with no three points collinear forms a
$(2,3)$-sparse graph (\cref{lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two}). In
this section we prove the other half: the edge set of every
$(2,3)$-sparse graph is row-independent at some framework in the
plane, which Pollaczek-Geiringer proves in her~\S8 (p.~70). The two
halves together are Jord\'an's~\cite{jordan2016}
Theorem~2.2.1, which he credits to Laman, and we follow his proof
(p.~45). We then describe the rigidity matroid in the plane as a
matroid on the edges of a complete graph.

The proof is an induction that removes a vertex and puts it back by a
Henneberg move (\cref{sec:henneberg}). A vertex whose removal leaves
a smaller $(2,3)$-sparse graph exists by the reverse decomposition at
the end of \cref{sec:sparsity}
(\cref{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}). We show in
\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-lifts} that putting it back preserves
row-independence at a suitable framework, and we complete the induction
in \cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-hard-direction}.

For a finite set $V$, let $K_V$ be the complete graph on $V$. In
\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-instance} we define the planar rigidity
matroid $\mathcal{R}(V)$ by counting: its independent sets are the sets
of edges of $K_V$ that form $(2,3)$-sparse graphs. By the
characterization, these are the edge sets that are row-independent at
some framework in the plane. In
\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-linear-framing} we show that
$\mathcal{R}(V)$ is the rigidity matroid of a suitable framework of
$K_V$.

Throughout, frameworks and the rigidity map $R(G, p)$ are as in
\cref{sec:frameworks}, and we write $uv$ for the edge with endpoints
$u$ and $v$. The rows $r_e$ of $R(G, p)$ and row-independent edge sets
are defined in \cref{sec:laman-theorem}.

\begin{fmlnote}
  \label{fmlnote:reverse-vs-lift-form}
  The reverse decomposition
  (\cref{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}) describes the
  smaller graphs $G - v$ and $G - v + ab$ directly, and not as graphs
  from which a move rebuilds $G$. In the formalization the difference
  matters. A move produces a graph on a new vertex set
  (\cref{fmlnote:henneberg-new-vertex}), so the move
  applied to $G - v$ gives a graph isomorphic to $G$, not $G$ itself.
  A reverse decomposition starts from $G$, and it is stated in terms of
  $G$. The lifts of \cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-lifts}, like the results
  of \cref{sec:henneberg-rigidity}, are statements about what a move
  does, and they are stated for the graph that the move produces. Where
  a proof uses both kinds of result, as in
  \cref{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}, we pass between
  them by an isomorphism from $G$ to the graph obtained by the move,
  built from the neighbours of $v$.
\end{fmlnote}

\subsection{Lifting row-independence through Henneberg moves}
\label{sec:rigidity-matroid-lifts}

In this subsection we prove the inductive step of the induction in
\cref{sec:rigidity-matroid-hard-direction}: if $E(G)$ is
row-independent at a framework $p$, and $G'$ is obtained from $G$ by a
move, then $E(G')$ is row-independent at some framework. For the
Type~I move at $a$ and $b$ we assume that $p_a \ne p_b$. In Jord\'an's
proof the step is his Lemmas~1.2.2 and~1.2.3 (p.~38): if the points
involved are in general position, a $0$-extension or a $1$-extension
in the plane raises the rank of the rigidity matrix by two. As in
\cref{sec:henneberg-rigidity}, we first prove the statement for the
framework $\hat p$ that extends a given framework $p$ by placing the
new vertex $v$ at a given point $q$, and then choose $q$.

\begin{lemma}[Row-independence under the Type~I move]
  \label{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_edgeSetRowIndependent_extend}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, and let $p$ be a framework on
  $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is row-independent. Let $a, b \in V$,
  and let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $b$,
  with new vertex $v$. Let $q \in \R^{2}$ be a point such that
  $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly independent, and let $\hat p$ be
  the framework on $V \cup \{v\}$ that agrees with $p$ on $V$ and
  places $v$ at $q$. Then $E(G')$ is row-independent at $\hat p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply, lem:typeI-edgeSet}
  For an edge $e$ of $G$, let $r_e$ be the rigidity row of $G$ at $p$
  indexed by $e$, and for an edge $e$ of $G'$, let $\hat r_e$ be the
  rigidity row of $G'$ at $\hat p$ indexed by $e$
  (\cref{def:rigidityRow}). Since $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly
  independent, $a \ne b$. So the edges of $G'$ are the edges of $G$ and
  the two new edges $va$ and $vb$ (\cref{lem:typeI-edgeSet}). Suppose
  that
  \[
    \sum_{e \in E(G)} c_e\, \hat r_e + c_a\, \hat r_{va}
      + c_b\, \hat r_{vb} \;=\; 0.
  \]
  We show that every coefficient is zero.

  For $\alpha \in \R^{2}$, let $x^{\alpha} \in (\R^{2})^{V \cup \{v\}}$
  give $v$ the velocity $\alpha$ and every vertex of $G$ the velocity
  zero. If $e$ is an edge of $G$, then $\hat r_e(x^{\alpha}) = 0$,
  since both ends of $e$ have velocity zero
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}). At a new edge,
  $\hat r_{va}(x^{\alpha}) = \langle q - p_a,\, \alpha \rangle$.
  Evaluating the relation at $x^{\alpha}$ therefore gives
  $\langle c_a (q - p_a) + c_b (q - p_b),\, \alpha \rangle = 0$ for
  every $\alpha$. Hence $c_a (q - p_a) + c_b (q - p_b) = 0$, and
  $c_a = c_b = 0$, since $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly
  independent.

  It remains to show that $\sum_e c_e\, \hat r_e = 0$ forces every
  $c_e$ to be zero. Let $\rho : (\R^{2})^{V \cup \{v\}} \to (\R^{2})^{V}$
  be the restriction $x \mapsto x|_V$. If $e = uw$ is an edge of $G$,
  then both $\hat r_e(x)$ and $r_e(\rho(x))$ equal
  $\langle p_u - p_w,\, x_u - x_w \rangle$, so
  $\hat r_e = r_e \circ \rho$. Hence
  $\bigl(\sum_e c_e\, r_e\bigr) \circ \rho = 0$. Since $\rho$ is
  surjective, $\sum_e c_e\, r_e = 0$. So every $c_e$ is zero, because
  $E(G)$ is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Type~I lift of row-independence]
  \label{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-lift}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_edgeSetRowIndependent_lift}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeI, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, and let $p$ be a framework on
  $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is row-independent. Let $a, b \in V$
  with $p_a \ne p_b$, and let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I
  move at $a$ and $b$, with new vertex $v$. Then there is a framework
  $\hat p$ on $V \cup \{v\}$ that agrees with $p$ on $V$ and at which
  $E(G')$ is row-independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}
  Since $p_a \ne p_b$, there is a unique line through $p_a$ and $p_b$.
  Choose a point $q$ off this line. Then
  $q - p_a$ and $q - p_b$ are linearly independent. Let $\hat p$ extend
  $p$ by placing $v$ at $q$. Then $E(G')$ is row-independent at
  $\hat p$ (\cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}).
\end{proof}

A vertex of degree one, a pendant vertex, is put back by the Type~I
move at $a$ and $a$, which adds a new vertex $v$ and the single edge
$va$ (\cref{def:typeI}). Jord\'an's proof treats this case together
with the Type~I move.

\begin{lemma}[Pendant lift of row-independence]
  \label{lem:pendant-rowIndependent-lift}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeI_pendant_edgeSetRowIndependent_lift}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $p$ be a framework on $V$
  in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is row-independent, and let $a \in V$.
  Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~I move at $a$ and $a$, with
  new vertex $v$. Then there is a framework $\hat p$ on $V \cup \{v\}$
  that agrees with $p$ on $V$ and at which $E(G')$ is row-independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply, lem:typeI-edgeSet}
  Choose a point $q \ne p_a$, and let $\hat p$ extend $p$ by placing
  $v$ at $q$. Let $r_e$, $\hat r_e$ and $x^{\alpha}$ be as in the proof
  of \cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}. The edges of $G'$ are the
  edges of $G$ and the new edge $va$. Suppose that
  $\sum_{e \in E(G)} c_e\, \hat r_e + c_a\, \hat r_{va} = 0$.
  Evaluating this relation at $x^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha = q - p_a$
  gives $c_a |q - p_a|^{2} = 0$, so $c_a = 0$. Then
  $\sum_e c_e\, \hat r_e = 0$, and every $c_e$ is zero, as in the proof
  of \cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Row-independence under the Type~II move]
  \label{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-extend}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_edgeSetRowIndependent_extend}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $ab$ be an edge of $G$,
  and let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is
  row-independent. Let $c \in V$, and let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by
  the Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$, with new vertex $v$. Let
  $q = p_a + s\,(p_b - p_a)$ for a real number $s \ne 0, 1$, suppose
  that $q - p_a$ and $q - p_c$ are linearly independent, and let
  $\hat p$ be the framework on $V \cup \{v\}$ that agrees with $p$ on
  $V$ and places $v$ at $q$. Then $E(G')$ is row-independent at
  $\hat p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply, lem:typeII-edgeSet}
  Let $r_e$, $\hat r_e$, $x^{\alpha}$ and $\rho$ be as in the proof of
  \cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-extend}. The vertices $a$, $b$ and $c$
  are distinct: $a \ne b$ since $ab$ is an edge, and $c \ne a, b$ since
  $q - p_a$ and $q - p_c$ are linearly independent. So the edges of
  $G'$ are the edges of $G$ other than $ab$ and the three new edges
  $va$, $vb$ and $vc$ (\cref{lem:typeII-edgeSet}). Suppose that
  \[
    \sum_{e} c_e\, \hat r_e + c_a\, \hat r_{va} + c_b\, \hat r_{vb}
      + c_c\, \hat r_{vc} \;=\; 0,
  \]
  where $e$ runs over the edges of $G$ other than $ab$.

  Let $\delta = p_b - p_a$, so that $q - p_a = s\,\delta$ and
  $q - p_b = (s - 1)\,\delta$. Evaluating the relation at $x^{\alpha}$
  gives
  \[
    \bigl\langle (s c_a + (s - 1) c_b)\,\delta + c_c\,(q - p_c),\;
      \alpha \bigr\rangle \;=\; 0
  \]
  for every $\alpha$, so
  $(s c_a + (s - 1) c_b)\,\delta + c_c\,(q - p_c) = 0$. Since
  $s \ne 0$, the vector $\delta$ is a multiple of $q - p_a$, and
  $q - p_a$ and $q - p_c$ are linearly independent. Hence
  $s c_a + (s - 1) c_b = 0$ and $c_c = 0$.

  We use the first equation to recover the row of the deleted edge
  $ab$, as in the proof of
  \cref{thm:typeII-isInfinitesimallyRigid-extend}. For every $x$ we
  have, by \cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply} and the first equation,
  \[
    c_a\, \hat r_{va}(x) + c_b\, \hat r_{vb}(x)
    \;=\; s c_a \langle \delta,\, x_v - x_a \rangle
      + (s - 1) c_b \langle \delta,\, x_v - x_b \rangle
    \;=\; s c_a \langle \delta,\, x_b - x_a \rangle,
  \]
  and $\langle \delta,\, x_b - x_a \rangle
  = \langle p_a - p_b,\, x_a - x_b \rangle = r_{ab}(\rho(x))$. So
  $c_a\, \hat r_{va} + c_b\, \hat r_{vb} = s c_a\, (r_{ab} \circ \rho)$.
  The other rows satisfy $\hat r_e = r_e \circ \rho$, so the relation
  becomes
  \[
    \Bigl(\sum_{e} c_e\, r_e + s c_a\, r_{ab}\Bigr) \circ \rho \;=\; 0.
  \]
  Since $\rho$ is surjective, $\sum_e c_e\, r_e + s c_a\, r_{ab} = 0$.
  As $E(G)$ is row-independent at $p$, every $c_e$ is zero and
  $s c_a = 0$. So $c_a = 0$, since $s \ne 0$, and then $c_b = 0$, since
  $s \ne 1$.
\end{proof}

For the Type~II move we move one point of the framework slightly, as
in the proof of \cref{thm:typeII-isGenericallyRigidInj-two}. This
needs row-independence to be an open condition on the framework, as
infinitesimal rigidity is (\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-eventually}).
In the induction we also transport row-independence along graph
isomorphisms, as we did for infinitesimal rigidity
(\cref{lem:isInfinitesimallyRigid-iso}).

\begin{lemma}[Row-independence is an open condition on the framework]
  \label{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.EdgeSetRowIndependent.eventually}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent, def:rigidityRow}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $I \subseteq E(G)$, and
  let $p_0$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$ at which $I$ is
  row-independent. Then $I$ is row-independent at every framework in
  some neighbourhood of $p_0$ in $(\R^{d})^{V}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Fix a basis $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n$ of $(\R^{d})^{V}$. A linear
  functional $\ell$ on $(\R^{d})^{V}$ is determined by the vector
  $(\ell(\xi_1), \dots, \ell(\xi_n)) \in \R^{n}$, and the map sending
  $\ell$ to this vector is a linear isomorphism. For a framework $p$,
  let $r_e$ be the rigidity row of $G$ at $p$ indexed by $e$
  (\cref{def:rigidityRow}). By
  \cref{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow} and
  this isomorphism, $I$ is row-independent at $p$ if and only if the
  vectors $(r_e(\xi_1), \dots, r_e(\xi_n))$, for $e \in I$,
  are linearly independent in $\R^{n}$.

  For $e = uw$, the entry $r_e(\xi_k)$ is the inner product of
  $p_u - p_w$ with $(\xi_k)_u - (\xi_k)_w$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), so it depends continuously on $p$.
  Linear independence of finitely many vectors in $\R^{n}$ is an open
  condition. Hence it holds for every $p$ in some neighbourhood of
  $p_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Row-independence transports along a graph isomorphism]
  \label{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iso}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.EdgeSetRowIndependent.iso}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent, def:rigidityRow}
  Let $G$ and $H$ be graphs on finite sets $V$ and $W$, let $\varphi$
  be an isomorphism from $G$ to $H$, and let $q$ be a framework on $W$
  in $\R^{d}$. If $E(H)$ is row-independent at $q$, then $E(G)$ is
  row-independent at $q \circ \varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow,
    lem:rigidityMap-apply}
  Let $L : (\R^{d})^{V} \to (\R^{d})^{W}$ be the linear isomorphism
  $x \mapsto x \circ \varphi^{-1}$. For an edge $e = uv$ of $G$, let
  $r_e$ be the rigidity row of $G$ at $q \circ \varphi$ indexed by $e$,
  and let $r^{H}_{\varphi(e)}$ be the rigidity row of $H$ at $q$
  indexed by the edge $\varphi(u)\varphi(v)$ (\cref{def:rigidityRow}).
  Both $r_e(x)$ and $r^{H}_{\varphi(e)}(L(x))$ equal
  $\langle q_{\varphi(u)} - q_{\varphi(v)},\, x_u - x_v \rangle$, so
  $r_e = r^{H}_{\varphi(e)} \circ L$.

  Suppose that $\sum_e c_e\, r_e = 0$. Then
  $\bigl(\sum_e c_e\, r^{H}_{\varphi(e)}\bigr) \circ L = 0$, and so
  $\sum_e c_e\, r^{H}_{\varphi(e)} = 0$, since $L$ is surjective. The
  map $e \mapsto \varphi(e)$ is a bijection from the edges of $G$ to
  those of $H$, and $E(H)$ is row-independent at $q$. So every $c_e$
  is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Type~II lift of row-independence]
  \label{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-lift}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.Henneberg.typeII_edgeSetRowIndependent_lift}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:typeII, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    lem:typeII-rowIndependent-extend}
  Let $G$ be a graph on a finite set $V$, let $ab$ be an edge of $G$,
  and let $c \in V$ be distinct from $a$ and $b$. Let $p$ be a
  framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is row-independent, and
  let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by the Type~II move on $ab$ and $c$,
  with new vertex $v$. Then there is a framework on $V \cup \{v\}$ at
  which $E(G')$ is row-independent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-extend,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}
  We shall place $v$ on the line through $p_a$ and $p_b$, which needs
  $p_c$ off this line (\cref{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-extend}). So we
  first move $p$, keeping $E(G)$ row-independent, until $p_a$, $p_b$
  and $p_c$ are not collinear. The row $r_{ab}$ of $R(G, p)$ is not
  zero, since $E(G)$ is row-independent at $p$. Hence $p_a \ne p_b$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}). If $p_b - p_a$ and $p_c - p_a$ are
  linearly independent, we keep $p$.

  Otherwise $p_c - p_a = \gamma\,(p_b - p_a)$ for some $\gamma \in \R$.
  Choose a vector $w \in \R^{2}$ such that $w$ and $p_b - p_a$ are
  linearly independent. For $t \in \R$, let $p^{t}$ be the framework
  that agrees with $p$ except at $c$, where $p^{t}_c = p_c + t\,w$. The
  map $t \mapsto p^{t}$ is continuous, and $p^{0} = p$. So $E(G)$ is
  row-independent at $p^{t}$ for every $t$ close enough to $0$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}). Since $c$ is neither
  $a$ nor $b$, we have $p^{t}_b - p^{t}_a = p_b - p_a$ and
  $p^{t}_c - p^{t}_a = \gamma\,(p_b - p_a) + t\,w$. For $t \ne 0$ these
  two vectors are linearly independent, because $w$ and $p_b - p_a$
  are. Choose $t \ne 0$ close enough to $0$, and replace $p$ by
  $p^{t}$.

  Now let $q = p_a + \tfrac{1}{2}(p_b - p_a)$, the midpoint of $p_a$
  and $p_b$. Then $q - p_a = \tfrac{1}{2}(p_b - p_a)$ and
  $q - p_c = \tfrac{1}{2}(p_b - p_a) - (p_c - p_a)$. These two vectors
  are linearly independent, because $p_b - p_a$ and $p_c - p_a$ are.
  So \cref{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-extend} applies with
  $s = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and $E(G')$ is row-independent at the framework
  that extends $p$ by placing $v$ at $q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Row-independent edge sets in the plane}
\label{sec:rigidity-matroid-hard-direction}

We now complete the induction. Together with
\cref{lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two}, its result characterizes the
edge sets that are row-independent at some framework in the plane.

\begin{theorem}[Every $(2, 3)$-sparse graph has a row-independent framework in the plane]
  \label{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsSparse.exists_rowIndependent_placement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isSparse, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse,
    lem:pendant-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:typeI-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:typeII-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iso}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $G$ be a $(2, 3)$-sparse graph on $V$.
  Then there is a framework $p$ on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is
  row-independent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse,
    lem:pendant-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:typeI-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:typeII-rowIndependent-lift,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iso,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}
  We use induction on $|V|$; Jord\'an's proof inducts on the number of
  edges instead. If $G$ has no edges, for instance if $|V| \le 1$, then
  $E(G)$ is row-independent at every framework, since the empty family
  of rows is linearly independent.

  Suppose that $G$ has an edge. By
  \cref{thm:isSparse-exists-typeI-or-typeII-reverse}, $G$ has a vertex
  $v$ of degree one, two or three for which the following graph $H$ on
  $V \setminus \{v\}$ is $(2,3)$-sparse. If $v$ has degree one or two,
  with neighbour $a$ or neighbours $a$ and $b$, then $H = G - v$. If
  $v$ has degree three, with neighbours $a$, $b$ and $c$, where $a$ and
  $b$ are not adjacent, then $H = G - v + ab$. By induction, $E(H)$ is
  row-independent at some framework $p$ on $V \setminus \{v\}$ in
  $\R^{2}$.

  Let $H'$ be obtained from $H$ by the move that puts $v$ back: the
  Type~I move at $a$ and $a$ if $v$ has degree one, the Type~I move at
  $a$ and $b$ if $v$ has degree two, and the Type~II move on $ab$ and
  $c$ if $v$ has degree three. The map $\varphi$ that sends $v$ to the
  new vertex of $H'$ and fixes every other vertex is an isomorphism
  from $G$ to $H'$ (\cref{fmlnote:reverse-vs-lift-form}). If $E(H')$ is
  row-independent at a framework $\hat p$, then $E(G)$ is
  row-independent at $\hat p \circ \varphi$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iso}). So it remains to find such a
  framework $\hat p$.

  If $v$ has degree one, then \cref{lem:pendant-rowIndependent-lift}
  gives $\hat p$. If $v$ has degree three, then $ab$ is an edge of $H$
  and $c$ is distinct from $a$ and $b$, so
  \cref{lem:typeII-rowIndependent-lift} gives $\hat p$.

  Let $v$ have degree two. If $p_a \ne p_b$, then
  \cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-lift} gives $\hat p$. Otherwise we
  first move the point $p_a$. Choose a nonzero vector $w \in \R^{2}$,
  and for $t \in \R$ let $p^{t}$ be the framework that agrees with $p$
  except at $a$, where $p^{t}_a = p_a + t\,w$. The map
  $t \mapsto p^{t}$ is continuous, and $p^{0} = p$. So $E(H)$ is
  row-independent at $p^{t}$ for every $t$ close enough to $0$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}). Since $a \ne b$, we
  have $p^{t}_a - p^{t}_b = t\,w$, which is not zero for $t \ne 0$.
  Choose $t \ne 0$ close enough to $0$. Then
  \cref{lem:typeI-rowIndependent-lift} applies to $p^{t}$ and gives
  $\hat p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Planar row-independence is $(2, 3)$-sparsity (Pollaczek-Geiringer, Laman)]
  \label{thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.edgeSet_rowIndependent_iff_isSparse_dim_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent, def:isSparse,
    thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement,
    lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two,
    lem:exists-affinelySpanning-of-eventually,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}
  Let $V$ be finite, let $G$ be a graph on $V$, and let
  $I \subseteq E(G)$. Then $I$ is row-independent at some framework on
  $V$ in $\R^{2}$ if and only if the spanning subgraph of $G$ with edge
  set $I$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement,
    lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two,
    lem:exists-affinelySpanning-of-eventually,
    lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}
  Suppose first that $I$ is row-independent at a framework $p_0$. Then
  $I$ is row-independent at every framework in some neighbourhood of
  $p_0$ (\cref{lem:edgeSet-rowIndependent-eventually}). So there is a
  framework $p$ at which $I$ is row-independent and no three of the
  points $p_v$ are collinear
  (\cref{lem:exists-affinelySpanning-of-eventually} at $d = 2$). Hence
  the spanning subgraph of $G$ with edge set $I$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse
  (\cref{lem:isSparse-of-rowIndependent-two}).

  Conversely, let $H$ be the spanning subgraph of $G$ with edge set
  $I$, and suppose that $H$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse. Then $E(H) = I$ is
  row-independent at some framework $p$
  (\cref{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}). For an edge
  $e = uv$ in $I$, the rigidity rows of $H$ and of $G$ at $p$ indexed
  by $e$ are the same functional
  $x \mapsto \langle p_u - p_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}). So $I$, as a set of edges of $G$, is
  row-independent at $p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every Laman graph has a row-independent framework in the plane]
  \label{cor:isLaman-exists-rowIndependent}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.IsLaman.exists_rowIndependent_placement}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:isLaman, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $G$ be a Laman graph on $V$. Then there is
  a framework $p$ on $V$ in $\R^{2}$ at which $E(G)$ is
  row-independent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}
  A Laman graph is $(2, 3)$-sparse (\cref{def:isLaman}), so
  \cref{thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The rigidity matroid as a count matroid}
\label{sec:rigidity-matroid-instance}

Let $V$ be finite. For $\ell < 2k$, the sets of edges of $K_V$ that
form $(k, \ell)$-sparse graphs are the independent sets of a matroid on
$E(K_V)$, the $(k, \ell)$-count matroid. We prove this in the next
section (\cref{sec:count-matroid}), and use it here for
$(k, \ell) = (2, 3)$, where $3 < 4$. We take the $(2, 3)$-count matroid
as the definition of the rigidity matroid in the plane, and then
identify its independent sets with the edge sets that are
row-independent at some framework.

\begin{definition}[Planar rigidity matroid]
  \label{def:rigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.rigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:countMatroid}
  Let $V$ be finite. The \emph{planar rigidity matroid} $\mathcal{R}(V)$
  is the $(2, 3)$-count matroid on $V$ (\cref{def:countMatroid}): the
  matroid on $E(K_V)$ whose independent sets are the sets
  $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ for which the graph on $V$ with edge set $I$ is
  $(2, 3)$-sparse.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Independent sets of the planar rigidity matroid]
  \label{thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.rigidityMatroid_indep_iff_edgeSetRowIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityMatroid, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse}
  Let $V$ be finite, and let $I \subseteq E(K_V)$. Then $I$ is
  independent in $\mathcal{R}(V)$ if and only if $I$ is row-independent
  at some framework on $V$ in $\R^{2}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:countMatroid-indep-iff,
    thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse}
  Since $I \subseteq E(K_V)$, the set $I$ is independent in
  $\mathcal{R}(V)$ if and only if the graph on $V$ with edge set $I$ is
  $(2, 3)$-sparse (\cref{thm:countMatroid-indep-iff}). This graph is
  the spanning subgraph of $K_V$ with edge set $I$. So the claim is
  \cref{thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse} at $G = K_V$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The rigidity matroid of a framework}
\label{sec:rigidity-matroid-linear-framing}

Jord\'an (p.~39) defines the rigidity matroid of a framework $(G, p)$
as the matroid on $E(G)$ whose independent sets are the edge sets that
are row-independent at $p$, and notes that all generic frameworks of
$G$ have the same rigidity matroid. By
\cref{thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}, a set of edges of
$K_V$ is independent in $\mathcal{R}(V)$ exactly when it is
row-independent at some framework, which may depend on the set. Rather
than define genericity, we find a single framework $p$ at which every
independent set of $\mathcal{R}(V)$ is row-independent. This
identifies $\mathcal{R}(V)$ with the rigidity matroid of the framework
$(K_V, p)$.

We now define the rigidity matroid of a framework of $K_V$, in any
dimension. Its formal construction starts from the rigidity rows of
$K_V$, extended to all unordered pairs of vertices.

\begin{definition}[The rigidity rows extended to all pairs of vertices]
  \label{def:linearRigidityRow}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.linearRigidityRow}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:rigidityRow}
  Let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$, and let $r_e$, for
  $e \in E(K_V)$, be the rigidity rows of $K_V$ at $p$
  (\cref{def:rigidityRow}). The \emph{extended rigidity rows} at $p$ are
  the functionals $\tilde r_e \in ((\R^{d})^{V})^{*}$, for unordered
  pairs $e$ of elements of $V$, given by $\tilde r_e = r_e$ if
  $e \in E(K_V)$ and $\tilde r_e = 0$ if $e = \{v, v\}$ for some
  $v \in V$.
\end{definition}
\begin{fmlnote}
  The extension is needed only for the formal construction of the
  matroid in the next definition. That construction,
  \texttt{Matroid.ofFun} in Nelson's matroid library, takes a function
  defined on all unordered pairs of vertices, together with a ground
  set. The pairs $\{v, v\}$ lie outside the ground set $E(K_V)$, so
  the values $\tilde r_{\{v, v\}} = 0$ do not affect the matroid.
\end{fmlnote}

\begin{definition}[Linear rigidity matroid at a framework]
  \label{def:linearRigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.linearRigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:linearRigidityRow}
  Let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$. The \emph{linear rigidity
  matroid} $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ at $p$ is the matroid on $E(K_V)$
  represented by the extended rigidity rows at $p$: a set
  $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ is independent if the functionals $\tilde r_e$,
  for $e \in I$, are linearly independent.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Independent sets of the linear rigidity matroid]
  \label{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.linearRigidityMatroid_indep_iff_edgeSetRowIndependent}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:linearRigidityMatroid, def:edgeSet-rowIndependent}
  Let $p$ be a framework on $V$ in $\R^{d}$, and let
  $I \subseteq E(K_V)$. Then $I$ is independent in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$
  if and only if $I$ is row-independent at $p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow}
  For $e \in E(K_V)$, the extended row $\tilde r_e$ is the rigidity row
  $r_e$ of $K_V$ at $p$. So $I$ is independent in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ if
  and only if the rows $r_e$, for $e \in I$, are linearly independent,
  that is, if and only if $I$ is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{lem:edgeSetRowIndependent-iff-linearIndepOn-rigidityRow}).
\end{proof}

So $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$ is the rigidity matroid of the framework
$(K_V, p)$, and it remains to choose $p$.

\begin{lemma}[A framework at which every $(2, 3)$-sparse edge set is row-independent]
  \label{lem:exists-uniform-rowIndependent-placement}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.exists_uniform_rowIndependent_placement_dim_two}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:edgeSet-rowIndependent,
    thm:isSparse-exists-rowIndependent-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite. Then there is a framework $p$ on $V$ in $\R^{2}$
  such that every $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ is row-independent at $p$
  whenever the graph on $V$ with edge set $I$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse}
  Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the family of sets $I \subseteq E(K_V)$ for
  which the graph on $V$ with edge set $I$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse. It is
  finite, since $E(K_V)$ is. We show, by induction on $n$, that for any
  $n$ sets in $\mathcal{S}$ there is a framework at which all of them
  are row-independent; for $n = |\mathcal{S}|$ this is the lemma. For
  $n = 0$ any framework will do.

  Let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ have $n$ elements, let $p_0$
  be a framework at which every set in $\mathcal{F}$ is row-independent,
  and let $I_0 \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \mathcal{F}$. Then $I_0$ is
  row-independent at some framework $q$
  (\cref{thm:edgeSet-rowIndependent-iff-isSparse} at $G = K_V$). For
  $t \in \R$, let $p^{t} = p_0 + t\,(q - p_0)$, so that $p^{0} = p_0$
  and $p^{1} = q$. The rigidity row of an edge $uv$ at a framework $p'$
  is $x \mapsto \langle p'_u - p'_v,\, x_u - x_v \rangle$
  (\cref{lem:rigidityMap-apply}), which depends linearly on $p'$. So the
  row $r^{t}_e$ of an edge $e$ at $p^{t}$ is $r^{0}_e + t\,s_e$, where
  $s_e$ is the row of $e$ at $q - p_0$.

  Let $I$ be a set in $\mathcal{F}$, or $I_0$, and let $t_0$ be $0$ or
  $1$ accordingly, so that the rows $r^{t_0}_e$, for $e \in I$, are
  linearly independent. Fix a basis of $((\R^{2})^{V})^{*}$. The
  coordinate vectors of the rows $r^{t}_e$, for $e \in I$, are the rows
  of a matrix $A + t\,B$, where $A$ and $B$ do not depend on $t$. Since
  the rows of $A + t_0 B$ are linearly independent, some
  $|I| \times |I|$ submatrix of $A + t_0 B$ has nonzero determinant. The
  determinant of the same submatrix of $A + t\,B$ is a polynomial in $t$
  that is nonzero at $t_0$, so it has finitely many roots. At every
  other $t$, the rows $r^{t}_e$, for $e \in I$, are linearly independent.

  Hence there are only finitely many $t \in \R$ at which some set in
  $\mathcal{F}$, or $I_0$, fails to be row-independent at $p^{t}$.
  Choose $t$ outside these finitely many values. Then every set in
  $\mathcal{F}$, and $I_0$, is row-independent at $p^{t}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The planar rigidity matroid as a linear matroid]
  \label{thm:linearRigidityMatroid-eq-rigidityMatroid}
  \lean{SimpleGraph.linearRigidityMatroid_eq_rigidityMatroid}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:linearRigidityMatroid, def:rigidityMatroid,
    thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent,
    lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff,
    lem:exists-uniform-rowIndependent-placement}
  Let $V$ be finite. Then there is a framework $p$ on $V$ in $\R^{2}$
  such that $\mathcal{L}_p(V) = \mathcal{R}(V)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Let $p$ be a framework as in
  \cref{lem:exists-uniform-rowIndependent-placement}. Both matroids have
  ground set $E(K_V)$, so it suffices to show that they have the same
  independent sets. Let $I \subseteq E(K_V)$. If $I$ is independent in
  $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$, then $I$ is row-independent at $p$
  (\cref{lem:linearRigidityMatroid-indep-iff}), and so $I$ is
  independent in $\mathcal{R}(V)$
  (\cref{thm:rigidityMatroid-indep-iff-rowIndependent}). Conversely, if
  $I$ is independent in $\mathcal{R}(V)$, then the graph on $V$ with
  edge set $I$ is $(2, 3)$-sparse (\cref{def:rigidityMatroid}). So $I$
  is row-independent at $p$, by the choice of $p$, and it is
  independent in $\mathcal{L}_p(V)$.
\end{proof}
