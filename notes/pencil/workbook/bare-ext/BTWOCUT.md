### Step BE27 — the cross-pair closure gap, which is the induction's one genuinely new obligation

> **(BE-28)(i)** *(the obligation, stated exactly)* Let `G = G₁ ∪ G₂` with
> `V₁ ∩ V₂ = {u,v}`. Take a pair `{x,y}` with `x ∈ V₁∖{u,v}` and
> `y ∈ V₂∖{u,v}` — a **cross pair**. Then `G/xy` is **not** decomposable at
> `{u,v}`: the merged vertex is adjacent to both sides, so `{u,v}` is no longer a
> separator (`{u,v,[xy]}` is a 3-separator instead). Consequently **the (BE-21)
> `def₃` law and the (BE-22)(i) fibre-product law both fail to apply to `G/xy`**,
> and **S-all**'s closure obligation at cross pairs has **no composition law behind
> it**. Explicitly, what would have to be shown is
>
> `dim { β₂(m₂) − β₁(m₁) : α₁(m₁) = α₂(m₂) } = δ^G_{xy}`,
>
> with `α_i(m) = m(v) − m(u)` and `β₁(m) = m(x) − m(u)`, `β₂(m) = m(y) − m(u)` —
> a **coupled** three-point condition, not a two-point one. **Same-side pairs are
> not affected**: contracting a pair inside one side leaves `{u,v}` a 2-cut, so
> the same two laws apply to `G/xy` and the obligation is bookkeeping.
>
> **This is the design decision's whole cost, and it is why **S-mark** exists.**
