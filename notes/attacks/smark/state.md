## Where it breaks <!-- budget 10 -->
The codimension counts of S16(iv) assume that an incidence `p_b ∈ π_a` between a hub plane and a non-adjacent vertex, or a coincidence `π_a = π_{a′}` of two hub planes, is a **non-trivial** condition on the irreducible partial tower `T_k` — i.e. that the incidences the edges impose never *force* an extra one. Concretely: order the hubs 2-degenerately; at the step placing a hub `z` with earlier hub neighbours `z₁, z₂`, stratum (a) needs `{π_{z₁} = π_{z₂}}` of codimension 3 in `T_k`, stratum (b) needs `{p_{z₁} ∈ π_{z₂}} ∩ {p_{z₂} ∈ π_{z₁}}` of codimension 2, and at a length-2 branch `z — y — z′` stratum (d) needs `{π_z = π_{z′}}` of codimension 3 (it is 3 when `z′`'s plane has a free `P¹` or `P²`, and reduces to point incidences of `z′`'s earlier neighbours when it is determined). The proof should be an induction along the tower: at the generic point of `T_k` no hub plane contains a non-neighbour's point and no two hub planes coincide, using girth `≥ 7` (two hubs never share two hub neighbours; a hub is never adjacent to two vertices of one placed branch) — S13(v) ran the same ordering argument for a path. A single habitat configuration violating a forced-looking incidence per stratum type would also do (a polynomial condition on an irreducible variety is trivial or codimension `≥ 1`), so the fresh reader can attack this with one exact draw per stratum on the tower's own construction.
