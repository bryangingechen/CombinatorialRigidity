## Where it breaks <!-- budget 10 -->
Two pure degeneracy types each carry surplus exactly `1` (Lemma P tight at an adjacent parallel pair, Lemma L tight at a hub path `a — z — b`), so when one connected pattern mixes them the surpluses may cancel: a class `A` that both has parallel members and is a point of a line `F`, or a class that is the third point of two lines (cost `3`, not `4`), or a line whose two defining classes are themselves parallel-heavy. The bound must be shown to survive the mixing. Concretely the first case to settle: hub path `a — z — b` with `φ(a), φ(z), φ(b)` collinear and `a ∥ a′` for a hub `a′` elsewhere on a line `F′ ∋ φ(a)`: cost `3 + 2 + 2`, and `J = J_{φ(a)} + 2`; Lemma P gives `J_{φ(a)} ≤ 2`, so `7 ≥ 5`, fine — the danger is only at patterns where many hyperedges see the same class, which girth and the habitat count limit (the `Y_A` bound `|Y_A| ≤ 1.5(|A| − 1)`). A brute-force checker over all rank patterns on `≤ 6` marked vertices (partition × partial linear space, codimension lower-bounded by the Jacobian rank of the pattern's minors at an exact random realisation) is the control; if it finds `cost ≤ J`, the pattern is the counterexample to (★) and the S16(iv) route.
